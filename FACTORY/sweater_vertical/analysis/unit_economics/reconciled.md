# Unit Economics — Reconciled (T9): Landed Cost (Analyst A) × D2C P&L (Analyst B)

**For:** the owner of a small, AI-designed D2C knitwear brand sourcing in Ludhiana, selling in California / Turlock.
**What this file is:** the merge of two T9 analysts — **A** (landed cost: India→California import stack) and **B** (D2C P&L: contribution-after-CAC viability) — into one balanced picture. It separates **agreed facts** (tagged), the **decision logic** both sides imply, and the **contested / uncertain points** where they diverge or over-reach. It downgrades over-confident claims and keeps every honesty tag. It feeds the DEEPDIVE writer and, downstream, T10's decision memo.

**Tag discipline (binding, inherited T0–T4):** **[FACT]** = cited public source / true-by-definition. **[ESTIMATE, conf]** = method + basis + confidence, carries the literal word *estimate*. **[UNKNOWN]** = genuinely not known; a valid, frequent answer (coverage is a floor). **Untagged** = arithmetic identity. **[DOWNGRADED]** = a claim one analyst stated more strongly than the evidence supports, weakened here.

> **The one sentence to remember.** *The two analysts **agree on the spine and disagree only on levels**: both find the **32% MMF duty on acrylic [FACT]** erases A2's factory-gate cost lead, both find **de-minimis is dead (Aug 29, 2025) [FACT]** so the venture must be a bulk importer→3PL not a ship-direct shop, and both treat the **India surcharge as a volatile band on top of MFN**; where they part is **how the line should be ranked for the business** — A ranks on **landed cost** (A1≈A2<A3, cotton the friendly anchor) while B ranks on **contribution-after-CAC** (A3 premium the only reliable earner, A2 a structural loss leader) — and these are **not contradictory, they are two different cuts** that combine into one instruction: **lead premium + bundles for survival, lean cotton for the margin-friendly core, and never lead with single acrylic on cold paid social.***

## 1. AGREED FACTS — both analysts independently land here (tagged)

These are the load-bearing claims both A and B reached separately. Convergence from two angles raises confidence; the tags are the *floor* each carried.

### 1a. Duty by fibre — the [FACT] both built on
| Article | HTS line | **MFN base duty** | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 cotton-blend core** | 6110.20.20 (cotton, nesoi) | **16.5%** | **[FACT — USITC HTS]**, both analysts identical |
| **A2 fine-acrylic value** | 6110.30.30 (man-made fibre) | **32%** | **[FACT — USITC HTS; CBP ruling N272437]**, both identical |

Sweaters of cotton are **16.5%** (6110.20.20), not 7% — the 7% is a different 6110 sub-line. **[FACT — correction; both A §1a and B §0/§7 independently caught it].** Every upstream margin reference using ~7% cotton duty must be corrected up. - **India gets no FTA preference on 6110** — the "Free" column lists Australia, Korea, Israel, etc., not India. **[FACT — USITC special-rate column, B §1a].**

### 1b. The ranking inversion — agreed mechanism, agreed direction
**Acrylic (A2) is the cheapest fibre ex-factory but carries the highest US duty (32%, ~2× cotton's 16.5% and wool's 16%).** Both analysts derive the same consequence: **A2's factory-gate cost advantage does not survive the US border** — landed, A1 (cotton) and A2 (acrylic) sit in essentially the same band because the 32% MMF duty eats acrylic's ~$0.5–1 ex-factory saving. **[FACT on the three rates; arithmetic on the inversion — both A §1a/§5b and B §1a/§1e converge].**

### 1c. De minimis is dead — agreed [FACT], agreed model-flip
**The <$800 Section 321 de-minimis duty-free exemption was suspended for ALL countries (EO 14324 signed Jul 30, 2025; effective 12:01 a.m. EDT Aug 29, 2025), India included.** **[FACT, dated — EO 14324; Federal Register 2025-16802; CBP fact sheet; both A §3 and B §1c].** Both reach the identical structural conclusion: the "knit in Ludhiana, ship each order direct duty-free" model the original D2C concept silently assumed **no longer exists**; the compliant path is **bulk-import to a US 3PL (Turlock/CA) → ship domestically.** This is a **model-flip, not a cost tweak.** [ESTIMATE, HIGH on the model-flip — both analysts, structural].

### 1d. The surcharge is real, additive, and volatile — agreed mechanism
Since 2025 the US layers country-specific reciprocal/IEEPA (now Section 122) tariffs **additively on top of MFN** for India. Both analysts model it as a **band, central case ~10% (Section 122, mid-2026), with history that hit a ~50% peak (Aug 2025) and a forward level that is [UNKNOWN].** **[FACT on the additive-stacking mechanism and the current ~10%; ESTIMATE/volatile on the forward level — both A §2 and B §1b].**

### 1e. Bulk beats per-order on every fee line — agreed [FACT]-driven
**MPF (FY2026): 0.3464% of entered value, min $33.58 / max $651.50 per formal entry. HMF: 0.125%, ocean only, no cap.** **[FACT — CBP, A §4c].** The **$651.50 MPF cap** plus per-entry broker/bond/ISF amortisation means per-order import is structurally punished and bulk rewarded — the fee structure *alone* (independent of duty) kills ship-direct. Both analysts assume the bulk-import model on these grounds. [FACT on rates; ESTIMATE on amortisation].

### 1f. Gross margin is a vanity number — agreed framing
Both treat gross margin over landed COGS (≈75–86%) as **healthy and misleading**, and both defer to the glossary's binding rule #8: **contribution margin after CAC, not gross margin, decides viability.** [FACT — glossary rule; both, B §0/§2 explicitly, A §0/§6 by deferral].

## 2. THE DECISION LOGIC — how the two cuts combine (not compete)

The single most important reconciliation point: **A and B are not two answers to one question; they are two different questions whose answers stack.**

- **A answers "what does it cost landed?"** → ranks the line on **landed COGS**: **A1 ≈ A2 < A3**, with cotton (A1) the structurally friendliest article into the US.
- **B answers "does the order make money after CAC?"** → ranks the line on **contribution-after-CAC**: **A3 > A1 > A2**, with premium (A3) the only article that reliably pays for its own customer and A2 a structural loss leader.

**These re-rank in opposite directions on purpose, and both are right within their boundary.** A2 is *cheapest-ish landed* (A's cut) **and** *least viable per order* (B's cut) — because its low landed cost still buys a low MSRP ceiling ($55) that cannot carry a $45–70 CAC. The combined instruction the two cuts produce:

1. **Lead premium (A3, $145) + 2-unit bundles (AOV ~$120–150)** — the only configs with positive contribution at realistic CAC and the only ones clearing LTV:CAC > 3 (B §3–4). The premium *price*, not the premium fibre, pays for the customer.
2. **Use A1 (cotton) as the margin-friendly core** — low-duty (~16.5%, second-lowest, ≈ wool; acrylic is the 32% outlier), landed and ex-factory stories agree (A §5b), but it is **CAC-fragile** (breaks even only at CAC < ~$38, below the realistic $45–70 floor) so it needs repeat purchase and a held-down blended CAC (B §3).
3. **Use A2 (acrylic) as a deliberate basket-filler inside bundles, never a standalone hero** — its landed cost is *not meaningfully below* cotton (A's finding) and it loses money alone at any realistic CAC (B's finding). Both cuts independently demote the "value driver."
4. **Hold blended CAC under ~$35** → acquisition cannot come mostly from cold paid social (where new-cohort apparel CAC is $45–70+); it must lean organic/community/affiliate (B §6). This is a **go-to-market precondition**, the single most consequential lever in the whole stack, and it sits in B's domain — A never models it.
5. **Plan as a bulk importer of record → CA 3PL → domestic ship** (both), keep fixed cost lean (~$1.5–3k/mo), don't launch into the Jul–Oct cluster peak (carried from T4).

**The honest combined verdict (both analysts support it):** the venture is **viable as a small premium/community brand, NOT as a value/performance-marketing brand.** The most dangerous mistake the upstream plan invites is **leading with A2** — the article that, after both the 32% duty (A) and CAC (B), cannot pay for itself.

## 3. CONTESTED / UNCERTAIN — where A and B diverge, and the reconciled call

These are the genuine tensions. Most are **level disagreements inside overlapping bands**, not contradictions — but they must be shown, not smoothed.

### 3a. Landed COGS per article — the numbers don't match; reconcile to the wider band
The two analysts price landed COGS differently because they make different freight/method choices:

| Article | A's landed band (§5a, full ex-factory band) | B's landed point (§1e, ex-factory **midpoint** + $2.5 freight) | Reconciled |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 cotton** | **≈ $6–14** | **≈ $11** | **$6–14, central ~$11** |
| **A2 acrylic** | **≈ $6–14** | **≈ $11** | **$6–14, central ~$11** |
| **A3 merino** | **≈ $9–31+** | **≈ $20** | **$9–31+, central ~$20** |

- **Why they differ:** A carries T4's *full* ex-factory band through (so its landed band is wide); B collapses to the ex-factory *midpoint* + a flat ~$2.5 inbound freight (so its landed number is a point inside A's band). **B's points sit inside A's bands — they are consistent, not conflicting.** [ESTIMATE, LOW-MED — both].
- **The reconciled call:** use **A's bands** as the honest envelope and **B's midpoints** as the planning central case, but label B's points as *illustrations inside the band*, not estimates of a tighter true value. Both inherit T4's **[UNKNOWN] knit+LINK job-work rate** as the dominant internal uncertainty — neither can narrow the band until that quote exists.
- **[DOWNGRADED]:** B's single-point landed figures ($11/$11/$20) read as more precise than the evidence supports. They are **midpoint illustrations**, not measured COGS; treat them as A's band-centres, not as quotes.

### 3b. The freight-per-unit allocation — a real spread, volume-dependent
- **A:** ocean FCL ~**$0.5–1.5/garment**, LCL ~$1–6 + fixed CFS fees, air only for samples (§4b). A's frame is *bulk at scale*.
- **B:** inbound allocation ~**$1.5–3.5/unit** at a few-hundred-unit launch run, spiking to **$5+** if forced to air a tiny capsule (§1d). B's frame is *micro-launch volume*.
- **Reconciled:** **not a conflict — a volume curve.** A's $0.5–1.5 is the right number at FCL scale; B's $1.5–3.5 is the right number at a first LCL launch run; both agree air is $3–6+ and only for samples. **Per-unit inbound freight falls in steps with volume** (the same step-function T4 §5 describes). Plan launch at **B's ~$1.5–3.5/unit**, model maturity at **A's ~$0.5–1.5**. [ESTIMATE, MED — reconciled as a curve].

### 3c. The surcharge timeline — same shape, different dates/precision
Both tell the same story (25%→50% peak→cut→SCOTUS-struck IEEPA→Section 122 ~10%→~Jul-2026 sunset, 301 risk), but:
- **A** dates it precisely and tags most steps **[FACT]** (Apr 2 2025 25%; Aug 27 2025 50% peak; Feb 6 2026 interim 18%; Feb 20–24 2026 SCOTUS + Section 122; ~Jul 24 2026 sunset).
- **B** tags the same sequence more cautiously — "**confidence MED on exact dates**" — and foregrounds the **USTR Section 301 investigation (Mar 2026)** as the live forward risk that could *replace* the lapsing baseline with a permanent sector-targeted tariff.
- **Reconciled:** keep **A's dated timeline** but adopt **B's caution on date precision** and **B's Section 301 forward flag**. The reconciled stance: **current ~10% [FACT]; the history (incl. the 50% spike) [FACT]; the forward level [UNKNOWN]**, with a **301-driven permanent replacement as the named tail risk dated to ~Jul 2026 — exactly the launch window.** Model a **0–15% band (central ~10%) plus an explicit 25–50% spike scenario** (A's tail case; B's stress column). [FACT history; ESTIMATE/volatile forward — reconciled].
- **[DOWNGRADED]:** A's precise dates are reported, not all equally verified; carry them with B's "MED on exact dates" caveat rather than as hardened [FACT] on every step. The *current ~10%* and the *direction* are the durable facts; the exact transition dates are reported history that should not be over-trusted.

### 3d. Stress-case landed COGS — the magnitudes roughly agree
- **A's tail (50% surcharge peak):** total duty A1 ~66.5% / A2 ~82% / A3 ~66%; an acrylic sweater's duty alone nearly equals its ex-factory cost.
- **B's stress (ex-factory-high + 25% surcharge + air freight):** landed A1/A2 ~$16–17, A3 ~$33+.
- **Reconciled:** both show a **~50%+ swing in landed COGS is a live, already-realised risk**, not hypothetical. A models it as a duty-rate spike; B as a combined cost-stack stress. **Carry both into T10's sensitivity: the venture must survive a return to 25–50% surcharge, not just today's ~10%.** [FACT that the spike happened; ESTIMATE that it could recur — both].

### 3e. CAC, returns, fixed cost — entirely B's domain, A is silent
A computes landed COGS and **explicitly stops there** (A §0: "not a P&L… contribution after CAC decides viability"). All the survival-deciding numbers are B's and are **[ESTIMATE]** benchmarks, not facts:
- **CAC $45–70** new-cohort apparel (worst-quartile $187, best $42); **+~35% CPM penalty for a no-name 2025–26 brand** → B models $45–70, not $25. [ESTIMATE, MED].
- **Returns ~22%** (range 20–30%; fit-driven; a fine-gauge fit-sensitive sweater is high-return by category), ~$10–12/return + 20% dead-stock. [ESTIMATE, MED-HIGH].
- **Payment 2.9% + $0.30** [FACT — Shopify 2026]; **ship-to-customer ~$7–8/order** [ESTIMATE, MED]; **fixed nut $1.5–6k/mo** [ESTIMATE].
- **Reconciled:** these are **uncontested only because A didn't model them** — they are not cross-examined by a second analyst, so they carry **single-analyst risk**. They are the *governing* terms (CAC is the same order of magnitude as the whole gross margin) yet the *softest* (all [ESTIMATE], all needing a live ad-account test). **Flag for T10: the viability verdict rests on B's un-cross-examined CAC/returns benchmarks; one $1–2k ad test collapses the biggest uncertainty.** [ESTIMATE — B only; flagged as single-source].

### 3f. The contribution math and the "where it fails" verdict — B only, but internally honest
B's contribution table (A2 $55 loses money at any realistic CAC; A1 $75 marginal/CAC-fragile; A3 $145 + bundles the only robust earners) is **arithmetic on §2 benchmark inputs** — method sound, absolute levels LOW-MED because landed COGS and CAC are both bands. [ESTIMATE, MED on direction, LOW-MED on levels — B only].
