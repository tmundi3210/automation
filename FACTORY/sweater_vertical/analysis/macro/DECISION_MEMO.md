## 3. WHERE THE MONEY IS — and WHAT KILLS IT

### Where the money is
- **Downstream, in US D2C margin capture — not at the factory gate.** Gross margin over landed COGS is **80–86%** (A3 at $145 on ~$20 landed ≈ 86%) **[ESTIMATE on levels; the spread is robust because landed COGS is single-digit dollars and MSRP is $55–145]**. Yarn is only **~$1–2.50 of fibre per sweater** and well-anchored, so the input you can least control matters least — **do not chase cheaper yarn; spend effort downstream where the margin lives.** **[ESTIMATE, HIGH on direction.]**
- **In the A3-led, bundled basket specifically.** A3 is the only article that reliably pays for its own customer; bundles amortise the one cost (CAC) that governs survival across a bigger basket. The premium hero + the bundle are the engine; A1 is the flywheel (repeat), A2 is filler.
- **In the buy-nothing posture as an asset, not just a cost-saver.** Owning only design + brand + import means **no captive plant to strand** — every rung is reversible; you can re-spec, re-source across the mesh, or pause. The cluster's one tailwind that actually helps a tiny brand — Ludhiana's walking-distance small-batch job-work mesh — fits an AI-design brand's small/fast/varied runs better than long-run offshore.

> **Honesty correction (carried from the ledger):** "80–86% gross margin" is a **vanity number**. The metric that decides viability is **contribution-after-CAC**, which is **negative-to-marginal on A1 and A2** at realistic CAC and **leaves A3 + bundles as the only earners.** Read the business on contribution, never on gross margin. And the cluster-winner analogy (Monte Carlo: FY26 ₹1,275.9 cr, ~17.8% EBITDA, 460+ EBOs **[FACT]**) proves only the **direction** — durable profit sits *downstream of the loom* — **not** that a 3-SKU buy-nothing US capsule succeeds; that firm is a vertically-integrated, captive-spinning, 460-EBO incumbent, the structural opposite of this venture.

### What kills it (ranked by how the venture actually dies)
1. **CAC > contribution — bleeding on acquisition (the primary kill).** CAC ($45–70 benchmark, no-name penalty, worst-quartile $187) is the same order of magnitude as the *entire* gross margin on an $11–20 landed COGS. If organic under-delivers and you fall back to cold paid social, the model loses money. The whole favourable verdict rests on **one un-cross-examined analyst's band [ESTIMATE, single-analyst]**. **This is the governing kill-risk.**
2. **The working-capital trap — the modal death.** You are **low *sunk*-capital but NOT low *working*-capital.** Dye-lot MOQ (500+ units/colour) + ocean lead time + returns settlement = a months-long cash-out that **bankrupts profitable-on-paper brands.** Its *size* is **[UNSIZED]** today because it depends on the [UNKNOWN] dye-MOQ and the [UNKNOWN] job-work rate — **you cannot fund a cycle you cannot compute.** Corrected framing: the venture is **hard to ruin catastrophically (bounded sunk loss) but easy to ruin ordinarily (working-capital starvation).**
3. **The unmeasurable cost foundation.** The **knit+LINK per-piece job-work rate is [UNKNOWN]** and is ~25–45% of the conversion subtotal — until measured, every ex-factory band, every landed band, the make-vs-buy crossover, *and* the size of the working-capital cycle are illustrations, not a model.
4. **Pilling on A2 (and any acrylic content).** Post-wash anti-pill grade on *our actual constructions* is **[UNKNOWN] until a PPS swatch**; **one viral pilling video is brand-ending.** Won/lost at the yarn PO; finishing buys only a fraction of a grade.
5. **The 32% MMF duty + chief-weight cliff.** Acrylic's whole cost lead dies at the border; worse, a supplier quietly under-blending a "cotton-blend" past 50% MMF flips a 16.5% garment to 32% and leaves you (the IOR) **mis-declaring duty** — a compliance liability on top of a cost one.
6. **The legal naming trap.** Building the brand on a **real athlete / celebrity / event / meme name** stacks **§3344 / §3344.1 / Lanham §43(a) / Class-25** liability (Giannis ~$2M anchor **[FACT]**) — a **hard legal no.** The name must be original/coined/Spanish-read/§1(b)-filed.

> **Honesty correction (carried):** the bull's "moat" is downgraded to **a real-but-undefensible content edge + an unvalidated quality gate + a low-capping (~$50–110k/yr) acquisition channel.** Keep the *tactical* use of all three; discard the words "moat," "durable," and "scalable" until proven.

## 5. THE CHEAPEST DE-RISKING TESTS — in order, with what each resolves

Total spend to convert the four hard gates from [UNKNOWN] toward [FACT]: **~$1–2k of ad spend + two on-the-ground quote runs + one swatch lab test.** Run them *before* any inventory cash. Ordered by verdict-weight per dollar.

1. **The ~$1–2k live blended-CAC ad test (G1) — RUN THIS FIRST.**
   *Resolves:* the single governing term. A small live campaign on your intended channels measures real blended CAC for a no-name brand. **Under ~$35 organic-led → the GO is earned; $45–70 with no organic path under $35 → the model is dead.** This one number breaks the entire advocate↔neutralize tie. **A test that can confirm the kill is a reason to withhold belief until it runs, not to pre-believe.**

2. **The Ludhiana per-piece knit + LINK job-work quote run (G2) — on-the-ground, in parallel.**
   *Resolves:* the cost foundation. A real ₹/piece for knitting and for linking (at the relevant gauges) pins every ex-factory band, every landed band, and the make-vs-buy crossover. **On-the-ground only — no desk research can fabricate it; do not let anyone invent a number here.** Until it lands, the cost model is an illustration.

3. **The dye-lot-MOQ quote (feeds G3) — on-the-ground, alongside #2.**
   *Resolves:* the *size* of the working-capital cycle. MOQ per colour/yarn lot + lead time, combined with #2's job-work rate, makes the months-long cash-out **computable** — and only then can you answer "can I fund it?" (G3). **You cannot fund a cycle you cannot compute**; this is what turns "low-capital" optimism into a real number.

4. **The PPS anti-pill swatch test (G4) — ~$30–250/lot.**
   *Resolves:* whether A2 (and any acrylic content) is a return machine. A pre-production swatch wash-tested to **ICI ≥3–4 post-wash on our actual constructions** validates the contractual gate on *our* knits, not a vendor's brochure. **Non-passing = NO-GO on A2 until fixed at the yarn PO.**

5. **(If proceeding) name clearance + a measured Central-Valley-youth hype read (G8) — pre-launch ship-gate.**
   *Resolves:* whether you can actually ship the brand and aim the content engine. Clear an **original, coined, Spanish-read, §1(b)-filed** name (the instinct-name is a hard legal no) and replace the *guessed* hype signal with a *measured* one. Cheap relative to inventory; required before the brand and its content have a target.

**Sequencing logic:** #1 alone can kill the venture, so it goes first and gates the spend on everything else; #2 and #3 are on-the-ground and run in parallel because #3 needs #2; #4 gates A2 specifically; #5 is the pre-launch ship-gate only if #1–#4 clear.
