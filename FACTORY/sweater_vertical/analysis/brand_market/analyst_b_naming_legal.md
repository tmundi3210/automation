# Analyst B — Naming, Brand Identity & Legal (T7 input)

**Role:** brand identity + naming + legal skeptic for the 3-article California/Turlock D2C knitwear line (A1 cotton-blend 12GG core · A2 fine-acrylic value · A3 merino/lambswool premium capsule, per `yarn/DEEPDIVE.md`). **My job is to be the adult in the room on legal risk** — the owner floated naming the brand after an athlete / a current event / a meme tied to California hype. The short version: **do not do the literal version of any of those.** This memo gives the honest read, then maps the safe, hype-*adjacent* path.

**Honesty tags (binding, per SCOPE_GLOSSARY):** `[FACT]` = cited public source; `[ESTIMATE]` = method + basis + confidence, literal word *estimate*; `[UNKNOWN]` = not reliably known. **I am not your lawyer and this is not legal advice** — it is a structured risk map to take *to* a trademark attorney before you spend money on a name. `[FACT — disclaimer]`

> **The one sentence to remember.** *A real athlete's or celebrity's name/face/persona is a triple landmine — California right of publicity (Civ. Code §3344) + federal false-endorsement (Lanham Act §43(a)) + trademark — and an event/meme name is a trademark-plus-transience trap; the entire upside the owner wants (California/youth/sport energy, current-hype relevance) is fully capturable with an **original, hype-adjacent coined name** that evokes the vibe and that **you can actually own**, clear it via a TESS-II knockout + classes 25/35 + common-law + domain/handle sweep, and budget ~$350–$2,000+/class to file.*

## 1. Naming strategy — the honest legal read (the skeptic's section)

The owner's three floated directions, in descending order of how fast they get you sued or left holding a worthless name:

### 1a. Naming the brand after a real athlete / celebrity — **HARD NO as floated. This is the worst idea on the table.**

Using a living (or recently-dead) real person's **name, nickname, likeness, signature, voice, or recognizable persona** to sell sweaters, *without a signed license*, exposes you to **three independent legal theories at once** — you don't have to lose all three, losing one is enough:

1. **California statutory right of publicity — Civil Code §3344.** Knowing use of another's **name, voice, signature, photograph, or likeness** on or in products / for advertising **without prior consent** is liable for the **greater of $750 or actual damages, PLUS the infringer's profits attributable to the use, PLUS punitive damages, PLUS the prevailing plaintiff's attorneys' fees.** `[FACT — Cal. Civ. Code §3344(a); statutory floor $750 and fee-shifting are the load-bearing facts]` The **attorneys'-fee shift is the killer for a micro-brand** — even a "small" case you lose means you pay *their* lawyers; even one you'd win is expensive to defend. Selling sweaters is squarely "products" + "advertising," i.e. dead-center in the statute. `[ESTIMATE: legal-reasoning, HIGH]`
2. **California common-law right of publicity** — broader than the statute: reaches *identity* evoked even without the literal name/photo (e.g. evocation via distinctive attributes). Runs *alongside* §3344, not instead of it. `[FACT — California recognizes both statutory and common-law right of publicity]`
3. **Federal false endorsement — Lanham Act §43(a) (15 U.S.C. §1125(a)).** Using a person's identity in a way that **falsely implies they endorse / are affiliated with the brand** is a federal unfair-competition claim — *no registration required to sue you*, and a celebrity plaintiff doesn't even need a registered mark. `[FACT — Lanham Act §43(a) supports false-endorsement claims]` A sweater brand *named after* an athlete inherently implies endorsement; that's the whole reason it'd "work" — and that's exactly the liability.

**Post-mortem doesn't save you:** California §3344.1 makes the right **descendible for 70 years after death** (covers any deceased personality dying after Jan 1, 1938). `[FACT — Cal. Civ. Code §3344.1; 70-year post-mortem term]` So "they're a legend who passed" is *not* a clearance — the heirs/estate own it. `[ESTIMATE: legal-reasoning, HIGH]`

**On top of all that, the athlete may own trademarks.** Top athletes routinely register their **name and slogans as trademarks in Class 25 (clothing)** specifically to control apparel. So even setting publicity aside, the name may be a registered mark you'd infringe. `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore, HIGH — athlete-name apparel marks are common]`

> **Verdict: do NOT wave this through.** There is no clever framing ("homage," "tribute," "inspired by," initials, a near-spelling) that makes the *literal* use safe; near-spellings and evocations are exactly what common-law publicity + false endorsement are built to catch. The *only* compliant way to use a real person is a **negotiated, written license / endorsement deal** with money and approvals attached — which is a different (and expensive) business model, not a naming hack. `[ESTIMATE: legal-reasoning, HIGH]`

**Special case the owner should hear explicitly — NIL athletes:** the current college-athlete **NIL (name/image/likeness)** market means even non-pro local/college athletes can now *license* and *enforce* their NIL. So "it's just a local college kid, not a celebrity" is **not** a safety margin — it's a *cheaper license to buy*, but still a license you must buy. `[FACT — NIL licensing is now an established, enforceable market]`

### 1b. Event names / meme names — **trademark risk + a transience problem that quietly wastes your brand spend**

Two separate failures:

- **Trademark / ownership risk.** Many "events" and viral phrases are **owned**: sports leagues, festivals, and organizers aggressively trademark event names and slogans in Class 25. Naming a sweater line after the event invites a 2(d) refusal and/or a cease-and-desist. Even a *meme* can be trademarked by whoever moved first (and a flood of opportunistic Class-25 meme filings is common). `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore, HIGH]` Worse, a generic meme phrase you *can't* register (because it's not distinctive / everyone uses it) gives you a name **you can't defend against copycats** — the value leaks out the bottom.
- **Transience — the business risk that's easy to miss.** A meme/event name is **dated the moment the hype cycle turns** (often weeks). You'd be permanently anchoring a multi-year brand (with inventory, dye-lot MOQs of 150–500 kg/colour per `yarn/DEEPDIVE.md`, and 6–12-week lead times) to a reference that's stale before your *second* drop ships. The hype calendar moves faster than your supply chain. `[ESTIMATE: design-framing, HIGH]`

> **Verdict:** event/meme names are a **drop/limited-collab tactic at most** (and only with cleared rights), **never the master brand.** Put transient energy in a *seasonal drop name or graphic*, keep the house brand evergreen and ownable.

### 1c. The safe path — **hype-ADJACENT, original, and clearable**

The goal is to **capture the *vibe* (California / youth / sport-adjacent / current energy) without borrowing anyone's *identity***. That means an **original coined or arbitrary/suggestive word** you can own outright. Concretely:

**Clearance pipeline (run for every candidate, in this order — cheapest knockout first):**
1. **USPTO knockout search.** TESS was retired (Nov 2023); the federal search is now the **cloud-based "Trademark Search" system (a.k.a. TESS-II)** at uspto.gov/trademarks/search. `[FACT — USPTO replaced TESS with a new cloud-based Trademark Search system in 2023]` Search the exact mark **and** phonetic/visual near-variants (a knockout catches exact hits but **misses sound-alikes, look-alikes, and common-law marks** — so search variants by hand). `[FACT — knockout-search limitations]`
2. **Search the right classes.** Apparel is **International Class 25 (clothing/footwear/headwear)**; if you'll run a webstore/retail, also **Class 35 (online retail store services)**. The USPTO treats **25 and 35 (and 18/14) as *coordinated classes*** when judging likelihood of confusion — so a conflicting mark in 35 can still sink a 25 application. **Search both.** `[FACT — Class 25 = apparel; Class 35 = retail; USPTO uses coordinated classes for likelihood-of-confusion]`
3. **Likelihood-of-confusion judgment, not exact-match.** A mark that's *confusingly similar in sight, sound, or meaning* on *related goods* is the #1 refusal ground (Lanham §2(d)). So "it's not identical" is not clearance — assess sound/appearance/meaning + relatedness of goods. `[FACT — likelihood of confusion is the most common refusal ground]`
4. **Common-law + state sweep.** Unregistered marks are still enforceable. Do a plain web search, Instagram/TikTok handle search, state business-name search, and Amazon/Etsy seller search. `[FACT — common-law (unregistered) trademark rights exist and are enforceable]`
5. **Domain + social handle availability.** `.com` exact-match + the matching IG/TikTok handle should be obtainable (or close) **before** you fall in love. A name you can't get the handle for is a marketing tax forever. `[ESTIMATE: D2C-practice, HIGH]`
6. **Then, and only then, a professional full clearance search** (attorney/search-firm) before filing — the knockout is a *filter*, not a green light. `[ESTIMATE: legal-practice, HIGH]`

**Distinctiveness — pick a name from the strong end of the spectrum.** Trademark strength runs: **fanciful/coined (strongest) → arbitrary → suggestive → descriptive (weak, needs secondary meaning) → generic (unregistrable).** `[FACT — trademark distinctiveness spectrum]` Avoid descriptive names ("California Knitwear," "SoftSweater") — weak, hard to register, hard to defend. Aim **coined/arbitrary/suggestive**. This is *also* why a meme phrase is bad (often generic/descriptive) and a coined word is good (inherently distinctive + handle-available).

## 2. What makes a defensible apparel brand name + identity

A name is **defensible** when it is simultaneously *ownable in law* and *workable in market*:

- **Legally ownable:** distinctive (coined/arbitrary/suggestive), clears classes 25/35 knockout + common-law, registrable, not confusingly similar to a live mark, and **borrows no real person's identity**. `[FACT/ESTIMATE blend, HIGH]`
- **Available across surfaces:** `.com` + IG + TikTok handle obtainable; spellable/sayable on first hearing (no "how do you spell that?" tax); not an unintended slur/obscenity in Spanish given the Central Valley's large Spanish-speaking population (Turlock/Central Valley) — **check the Spanish reading of any candidate.** `[ESTIMATE: market-fit, MED-HIGH]`
- **Roomy, not boxed-in:** doesn't hard-code one fibre/season/place you'll outgrow (the line spans cotton core → acrylic value → wool capsule across a 12-month calendar, per `yarn/DEEPDIVE.md`; don't name it "Merino___" or "Summer___").
- **Identity that carries the vibe legitimately:** the *energy* (California light/warmth, youth, sport-adjacent, drop culture) lives in the **wordmark, palette, typography, photography, and drop names** — not in a borrowed face. A clean evergreen house brand + rotating *cleared* drop/capsule names is the durable structure (house brand evergreen; transient hype quarantined to drop names). `[ESTIMATE: brand-design, HIGH]`
- **Fits the product truth:** the line is *elevated lightweight basics for a warm climate* (12GG fine-gauge core), not heavy winter woollens — the identity should read clean/light/Californian, not alpine/heritage-wool. This keeps brand promise and product honest (anti-pill spec, breathable cotton core).

## 3. Illustrative naming DIRECTIONS (not final names — *vibes to brief, then clear*)

**These are illustrative DIRECTIONS to show the legitimate lane, NOT cleared names — every candidate must run the §1c pipeline and a Spanish-reading check before use.** `[ESTIMATE: creative-illustration; explicitly UNCLEARED]` The point is that California/youth/sport-adjacent energy is *fully reachable* without anyone's identity:

1. **Coined/abstract "warm-light" words** — invented or arbitrary words evoking sun, haze, coast, valley light, dawn — leaning on *climate and feeling*, not a person. (Coined = strongest mark + best handle odds.)
2. **Geography-of-feeling, not literal place** — evoke the Central Valley / California-casual sensibility via an *oblique, coinable* reference rather than a literal city/landmark name (literal place names register weakly and box you in). Avoid actual event/venue/team names.
3. **Sport-*adjacent* motion words** — verbs/nouns of ease, movement, warmth, off-duty athletic energy ("the vibe after the game," not the game/league/athlete). Captures sport energy with zero NIL exposure.
4. **Youth/drop-culture cadence** — short, punchy, lowercase, ownable coinages that fit a drop/scarcity model (per GLOSSARY §8 "drop") and read native on IG/TikTok — built to be *the* handle, not chasing a meme.
5. **Material-honest-but-elevated** — a suggestive (not descriptive) nod to soft/light knitwear that hints at the product without naming the fibre or a season — registrable as suggestive, roomy across the 3 articles.

Across all five: **coin or arbitrarily-assign the word, then clear it.** None depend on a real person, event, or meme — so the *upside the owner wanted is preserved and the entire §1 landmine is removed.*

## 4. Trademark-registration basics + cost/time `[ESTIMATE]`

**Basics.** You can use **™** the moment you adopt a mark in commerce (signals a claim, no filing needed); **®** is only legal **after federal registration**. `[FACT]` File at the USPTO via the **Trademark Center** (the new application portal; the old TEAS Plus / TEAS Standard two-tier was replaced in 2025 by a **single base application**). `[FACT — 2025 USPTO move to a single base application fee, effective ~Jan 18 / Feb 18 2025]` File based on **§1(a) "use in commerce"** (already selling) or **§1(b) "intent to use"** (lock the name *before* launch — recommended here so the name is reserved while you tool up the supply chain). `[FACT — 1(a) vs 1(b) bases]`

**Cost (USPTO filing fees only — fees are *per class*):**
- **Base application fee: ~$350 per class** (2025 single base fee). `[FACT — USPTO 2025 base application fee $350/class]`
- **+ ~$200/class surcharge** if you write a **custom (free-text) goods/services description** instead of picking from the USPTO's pre-approved **ID Manual** → *pick from the ID Manual to avoid this.* `[FACT — $200/class custom-identification surcharge, 2025]`
- **+ ~$200/class** for **over-long identifications** (per extra 1,000 characters). `[FACT — 2025 long-ID surcharge]`
- **+ ~$100–$200/class** later if filing **§1(b)** (Statement of Use / extension fees come due after allowance). `[ESTIMATE: from USPTO post-allowance fee structure, MED]`
- **Two classes (25 + 35)** ≈ **2 × base** = on the order of **~$700+** in government fees alone. `[ESTIMATE: arithmetic on the $350/class fact, HIGH]`
- **Attorney / search-firm fees (separate, optional but advised):** a professional **clearance search ≈ $300–$1,000+**, and **filing prep ≈ $500–$1,500/class**, varying widely by firm. **Total realistic out-the-door for a clean, attorney-filed single-class mark ≈ $1,000–$2,500; DIY filing can be ~$350–$550/class** if you self-search and self-clear (riskier). `[ESTIMATE: triangulated from 2025 USPTO fees + typical IP-firm pricing; MED confidence on the attorney bands]`

**Time.** **~12–18 months** from filing to registration in a clean case; **longer (multiple years) if you draw an Office Action, an opposition, or a likelihood-of-confusion refusal.** After examination the mark is **published for opposition (~30-day window)** during which third parties can challenge. `[FACT — USPTO: typically 12–18 months; publication/opposition stage]` **Implication for the drop calendar:** file **§1(b) intent-to-use *early*** (months before launch) — registration won't finish before your first drop, and that's fine; the **filing date secures priority** while the goods ship. `[ESTIMATE: legal-practice, HIGH]`

**Maintenance (so the brain knows the recurring cost exists):** federal registrations require **§8 declaration of use between years 5–6**, and **§8 + §9 renewal every 10 years** — registration is not "file once, own forever." `[FACT — USPTO §8/§9 maintenance windows]`

## 5. Bottom line for the owner

1. **The athlete/celebrity name idea is a hard no as floated** — it stacks **California §3344 (≥$750 + profits + punitives + *their* attorneys' fees), common-law publicity, and Lanham §43(a) false-endorsement**, and the athlete may *also* own the Class-25 mark. Post-mortem (70-yr §3344.1) and "just a local NIL kid" are **not** loopholes. The only legal version is a **paid, written license** — a different business, not a naming trick. `[FACT/ESTIMATE blend, HIGH]`
2. **Event/meme names = trademark risk + transience.** At most a *cleared* limited-drop name; **never the house brand** (your 6–12-wk supply chain outlives the meme).
3. **The vibe is fully reachable legitimately:** an **original, coined/arbitrary/suggestive, hype-ADJACENT** name owns the California/youth/sport energy *and* is yours to defend.
4. **Clear every candidate:** USPTO **Trademark Search (TESS-II)** knockout → **classes 25 + 35 (coordinated)** → likelihood-of-confusion (sound/look/meaning) → common-law + handle/state sweep → **.com + IG/TikTok** → **professional full search before filing** → **Spanish-reading check** for the Central Valley.
5. **Budget/time:** **~$350/class** USPTO base fee (pick ID-Manual descriptions to dodge the +$200 surcharges); realistic attorney-filed **~$1,000–$2,500**; **~12–18 months** to register; **file §1(b) intent-to-use early** to lock priority before the launch drop. `[ESTIMATE/FACT blend]`
