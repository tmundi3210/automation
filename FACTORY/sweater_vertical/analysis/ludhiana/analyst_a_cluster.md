## 1. What the cluster is and its scale

**Identity.** Ludhiana is India's knitwear/"hosiery" capital and the dominant centre for **woollen + acrylic** knitwear specifically. Nicknamed "the Manchester of India." It pairs a large downstream garment cluster with a heavy upstream **yarn-spinning** base (cotton, acrylic, blended yarn), making it unusually vertically deep for an Indian apparel town.

**Share of national woollen/acrylic knitwear.**
- ~95% of India's woollen + acrylic knitwear is made in Ludhiana [FACT — filmingindo / txcindia cluster profile; widely repeated figure, treat the exact % as ~90–95% band].
- ~90% of the country's woollen hosiery industry sits in Ludhiana [FACT — same sources].

**Units & employment.**
- ~10,000–12,000 units across the value chain (spinning, knitting, dyeing/processing, sewing, chemicals, accessories), of which ~2,500 are export+domestic garment production plants; ~10,000 hosiery/knit garment units [FACT — txcindia/filmingindo cluster profiles; [ESTIMATE] on precision — counts vary by source and year, medium confidence].
- Direct + indirect employment ~350,000–400,000 ("4 lakh") people [FACT — cluster profiles]. Heavily **migrant** labour from Uttar Pradesh & Bihar [FACT — Fibre2Fashion, ThePrint].

**Output & exports (order of magnitude).**
- Installed/annual hosiery production on the order of ₹5,000 crore (~US$600M), of which roughly ₹1,000–2,000 crore (~US$120–240M) is exported [FACT — cluster profiles; [ESTIMATE] these are dated, pre-2020-ish figures, low-medium confidence on current value]. Ludhiana is a designated **"Town of Export Excellence"** (Dept. of Commerce, GoI) [FACT].
- **Critical framing:** Ludhiana is overwhelmingly a **domestic-market** cluster. Its export share is small versus Tirupur (see §4). Its strength is winter/woollen knitwear for the Indian market, where domestic seasonal demand and brand distribution — not exports — drive the economics.

**Upstream spinning base.** Ludhiana/Punjab hosts some of India's largest spinners (Vardhman, Nahar Spinning, Sportking) running >2 million spindles combined [ESTIMATE — sum of company-disclosed spindle counts below, high confidence], supplying cotton and acrylic/blended yarn both locally and to other Indian clusters and exports.

## 3. HOW they survive there (the competitiveness machine)

1. **Vertical integration (fibre→yarn→fabric→garment).** Vardhman runs the entire spine; Duke is integrated knitting→dyeing→finishing→print; Nahar/Oswal spans spinning→knitwear→brand. [FACT — company descriptions]. **Mechanism:** integration captures margin at each stage, smooths downturns (reallocate within the chain), and de-risks input price/quality — Vardhman explicitly cites flexibility to "ramp faster" through cycles [FACT — Vardhman]. For a cluster otherwise disadvantaged on logistics, owning the chain is the moat.
2. **Captive / self-generated power.** Spinning is power-intensive; grid power in Punjab is costly/unreliable, so large players run **captive power** (thermal historically, now adding solar — Sportking added solar in FY24, Vardhman has captive units) [FACT — company disclosures]. **Mechanism:** power is a top-3 cost in spinning; captive generation insulates against tariff and outage, a decisive survival lever for the big firms and a barrier the small units cannot match.
3. **Low labour cost + migrant supply.** Cheap migrant labour (UP/Bihar) historically underwrote competitiveness [FACT]. India garment wage ~US$145–172/month [FACT — ILO/comparative], far below China but ABOVE Bangladesh.
4. **Agglomeration / cluster effects.** Dense co-location of spinners, knitters, dyers, accessory makers, machine dealers, job-workers and traders → low search cost, fast turnaround, shared tacit know-how, a "backward link" (the cluster grew its own upstream) [FACT — cluster studies]. **Mechanism:** the cluster IS the infrastructure; a small unit can subcontract knitting, dyeing, linking, embroidery within walking distance.
5. **Scale (the big spinners).** >1M-spindle plants (Vardhman) give unit-cost and export-house status that small units can't reach [FACT].
6. **Domestic-market dominance, not exports.** The core survival fact: Ludhiana owns ~90–95% of India's **woollen/acrylic** knitwear for the **domestic** winter market — a defensible niche where its acrylic/wool know-how and brand distribution (Monte Carlo etc.) beat coastal cotton-knit hubs. Brands are pivoting manufacturing → **retail/EBO** to capture downstream margin [FACT — apparelsourcingweek]. [ESTIMATE: discussion] The domestic, seasonal, brand-led model is what keeps Ludhiana alive while its export competitiveness erodes.
7. **Government incentives.** "Town of Export Excellence" status; eligibility for national textile schemes (historically TUFS/ATUFS capital subsidy; state Punjab industrial-policy power/stamp-duty incentives) [FACT — designation; [ESTIMATE] on which specific schemes each firm draws, low confidence — varies by year/firm].

## 4. Where the economically-better alternatives are — and WHY

### Domestic alternatives
| Cluster | What it does | Why it can be economically better | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tirupur** (Tamil Nadu) | **Cotton knitwear, export hub.** ~6,000+ factories. | FY24-25 knitwear exports **~₹40,000 cr** (~US$5B, +20% YoY) [FACT — Textile Insights]; **~90% of India's cotton-knit exports, ~55% of all knitwear exports**, ~80% of hosiery exports [FACT]. Coastal (near Tuticorin/Chennai ports → low export logistics cost), cotton-belt proximity, cheaper early labour, export ecosystem & buyer relationships [FACT]. **This is the structurally superior location for EXPORT cotton knits.** Ludhiana cannot match it on export cotton. | [FACT] |
| **Panipat** (Haryana) | **Home textiles + recycled-fibre / shoddy yarn.** | India's largest home-textile export cluster (~US$1B+ exports); processes >100,000 tonnes/yr of recycled/discarded clothing [FACT]. Different product, but the **recycled-wool/shoddy** capability competes with Ludhiana on low-cost woollen feedstock. | [FACT] |
| **Bhilwara** (Rajasthan) | **Synthetic suiting / polyester-viscose.** | >1 million synthetic spindles — "Asia's largest synthetic textile hub" [FACT]; integrated suiting (PV) supply. Competes on **synthetic yarn/fabric scale**, not knitwear directly. | [FACT] |

**Why Ludhiana loses on export economics:** landlocked (far from any sea port → high export freight & lead time), Punjab power cost/reliability issues, high working-capital interest rates, outdated migrant skill base, and product lock-in to seasonal woollens [FACT — Fibre2Fashion, ThePrint, TheCitizen]. ThePrint reported ~30% of the workforce left as work shrank [FACT — pre/around late-2010s, dated]. Net: for **cotton-knit exports**, Tirupur dominates; Ludhiana's edge is **domestic woollen/acrylic**.

| Country | Labour cost anchor | Trade/scale edge | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bangladesh** | RMG min wage **~US$95–114/month** (12,500 BDT); ~25% below India, ~40% below Vietnam [FACT — ILO/Cornell GLI 2024] | World's #2 RMG exporter; cotton-knit specialised (~56% of RMG value); EU **EBA duty-free** access (graduating 2026, safeguards looming) [FACT] | [FACT] |

**Why offshore can beat Ludhiana:** Bangladesh on **pure labour cost + EU duty-free**; Vietnam on **productivity + trade-deal market access**; China on **scale + integrated ecosystem**. India (incl. Ludhiana) sits in an awkward middle — labour cheaper than Vietnam/China but pricier than Bangladesh, without Bangladesh's tariff preference or Vietnam's FTA web. [ESTIMATE: synthesis, medium-high confidence] For a price-driven export buyer, Ludhiana is rarely the cost-optimal source; its rationale is **woollen/acrylic specialisation, domestic-market access, and vertical-integration speed**, not landed cost.

## 5. High-level cost structure (yarn / power / labour share)

Spinning and garment-making have different cost stacks; both matter for a vertical play.

**Yarn-spinning cost stack (large integrated spinner, e.g. Vardhman-class):** [ESTIMATE: anchored to Vardhman AR + industry norms, medium confidence]
- **Raw material (fibre/cotton): ~50–60%** of revenue/cost. Anchor: Vardhman FY20 raw material consumed = **54% of total revenue** [FACT].
- **Power & fuel: ~12–18%** of cost — spinning is electricity-intensive; this is why captive power is decisive [ESTIMATE — industry norm; high confidence it is the #2 line].
- **Labour/employee: ~8–12%** [ESTIMATE — Indian spinning norm, medium confidence].
- Remainder: stores/packing, finance cost, depreciation, overhead.
- **Takeaway:** in spinning, the controllable competitive levers are **fibre procurement** and **power** — exactly the two things the big Ludhiana groups internalise (integration + captive power).

**Knit-garment cost stack (sweater/knitwear):** [ESTIMATE: industry norm, medium confidence]
- **Yarn/material: ~50–65%** of garment ex-factory cost (yarn is the dominant input for a sweater).
- **Labour (knitting, linking, finishing, checking): ~15–25%** — knitwear is more labour-intensive than woven, and linking/finishing is hand-skilled.
- **Power: ~5–10%**; overhead/dyeing/finishing/packing: remainder.
- **Takeaway:** for the downstream brand, **yarn cost and labour** dominate; brand margin is captured in distribution/retail (why Ludhiana houses are pushing into EBO retail).

**Single most load-bearing structural fact:** across both stacks, **material (yarn/fibre) is ~50–60%** and **power is the key controllable in spinning** — which is precisely why Ludhiana's survivors are vertically integrated with captive power, and why a small non-integrated unit (buying yarn at market, on grid power, paying market interest, far from a port) is structurally squeezed.
