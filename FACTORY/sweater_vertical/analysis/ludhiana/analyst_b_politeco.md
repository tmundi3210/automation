# Ludhiana Knitwear Unit — Political Economy, Operations & Off-Paper Reality (Analyst B)

Subject: the **human + regulatory + off-paper** reality of running a woollen/acrylic knitwear unit in
Ludhiana, Punjab. Scope is the *operating* layer a NEW entrant actually hits — not the marquee groups.
Honesty discipline (MASTER_PLAN §"Honesty discipline") is binding: every money/capacity/regulation/
behaviour claim is tagged **[FACT]** (public source), **[ESTIMATE]** (method + basis + confidence +
literal word "estimate"), or **[UNKNOWN]**. Off-paper practice is described as **mechanism / structural
incentive**, tagged **[ESTIMATE: discussion/industry-lore]**, never as accusation against any named
party. Coverage is a **floor, not a ceiling**: "unknown" appears often and on purpose.

---

## 0. One-paragraph orientation

Ludhiana is "the Manchester of India" for knitwear: it makes roughly **90–95% of India's woollen/acrylic
hosiery** [FACT — txcindia cluster profile + Tribune; both state ~90–95%]. The cluster's defining traits
for an entrant: (a) it runs on **migrant labour** from UP/Bihar/Jharkhand/West Bengal, organized through
**contractors (thekedars)** and paid largely **piece-rate**; (b) demand is **violently seasonal** —
a winter product made in a summer-heavy calendar; (c) the formal regulatory stack (Factories Act, GST,
EPF/ESI, PPCB effluent rules, export docs) is real but unevenly enforced, which creates the **off-paper
economy** the owner wants understood. The single most important structural fact: *the gap between paper
cost and real cost is itself the business model the incumbents have optimized, and a compliant new
entrant pays the full paper cost while competing against people who don't.*

## 1. LABOUR — the migrant workforce, wage structures, skills, seasonality

### 1.1 Who the workers are
- **~90–95% of the Ludhiana knitwear/textile workforce is migrant** [FACT — Tribune: "relies nearly
  95 percent on migrant labour"], drawn from **Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal** [FACT —
  Tribune; Springer "Migrant Workers in Ludhiana"; idronline].
- The labour relation is **contractor-mediated**: a *thekedar* (labour contractor) supplies and manages
  crews; the unit owner often does not employ workers directly. "A combination of contracting and the
  use of migratory labour makes business viable and labour subdued and manageable" [FACT — idronline,
  quoting the structural arrangement]. This is the hinge that makes most of the off-paper economy (§4)
  mechanically possible: the contractor layer absorbs headcount, identity, and cash.
- **Skill stagnation is a named industry problem**: "outdated skills of these workers have stagnated the
  growth of the industry" [FACT — ResearchGate, "Human Resource — A Cause of Concern for Ludhiana
  Hosiery Industry"]. An entrant cannot assume a deep bench of operators for modern computerized flat-bed
  machines (Shima/Stoll-class); hand-flat and older power-flat skills are more abundant than CAD/computer
  flat-knit programming skill. [ESTIMATE: industry-lore — confidence MEDIUM, basis: HR-stagnation source
  + the general scarcity of programmers vs. operators in Indian flat-knit clusters.]

### 1.2 Wage structures — piece-rate vs monthly
- **Two coexisting systems.** Core/permanent staff (supervisors, master karigars, some checkers) may be
  **monthly**; the bulk of throughput labour — knitting operators, **linkers**, checkers, finishers,
  packers — is typically **piece-rate** ("per dozen", "per piece", "per panel") routed through the
  thekedar. [ESTIMATE: industry-lore — confidence HIGH that piece-rate dominates throughput roles;
  basis: idronline/Springer descriptions of contracting + piece-rate norm across Indian garment clusters.
  Exact per-piece rupee values for Ludhiana sweaters are **[UNKNOWN]** — no reliable public figure; do
  not fabricate one.]
- **Statutory floor (the paper number).** Punjab minimum wage (Shops & Establishments, effective
  01-Sep-2024): **Unskilled ₹422.92/day, Semi-skilled ₹452, Skilled ₹487, Highly-skilled ₹527/day**;
  revised to **Unskilled ₹438.06/day (~₹11,390/month)** effective 01-Mar-2025 [FACT — Punjab Labour Dept
  notification 28-Nov-2024 + Mar-2025 amendment, via SGCMS / hrinformative / cleartax]. The literacy
  point for an entrant: minimum wage is a **monthly-equivalent ~₹11–13k floor**, but **piece-rate is
  paid to beat or detach from it** — workers can out-earn the floor in peak season and fall below it (or
  go idle) in lean season. [ESTIMATE: discussion/industry-lore — that piece-rate is structurally used to
  avoid a guaranteed monthly floor; confidence HIGH on the mechanism, basis: MSE/Sage bribery-and-
  informality papers + idronline "labour subdued and manageable".]
- **Wage suppression mechanism:** migrants "doing the same jobs as local labour for lower wages, in
  harsher living conditions" [FACT — idronline]. Migrancy + contractor intermediation + piece-rate
  together compress effective wages below what a directly-employed, locally-resident worker would command.

### 1.3 Key skills (and their relative scarcity)
| Skill | Role | Scarcity for a new entrant |
|---|---|---|
| **Knitting** (power-flat / computerized flat-bed operation) | makes panels | operators moderately available; *programmers* for Shima/Stoll scarce [ESTIMATE, MED] |
| **Linking** | joins panels (shoulder/side) on a linking machine — the quality-critical seam | skilled linkers are a recognized bottleneck; piece-rate, eyesight-intensive [ESTIMATE, MED-HIGH] |
| **Checking** (inspection) | catch dropped stitches, holes, shade, measurement | abundant but low-paid; quality depends on incentive design [ESTIMATE, MED] |
| **Finishing** (mending/kitting, pressing, trimming, packing) | cosmetic + dispatch-ready | abundant, low-skill, seasonal-casual [ESTIMATE, MED] |

Linking is the role where a small unit's quality reputation is won or lost; it is also where piece-rate
incentives most directly trade speed against defect rate. [ESTIMATE: industry-lore, confidence MEDIUM.]

### 1.4 Seasonality — the calendar that shapes everything
- The product is **winter wear** (sweaters, cardigans, pullovers, hoodies, shawls) [FACT — Tribune,
  leenknits]. **Order-lifting peaks around October**; production ramps **roughly July–October** ahead of
  the winter retail window, with a **lean / warm-weather trough** when "limited requirement during warmer
  weeks" bites [FACT — Tribune "October used to be a hectic time"; the trough is named generically].
  Exact month-by-month volume curve is **[UNKNOWN]** for any specific unit.
- **Consequence for the entrant — twin shocks:**
  1. **Working-capital shock:** yarn bought and goods made months before cash comes back; receivables
     stretch across the season. [ESTIMATE: industry-lore, HIGH — standard seasonal-apparel cash cycle.]
  2. **Labour-retention shock:** in the lean season migrants **go home** and may not return — "many
     migrant workers who had returned home… have not returned" [FACT — Tribune, re: 2025 Indo-Pak
     tension + general return pattern]. Skilled linkers/operators are the hardest to re-hire, so units
     over-pay or hoard core staff through the trough. This is a direct driver of both wage structure
     (retainer-style monthly for key skills) and the off-paper cash float (§4) used to keep crews loyal.

## 2. LIVING COST IN LUDHIANA — and how it shapes wages

- **Single-person monthly cost ex-rent ≈ ₹29,000–30,000** per cost-of-living aggregators [ESTIMATE —
  Nomadlio cites ~₹29,864/month single person ex-rent; aggregator data, confidence LOW-MED, these
  indices skew toward middle-class/expat baskets, NOT a migrant worker's basket].
- **Migrant worker's real basket is far lower** because housing is shared/employer-linked: PG/shared
  accommodation **₹8,000–8,500/month including food** for twin/triple sharing [FACT — rentmystay/
  squareyards PG listings]; worker accommodation near the workplace can **save ₹5,000–10,000/month** vs.
  living far and commuting (₹50–150/day transport) [FACT — nia.one]. Many migrant workers live in
  **factory-adjacent rooms / informal settlements**, compressing the true cash cost of living to a few
  thousand rupees a month. [ESTIMATE: industry-lore, MED.]
- **How this shapes wages (mechanism):** because a migrant's survival basket in Ludhiana is low and
  housing is often work-tied, the **reservation wage is low**, which is *why* the ~₹11–13k statutory
  monthly floor is even viable as a market wage and why piece-rate at modest per-piece values still
  attracts labour. The same dynamic gives the owner leverage: housing-tied labour is **less mobile and
  more retainable**, which feeds the lean-season retention strategy and the contractor/cash model. The
  flip side is fragility — when migrants leave en masse (festivals, harvest, security scares), the cluster
  faces an acute labour crisis [FACT — Tribune "labour crisis as migrants stay away"]. [ESTIMATE:
  discussion/industry-lore, confidence MED-HIGH for the wage-formation logic.]

## 3. RULES & REGULATION — the formal stack an entrant must clear

### 3.1 Establishment, licensing, factory status
- **Factories Act, 1948** applies once a unit is a "factory" (broadly: **10+ workers with power / 20+
  without**); requires **factory registration + licence** from the state Chief Inspector of Factories,
  plus building/fire/safety compliance. [FACT — Factories Act thresholds are standard; the EPFO note
  confirms factory licence details are recorded at EPF registration.] Many small Ludhiana units stay
  **below the factory threshold on paper** by fragmenting headcount across contractors/job-workers — a
  structural reason the cluster is a mesh of tiny units. [ESTIMATE: discussion/industry-lore, MED-HIGH.]
- **GST registration** mandatory above turnover threshold (₹40 lakh goods, lower in some cases);
  required to invoice, claim input credit, and export.
- **Shops & Commercial Establishments Act (Punjab)** registration for the establishment; this is also the
  schedule under which the minimum-wage notification in §1.2 sits. [FACT — Punjab notification context.]

### 3.2 Labour-law compliance stack (the cost of being formal)
- **EPF (Provident Fund): mandatory at 20+ employees**, register within 1 month; **once-covered-always-
  covered** even if headcount later drops [FACT — EPFO / vakilsearch]. Employer contribution ~12%.
- **ESI (health insurance): mandatory for factories with 10+ persons** (and 10+ non-factory in extended
  areas); register within 15 days; **contract workers at the premises are covered** [FACT — ourtaxpartner
  / razorpay]. Employer contribution 3.25%.
- **Contract Labour (Regulation & Abolition) Act** governs the thekedar arrangement (principal-employer
  liability, contractor licensing) once thresholds are met.
- **Net effect:** EPF+ESI add **~15%+ on top of cash wages** for fully-on-roll labour — a real margin gap
  vs. units that keep workers off-roll via contractors. This single delta is the largest *legal* cost
  disadvantage a compliant entrant carries. [ESTIMATE: arithmetic from statutory rates, confidence HIGH
  on the ~15% loading; the *avoidance* of it via contracting is the §4 mechanism.]

### 3.3 GST / duty structure (textile-specific traps)
- **Apparel GST: 12% on stitched garments** (with a value-threshold history of 5% vs 12%); the sector
  suffers a well-documented **inverted duty structure** — inputs (yarn, MMF) taxed higher relative to
  output, trapping working capital in refund claims [FACT — razorpay/themunim; thefederal + a2ztaxcorp on
  inverted structure]. **AEPC has lobbied for a uniform/5% structure** [FACT — shipglobal/AEPC]. For an
  entrant using **acrylic (MMF) yarn**, the inverted-duty cash trap is material and refunds are slow.
- **Export incentives:** **RoDTEP** and **RoSCTL** rebate embedded state/central taxes on exported
  apparel/made-ups [FACT — government schemes]. These partly offset the duty drag but require clean
  documentation (§3.5).

### 3.4 Pollution / effluent control (dyeing — the highest-regulatory-risk node)
- **Dyeing is the binding environmental constraint** and the part of the chain most exposed to shutdown.
  Dyeing units are "the prime reason for pollution in **Buddha Nullah**" feeding the Sutlej [FACT —
  Tribune]. There are **three CETPs** (Common Effluent Treatment Plants) in Ludhiana; CPCB found all
  **three non-compliant**, levied **penalties "worth crores"**, and **NGT reviewed 14 combined petitions**
  on their legality [FACT — KNN India / Tribune]. Some units "**flouting norms… boring deep wells to
  flush effluents** into the soil" [FACT — Tribune].
- **Entrant implication:** if the venture does **in-house dyeing**, it inherits PPCB consent-to-operate,
  CETP membership, and **standing closure/NGT risk**; if it **outsources dyeing or buys dyed yarn**, it
  pushes that risk onto suppliers but loses colour control and inherits supplier-shutdown supply risk.
  Strategically, a small new entrant should almost certainly **not own a dyehouse**. [ESTIMATE: judgment,
  confidence HIGH, basis: the documented enforcement environment.]

### 3.5 Power / electricity and export documentation
- **Power:** Punjab industrial power tariff + reliability is a recurring cost/uptime variable; knitting
  machines and (especially) dyeing/finishing are energy-intensive. Exact current Ludhiana industrial
  tariff is **[UNKNOWN]** here (PSPCL schedule, varies by load slab) — flag for §T4 cost model rather
  than asserting a figure.
- **Export documentation:** IEC (Importer-Exporter Code), GST/LUT for zero-rated exports, AEPC RCMC for
  apparel-export council benefits, shipping bill, commercial invoice/packing list, certificate of origin,
  and HTS classification on the US side. [FACT — standard Indian apparel-export doc set; AEPC RCMC per
  shipglobal.] Errors here are where RoDTEP/RoSCTL refunds get stuck.

## 4. THE OFF-PAPER ECONOMY — mechanism, not accusation

> Framing (binding): everything below is **how such practices are generally reported to operate in Indian
> industrial clusters** and the **structural incentives** that produce them. **No named person or firm is
> alleged** to do any of this. No rupee/dollar figure below is asserted as fact. All tagged
> **[ESTIMATE: discussion/industry-lore]** with confidence. The owner asked for the *mechanism* — "maybe
> it's all not in papers, but it can be in discussions" — and that is exactly the register here.

### 4.1 Why an off-paper layer exists at all (the incentive)
Academic work on India's informal sector is explicit: a dense, multi-agency regulatory stack (labour,
factory, GST, pollution, fire, weights) **"bred corrupt regulating inspectors"** and firms face
"regulatory costs imposed by increased interaction with labor inspectors **extracting bribes in addition
to or in lieu of compliance costs**"; **"firms that evaded taxes were found to pay higher bribes."**
[FACT — these are the *findings of the literature*: MSE Working Paper 291; Sharma & Biswas (Sage 2020);
ScienceDirect "Bribery and firm performance in India"; Business Standard "Inspector Raj".] That is the
load-bearing, citable backbone under everything in §4 — the *individual* applications below are the
[ESTIMATE] inferences from it.

### 4.2 The recurring mechanisms (each as structural incentive)
1. **"Speed money" / facilitation payments.** Discretionary, slow, multi-window approvals (factory
   licence, pollution consent, electricity load, GST refund release) create **rent at each gate**; small
   payments "grease" timing. Mechanism: discretion + delay = price. [ESTIMATE: discussion/industry-lore,
   confidence HIGH on the *mechanism's existence in the cluster type*; specific amounts **[UNKNOWN]**.]
2. **Inspector rent-seeking / "inspector raj."** Periodic inspections (labour, boiler, fire, pollution,
   weights) where the choice is *comply-and-pay-cost* vs *settle-with-inspector*; literature says larger/
   more-regulated and tax-evading firms pay more, and the informal sector is structurally vulnerable.
   [FACT for the literature finding; ESTIMATE: that Ludhiana knitwear units sit squarely in this
   vulnerable class — confidence MED-HIGH.]
3. **Cash dealing.** A parallel cash economy for wages (via thekedar), local job-work, and some sales
   reduces the on-paper turnover that triggers thresholds (factory status, EPF/ESI, GST slabs) and
   reduces the visible base for tax. Mechanism: thresholds + piece-rate + contractor layer make cash the
   path of least resistance. [ESTIMATE: discussion/industry-lore, confidence MED-HIGH on the mechanism.]
4. **Under-invoicing.** Reporting a lower transaction value than real — domestically to shrink GST/income
   base, or in trade to manage duty/forex. Mechanism: tax/duty wedge + weak third-party verification of
   true price. [ESTIMATE: discussion/industry-lore; confidence MED on prevalence, the *incentive* is
   unambiguous; **any specific figure is [UNKNOWN]** and must never be fabricated.]
5. **Political bias / capture.** Clusters with employment weight gain political salience; that can convert
   into **softer enforcement, tariff/scheme lobbying (AEPC-style, which is legal and public), preferential
   land/power, and selective leniency on pollution/labour orders**. Mechanism: jobs + votes + association
   muscle → discretionary relief. [ESTIMATE: discussion/industry-lore; AEPC lobbying is [FACT] and legal;
   *selective enforcement relief* is the [ESTIMATE] inference, confidence MED. **No named actor accused.**]
6. **Pollution-norm arbitrage.** Where CETP compliance is costly and enforcement episodic (documented
   non-compliance + deep-well discharge above), the *temptation structure* is to under-treat and settle if
   caught. [FACT that non-compliance and deep-well discharge are documented at the cluster level;
   ESTIMATE that this reflects a settle-vs-comply calculus — confidence MED-HIGH.]

### 4.3 What this does to a NEW entrant's real cost & risk (the part the owner needs)
- **The compliance penalty is real and asymmetric.** A fully-compliant entrant pays: EPF+ESI (~15%+ on
  labour), full GST with capital trapped in inverted-duty refunds, genuine effluent/CETP cost (if dyeing),
  and full statutory wages — **while competing on price against units that off-load some of these via
  cash/contractor/under-treatment.** The compliant unit's landed cost is structurally higher. [ESTIMATE:
  synthesis, confidence HIGH on direction, magnitude **[UNKNOWN]**.]
- **But the off-paper route is a risk position, not free money.** It carries: penalty/closure exposure
  (NGT/CPCB "penalties worth crores"; pollution shutdowns), **once-covered-always-covered** EPF lock-in
  once crossed, GST/income reassessment risk, recurring rent payments that scale *up* with size and
  visibility ("larger firms pay more"), and labour-relations fragility. The informal route caps scale —
  the literature's core finding is that **informality is precisely why these firms cannot grow** (fixed
  investment is disincentivized). [FACT — Mercatus/1991Project + MSE papers.]
- **Strategic read for an export-oriented D2C brand (this venture):** export buyers, US import compliance,
  and any future brand/ESG story **reward the paper-clean position** (RoDTEP/RoSCTL refunds need clean
  docs; export producers are the one segment where the bribery-performance literature finds *less*
  negative impact). The realistic posture for this venture is **compliant-core + outsource-the-dirty-
  node (dyeing) + budget explicitly for the compliance-cost premium and for friction/"speed-money" at
  approval gates as a line item**, rather than building a business model on the off-paper arbitrage that
  caps scale and blocks export legitimacy. [ESTIMATE: judgment, confidence MED-HIGH.]
