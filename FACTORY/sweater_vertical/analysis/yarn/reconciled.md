> **The one reconciled sentence.** *For a warm-California D2C sweater brand the fibre choice is a climate-and-margin decision executed through a numeric, USTER-graded, pilling-gated contractual spec: source acrylic/blend/cotton-blend/lambswool in-cluster and import only premium merino tops/cashmere, knit the core at 12GG fine gauge — and accept that the two things that actually decide the line are **dye-lot MOQ per shade (≈150–500 kg/colour ⇒ 2–4 launch colours)** on the commercial side and **a contractual anti-pill spec gated at ICI Pilling-Box grade ≥3–4 post-wash** on the quality side, because yarn price (~$3–5/kg, ~$1–2.50/garment) is small and well-anchored and is not the binding constraint.*

## 1. Agreed facts (the spine both analysts stand on)

These survived both angles and the reconciler's check. Tagged at the **lower** of the two analysts' confidences where they differed.

**[AGREED A+B — climate reframes the line.]** Turlock/Central-Valley CA sells light/mid/transitional knits most of the year (≈Sept–April) and true heavy wool only Nov/Dec–Feb. ⇒ **core = lightweight fine-gauge** (cotton-blend / fine acrylic / viscose-blend), **wool/merino = thin seasonal premium capsule, not the base.** This deliberately *inverts* Ludhiana's own winter-woollen product weighting. `[ESTIMATE: climate-reasoning; direction HIGH, month bands MED]` (A §0; consistent with B's "for the warm California market a finer, softer hand sells," B §1d.)

**[AGREED A+B — the fibre tier ladder.]** (a) value base = **fine acrylic / acrylic-rich blend**; (b) 12-month core = **cotton-blend at fine gauge**; (c) premium capsule = **merino or lambswool**, thin/seasonal; (d) optional halo = a **cashmere-blend** drop. `[ESTIMATE: design-framing; MED-HIGH]` A derived it from climate+margin; B independently lands the same place from the quality side (acrylic default per Ludhiana reality, merino/cotton as premium/warm-market lines).

**[AGREED A+B — blend ratio is the brand's #1 controllable lever.]** The ratio on the care label moves cost, hand, warmth, care, perceived value, **and US import duty** (wool 6110.11 ~16% vs cotton 6110.20 ~7%). A frames it as the margin lever; B adds the enforcement: **write it into the PO with a tolerance** (e.g. "70/30 ± 3% by mass, verified by fibre-composition test") because blend drift is both a cost cheat and a duty-misdeclaration risk. `[FACT on duty lines; ESTIMATE: industry-lore on lever-status, HIGH]`

**[AGREED A+B — yarn price is NOT the binding constraint.]** Value/core yarn input is **~$3–5/kg**; at ~300–500 g/sweater that's **~$1–2.50 of yarn per garment** (A §4a). B independently reaches the same conclusion from the other side: the binding lever is the *spec/pilling gate*, not the cost of the fibre. **Both agree the constraints that actually bind are (commercial) dye-lot MOQ and (quality) the pilling gate — not ₹/kg.** `[ESTIMATE: MED]`

**[AGREED A+B — never own a dyehouse; launch on bought-dyed stock shades.]** Inherited from DEEPDIVE §5b/§6 (CETP non-compliance, NGT, crore-scale penalties, closure risk). A operationalises it: **(A) buy dyed yarn = launch default** vs **(B) greige + job-work dye = graduate to it only for a proven hero colour.** B's dyeing-stage trade-off (later-stage dye = faster trend response, more shade risk) is the same axis from the QC side. `[ESTIMATE: judgment; "no captive dyehouse" HIGH]`

**[AGREED A+B — where the best yarn is from.]** Acrylic / acrylic-blend / melange / lambswool / blended-spun = **domestic in-cluster** (Vardhman, Sportking, Nahar, Oswal — cost + MOQ-flex + lead-time all favour local). Merino/superfine **tops** = imported AU/NZ (RNB-class importers warehouse incl. Ludhiana); cashmere imported; filament viscose/modal/fine nylon imported and blended in. **Import only where the quality ceiling justifies the cost/MOQ/lead-time penalty.** `[FACT on player roster + import origins; ESTIMATE: HIGH on the source-split logic]`

**[AGREED A+B — match the bar to the tier AND the supplier.]** A: large integrated spinners support count availability/shade cards/restock; micro-units flex MOQ but vary. B: large spinners (Vardhman/Nahar/Sportking) run lab QC and can hit USTER-percentile clauses and folded-count specs natively; **micro-spinners can't self-test** → push to a USTER-equipped spinner or accept third-party testing. **Don't demand 5% USTER on a mainstream acrylic lot — it's unquotable or luxury-priced; 25% USTER is the sane mainstream bar.** `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore/judgment, MED-HIGH]`

## 2. The fibre / count / quality decision logic for OUR brand (the merged operating model)

This is the single decision path the brand runs per style. It fuses A's *what-to-buy* with B's *how-to-spec*.

**Step 1 — Pick the (fibre, count, GG, g/garment) quadruple, not a "weight."** (A §2 + B §4 field 2 + GLOSSARY rule #4.) Don't choose a vague "midweight" — choose four numbers that agree:

| Gauge | Resultant count (typical) | Sweater weight band | Role in our line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3GG | very coarse | ~700 g–1 kg+ | Chunky **Dec–Feb capsule only** — wrong as a core for our climate |
| 5GG | coarse (~Nm 5–6) | ~500–800 g | Limited; heavy statement pieces |
| **7GG** | **Nm 6–8 resultant (2/30, 2/24, Nm12/2, Nm15/2)** | ~350–550 g — **commercial default pullover** `[FACT — GLOSSARY §3]` | Mainstream mid sweater; fine Oct–Feb |
| 9–10GG | medium-fine | ~280–400 g | Good transitional weight |
| **12GG** | **fine (2/48 Nm-class, high-Ne cotton)** | ~200–350 g | **Our core gauge** — lightweight, 12-month, drapey `[FACT — GLOSSARY §3 that 12GG is the warm-CA "elevated basics" tier]` |
| 14/16/18GG | very fine, near-jersey | ~150–280 g | Premium fine knit; slower→costlier, MOQ/skill risk |

**[AGREED A+B — the count→gauge map is consistent across both files.]** This was the most likely seam to conflict (A flagged it for cross-check); **A and B agree** that 7GG ≈ Nm 6–8 resultant / 2/30 / 2/24, and 12GG is the fine warm-market tier. **One arithmetic caution carried from A's worked check — see §4 (Contested/uncertain) point C1.**

**Step 2 — Set the five spinning dials (B's core contribution) to the tier.** Quality is five mostly-independent dials, each mapping to one thing the customer feels:
- **Staple length** — longer → less pill, more strength, smoother. *Name a minimum staple (mm) or fibre grade in the PO*, not just the count.
- **Twist (TPM/TPI + S/Z, or twist-multiplier K)** — the strength-vs-softness optimum; under-twist pills/breaks, over-twist feels wiry + causes spirality. Knitwear-soft ≈ K~3; require **balanced ply** to kill the twisted-side-seam defect. *The dial most often left blank in a naive PO.*
- **Ply** — 2-ply binds better than singles (singles skew/torque flat panels — a QC reject). *Confirm singles count + ply + ends fed before judging fabric weight* (2/30 vs 1/30 is a doubling error).
- **Fibre fineness (micron / denier-per-filament)** — **the counter-intuitive dial: finer = softer but pills MORE and is weaker.** *Spec a micron ceiling (e.g. "≤19.5 µm"), not the word "merino"*; for acrylic *spec an anti-pill / higher-dpf grade (~2.5 dpf), not the cheapest 1D.*
- **Blend ratio ± tolerance** — the margin/positioning/duty lever (see §1); a few % nylon in a soft wool/cashmere blend sharply improves pill+abrasion (the cheapest quality lever).

**Step 3 — Defend against pilling (the #1 return driver) at every stage.** Causes: **short, fine, low-twist, loose-gauge, hairy** yarn (mnemonic). Levers, in order of control: longer staple → higher twist → ply → **anti-pill/higher-dpf grade (the single biggest lever for acrylic)** → a few % nylon (for soft wool/cashmere) → lower hairiness (compact/air-jet spun) → **tight gauge + high stitch density** (don't let a loose knit undo a low-pill yarn — T3/T8) → finishing (singe/cellulase bio-polish/heat-set — T6). **None of it is real until it passes the gate (Step 5).**

**Step 4 — Source per the split (A).** Acrylic/blend/cotton-blend/lambswool **in-cluster**, **bought-dyed stock shades at launch**; import **only** merino tops/cashmere via a warehousing importer. Constrain the line by **dye-lot MOQ, not yarn price**.

**Step 5 — Gate with numbers, not adjectives (B).** Grade yarn lots against **USTER percentiles on CV% + IPI** (state thin −50/thick +50/nep +200 sensitivities), plus hairiness, tenacity, count-CV; gate every "anti-pill" claim on **ICI Pilling Box (ISO 12945-1) grade ≥ 3–4 after ≥1 home-wash cycle** (post-wash, because washing drops the grade and that's what predicts returns). Micro-brand posture = **contractual QC** (demand per-lot USTER report + percentile clause + 3rd-party spot-check + ICI pilling gate), **not in-house metrology** (a USTER tester is 6-figure-USD class; exact list price [UNKNOWN]). This is the loop the **T5 "thread-checker"** is meant to close.

**The enforceable spec sheet (B §4, carried verbatim as the brand's PO attachment):** fibre+blend (micron ceiling / anti-pill dpf, ±% by mass, fibre-composition verified) · count (Ne/Nm folded + Tex in brackets; singles/ply/ends-fed) · twist (TPM+S/Z or K, "balanced ply") · min staple · USTER clause (≤25% USTER on CVm% + IPI + sensitivities; hairiness band; min tenacity; count-CV ceiling) · pilling acceptance (ICI ≥3–4 post-wash) · colour (shade std, lot-to-lot ΔE, fastness minima) · the GG + GSM/g-per-garment it must knit at · AQL sampling + reject rule · commercials (Incoterm, MOQ, lead time, price — to T9).

### 3b. MOQ bands — **the binding commercial constraint** (separate the three MOQs)
- **Greige/undyed yarn purchase:** small — **tens of kg** for a relationship buyer, up to ~a carton/quintal; more mill-direct. `[ESTIMATE, MED]`
- **Dyed yarn per shade — THE binding number:** floor **~150–500 kg/colour**; dye-house economic MOQ **~500–2,000 kg/colour**; **+20–50% small-batch surcharge** below it; one industry example ~200 kg. ⇒ at ~300–400 g/sweater, **200 kg ≈ 500–650 sweaters of one colour ⇒ a micro-capsule affords only ~2–4 colours** without surcharge or dead stock. `[ESTIMATE; existence of a few-hundred-kg-per-colour floor MED-HIGH]`
- **Garment / CMT MOQ per style-colour:** **~100–300 pcs** at small Ludhiana units (job-work mesh flexes below long-run Bangladesh/Vietnam). `[ESTIMATE, MED]`

### 3c. Lead-time bands (ex-factory; ocean India→US is separate, T9)
- In-stock greige: days. **Dyed-to-shade: ~2–4 weeks** (lab dips + dye-lot scheduling).
- Sampling (proto→fit→PP): **~2–5 weeks** (FF linking is skill-bound — DEEPDIVE §4 linker bottleneck).
- Bulk knit→link→finish (small run): **~3–6 weeks**, longer in the **Jul–Oct cluster peak** (avoid committing a launch drop into it).
- **Yarn-in → goods-ready ex-factory: ~6–12 weeks** for a small dyed-shade order; add ocean (T9) for landed. `[ESTIMATE, MED; seasonal-contention direction MED-HIGH]`

## 4. Contested / uncertain points, and confidence downgrades

The two analysts rarely contradict each other; the friction is mostly *reconciler-imposed* caution on over-anchored numbers.

- **[C1 — arithmetic wobble in A's 12GG worked example, CONTESTED by reconciler].** A's §2 sanity check states "2/48 Nm resultant ≈ 24 Nm" and calls it the move to *finer* 12GG — but a 2/30 Ne cotton resolves to **~25.4 Nm resultant** by A's own math, so **2/48 Nm (≈24 Nm resultant) is *not meaningfully finer* than 2/30 Ne (≈25 Nm resultant)** on resultant count alone. A half-acknowledges this ("finer *singles* but ply brings it back — the lever is singles count + ends fed"). **Reconciled position:** the count→gauge *map* (7GG↔2/30; 12GG↔fine) is **[AGREED A+B]** and sound, but the **specific "2/48 Nm" exemplar is a weak illustration of the 12GG jump** — the real levers to reach 12GG are **finer singles + fewer ends fed + tighter knit**, confirmed at sampling. Don't quote "2/48 Nm = 12GG" as a rule. `[ESTIMATE: MED]`

- **[C2 — vendor-reported pilling magnitudes, DOWNGRADED].** B cites "anti-pill/higher-dpf acrylic cuts complaints 30–50% / returns ~45%" and "compact spinning cuts shedding ~30%." B already tags these **[ESTIMATE: vendor-sourced, medium]**. **Reconciled:** keep the **direction** (these levers materially reduce pilling — HIGH), **distrust the percentages** (single-vendor case studies, not independent trials — treat as illustrative, not as a target to promise a customer). `[DOWNGRADED to: direction HIGH, magnitude LOW-MED]`

- **[C3 — "−1 µm ≈ +15–20% perceived softness", DOWNGRADED].** Vendor-stated; no independent psychophysical anchor. Keep as a *rough* ordering aid (finer micron reads softer, in steps), **not** a linear formula. `[DOWNGRADED to ESTIMATE: vendor-sourced, LOW-MED]`

- **[C4 — premium-fibre price rows, DOWNGRADED — see §3a].** Lambswool/merino/cashmere ₹/kg bands rest on a single stale (2018) anchor or none. **Order-of-magnitude only; importer quote required before any margin use.**

- **[C5 — dye-lot MOQ floor, partial uncertainty].** Both the **existence** of a few-hundred-kg-per-colour floor (MED-HIGH) and its **consequence** (2–4 launch colours) are robust; the **exact kg at a named Ludhiana job-work dyer is [UNKNOWN]** and is A's #1 carried open question. The colour-count conclusion is **not** sensitive to the exact floor within ~150–500 kg, so it survives the uncertainty.

- **[C6 — no genuine A-vs-B contradiction on fibre/spec found].** A's market tiering and B's quality-spec are complementary; they agree on acrylic-base / cotton-blend-core / merino-capsule, on never owning a dyehouse, and on the count→gauge map. **No claim from one analyst had to be overruled by the other.** The only over-confidence to strip was on *numbers* (C1–C4), not on *logic*.

## 6. Bottom line (reconciled)

1. **The line is climate-first:** fine-gauge lightweight core (cotton-blend / fine acrylic at **12GG**), heavy wool demoted to a thin Nov–Feb capsule — inverting Ludhiana's winter-woollen default. **[AGREED A+B]**
2. **Two constraints bind, and yarn price isn't one of them:** commercially, **dye-lot MOQ ≈150–500 kg/colour ⇒ only 2–4 launch colours**; on quality, **a contractual anti-pill spec gated at ICI ≥3–4 post-wash.** Yarn is ~$1–2.50/garment. **[AGREED A+B]**
3. **Source local, import only the premium:** acrylic/blend/cotton-blend/lambswool in-cluster on **bought-dyed stock shades**; merino tops/cashmere imported; **never own a dyehouse** (NGT/closure risk), graduate to greige+job-work-dye only for a proven hero colour. **[AGREED A+B]**
4. **Quality is five spinning dials enforced by a number, not an adjective:** staple, twist, ply, fineness (micron/dpf), blend ratio — written into a **count-anchored, USTER-percentile-claused (25% USTER mainstream, not 5%), pilling-gated, fibre-composition-verified, AQL-sampled** PO; **contractual QC, not in-house metrology** (the gap T5 targets). **[AGREED A+B]**
5. **Watch the stale numbers:** acrylic/cotton price bands rest on 2022/2024 points and premium-fibre bands on a stale-2018 anchor — **bands stand, points don't; get importer + mandi quotes before any margin model.** **[DOWNGRADED]**
6. **One arithmetic caveat:** don't quote "2/48 Nm = 12GG" as a rule — the count→gauge map is sound but the 12GG jump comes from finer singles + fewer ends fed + tighter knit, confirmed at sampling. **[CONTESTED → resolved]**
