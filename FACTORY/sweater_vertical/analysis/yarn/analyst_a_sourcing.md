# T2 Yarn & Fibre — Analyst A: Sourcing & Market

**For:** the owner of a small, AI-designed D2C knitwear brand sourcing yarn in/through Ludhiana and selling sweaters in **warm-ish California / Turlock** (Central Valley: hot summers ~35–40°C, mild winters ~5–15°C — a market where heavy wool sells *few* months and "elevated lightweight" sells most of the year).

**Angle (this analyst):** the *buy-side / market* view — which fibre to put on the care label for *our* climate and price tier, what count+gauge gives which sweater weight, where the genuinely-best version of each yarn comes from, and the four sourcing realities the Ludhiana dive flagged `[UNKNOWN]` (MOQ, price, lead time, dyed-vs-greige) hardened into `[ESTIMATE]` bands with method + confidence. Analyst B takes the technical-quality/spec angle; we reconcile after.

**Honesty tags (binding, from SCOPE_GLOSSARY):** **[FACT]** = cited public source; **[ESTIMATE]** = method + basis + confidence + literal word *estimate*; **[UNKNOWN]** = not reliably known, a valid answer. Count conversions are untagged dimensional identities. **Every price/MOQ/lead-time band below is an `[ESTIMATE]`** — I give a method and confidence and never present a precise number as fact.

> **The one sentence to remember.** *For a warm-California D2C sweater brand, the fibre decision is a climate-and-margin decision before it is a quality decision: Ludhiana's native acrylic/acrylic-blend is the right cost-floor base, cotton/cotton-blend and 12GG fine-gauge is the right "12-month-wearable" core, and wool/merino is a thin premium capsule — and at our volume the binding constraint is not yarn price (which is small and well-anchored) but the **dye-lot MOQ per shade**, which sets how many colours a tiny brand can afford.*

## 0. How the climate reframes everything (why this isn't a generic yarn memo)

Turlock/Central-Valley CA is **not** a heavy-knitwear market the way Ludhiana's own domestic market is. Cooling-degree-days dominate; true cold is ~Nov–Feb. That single fact rewrites fibre priority versus Ludhiana's winter-wool default:

- **Months that sell:** light/mid sweaters and "transitional" knits sell roughly **Sept–April**; true heavy wool sells a **narrow Dec–Feb window**. So the brand's *core* should be **lightweight, fine-gauge, breathable** (cotton/cotton-blend, fine acrylic, viscose-blend at 12GG), with wool/merino as a **small seasonal premium capsule**, not the base. `[ESTIMATE: climate-reasoning from CA Central Valley seasonality + apparel seasonality norms; confidence HIGH on direction, MED on the exact month bands]`
- **Consequence for fibre mix:** the Ludhiana cluster's deepest, cheapest competence (acrylic + woollen-system bulky knits) is only a *partial* match to our climate. We exploit Ludhiana's **acrylic spinning** and **fine-gauge cotton-blend** capability, and treat its heavy-woollen specialism as the *capsule*, not the *core* — inverting the cluster's own product weighting. (Connects to DEEPDIVE §6: "product lock-in to seasonal woollens" is a cluster strength we only *partly* want.)

## 2. Count systems applied — what count + gauge gives which sweater weight

The brand specifies a sweater by **fibre + yarn count + gauge (GG) + target g/garment** (GLOSSARY cross-rule #4). The count systems (GLOSSARY §2):

- **Ne** (English cotton count, indirect, higher=finer) — what Ludhiana **quotes**, esp. cotton/spun, folded "2/30".
- **Nm** (metric, indirect) — wool/worsted and European sweater specs.
- **Denier/dtex/Tex** (direct, higher=thicker) — filament viscose/nylon and QC reports.
- **Identities (untagged):** `Tex = 1000/Nm = 590.5/Ne`; `Nm = 1.693×Ne`; `Denier = 9×Tex`. Folded **2/30 ≈ resultant 15s**; misreading 1/30 vs 2/30 is a fabric-weight doubling error.

**Count → gauge → weight map** (anchored to GLOSSARY §3 GG→product map + machine-knit yarn-matching sources):

| Gauge | Typical resultant count | Sweater weight band | Where it fits our line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3GG** | very coarse (e.g. Nm ~3–4 resultant; chunky 2/8-ish or multiple ends) | ~700g–1kg+ | Chunky **Dec–Feb capsule only** — wrong for our climate as a core |
| **5GG** | coarse (~Nm 5–6) | ~500–800g | Limited; heavy-knit statement pieces |
| **7GG** | **Nm 6–8 resultant** — e.g. **2/30, 2/24, Nm12/2, Nm15/2** `[FACT — yarn-store / Maruyasu / machine-knit guides]` | ~350–550g — **commercial default retail pullover** `[FACT — GLOSSARY §3, mysweaterfactory/ritacashmere]` | The mainstream mid sweater; fine for Oct–Feb |
| **9–10GG** | medium-fine | ~280–400g | Good transitional weight |
| **12GG** | fine — **the right tier for warm-CA "elevated basics"** `[FACT — GLOSSARY §3]`; 2/48 Nm-class, fine cotton high Ne | ~200–350g | **Our core gauge** — lightweight, 12-month-wearable, drapey |
| **14/16/18GG** | very fine, near-jersey; needs fine high-count yarn | ~150–280g | Premium fine knit; **slower → costlier**, MOQ/skill risk |

**Worked sanity check (untagged identity math):** A **2/30 Ne** cotton yarn → singles 30 Ne → `Nm = 1.693×30 ≈ 50.8` per single → resultant `≈ 25.4 Nm` (2-ply ≈ singles÷plies) → `Tex ≈ 1000/25.4 ≈ 39 g/1000m`. That folded count knits cleanly around **7GG**; to reach **12GG** you move finer (e.g. 2/48 Nm resultant ≈ 24 Nm... note: 2/48 Nm = two 48Nm singles → resultant ≈ 24 Nm; finer *singles* but ply brings it back — the lever is **singles count + ends fed**, GLOSSARY §2). The practical rule the brand carries: **don't pick a "weight," pick a (count, GG, g/garment) triple and confirm all three agree.**

## 3. WHERE the best yarn is from — domestic (Ludhiana) vs import

**Domestic / in-cluster (Ludhiana / Punjab spinners):**
- **Acrylic, acrylic-blend, melange, blended spun yarn** — *this is the cluster's genuine edge.* Vardhman (India's largest yarn producer; acrylic fibre + blended yarn, >1.1M spindles, >580 MT/day), Sportking (cotton/acrylic/blended/melange, yarn *exporter*), Nahar (cotton), Oswal Woollen Mills (woollen/acrylic yarn). `[FACT — DEEPDIVE §2]` For acrylic and acrylic-rich blends, **buying in-cluster is both cheapest and best-supported** (count availability, shade cards, restock proximity, agglomeration). `[ESTIMATE: from DEEPDIVE §3 agglomeration + §2 player roster; confidence HIGH for acrylic/blends]`
- **Woollen-system / lambswool** — a real Ludhiana competence (it's the "woollen/acrylic" cluster), so lambswool and wool-blend spun yarn are sourceable in-cluster, though raw merino *tops* are imported.

**Imported (ex-cluster, warehoused-in):**
- **Merino & superfine wool tops** — best fibre is **Australian/New Zealand** Merino; importers (RNB-class) bring tops in and warehouse across Indian wool centres **including Ludhiana** `[FACT — search]`. India's imported-merino price series exists (CEIC) but the public point is stale (₹1,253/kg, **2018** — `[FACT but STALE, do not quote as current]`).
- **Cashmere** — imported (Chinese/Mongolian goat undercoat); luxury, low-yield, **use as blend %** not 100% for a micro-brand.
- **Filament viscose/modal & fine nylon** — typically imported filament blended into spun yarn.

**Domestic-vs-import trade-offs** `[ESTIMATE: trade-craft synthesis; confidence MED-HIGH]`:
| | Domestic in-cluster (acrylic/blend/lambswool) | Imported (merino tops / cashmere / filament) |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Lowest; no import duty/freight on yarn | Higher; FX + freight + importer margin |
| MOQ flexibility | **Best** — cluster + job-work mesh tolerates small lots | Worse — importer lot sizes, warehouse minimums |
| Lead time | Shortest (walking-distance restock) | Longer; tied to import shipments |
| Shade/count range | Wide for acrylic/blend | Narrower locally; book against warehoused stock |
| Quality ceiling | Mid (great for value/core) | **High** (the premium-capsule reason to import) |

**Verdict:** source **acrylic/blend/lambswool domestically in-cluster** (cost + MOQ + lead-time all favour it) and **import only the premium fibre** (merino tops, cashmere) where the quality ceiling justifies the cost/MOQ/lead-time penalty. This matches the brand's tier structure in §1.

> **Brand-relevant read:** for the value/core line the yarn input is **~$3–5/kg** — at ~300–500g/sweater that's **~$1–2.50 of yarn per garment**. Yarn cost is **not** the binding constraint for a tiny brand; the **MOQ and dye-lot** below are.

### 4b. MOQ bands (the actually-binding constraint)
**Method:** combine the cluster's MSME/job-work flexibility (DEEPDIVE §1, ~14,000 MSMEs, walking-distance subcontracting) with published knitwear/dye-house minimums; separate **greige-yarn purchase MOQ** from **dye-lot MOQ per shade** from **garment/CMT MOQ per style**. **Confidence MED.**

- **Greige/undyed yarn purchase:** small — **a few bags up to ~one carton/quintal** is buyable in-cluster; effectively **tens of kg** for a relationship buyer, more from a mill direct. `[ESTIMATE, MED]`
- **Dyed yarn per shade (THE binding number):** **~150–500 kg per colour** is the realistic floor; dye-houses' economic MOQ is **~500–2,000 kg/colour**, with **+20–50% small-batch surcharge** below it; one industry example cites **~200 kg** for knit products. `[ESTIMATE anchored to dye-house MOQ reportage; confidence MED-HIGH on the *existence* of a few-hundred-kg-per-colour floor]`
  → **This sets the brand's colour count.** At ~300–400g/sweater, 200 kg of one dyed shade ≈ **500–650 sweaters of that colour** — so a micro-capsule can afford only **2–4 colours** without eating surcharge or carrying dead stock.
- **Garment / CMT MOQ per style-colour:** commonly **~100–300 pcs** at small Ludhiana units; the job-work mesh can flex lower than long-run Bangladesh/Vietnam factories (DEEPDIVE §6, the iteration-speed advantage). `[ESTIMATE, MED]`

### 4c. Lead-time bands
**Method:** decompose into yarn-procure + sample + bulk-knit/link/finish, using the cluster's proximity advantage and standard knitwear/sampling cadence; **ocean India→US is separate (T9).** **Confidence MED.**

- **In-stock greige yarn:** days. **Dyed-to-shade yarn:** **~2–4 weeks** (lab dips + dye-lot scheduling). `[ESTIMATE, MED]`
- **Sampling (proto→fit→PP):** **~2–5 weeks** depending on iterations (FF linking is labour/skill-bound — DEEPDIVE §4 linker bottleneck). `[ESTIMATE, MED]`
- **Bulk knit→link→finish (small run):** **~3–6 weeks**, longer in the **Jul–Oct peak** when cluster capacity is contended (DEEPDIVE §4 seasonality). `[ESTIMATE, MED-HIGH on the seasonal-contention direction]`
- **Yarn-in → goods-ready, ex-factory:** **~6–12 weeks** for a small dyed-shade order; **add ocean freight (T9)** for landed.

### 4d. Dyed yarn vs greige + outsource-dye — the decision
**This is a sourcing-model choice, and the DEEPDIVE already half-decided it:** *do not own a dyehouse* (CETP non-compliance, NGT, crore-scale penalties, closure risk — DEEPDIVE §5b/§6). So the brand's real options are **(A) buy yarn already dyed** vs **(B) buy greige + send to a job-work dye-house.** `[ESTIMATE: judgment; confidence HIGH on "no captive dyehouse"]`

| | (A) Buy dyed yarn | (B) Greige + outsource-dye |
|---|---|---|
| Colour control | Limited to mill shade cards | Full (custom shades, lab dips) |
| Lead time | Shorter (off-the-shelf shades) | +2–4 wks (lab dip + dye lot) |
| MOQ exposure | Lower per shade (share mill's lot) | **Higher** — you trigger the dye-house per-colour MOQ |
| Trend response | Slower (stock shades) | **Faster** (GLOSSARY §4: later-stage dye = faster trend, more shade-risk) |
| Risk pushed to supplier | Yes (incl. effluent/NGT risk on supplier) | Partially — you still book the lot |
| **Verdict for micro-brand** | **Default for launch** — minimise MOQ + lead time, push effluent risk out | Use **once a shade proves out** and volume justifies a custom lot |

**Net:** **launch on bought-dyed stock shades** to dodge per-colour MOQ and effluent risk; **graduate to greige+outsource-dye** only for a hero colour with proven sell-through. Never own dyeing.

## 5. The yarn → knit pipeline a tiny brand can actually buy

**What's purchasable, in order:**
1. **Pick the (fibre, count, GG, g/garment) triple per style** (§2) — fibre from the §1 tier map, gauge ~**12GG core / 7GG mid**.
2. **Buy yarn in-cluster** for acrylic/blend/cotton-blend/lambswool; **import** only merino tops/cashmere via a warehousing importer. Prefer **bought-dyed stock shades** at launch (§4d).
3. **Constrain the line by dye-lot MOQ, not yarn price** — `[ESTIMATE]` ~150–500 kg/colour floor ⇒ **2–4 colours** for a micro-capsule; size the SKU matrix (GLOSSARY §8: styles×colours×sizes) against that, not against fabric cost.
4. **Knit/link/finish via the job-work mesh** — CMT/job-work lets the brand own yarn+design+IP and rent stitching (GLOSSARY §7, DEEPDIVE §3 agglomeration). FF+linking is the quality/price story but the **linker bottleneck** caps throughput (DEEPDIVE §4).
5. **Spec QC against USTER on the yarn** (CV%, IPI) and **pilling grade 1–5** on the finished knit (GLOSSARY §5) — especially for acrylic (pilling is the #1 return driver) and cashmere (pills more).
6. **Lead time:** budget **~6–12 weeks yarn-in→ex-factory** + ocean (T9); avoid committing a launch drop into the **Jul–Oct cluster peak**.

**What a tiny brand canNOT realistically buy:** captive spinning, custom fibre engineering, single-shade tiny dye lots without surcharge, or whole-garment programming at micro-volume. The buyable reality is **stock-count yarn (mostly acrylic/blend, in-cluster) + stock dyed shades + job-work knit/link** — exactly the cluster's agglomeration strength, and exactly why Ludhiana fits the *iteration* game even though it loses the *commodity-export-cost* game (DEEPDIVE §6).
