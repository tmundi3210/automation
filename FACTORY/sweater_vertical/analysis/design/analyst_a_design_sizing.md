## 1. What designs actually work for this consumer (silhouette → stitch → colour → detail, per article)

### 1a. The four constraints that pre-decide the design space (before any aesthetics)
The line is **not** designed from a trend board; four upstream facts cap the option space, and the design lives inside them. `[ESTIMATE: synthesis of upstream files, HIGH]`
1. **Colour budget ≤ 2–4 launch colours** — the dye-lot MOQ (~150–500 kg/colour) makes the line **colour-starved, not style-starved** (`yarn/DEEPDIVE §3b`). ⇒ *the visual interest must come from **stitch texture and silhouette, not from colour-count or multi-colour jacquard/intarsia.***
2. **Warm-California weight = 12GG, ~200–350 g** (`yarn §1c`). ⇒ *no chunky 3–5GG hero knits as the core; "elevated-basic" fine-gauge is the canvas. Chunky texture is faked with surface stitch (cable, ribs, Milano) at a finer gauge, not with bulky low-GG yarn.*
3. **Linking is the throughput bottleneck** (`machines/DEEPDIVE §1`). ⇒ *every extra fully-fashioned panel, raglan seam, or colour-block join adds linker minutes = capacity cost. Favour **fewer, cleaner seams**; reserve fashioning marks as an authenticity cue (`SCOPE_GLOSSARY §3`) on the premium A3, not everywhere.*
4. **Anti-pill is a brand-ending gate on acrylic A2** (`yarn §4`, `brand_market §3`). ⇒ *A2's looks must survive a high-abrasion stitch choice; avoid long floats and loosely-knit brushed surfaces on acrylic — they pill fastest.*

**The design rule that falls out:** **texture-over-colour, silhouette-over-print, single-colour-body with at most a contrast rib/tip.** This is also *on-trend* for 2026 (§3), so the constraint and the fashion point the same way — a rare alignment.

### 1b. The consumer (who we are designing for)
Youth-leaning, value-quality California shopper who discovers on TikTok FYP / IG Reels / Pinterest, plus the ~46%-Hispanic, CSU-Stanislaus college-town Turlock seed (`brand_market §1`). `[FACT — demographics carried from T7]`. Design implications: **gender-neutral / unisex-leaning** cuts widen the addressable buyer per SKU (critical when SKUs are MOQ-expensive); **slightly-oversized, layering-friendly** fits match both the 2026 silhouette trend (§3) and the warm climate (worn open / layered, not as sole insulation); **clean, photographs-well-on-a-phone** surfaces matter more than runway complexity because the product *is* the content (`brand_market §3`).

### 1c. Silhouette + fit (the spine of the line)
The 2026 youth direction is **boxy / relaxed / slightly-oversized with room to breathe**, gender-neutral, layered. `[ESTIMATE: trend-read, MED-HIGH — multiple 2026 trend compilations]` `[FACT — Gen-Z "boxy sweaters, cropped cardigans, oversized with personality"; gender-neutral oversized silhouettes]`. Translated into a buildable fit block:
- **Core fit = "relaxed straight / soft-boxy":** dropped or soft-set shoulder, straight-to-slightly-tapered body, ~4–6" of ease over the body chest measurement (standard sweater layering ease — see §2). This single block, graded across XS–XXL, serves the unisex core. `[FACT — 4–6" sweater ease standard]`
- **Two silhouette variants off one block** (keeps the line tight): (a) **crew / mock-neck pullover** (the volume seller, simplest to link); (b) **a cardigan or quarter-zip** for layering. Avoid a third base shape at launch — SKU/MOQ discipline (`brand_market §3`, line is colour-starved).
- **Length / crop:** a **slightly-cropped or true-length unisex body**; the crop reads youth/2026 but alienates older Turlock seed buyers, so keep the *core* at true length and reserve crop for the A2 youth drop. `[ESTIMATE: MED]`
- **Necklines:** crew + mock are the safe, photogenic, link-simple defaults; a polo/collared knit is a possible A1 elevation but adds linking. **Avoid heavy shawl-collar / chunky funnel** (reads alpine-heritage, which T7 explicitly bans — "clean/light/Californian, never alpine," `brand_market §3`).

### 1d. Stitch / knit looks — mapped to what the gauge, colour budget, and machine allow
Vocabulary per `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §3`. The looks split into **single-colour structural** (cheap on colour, the line's backbone) vs **multi-colour** (expensive on colour, mostly excluded):
- **USE freely (single-colour, texture = interest):** **jersey/stockinette** (the light base; needs edge finishing, curls); **rib 1×1 / 2×2** (cuffs, hems, collars *and* as a full-body "ribbed sweater" hero look — strong recovery, very 2026); **half-Milano / Milano / half-cardigan** (full-needle textured double-knit faces that fake density/weight at a fine gauge — the trick for a warm-climate "substantial" feel without chunky yarn); **cable** (the classic texture-value look — but it *uses more yarn + machine time*, `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §3`, so price it as a premium/A3 detail, not an everywhere-default); **links-links / purl texture** and **pointelle** (lacy, lightweight — fits warm-CA spring fine-gauge, `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §3`).
- **USE sparingly (colour-cheap accents):** **plating** (two yarns, one face/one back — a cheap way to a luxe face or to plate elastane for stretch; near-zero colour cost); **tipping / contrast rib** (one accent colour at cuff/collar — buys a two-tone look from the 2–4-colour budget without a true colourwork lot).
- **AVOID / reserve (colour-hungry or pill/quality-risky):** **jacquard** (Fair-Isle florals/geos — needs multiple colours + back-float management, *heavier, a quality issue*, `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §3`; the colour budget can't fund it and floats pill on acrylic); **intarsia** (clean colour-blocks/logos with no floats — *slower, skilled, machine-dependent*; viable only as a **single hero graphic on one premium drop**, never the line default). `[ESTIMATE: HIGH that colourwork is mostly out on this colour budget]`

### 1e. Colour stories (the 2–4-colour palette, mapped to 2026)
The line gets **~2–4 colours total** (`yarn §3b`), so each must (a) sell, (b) photograph on a phone, (c) ideally share a dye lot across articles. Build the palette as **2 evergreen anchors + 1–2 rotating trend accents**:
- **Anchors (always in line, lowest markdown risk):** a **warm off-white / cream** and a **mid neutral** (oat / greige / soft charcoal). These read "clean Californian" (`brand_market §3`) and, conveniently, **2026 Pantone Colour of the Year is "Cloud Dancer," a bright soft-white leaning putty** — the anchor is on-trend, not a compromise. `[FACT — Pantone 2026 "Cloud Dancer"]`
- **Trend accents (rotate per drop, ≤2 at a time):** 2026 swings **back to pigment after greige-minimalism** — credible knit-relevant accents include **deep eggplant/plum, marigold/butter-yellow, teal, and pillar-box red** (all called out specifically in knitwear on AW26 runways). For a *youth* California read, **icy blues/purples and dusty pastels** are the softer Gen-Z signal. `[FACT — AW26 runway knit colours: eggplant, marigold, teal, red; ESTIMATE on icy-blue/pastel youth fit, MED]`
- **The hero-colour discipline:** the line is colour-starved, so **prove a hero colour before paying a small-batch surcharge or a second lot** (`yarn §3b`). Launch ~2 anchors + 1 trend accent; promote the accent to a re-order only on sell-through signal (`brand_market §4`).
- **Spanish-read + community note:** Turlock is ~46% Hispanic (`brand_market §1`); colour names and any slogan-knit text must clear a Spanish reading (mandatory, T7). `[FACT — carried]`

### 1f. Details (the cheap differentiators)
Low-cost, high-signal details that read "designed, made to last" without colour or linker cost: **clean self-finished ribbed hems/cuffs/collars** (recovery = perceived quality, `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §3`); **fashioning marks** as a visible FF-authenticity cue on A3 (`SCOPE_GLOSSARY §3`); a **woven/leather-look care-and-origin tab** ("from Turlock," the T7 story pillar); **tonal or contrast-tipped rib**; **minimal or no external logo** (quiet-luxury-adjacent, and dodges the trademark/logo exposure T7 flags). Avoid zippers/hardware beyond one quarter-zip option (cost + return-driving fit complexity).

## 2. Sizing + grading math (the part that must be exact)

### 2a. The size set and the WHY of finished-garment specs
**US size set: XS · S · M · L · XL · XXL** (6 sizes). A **unisex/gender-neutral** core is sized on a **men's-derived block** (broader, straighter) with the understanding that women size down 1–2 (publish a "size down for a fitted look / true to size for oversized" note — the standard unisex move). `[ESTIMATE: unisex-block convention, MED-HIGH]`

**Critical distinction (the #1 sizing error):** the **tech-pack spec is FINISHED-GARMENT (flat, half-)measurements, not body measurements.** Body chest + ease = garment chest. Sweaters carry **~4–6" of layering ease** over the body chest (`[FACT — sweater ease standard]`); our soft-boxy fit sits at the **upper end (~5–6")**. We grade the *garment*, then sanity-check it against body charts. Confusing the two ships a sweater a full size off.

### 2b. The four knit body/garment measurements + base spec (size M, FINISHED garment)
The four points knit QC and fit actually turn on (`SCOPE_GLOSSARY §3` — fit, recovery, drape): **chest (1" below armhole, flat), body length (HPS = high-point-shoulder to hem), sleeve length (centre-back or shoulder-seam to cuff), bottom hem / opening width.** Plus the secondary checks: **shoulder width, armhole depth, cuff & hem rib width.** Base size **M, soft-boxy unisex, FINISHED garment, inches** `[ESTIMATE: derived from public US sweater charts + 5–6" ease; MED-HIGH]`:

| Measurement (finished) | M base | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Chest** (½, pit-to-pit ×2 = full) | **42"** full (21" flat) | body chest ~38" M + ~4–5" ease → 42–43"; soft-boxy = top of ease band `[FACT — M body chest 38–40", 4–6" ease]` |
| **Body length** (HPS→hem) | **28"** | matches public M body-length ~28"; crop variant −2" `[FACT — M body length ~28"]` |
| **Sleeve length** (shoulder→cuff) | **35.5"** (full underarm-to-cuff style varies) | M sleeve ~35–35.5"; drop-shoulder shortens the set sleeve `[FACT — M sleeve 35–35.5"]` |
| **Bottom hem width** (½) | **20"** flat | slightly under chest for a clean straight body; ribbed for recovery |

*(Shoulder ~18.5", armhole depth ~10", cuff rib ~3.5", hem rib ~2.5–3" at M as working secondaries `[ESTIMATE: MED]`.)*

### 2c. The grade rule (per-size increments — the load-bearing math)
A **linear (even) grade** across XS–XXL: pick one increment per measurement and apply it uniformly. Industry-standard knit increments `[FACT — standard grade rule: chest 1.5–2"/size, body length 1–1.5"/size, sleeve 0.5–1"/size]`. Chosen rule:

| Measurement | **Grade / size** | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Chest (full)** | **+2"** | top of the standard 1.5–2" band; gives clean unisex steps `[FACT]` |
| **Body length** | **+1.5"** | mid of 1–1.5"; taller bodies in larger sizes `[FACT]` |
| **Sleeve length** | **+0.75"** | mid of 0.5–1"; longer arms scale gently `[FACT]` |
| **Hem width (full)** | **+2"** | tracks chest to keep the straight silhouette |
| **Shoulder** | **+0.5"** | keeps proportion `[ESTIMATE: MED]` |

**Resulting full-chest size run (finished garment):** XS **38"** · S **40"** · M **42"** · L **44"** · XL **46"** · XXL **48"**. These align cleanly with public US sweater chest charts (XS ~31–34" body → ~38" garment; XXL ~46–48" garment). `[FACT — cross-checked against published US sweater charts]`. Body length: XS 25.5" → XXL 33". Sleeve: XS 33.25" → XXL 37".

**Two refinements (don't ship a naive even grade blind):**
- **Break grade at the extremes** if pop-up data shows it: very small/large sizes often need a *non-linear* shoulder/armhole break, but **launch linear and let pop-up returns prove whether a break is needed** (don't pre-engineer complexity the data hasn't justified). `[ESTIMATE: HIGH that linear-first is correct]`
- **Grade in FINISHED dimensions, then back-out to stitch counts** (§2d) — never grade the body chart.

### 2d. How knit gauge & shrinkage interact with the spec (the bit unique to knitwear)
A sweater is **knit to a stitch/row count, not cut to a pattern** — so the grade rule must be **translated into courses & wales** before it can be programmed (`machines §6` — this is exactly the programmer's job). The chain:

1. **Gauge defines the stitch-to-inch conversion.** At a target **stitch density** (wales/inch × courses/inch, `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §4`), a finished width in inches = wales ÷ wales-per-inch. Example illustration `[ESTIMATE: arithmetic, method = plug a plausible 12GG density]`: at ~9 wales/inch on the finished, relaxed fabric, a **+2" chest grade = +18 wales per panel side**. The programmer adds those wales (and the row count for length) per size — *the grade rule must land on whole, knittable stitch numbers,* so round each graded dimension to the nearest achievable wale/course count. **This is why grading interacts with gauge: a 2" grade is only valid if it's an integer number of wales at the production density.**
2. **Loop length / stitch density is the lever that hits the spec** (`SCOPE_GLOSSARY §4`): the programmer tunes loop length to hit GSM + the dimension simultaneously. Spec **finished dimensions AFTER relaxation/steaming**, measured on a **washed, relaxed swatch**, not off-machine (off-machine fabric is under tension and reads narrow/long).
3. **Shrinkage / dimensional stability is a tolerance, not an afterthought** (`SCOPE_GLOSSARY §5` — *dimensional stability, shrinkage %, spirality*). Knit shrinks/relaxes after the customer's first wash:
   - **Spec residual shrinkage ≤ ~3–5% after a home-wash cycle**, written into the PO, and **build the size to the POST-WASH target** (i.e. knit slightly larger so it lands on-spec after relaxation). `[ESTIMATE: industry tolerance, MED-HIGH]`
   - **Cotton (A1) bags out / has low recovery** (`SCOPE_GLOSSARY §1`) → rib the hems/cuffs hard and consider compacting/sanforising (`SCOPE_GLOSSARY §4`) to hold size; this is a **returns lever** (a sweater that grows a size after one wash = a return).
   - **Spirality (twisted side seam)** from unbalanced singles twist (`yarn §4a`) is a *sizing-adjacent* defect — require **balanced ply** so panels don't torque off-measurement.
   - **Each fibre relaxes differently:** merino (A3) mills/relaxes more, acrylic (A2) is dimensionally stable but heat-sensitive (can't hard-press), cotton (A1) shrinks most. **So the grade rule is shared, but the knit-larger shrinkage allowance is per-article.** `[ESTIMATE: MED-HIGH]`
4. **Tolerance per measurement on the tech pack** (the AQL/measurement-spec gate, `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §5`): e.g. chest ±0.5", length ±0.75", sleeve ±0.5" — out-of-tolerance = reject. Without tolerances the grade rule is decorative.

### 2e. Fit-calibration via the Turlock pop-ups — the T7 size-curve link
This is the **single highest-value design input the brand owns** (`brand_market §1, §4` flags the size curve as `[UNKNOWN until pop-ups run]`). The pop-ups are not just sales — they are a **fitting lab**:
- **Capture the size curve:** record, per fitted customer, the size they buy and (ideally) their measured body chest. The **distribution of sizes sold = the production size curve** (how many XS:S:M:L:XL:XXL to make). A wrong curve = dead stock in the tails + stock-outs in the middle = markdown (`SCOPE_GLOSSARY §8`). `[ESTIMATE: HIGH this is the right method]`
- **Validate the grade + ease:** in-person try-on surfaces whether the +2"/size grade and the 5–6" ease feel right *to this body population* (Central-Valley, ~46% Hispanic — body distribution may differ from a generic US chart; **don't assume the national chart fits the local curve**). `[ESTIMATE: MED — local body distribution UNKNOWN]`
- **Buy down the online return rate:** every garment fitted offline calibrates the online size guide; online returns run ~24% vs ~6% in-store and **size is the #1 apparel return reason** (`brand_market §4`). The pop-up size curve *directly* attacks the brand's stated kill-adjacent metric.
- **Loop:** pop-up curve → adjust grade/ease + production size ratio → next drop. This is the **iteration protocol** the design specialist must run every drop, not once.

## 3. Trend ingestion (runway/retail → the live hype signal → drop calendar)

### 3a. How to read trends FOR THIS BRAND (filter, don't follow)
The brand is **colour-starved, fine-gauge, anti-pill-constrained, and youth-fronted** — so it cannot chase runway literally. The ingestion method is a **three-filter funnel** `[ESTIMATE: method, MED-HIGH]`:
1. **Macro layer (slow, 12–18 mo lead) — runway + colour authorities:** read WGSN/Coloro/Pantone + the major fashion-week knit looks for **direction**: silhouette (boxy/oversized), texture (rib/cable revival), and the **palette** (2026 = pigment-return after greige; Cloud Dancer anchor; eggplant/marigold/teal/red knit accents). `[FACT — 2026 colour + silhouette sources]`. Use this to pick the **1–2 rotating trend accents** and confirm the texture-over-colour bet.
2. **Meso layer (faster, weeks–months) — retail + resale:** watch what **Everlane/Quince/J.Crew/Massimo Dutti** (the premium tier the brand prices against, `brand_market §3`) actually put into knitwear, and **Depop/thrift** signals for the youth read. This is the "is it shipping, not just on a runway" check.
3. **Micro layer (real-time, days) — the social hype signal:** **TikTok FYP / IG Reels / Pinterest** for *this consumer*. This is where the brand both **reads** and **publishes** (the AI-design process is itself the content, `brand_market §3`).

**Only signals that survive all three filters AND fit the 4 constraints (§1a) enter the line.** A trend that needs 6 colours or a brushed-pill surface is read, noted, and *declined*.

## 4. The line-plan logic for exactly 3 articles

**Why exactly three, and not two or five** `[ESTIMATE: synthesis, HIGH]`:
- **The 3 articles are a fibre/role ladder, not three styles** — each occupies a *distinct economic role* the others can't fill (`yarn §1b`, `brand_market §3`): **A1 = margin/LTV engine** (cotton, longest sell-window, best repeat), **A2 = acquisition front door** (acrylic, cheapest, youth/reach, highest return — bought for UGC not contribution), **A3 = halo/price-anchor** (merino, press + makes A1 look like value). Drop any one and you lose a *function*: no A1 = no margin; no A2 = no cheap reach; no A3 = no price anchor / no premium story.
- **Three is the MOQ-survivable maximum**, not a minimum — the line is **colour-starved (2–4 colours)** and **SKU-expensive** (style × colour × size; 3 styles × 3 colours × 6 sizes = **54 SKUs** already, each carrying MOQ + inventory math, `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §8`). A 4th article multiplies MOQ pain past what a micro-capsule's working capital and dye-lot ceiling can carry (`brand_market §3`, `yarn §3b`). **Two would forfeit the price-anchor/halo (A3) or the cheap front door (A2).** Three is the smallest set that covers all three economic roles **and** stays inside the colour/MOQ ceiling.
- **The capsule cross-merchandises:** shared neutral anchors + shared silhouette block + shared grade rule mean the 3 articles **bundle** to push AOV >~$90 (the CAC-headroom target, `brand_market §2`) and **share dye lots** where colours overlap (defends the colour budget, `yarn §3b`).
- **The line-plan matrix (the T8 deliverable, `SCOPE_GLOSSARY §8`):** styles × colours × sizes × price × drop. Launch posture: **3 articles × 2 silhouette variants (capped) × ~2–4 colours × 6 sizes**, sequenced across the 4 drops (§3c), priced on the T7 ladder (A2 ~$45–65 / A1 ~$70–95 / A3 ~$120–180, *positioning not a model — T9 sets floors against live landed cost*). Size ratio set by the **pop-up size curve (§2e), not a guess.**

**The one design-side viability check (carried, not owned here):** the line plan is sound on *design/role/MOQ* logic, but whether the price ladder clears the full variable stack (landed duty + CAC + ~24% returns) is **T9's call, not design's** (`brand_market §2`). Design's job is to make the 3 articles *worth* their tier and *cheap to vary*; economics decides if the tiers hold.
