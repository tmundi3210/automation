**Role.** Where Analyst A mapped *what each finishing step does*, this file makes the **decisions**: (1) the **best-quality finishing recipe per article** (A1 cotton-blend core, A2 fine-acrylic value, A3 merino/lambswool premium) tuned to beat **pilling and returns**; (2) the **make-vs-OUTSOURCE call per step** (consistent with the binding T1/T2 rule: never own a dyehouse / wet processing is outsourced); (3) **cost bands `[ESTIMATE]`**; and (4) the **finishing acceptance checks** that close the loop into **T5 QC** (the "thread-checker" scorecard-and-code). **Honesty tags (binding, same discipline as T1/T2/Analyst A):**
- **[FACT]** = cited public source / true-by-definition identity.
- **[ESTIMATE]** = reasoned, with method + basis + confidence + the literal word *estimate*. **Every cost/throughput/dose/temperature band is `[ESTIMATE]`.**

> **The one sentence to remember.** *The recipe is "set the floor upstream, finish to the ceiling, and gate the finished garment" — A1 cotton wins its grade with a **real finishing route** (gas-singe yarn + cellulase bio-polish, strength-capped), A2 acrylic's grade is **already lost or won at the yarn PO** (finishing only buys a fraction of a grade via a silicone anti-pill top-up), and A3 wool's grade is a **milling+boarding+relax** craft job; **every wet/regulated step is outsourced** (we own the tech-pack recipe and the post-wash test gate, not a single machine), and the whole thing is held together by a **per-garment finishing cost that is genuinely `[UNKNOWN]` until we get a Ludhiana job-work quote** — which blocks the T4 model exactly as the per-piece linking rate does.*

## 1. How to read a finishing "recipe" (the decision frame, not the chemistry)

A finishing recipe per article is a **5-tuple decision**, each entry chosen against the article's quality tier and the return-driver it defends:

1. **Route** — the ordered list of steps the garment actually passes (A picked the steps; I fix the *sequence + which are mandatory vs optional up-positioning*).
2. **Dial settings** — the small number of parameters that decide whether a step *helps* or *destroys* (bio-polish strength-cap; milling time; softener chemistry; compacting residual-shrink limit).
3. **Make-vs-outsource** — own the spec, buy the process (§4). Binding constraint: **all wet/regulated steps outsourced (T1).**
4. **Cost band** `[ESTIMATE]` — what the step adds per garment (§5), held honestly as a band with the point `[UNKNOWN]`.
5. **Acceptance check** — the numeric gate on the **finished garment** that proves the recipe worked, feeding the T5 scorecard (§6).

**The governing principle, stated once (it drives every recipe below):**
**Finishing is the *second* line of pilling defence, never the first.** The floor is set in the yarn PO (T2's five dials: staple, twist, ply, fineness, blend ratio). Finishing then either delivers a *real* additional grade (cotton: singe+bio-polish) or merely a *cosmetic fraction* (acrylic: chemical top-up). **A recipe that promises finishing will "fix" a cheap yarn is the single most common and most expensive spec lie** — it shows up as a great showroom sample and a field-return wave one wash later. `[ESTIMATE: judgment, HIGH — converges with A §3c and T2 §4]`

## 2. The three recipes (prescriptive, best-quality, return-minimising)

Each recipe is written as **what to put in the tech-pack**, with the mandatory core separated from optional up-positioning, the one or two dials that decide pass/fail flagged, and the acceptance check that closes it. Dose/temperature windows are inherited from A §1–7 and not re-derived; my contribution is the **sequencing, the mandatory/optional split, the cost band, and the gate**.

### A1 — Cotton-blend 12-month core (12GG): the recipe that does the most work

**Why this one matters most:** A1 is the everyday volume seller (T2), it's cellulosic so it has a *real, ownable, verifiable* anti-pill route, and cotton's failure modes (bagging-out / low recovery, relaxation shrinkage on a customer who tumble-dries) are exactly the warm-California returns we must kill.

**Recipe (mandatory core):**
1. **Yarn-stage gas-singe** (bought *in* on the yarn — see make-vs-buy §4) → low-hairiness, lustrous, fewer pill anchors. `[FACT — singeing removes surface fuzz, A §3a]`
2. **Scour** (de-oil; can be folded into the dye/bio-polish bath — bought dyed, T2). `[FACT — combined bath, A §1/§3b]`
3. **Cellulase bio-polish** — the load-bearing anti-pill step. **Dial that decides pass/fail: the strength cap.** Spec *both* a target hand *and* a **minimum retained bursting/tensile strength**; mandate enzyme inactivation. Over-treat = a weak, thin, returnable garment. `[FACT — strength-loss risk + mandatory kill, A §3b]`
4. **Hydrophilic-silicone soften** — *not* amino-silicone: A1 is marketed breathable, and hydrophobic amino-silicone kills the moisture-pickup the care story sells. **Dial: softener chemistry must be wash-durable + hydrophilic.** `[ESTIMATE: prescription, MED-HIGH — A §4]`
5. **Steam-press / light board** to tech-pack measurements.
6. **Wet-relax + compact** to a **residual-shrinkage limit (≤ ±3–5% target)** — cotton's relaxation shrinkage must be spent in the factory, not at the customer's tumble-dryer. **Dial: residual-shrink limit.** `[ESTIMATE: ±3–5% typical retail tolerance, MED — confirm per buyer; A §6]`

**Optional up-positioning (only if A1 is sold "premium cotton"):** **mercerised + gassed cotton yarn** bought in (lustre, strength, dye uptake). A yarn-stage buy, not a finisher step. `[FACT — A §7]`

**Best-quality verdict:** A1 is the article where finishing genuinely earns ~half a grade of pilling improvement and the entire home-wash dimensional promise. **Spec it fully; it's the recipe with the highest return-on-finishing.**

### A2 — Fine-acrylic value driver (7–12GG): the recipe that's mostly already decided upstream

**Why this one is a trap:** acrylic is thermoplastic (can't be cleanly gas-singed — flame beads/melts it) and non-cellulosic (cellulase does nothing). So **~80% of A2's pilling defence is in the yarn PO** (anti-pill / high-dpf ≥~2.5 dpf grade, higher twist, 2-ply, tight gauge+density), and finishing contributes only a **chemical/silicone anti-pill softener top-up worth a fraction of a grade.** `[ESTIMATE: process-reasoning, MED-HIGH — A §3c]`

**Recipe (mandatory core):**
1. **Light scour** (de-oil only; low felt/shrink risk).
2. **Chemical / silicone anti-pill softener** — the *only* finishing-stage anti-pill lever for acrylic; a resin/silicone that anchors surface fibres. **Dial: enough to anchor fibres without stiffening the hand or harming fastness.** `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore, MED-HIGH — A §3a/§4]`
3. **Steam-press** (acrylic heat-sets readily — *under-* rather than over-set; excess heat glazes/flattens acrylic hand). `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore, MED]`
4. **Relax + compact** to residual-shrink limit (acrylic shrinks less than cotton but still carries knit stress).

**Best-quality verdict (blunt):** **A2's ICI ≥3–4 post-wash grade is won or lost at the yarn PO, not the finishing line.** The single most important "finishing" decision for A2 is made one stage earlier: **buy an anti-pill / high-dpf acrylic grade, never the cheapest 1-D.** If the line is forced onto cheap acrylic, no finishing recipe rescues it — re-open the yarn spec, don't pile on chemical anti-pill (which just stiffens the hand and still fails the wash). `[ESTIMATE: judgment, MED-HIGH — A §3c, T2 §4]`

### A3 — Merino/lambswool premium capsule (thin Nov–Feb drop): the craft recipe

**Why this one is different:** A3 is **low-volume, high-skill, high-irreversible-risk**, and its *whole* quality story is finishing-delivered (milling + boarding + relax) rather than yarn-anti-pill-delivered. It's also the article where we push the bar to **ICI ≥4** (premium wool) and add a **few-% nylon upstream** as the cheapest pill+abrasion lever (T2). `[ESTIMATE — A §3a/§8, T2 §4]`

**Recipe (mandatory core):**
1. **Gentle scour** (felt risk starts here — minimal mechanical action).
2. **Milling / fulling — WOOLLEN-SPUN ONLY, worsted merino milled lightly or not at all.** **The single highest-risk finishing step in the entire line: irreversible felt-shrinkage.** **Dial: TIME (short, minutes), gated on a felt/area-shrinkage + hand limit, never "run to the clock".** Push to a **wool-experienced job-worker**; do not learn this in-house. `[FACT — over-milling irreversible, A §2]`
3. **Soften** (amino-silicone acceptable here — A3 sells on luxe hand, not breathability).
4. **Board / form-press** — the premium fully-fashioned hand and symmetry are set here; **critical, not optional, for A3.** `[ESTIMATE: FF-boarding link, MED-HIGH — A §5]`
5. **Wet-relax + shrink-control** (combines the felt-relax and the stress-relax).

**Few-% nylon caveat:** nylon is a *yarn* decision (T2), not a finishing step — but it's the reason A3 can hit ICI ≥4 with a soft hand. Confirm it's in the yarn PO before promising the grade.

**Best-quality verdict:** A3 is a **buy-the-craft** article: its quality is in a skilled wool finisher's hands (milling judgment + boarding), on the lowest volume, at the highest per-garment finishing cost. The recipe is sound; the **risk is capacity/skill availability in-cluster, which is `[UNKNOWN]`** (§7).

## 4. Make-vs-OUTSOURCE — the finishing boundary (the decision this file owns)

**The call, stated once and applied per step: OUTSOURCE 100% of the wet/skilled/equipment-heavy finishing; the brand owns only (a) the recipe in the tech-pack and (b) the post-wash test gate.** This is the *same* posture T2 took on yarn ("contractual QC, not in-house metrology") and the *same* boundary T1 drew on dyeing ("never own a dyehouse — highest-regulatory-risk node, CETP non-compliance, NGT, closure exposure"). Finishing inherits both rules; nothing here contradicts them. `[ESTIMATE: judgment, HIGH — inherited T1 §5b/§6, T2 §4c/§5]`

### 4a. Why outsource every wet step (not just dyeing)

| Reason | Applies to |
|---|---|
| **Regulatory/effluent risk** — wet processing generates effluent; the cluster's CETPs are non-compliant and under NGT review. Owning *any* wet node imports closure risk. `[FACT — T1 §5b]` | Scour, bio-polish, dye, milling, soften |
| **Capital + skill** — singe ranges, enzyme baths with strength control, milling machines, boarding forms, compactors are equipment-heavy and skill-bound; a 3-article micro-line can't utilise them. `[ESTIMATE: HIGH]` | All wet/mechanical finishing |
| **Volume** — at micro-capsule volumes, in-house finishing equipment sits idle; outsourcing converts a fixed cost to a per-garment job-work charge. `[ESTIMATE: HIGH]` | All |
| **Agglomeration tailwind** — Ludhiana's walking-distance job-work mesh (knit/dye/link/finish) is *built* for renting exactly these steps (T1 §3 moat #5 — the one moat that helps us). `[FACT — T1 §3]` | All |

### 4b. The single thing we DO own (and it's not a machine)

The brand owns the **tech-pack finishing recipe + the finished-garment acceptance gate** (§6). That's it. This is deliberately the *cheap, leverage-dense* half of the make-vs-buy split: **buy the process, own the spec and the test.** It mirrors T2's yarn posture exactly and is the same loop the **T5 thread-checker** closes (the per-lot scorecard-and-code returned to the named supplier — which the T5 deep-dive judged the *only* ownable sliver, cheap to build, valuable only with contractual leverage). `[ESTIMATE: design-framing, HIGH — T5 thread-checker analyst]`

### 4c. The one nuance worth flagging — yarn-stage vs garment-stage steps split across two vendors

Two steps in our recipes happen at the **yarn supplier**, not the garment finisher: **gas-singeing (A1)** and **mercerising (A1 optional)** are bought *in on the yarn* (T2 §… / A §7). The rest (scour, bio-polish, mill, soften, press, relax) happen at the **garment knitter/finisher** job-worker. **Consequence for the contract:** the finishing recipe spans **two POs** — the yarn PO (singe/mercerise/anti-pill grade/nylon%) and the CMT/finishing PO (the wet+mechanical garment steps) — and **both must carry the same post-wash ICI + shrinkage gate**, or a step skipped at one vendor passes the test at the other. This is the "one continuous contract" A §3d named, made explicit as a *two-PO* reality. `[ESTIMATE: judgment, MED-HIGH]`

## 5. Cost bands `[ESTIMATE]` — and the honest `[UNKNOWN]` that blocks the model

**Method (stated for honesty discipline):** there is **no clean public per-garment finishing job-work rate for Ludhiana** — the same gap as T1's missing per-piece *linking* rate and A's open question #3. So these bands are **triangulated, not measured**: (a) finishing is consistently a *minority* of the knit-garment cost stack (T1 §5a put labour-incl-link/finish at ~15–25% and dyeing/finishing/overhead/packing in "the rest"); (b) the dominant garment costs are yarn (~50–65%) and knit+link labour; (c) finishing-only (scour/soften/press/relax, excluding dye which is in the yarn price since we buy dyed) is a thin slice on top. Bands are **order-of-magnitude** and the **point is `[UNKNOWN]`**. **Do not feed a point into the T4 margin model without a live Ludhiana job-work quote.** `[ESTIMATE: triangulation, confidence LOW-MED on magnitude, HIGH on "it's a minority slice"]`

### 5a. Per-garment finishing cost bands (excluding dyeing — bought dyed, in the yarn price)

| Article | Steps costed | `[ESTIMATE]` USD/garment | Confidence | Driver of the band |

Keep the **direction** (folding polish into dye is cheaper, MED-HIGH); **distrust the exact %** (single-vendor). This is a *negotiation lever* with the dyer, not a modelled number. `[FACT — combined biopolish+dye cited; A §3b — exact % LOW]`

## 6. Finishing acceptance checks — closing the loop into T5 QC

This is the hand-off that makes the recipe enforceable. **The brand owns the spec and this gate (not the machines, §4).** Every check below runs on the **FINISHED garment** (after the *same* home-wash cycle, where wash-sensitive), and each produces a pass/fail the **T5 thread-checker scorecard** stamps into the per-lot grade code returned to the named supplier. This *extends* T2's yarn gate (which fired at the yarn lot) onto the finished garment — testing only the yarn lets a skipped finish or loose knit quietly fail; testing only the garment lets a bad yarn pass one lab dip. `[ESTIMATE: design-framing, HIGH — T2 §4c/§4d, A §3d/§10, T5 thread-checker]`

### 6a. The finishing acceptance checklist (attach to the CMT/finishing PO)

| # | Check | Method / standard | Accept criterion | Defends against | Article |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pilling (post-wash)** | ICI Pilling Box / **ISO 12945-1** (box method for soft sweater knits, **not** -2 Martindale), graded 1–5 to nearest half-grade, after ≥1 home wash | **A1/A2 ≥3–4; A3 ≥4** | #1 return driver | All |
| 2 | **Dimensional stability / shrinkage %** | Measure to tech-pack after the **same** home-wash cycle as #1 | Within **±3–5%** (confirm per buyer) | undersized-garment returns | All |
| 3 | **Spirality / twisted side-seam** | Visual + skew measurement after wash | Within tolerance (fixed upstream by **balanced ply**, T2 dial 2) | "twisted seam" complaint | All (esp. singles-risk) |
| 4 | **Retained bursting/tensile strength** | After bio-polish, vs un-polished baseline | **≥ stated minimum** (cap the enzyme strength-loss) | weak/thin garment | **A1 (mandatory)** |
| 5 | **Hand / softener durability** | Hand assessment **after wash**, not just showroom | Hand survives ≥1 wash (catches silicone masking) | "felt great, harsh after one wash" | All (esp. A1/A3) |
| 6 | **Felt / area-shrinkage limit (milling gate)** | Area-shrink + hand vs target | Within milling limit (irreversible if over) | over-milled write-off | **A3 (mandatory)** |
| 7 | **Shade match ΔE + colour fastness** | ΔE lot-to-lot; wash/rub/light fastness minima | ΔE + fastness minima met (in PO at the dye stage used) | shade-drift returns | All |
| 8 | **Workmanship / appearance** | **AQL** sampling (+ 4-point for any C&S panels) | Buyer AQL met | mixed defects, measurement faults | All |

### 6b. Why these specific checks (the recipe→check pairing)

Each acceptance check exists to verify **one dial in §2 actually held**:
- **Bio-polish strength cap (A1)** → check **#4** (retained strength). Without it, "softer hand" hides a weakened garment.
- **Milling time (A3)** → check **#6** (felt/area-shrink). The irreversible step needs a hard limit, not a clock.
- **Hydrophilic softener (A1) / softener masking** → check **#5** (hand *after* wash). Showroom hand is not the product.
- **Compact residual-shrink (all)** → check **#2** (post-wash shrinkage). The home-wash promise is the product for a US tumble-dry customer.
- **Balanced ply (upstream)** → check **#3** (spirality), exposed here, fixed at the yarn.
- **The anti-pill route (per fibre)** → check **#1** (post-wash ICI), the master gate that the whole recipe serves.

### 6c. How it closes into T5 (the scorecard-and-code)

The T5 thread-checker deep-dive concluded: **don't build the sensors/vision rig (Uster-class, premature); do build the cheap per-lot scorecard + grade-code loop on top of contractual QC + spot third-party lab tests.** This finishing checklist is **exactly the finished-garment subset of inputs to that scorecard** — checks #1 (pilling), #2 (shrinkage), #7 (shade) are the three the T5 device/loop is meant to verify cheaply and **code back to the named supplier**. So the finishing gate is not a separate system: it's the *garment-side half* of the same per-lot QC scorecard T2 opened on the yarn-side. The brand's leverage is **contractual/relational** (a supplier accumulating out-of-spec codes), not a clever device. `[ESTIMATE: design-framing, HIGH — T5 thread-checker analyst; consistent with A §10]`
