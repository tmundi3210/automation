## 1. The thesis both analysts converge on (AGREED)

**`[AGREED — A "one sentence" + B "one sentence", convergent, HIGH]`** Finishing is the **second line of pilling defence, never the first.** The yarn PO (T2's dials: staple, twist, ply, fineness, blend ratio) sets the floor; finishing then either delivers a *real* extra grade (cotton: singe + bio-polish) or only a *cosmetic fraction* (acrylic: chemical top-up). What finishing *does* genuinely own, for every article, is the **retail "hand"** and the **customer's home-wash dimensional experience.** The governing fault both flag in identical language: **a recipe that promises finishing will "fix" a cheap yarn is the single most common and most expensive spec lie** — great showroom sample, return wave one wash later.

**Three corollaries both state `[AGREED, HIGH]`:**
- **Finishing is fibre-route-specific** — there is **no universal "anti-pill finish."** The route is dictated by fibre chemistry, not by a generic line spec.
- **The brand owns the SPEC + the TEST GATE, not the machines** — "buy the process, own the contract." Identical to T2's yarn posture and the same loop the T5 thread-checker closes.
- **Finishing's *price* is not the binding constraint; its *quality gate* is.** Like yarn, the small absolute cost is decisive only because it governs returns.

## 2. The chain (AGREED process spine)

**`[AGREED — A §intro chain; B §2 sequences it; FACT for the step identities, ESTIMATE for "not every garment uses every step"]`**

`knit panels → linking/assembly → scour/wash → (wool only) mill/full → anti-pill route (per fibre) → soften → extract/dry → steam-press / board / block → relax & shrink-control → final QC.`
Dyeing is a **stage-of-the-chain choice** (fibre / yarn / piece / garment), not a single step — handled in §7.

Per-step agreed facts:

| Step | What it does | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| **Scour / wash** | First wet step; strips spinning oils / knit lubricants (paraffin/wax) / sizing / lint so finishes deposit evenly and loops relax; under-scouring → patchy softener uptake + shade unevenness. | `[FACT — wet-processing sequence; A §1]` (the "patchy uptake" consequence `[ESTIMATE: process-reasoning, HIGH]`) |
| **Milling / fulling — WOOL ONLY** | Controlled wet+heat+agitation felts/closes the surface → fuller, softer, warmer, hairier woollen hand; **same physics as accidental shrink-felting, stopped at a target.** Worsted merino milled lightly or not at all. **Irreversible if over-done — the single most destructive finishing failure.** Gate on dimensions + felt/area-shrink limit + hand, never "to the clock"; time is the dial and it is short (minutes). | `[FACT — woolmark/woolwise; GLOSSARY §4; A §2/B §2]`; the ~39–41 °C / 8–12 min window is `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore, single patent CN101385578A, MED — order-of-magnitude]` |
| **Anti-pill (per fibre)** | Four routes (§3); not universal. | see §3 |
| **Softening** | Silicone (amino or hydrophilic) / fatty-cationic deposit for the "expensive" 3-second hand; lowers wear abrasion; **but can MASK a harsh yarn** — heavily-softened sample feels great in showroom, harsh after one wash. | `[FACT — silicone softeners, USPTO 5102930]`; amino-silicone hydrophobicity/yellowing + masking trap `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore/judgment, MED-HIGH]` |
| **Steam-press / board / block** | Heat+moisture ± form sets final dimensions, relaxes knit stress; **where tech-pack measurements are actually hit**; steam-under-tension lowers later relaxation shrinkage + hygral expansion; rushed pressing → measurement + appearance defects (twisted seams, asymmetric panels, wavy hems). Critical for FF "premium" read. | `[FACT — researchgate steam-under-tension; GLOSSARY §4]`; the FF-boarding-premium link `[ESTIMATE, MED-HIGH]` |
| **Relax & shrink-control** | Relaxation shrinkage = stored knit stress released on first wetting; **common to all fibres, non-reversible**; spend it in the factory (wet/tumble/kier-relax) not at the customer's tumble-dryer; compact/"sanforise" to a residual-shrink limit; steam-set locks it. | `[FACT — relaxation-shrinkage, sciencedirect/researchgate; GLOSSARY §4]`; the ±3–5% residual limit `[ESTIMATE: typical retail tolerance, MED — confirm per buyer]` |
| **Mercerising (A1 cotton)** | Caustic-soda-under-tension on cotton → lustre, strength, dye uptake; a **yarn/fabric-stage buy-in**, not a finisher step; pairs with gassing ("gassed & mercerised" = standard premium-cotton combo). **Optional up-positioning, not base.** | `[FACT — GLOSSARY §4; A §7]` |

## 3. Anti-pill — the load-bearing section (AGREED four routes, fibre-matched)

**`[AGREED — A §3a/§8; B §2; FACT for mechanisms, ESTIMATE for per-fibre net effect]`** Pilling = tangled balls of broken/loose **surface** fibres. T2 set the upstream defence; finishing is the second line. Four real routes, each tied to a fibre:

| Route | Mechanism | Serves | Net effect | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Singeing / gassing** (yarn-stage) | Yarn through a gas flame burns off protruding fuzz → low-hairiness, lustrous, fewer pill anchors | **Cotton (A1)** + cotton-rich/viscose blends | "Virtually eliminates pilling"; cuts surface fibre + ~26% air-drag | `[FACT — textiletradebuddy/iosrjournals]`; the ~26% figure single-source `[ESTIMATE, order-of-magnitude]` |
| **Bio-polish / cellulase** (fabric/garment wet) | Cellulase hydrolyses protruding cellulosic micro-fibrils, light abrasion snaps them off → clean, low-fuzz, softer; **wash-proof, non-greasy** | **Cotton (A1) / cellulosics ONLY** — nothing on acrylic/wool | Significant durable pill reduction + softer hand; **must inactivate the enzyme** (alkali/heat) or it keeps eating cellulose → strength loss | `[FACT — sciencedirect/shreebschemicals]` |
| **Chemical / silicone anti-pill** (resin/silicone bonding) | Polymer/resin/silicone anchors surface fibres so they don't pull free | Acrylic (A2) primary; wool/cotton adjunct | Lifts grade but can stiffen hand + risk fastness/feel | `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore, MED-HIGH]` |
| **Low-pill fibre choice** (upstream, **not a finish**) | Higher-dpf/anti-pill acrylic grade; longer staple; nylon% in soft-wool blends; tighter twist/gauge | All — set in the yarn PO | **The biggest single lever; finishing cannot fully fix a high-pill yarn** | `[ESTIMATE: industry-lore, HIGH; T2 §4b]` |

**Per-fibre resolution (AGREED):**
- **A1 cotton** — a *real, ownable, verifiable* finishing route: **gas-singe (yarn) + cellulase bio-polish (garment)**, with a **mandatory retained-strength cap** (bio-polish removes fibre = controlled tensile/weight loss; spec both target hand AND minimum retained bursting/tensile strength; over-treat = weak, thin, returnable). `[FACT — strength-loss risk; A §3b/B §2]`
- **A2 acrylic — the hard case.** Thermoplastic → can't be cleanly singed (flame beads/melts it); non-cellulosic → cellulase does nothing. **~80% of A2's pilling defence is in the yarn PO** (anti-pill/high-dpf grade, higher twist, 2-ply, tight gauge); finishing adds only a **chemical/silicone top-up worth a fraction of a grade.** Both analysts state bluntly: **A2's ICI ≥3–4 gate is won or lost at the yarn PO, not the finishing line**; if forced onto cheap acrylic, re-open the yarn spec, don't pile on chemical anti-pill. `[ESTIMATE: process-reasoning/judgment, MED-HIGH]`
- **A3 wool/merino** — chemical anti-pill adjunct + a **few-% nylon upstream** (a *yarn* decision, the cheapest pill+abrasion lever, the reason A3 can hit ICI ≥4 with a soft hand). `[ESTIMATE — A §3a/B §2; T2 §4]`

**The contract mechanism (AGREED, A §3d / B §4c):** the yarn ICI clause and the finishing route are **one continuous contract**, not two — and B sharpens it to a **two-PO reality**: singe + mercerise happen at the **yarn supplier**, the wet+mechanical steps (scour/bio-polish/mill/soften/press/relax) at the **garment knitter/finisher**, so the recipe spans **two POs** and **both must carry the same post-wash ICI + shrinkage gate**, or a step skipped at one vendor passes the test at the other. ICI method (AGREED `[FACT]`): **ISO 12945-1 box method** for soft/wholegarment sweater knits (NOT -2 Martindale, which suits flatter/denser fabric), ~2,000 revolutions baseline, graded **1 (severe)–5 (none)** to the nearest half-grade, **after ≥1 home wash** (washing drops the grade — the post-wash grade predicts returns). Retail bar **≥3–4** (premium wool **≥4**). Spec the *method*, not just the number.

## 5. Make-vs-OUTSOURCE (AGREED — the boundary B owns, A states identically)

**`[ESTIMATE: judgment, HIGH — inherited T1 §5b/§6, T2 §4c/§5]`** **OUTSOURCE 100% of wet/skilled/regulated finishing; the brand owns only (a) the recipe in the tech-pack and (b) the post-wash test gate.** Same posture as T2 on yarn ("contractual QC, not in-house metrology") and T1 on dyeing ("never own a dyehouse — CETP non-compliance, NGT, closure exposure"). Finishing inherits both; nothing contradicts them.

Why outsource every wet step, not just dyeing:
- **Regulatory/effluent** — wet processing makes effluent; cluster CETPs non-compliant + under NGT review; owning *any* wet node imports closure risk. `[FACT — T1 §5b]`
- **Capital + skill** — singe ranges, strength-controlled enzyme baths, milling machines, boarding forms, compactors are equipment-heavy and skill-bound; a 3-article micro-line can't utilise them. `[ESTIMATE, HIGH]`
- **Volume** — at micro-capsule volume in-house finishing kit sits idle; outsourcing converts fixed cost to a per-garment job-work charge. `[ESTIMATE, HIGH]`
- **Agglomeration tailwind** — Ludhiana's walking-distance knit/dye/link/finish job-work mesh is built for renting exactly these steps (T1 §3 moat #5 — the one moat that helps us). `[FACT — T1 §3]`

**The one thing we DO own — and it's not a machine:** the **two-PO tech-pack recipe + the finished-garment acceptance gate (§6).** This is the cheap, leverage-dense half of the make-vs-buy split, and the same loop the **T5 thread-checker** closes (a per-lot scorecard + grade-code returned to the named supplier — T5 judged this the *only* ownable sliver, cheap to build, valuable only with contractual leverage). `[ESTIMATE: design-framing, HIGH — T5 thread-checker analyst]`

## 6. The finishing acceptance gate (AGREED — B's 8 checks, A's §10 framing)

Every check runs on the **FINISHED garment** (after the *same* home-wash cycle where wash-sensitive) and feeds the **T5 scorecard-and-code** returned to the named supplier. This *extends* T2's yarn-lot gate onto the finished garment: testing only the yarn lets a skipped finish/loose knit quietly fail; testing only the garment lets a bad yarn pass one lab dip. `[ESTIMATE: design-framing, HIGH — T2 §4c/§4d, A §3d/§10, B §6]`

| # | Check | Method / standard | Accept | Verifies which recipe dial | Article |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pilling (post-wash)** | ICI Box / **ISO 12945-1** (not -2), 1–5 to ½-grade, after ≥1 wash | A1/A2 **≥3–4**; A3 **≥4** | the per-fibre anti-pill route (master gate) | All |
| 2 | **Dimensional stability / shrinkage %** | Measure to tech-pack after the **same** wash as #1 | within **±3–5%** (confirm per buyer) | compact residual-shrink limit | All |
| 3 | **Spirality / twisted side-seam** | Visual + skew after wash | within tolerance | balanced ply (upstream, T2 dial 2) — exposed here | All (esp. singles) |
| 4 | **Retained bursting/tensile strength** | After bio-polish vs un-polished baseline | **≥ stated minimum** | bio-polish strength cap | **A1 (mandatory)** |
| 5 | **Hand / softener durability** | Hand **after wash**, not showroom | survives ≥1 wash | hydrophilic softener / anti-masking | All (esp. A1/A3) |
| 6 | **Felt / area-shrinkage limit (milling gate)** | Area-shrink + hand vs target | within milling limit | milling time (irreversible) | **A3 (mandatory)** |
| 7 | **Shade match ΔE + colour fastness** | ΔE lot-to-lot; wash/rub/light minima | ΔE + fastness met (PO at the dye stage used) | dye-stage choice (§7) | All |
| 8 | **Workmanship / appearance** | **AQL** (+ 4-point for any C&S panels) | buyer AQL met | press/board + assembly | All |

Checks **#1 (pilling), #2 (shrinkage), #7 (shade)** are exactly the finished-garment subset the **T5 device/loop** is meant to verify cheaply and code back to the supplier — so the finishing gate is **not a separate system**, it's the *garment-side half* of the per-lot QC scorecard T2 opened on the yarn side. The brand's leverage is **contractual/relational** (a supplier accumulating out-of-spec codes), **not a clever device.** `[ESTIMATE: design-framing, HIGH]`

## 7. Dyeing stage — yarn vs piece vs garment (AGREED; A owns the table, B defers to it)

Only Analyst A developed this; B explicitly defers ("bought dyed, in the yarn price"). No conflict. It's a **lead-time-vs-colour-risk trade**, with the *regulatory* boundary already fixed by T1 (**never own a dyehouse**) and the *commercial* boundary by T2 (**launch on bought-dyed stock shades**; dye-lot MOQ ~150–500 kg/colour ⇒ 2–4 colours).

| Option | Colour added | Strengths | Risks | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fibre / stock / top-dyed** | loose fibre pre-spin | deepest/most even penetration; best fastness; heathers/melange | longest lead; earliest commit; no agility | melange/marl (Ludhiana strength) — A2/A3 marl |
| **Yarn / package-dyed** | on the cone pre-knit | excellent consistency + fastness; enables stripe/jacquard/intarsia; durable | earlier commit; **lot-to-lot ΔE risk** → ΔE clause; longer lead | **DEFAULT** — T2 launch on bought-dyed; only route for colour patterning |
| **Piece-dyed** | knitted blank pre-cut | cheapest; standard lead; dye to demand | solids only; piece unevenness; less penetration | the **C&S value path** — off our FF quality story |
| **Garment-dyed** | finished garment | **fastest agility**; soft vintage hand | **highest risk: shifts final measurements (dye-bath shrinkage)**; splotching; seam/trim must co-dye; weakest fastness | **later hero-style drop only**, NOT launch default |

**Our call (inherited + extended) `[ESTIMATE: synthesis, MED-HIGH; regulatory/commercial anchors FACT from T1/T2]`:** **launch yarn/package-dyed (bought-dyed stock shades)** for consistency + patterning; **reserve garment-dye for a later proven hero style** (agility + vintage hand earn the measurement/fastness risk); **never piece-dye-and-cut as the core**; **whichever stage, the dye node is outsourced** (T1). Cross-cutting QC (carry to T5): **lot-to-lot ΔE + wash/rub/light fastness minima in the PO at whatever dye stage** — shade drift is a real defect a tiny brand can't eat (= check #7).

## 9. Reconciled bottom line

1. **Floor upstream, finish to the ceiling, gate the garment.** Finishing is the **second** line of pilling defence and is **fibre-route-specific**: **A1 cotton** has a *real, ownable* route (gas-singe yarn + cellulase bio-polish, strength-capped) — the highest return-on-finishing article; **A2 acrylic**'s ICI grade is **won or lost at the yarn PO** (finishing buys only a fraction via a chemical/silicone top-up — if forced onto cheap acrylic, re-open the yarn spec); **A3 wool** is a **buy-the-craft** milling+boarding+felt-relax job at **ICI ≥4** with a few-% nylon upstream.
2. **Milling is wool-only and irreversible** — high-skill, low-volume, push-to-specialist; never write "wash-and-mill" generically across cotton/acrylic; gate on a felt/area-shrink limit, not a clock.
3. **Hand = silicone softening but it can MASK a harsh yarn** — spec a **wash-durable** softener, test hand *after* wash, and don't hydrophobe-coat a "breathable" cotton article (hydrophilic silicone for A1; amino-silicone OK on A3).
4. **Boarding + relax/compact deliver the measurements and the home-wash promise** — gate dimensional-stability/shrinkage % after the *same* home wash as the ICI test (±3–5% working target); spirality is the balanced-ply sister defect exposed here.
5. **Dyeing is a fastness-vs-agility trade** — launch **yarn/package-dyed (bought-dyed stock shades)** for consistency + patterning; reserve **garment-dye for a later hero style**; never own the dye node (T1); carry **ΔE + fastness** in the PO at whatever stage.
6. **Make-vs-buy resolves to buy the process, own the spec + test gate** — **outsource 100% of wet/regulated finishing** (consistent with T1 no-dyehouse / T2 contractual-QC); own only the **two-PO recipe** (yarn PO: singe/mercerise/anti-pill grade/nylon%; finishing PO: wet+mechanical steps) **+ the finished-garment post-wash gate** — the **8-check** acceptance list that is the **garment-side half of the per-lot scorecard-and-code the T5 thread-checker returns** to the named supplier. Leverage is **contractual, not a device.**
7. **Finishing's *price* (~$0.25–1.80/garment `[ESTIMATE]`, point `[UNKNOWN]`) is not the constraint — its *quality gate* is.** The per-garment job-work rate **blocks the T4 bottom-up model** until a live Ludhiana quote.
