## The strictly better first answer would have...

Shipped the same scaffold — same roles, same gate, same header reconciliation, same calibration discipline — **plus roughly one day of work:**

1. **A fault-injection fixture suite** proving the gate rejects each defect class it claims (one seeded-broken KB per check family + a golden KB), converting the trust anchor from asserted to demonstrated (G3).
2. **A specialist contract with the same rigor as the KB contract**: required fields, KB-node groundedness resolution, a must-survive-standalone-load invariant, and the free CQ acceptance test (specialist answers its source KB's competency_questions) (G2).
3. **An explicit "gate limits" clause** — "structure + math only; prose is ungated" — written into PLAN §3 and README, plus a sketched second, non-deterministic lane: a sampled fresh-context adjudication pass and HUMAN_REVIEW_POLICY wiring for high-risk nodes (G1). This is the T12 critic loop, three years early, named as a planned mitigation rather than invented under fire.
4. **Locator fields + a sampled source-existence check** on source_registry / key_books entries, so "academically-grounded" is verified, not asserted (G4).
5. Four one-line hygiene items: orchestrator-always-re-gates clause (G5), SUPPORTED_SCHEMA_VERSIONS assertion (G6), failed_check_ids → refactor-proposal feedback clause (G7), and either the manifest writer or removal of the manifest promise (G11).

None of these required hindsight; every one is derivable from the kernel's own text plus 2026-baseline engineering practice. That is why the merged verdict is **correct: yes; most intelligent: near** — the architecture was right and durable, but the top-1% answer was concretely specifiable on day 1 and was not given.
