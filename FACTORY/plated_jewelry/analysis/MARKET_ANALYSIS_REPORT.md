## 1. Business model in one page (cost → price → margin → viability)

The money model, stepped from the economics section:

1. **The metal is not the cost.** For a plated piece the gold/rhodium consumed is a *small* line; the blank, plating labor, findings, packaging and yield dominate ex-works cost, and **CAC dominates the whole P&L**. Price on **perceived value inside a competitor band, not on a cost-multiple** [FACT].
2. **Build COGS from physics, then land it.** Plating-metal mass = `plated area (cm²) × thickness (cm) × density` (gold 19.32 g/cm³, rhodium ~12.4 g/cm³, troy oz 31.1035 g) [FACT]. A 2.5 µm heavy plate consumes **~14×** the gold of a 0.175 µm flash [FACT]. Then yield-adjust and carry through duty + freight + fees.
3. **The substrate is a duty decision bigger than its BOM line** [FACT-source: HTSUS]:
   - Gold-plated over **brass/base metal → heading 7117, ~11% MFN**.
   - Rhodium-over-**sterling/precious → heading 7113, ~5–5.8% MFN**.
   - **De-minimis is gone** (sub-$800 duty-free entry suspended 29 Aug 2025) — you import as Importer-of-Record and pay duty on a bulk entry; you cannot dropship duty-free [FACT-source].
4. **Price inside floor ≤ price ≤ ceiling.** Floor = contribution floor (keystone 2× is a sanity check only) [FACT]; ceiling = **measured** willingness-to-pay ([UNKNOWN] until a Van Westendorp / Gabor-Granger / live A/B test) [METHOD]; band = the public competitor map (~$20–60 mid-fashion) [FACT-source].
5. **Contribution, not gross margin, is the metric.** `contribution/unit = price − (landed COGS + payment fee [~2.9% + $0.30] + pick-pack + outbound shipping + expected return cost)` [FACT].

**One worked example — a pair of gold-plated brass huggie hoops [ESTIMATE, illustrative, not your cost]:**

| Line | Band-centre |
|---|---|
| Ex-works stack (blank+finding $1.50, barrier+plating $2.00, gold metal @1 µm $2.00, assembly/QC $0.75, packaging $2.25) | **~$8.50** (band $6–11) |
| ÷ first-pass yield ~0.88 | ~$9.65 |
| × duty (7117 base-metal ~11%) + freight/MPF/broker | **~$11** landed (band $9–13; surcharge [UNKNOWN] could add more) |
| Retail price picked mid-band (confirm ceiling with a live test) | **$34** |

| Contribution on a single $34 order | Free ship (subsidized) | Buyer pays ship |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution / order | **~$15.21 (~45% CM)** | **~$19.71 (~58% CM)** |

*(Gross margin over landed = 71% — the vanity number; ignore it.)* A **2-item $68 order** carries one shipping + one payment fee → contribution **~$36.73 (~54% CM)** [ESTIMATE].

**Viability — the load-bearing test (`contribution/order − CAC`):**

| CAC → | $20 | $35 | $50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Single-item order** (~$15.21) | **−$4.79** | **−$19.79** | **−$34.79** |
| **2-item order** (~$36.73) | **+$16.73** | **+$1.73** | **−$13.27** |

The launch funds its own growth from order one **only if AOV ≈ 2 items AND CAC ≲ ~$35** (or CAC driven to the mid-$20s via organic/UGC/referral) [ESTIMATE]. Realistic cold paid-social CAC planning band is **~$25–60** [ESTIMATE, bracketed by Meta ~$38 / TikTok ~$33 median CPA] [FACT-source]. LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 would need CAC ≲ ~$7 — **not attainable on cold paid social** — so **first-order economics, not LTV:CAC, is the gate**, and any healthy ratio runs through repeat/referral. Break-even ROAS ≈ 1/CM → **~2.2 at 45% CM**, ~1.85 at 54% [FACT]. **The likely point of death is working capital, not unit margin:** 6 × MOQ × landed + metal inventory + ocean lead time locks up cash for months. **Do not commit bulk MOQ before a $-capped live-ad CAC test** [METHOD].

## 3. Materials & finishes

**Base metal — a two-tier strategy, no universal winner:**

| Axis | Brass (Cu-Zn) | Sterling 925 | Stainless 316L |
|---|---|---|---|
| Relative cost | **Cheapest** | **Dearest** (tracks silver spot) | Cheap raw, plating/forming pushes cost up |
| Nickel status | Low-nickel itself; risk is a nickel **barrier** | **Nickel-free** (92.5% Ag) [FACT] | Nickel-**SAFE** but **not** nickel-free (~10–14% Ni) [FACT] |
| Best fit | Value gold-look, high volume | Premium tier, vermeil, rhodium-white | Sensitive-skin worn-bare / steel-tone / lightly PVD'd |

- **Value tier = brass + a nickel-FREE barrier (palladium / white-bronze, NOT bright nickel) + gold electroplate** — the pivotal skin-safety decision is that when the microns-thin gold wears or pores, the exposed layer must not be a nickel source [FACT].
- **Premium tier = sterling 925** for rhodium-white pieces and anything legally called **vermeil** (sterling is the *required* base for vermeil) [FACT].
- **"Surgical steel" is marketing, not a purity/release guarantee** [FACT]. 316L is hard to cold-form and hard to plate (passive film → adhesion failure without an activation strike) [FACT].

**Skin-safety is earned by a test, not by the word "hypoallergenic" (which is unregulated).** The gate is **EN 1811 nickel release run AFTER EN 12472 wear pre-conditioning**, on the worst-case exposed layer: **≤0.2 µg/cm²/week for pierced-ear posts, ≤0.5 for general prolonged contact** (EU REACH Annex XVII entry 27) [FACT]. The US has **no** federal nickel-release limit — so for you this is a **differentiation and safety bar, not a legal mandate** [FACT] — and worth holding, because a bad first pair of earrings permanently removes a young buyer. **"Nickel-free" ≠ "nickel-safe"** — never conflate them in copy [FACT]. The DMG spot test is a cheap screen only, never a certificate [FACT].

**Rhodium over silver — the correct science.** Silver tarnish is a chemical reaction (metallic silver + trace sulfur → black **Ag₂S**), not lost polish [FACT]. Rhodium (a hard ~800–1000 HV, bright bluish-white, inert platinum-group metal) does two jobs in one coat: **(1) an inert barrier that stops sulfur reaching the silver, (2) a hard, mirror-bright cosmetic** — so the owner's "keeps silver shiny / prevents un-shine" intuition is directionally right [FACT]. White gold is rhodium-plated for the same reason [FACT]. Thickness: flash ~0.05–0.1 µm (wears at high-touch points), decorative ~0.1–0.25 µm; above ~0.25–0.5 µm rhodium micro-cracks and cost climbs — **thicker is not simply better** [FACT]. **Re-dip is normal maintenance (~1–3 yrs on a worn ring), not a defect** [ESTIMATE — trade practice]; any "lasts N years" claim is [ESTIMATE gated by tests], never guaranteed. **Cost flag:** rhodium ≈ $8,000/oz vs gold ≈ $4,141/oz — **roughly 2× gold per ounce and the most volatile input in the stack**; per-piece mass is tiny but **re-pull spot before any COGS build** [FACT-source; METHOD].

**Gold-finish FTC labels (16 CFR Part 23 — naming boundaries, not opinions):**

| Term | Threshold (all conditions bind) | Fit |
|---|---|---|
| Gold flash / wash | **<0.175 µm** ≥10k — may **NOT** be called "plate" | low-touch decorative |
| **Gold electroplate / plated** | **≥10k, ≥0.175 µm** on all significant surfaces | **your value tier** |
| Heavy gold electroplate | same fineness, **≥2.5 µm** | high-touch (rings, clasps) |
| **Vermeil** | **sterling base + ≥10k + ≥2.5 µm** (all three) | premium tier — base-metal core is **never** vermeil |
| Gold-filled / rolled gold plate | karat layer **mechanically bonded, ≥1/20 weight** | decades of wear; not plating, not solid |

10k = 41.7% is the FTC floor for calling anything "gold" [FACT]. Hard rules you must not cross: no calling gold-over-brass "vermeil," no calling sub-0.175 µm flash "plated," no calling nitride-only PVD "gold plated" [FACT]. **Confirm any label word against an XRF/coulometric thickness + karat + base check at the worst-case point before printing, and clear borderline claims with counsel** [FACT].

**"High-quality" is a gate judged at the worst-case point (recess, ring interior), never on the proud average** [FACT]. Acceptance battery: thickness/label (XRF ISO 3497 + coulometric ISO 2177), adhesion (ASTM B571), tarnish/corrosion (salt spray ISO 9227), wear (Taber ASTM D4060), porosity (ferroxyl / ASTM B735), nickel release (EN 1811 after EN 12472) [FACT]. **The porosity gate is why thickness cannot simply be minimized** — a thin flash on an active base passes visual inspection and is a corrosion time-bomb [FACT]. Suggested SKU tiers: **A** brass + barrier + electroplate ≥0.175 µm (low-touch studs/pendants); **B** brass/sterling + barrier + heavy electroplate ≥2.5 µm (rings, huggies, bracelets); **C** sterling + rhodium 0.1–0.25 µm or genuine vermeil (premium white).

**Cost-down levers that hold quality fixed (move ONE lever, re-run the same battery; if it still passes at the worst case, the saving is real):**
1. **Base-metal lever** — cheapest substrate that still clears nickel + porosity for the duty (nickel-bearing base OK only behind a compliant nickel-free barrier).
2. **Selective thickness placement** — class thickness on high-wear/visible zones, thinner-but-porosity-safe in hidden recesses; **never below the FTC class or the ~1–2.5 µm porosity-safe band**.
3. **Barrier architecture (best lever — cuts cost AND raises durability)** — a hard Pd/white-bronze/sulfamate-nickel underlayer lets a thinner gold survive the wear gate; moves the whole cost-quality curve favorably.
4. **Finish to the gate, not to prestige** — rhodium only where a white piece must stay bright; e-coat-protected thinner gold may pass the same gates for less.
5. **Batch/run economics** — fill the bath, amortize setup and once-per-product accredited tests — **but per-batch drift checks survive any batch-size increase**.

**Refuse these false economies (they are quality cuts, not value engineering):** thinning noble metal below the porosity-safe band on an active base; deleting the barrier; a cheaper nickel-bearing base without a compliant barrier; skipping per-batch QC; quoting in-house-screen results as accredited claims [FACT].

## 4. Shapes to make

**Two production facts decide almost everything** [FACT-domain]: your route is small-batch DIY electroplating (barrel + modest rack), bought-in findings/chain, hand assembly — **no casting foundry, no automated stone-setting**. And **Faraday-cage recess starvation** means flat/gently-convex forms plate evenly while deeply-recessed, filigree, hollow or hinged forms plate thin/unevenly and wear early. This is why intricate statement pieces are both hard to make *and* hard to keep looking good.

**Producibility ranking (scored inside your envelope, orders categories more confidently than it spaces them):**

| Tier | Forms | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Simplest (launch-core-safe)** | Flat **stamped studs**; **pendant-on-bought-chain**; **endless/huggie hoops**; **anklets** and **chain/link bracelets** | Low component count, plating-friendly geometry, barrel-platable, mostly bought findings. The anklet is the **structural floor** (bought chain + clasp + jump rings — no forming/casting/setting). |
| **Middle** | Drop/dangle earrings, station chains, **plain/open/adjustable rings**, bangles | One more finding or a forming/temper step, still hand-ownable |
| **Hardest (avoid at launch)** | Cast-and-stone-set rings, tennis bracelets, rigid spring cuffs, deeply-recessed/hinged statement forms | Needs equipment you don't have **and** plates badly (Faraday-starved) |

**Standardization lever** [METHOD]: share one post style, one chain gauge, one clasp family, one bail across all six — vary the visible body, standardize the invisible findings.

**Photograph-and-convert.** A genuine imaging fact: a piece reads well at a ~150px thumbnail with an **unambiguous high-contrast silhouette, controlled reflectivity, a clear on-body scale reference, simple negative space** [FACT-imaging] — so **round/hoop, stud/point, bar/paperclip, simple pendant** silhouettes are structurally strong, and they are the *same* plating-friendly forms as above. Finish is a style lever: rhodium-white reads modern/minimalist, gold-tone reads classic/coastal — the same silhouette shifts family across the two finishes [FACT-domain]. **Hard boundary: "photographs well" is NOT "converts"** — an oversize hoop can be returned as too heavy; which silhouette actually converts for your buyer is [UNKNOWN] until measured via the view→ATC→purchase funnel per shape, controlled for price/photo/grid-position/seasonality/ad-spend confounds, weighting on-site conversion above social engagement [METHOD]. Route **durable silhouettes** (hoops, studs, huggies, paperclip chains, pendants) into the core; **cap fad motifs** at a small cheap test [METHOD].

**Sizing and returns — the decisive plated fact:** an electroplated layer is negligible for fit, **but you cannot bench-resize a plated ring** (cutting cracks the plating → full re-plate), so a wrong ring size is a **full exchange, not a resize** — far costlier, and it pushes the launch toward forms with **no fit dimension** [FACT-domain].

- **SIZELESS** (no fit) — studs, hoops, pendants on standard/adjustable chains, charms. Returns come from appearance/hygiene/allergy, never size.
- **LENGTH-CHOICE** (absorbed by a +1–3 in extender) — necklaces, anklets, clasp bracelets.
- **SIZED** (hard fit) — rings, rigid bangles.

**Return-minimizing ranking:** `LOWEST → sizeless (studs/hoops/pendants) → adjustable (open rings, cuffs, slider chains) → fixed-length chains → rigid bangles → HIGHEST fixed-size rings` [METHOD on ordering; absolute rates UNKNOWN]. Adjustable constructions (open rings flexing ~2–3 sizes) are the most powerful size-return lever after choosing sizeless categories. Sector benchmark (NOT your rate): jewelry returns **~8–20%, rings the sizing problem (~15% resized), adjustable necklaces/earrings lower** [FACT-source]. Two return drivers a size chart does NOT fix: **hygiene** (pierced earrings commonly final-sale — attack risk pre-purchase with to-scale images) and **nickel allergy** (the post is the strictest skin-contact case — a materials confirmation, not a sizing fix) [FACT-domain].

Two binding pre-design gates cleared before any render: **cultural/religious motif gate** (route sacred imagery to human review — "AI-designed" does not launder a sacred symbol; prefer secular/geometric) and **IP/originality gate** (no copying a fashion house's signature motif/trade-dress — keep forms generic/original by design) [FACT-legal / FACT-ethics].

## 6. Run this poll first

The decision this poll must make: **of the six launch SKUs, how do they split across category and finish?** Because that is a **ranking**, a folder of independent "do you like earrings?" yes/no polls is worthless (a friendly audience says "yes" to everything) — you need a **forced trade-off**. Run both a cheap single-choice screen and a forced-trade-off follow-up. A poll % is a responder **[SIGNAL]**, never demand.

### Poll A — single-choice category screen (copy-paste verbatim)

> **"Which piece do you reach for most?"**
> ◻ Earrings ◻ Necklaces ◻ Rings ◻ Bracelets ◻ Anklets *(◻ None of these)*

Options are MECE at one taxonomic level, with an explicit opt-out. Native stickers can't randomize order, so **counterbalance** by running it on ≥2 creatives with the category order swapped. A native IG poll sticker is exactly **2** options and a quiz is up to **4**, so the 5-way item ships either off-platform or as a paired-comparison funnel.

Two caveats: platform age/interest data is an **inferred proxy, not a census**, and **the optimizer, not you, curates the responder slice** [FACT]. **No protected-class targeting.** Use a cheap engagement objective for Poll A; optimize the ranking polls for Traffic/Leads (the completed off-platform answer is the cleanest vote).

**Incentive — built to MINIMIZE bias.** A broadcast free-jewelry giveaway recruits freebie-hunters whose intent doesn't match your buyer — and **bigger n does not fix self-selection; it is a more precisely wrong number** [FACT]. Levers: (1) give away **one of your own jewelry pieces** (filters toward jewelry-interested respondents), not cash; (2) **single random draw**, not "everyone gets $X"; (3) **decouple prize from the answer and say so** ("your answer doesn't affect your odds"); (4) **collect the preference first, offer entry after**; (5) **modest prize value**; (6) **measure the bias with an incentivized-vs-not A/B** — if the two arms rank categories differently, the incentive is distorting the read.

**Legal envelope (route to counsel):** a promotion combining **consideration + chance + prize** is an illegal lottery — fix it with **"No Purchase Necessary" + a free equal-odds AMOE** and published **Official Rules** [FACT]. **NY and FL require registration + a surety bond when the prize pool exceeds $5,000; RI requires registration for retail prizes over $500** [FACT-source] — **keep total prize ARV modest (≤ ~$500)** to stay under all three triggers (which is also the lower-bias choice). Disclose the material connection **clearly, in plain language, in the same place as the message** (FTC 16 CFR Part 255) [FACT]. Age-gate to 18+ US; data-minimize (CCPA/CPRA, CAN-SPAM, COPPA if reach could capture under-13s) [FACT].

**Sample size — the per-cell trap is load-bearing.** A 5-category poll **splits n across cells**; each option's own count sets its precision. To read the category rank at ~±8–10% per option, target **~100–150 usable responses per category → ~500–750 usable total** [METHOD]. MoE math: ~384 total ≈ ±5% overall, ~100 per cell ≈ ±10%. **This bounds only random sampling error — it does NOT fix self-selection or incentive bias;** a tight ±3% on an incentivized self-selected sample is precision without accuracy.

**Cost (illustrative arithmetic, every input flagged — NOT a quote):** benchmark CPM IG ~$6.70, TikTok ~$3.50–7 [FACT-source]. Assume CPM $7, link-CTR 1%, off-platform completion 40% → ~4 usable responses/1,000 impressions → **CPR ≈ $1.75** [ESTIMATE]. For ~500–750 usable responses ≈ **~$875–$1,310** [ESTIMATE]; a coarse n≈384 read ≈ **~$670** [ESTIMATE]. **Feasibility gate:** if a decisive per-cell n costs more than you'll spend, the honest output is "the poll can't answer that at that granularity" — simplify to fewer options or accept a coarser read.

**Read-out (feeds the assortment):** define a response as an *option choice* before counting; normalize to a **rate (responses per impression/dollar)**, not a raw count; rotate creative to control confounds; test separability (CI-overlap / two-proportion z-test with multiple-comparison caution); **apply the weak-signal rule — if top CIs overlap or n is below minimum, label the poll inconclusive and fall back to the market read + store sell-through rather than over-read noise.** A poll winner is a **directional hypothesis to validate against real sell-through** (stated ≠ revealed). Hand off reliability-weighted: **revealed store sell-through > validated poll > stated poll > inferred market estimate.**
