## Business Model, Cost & Price

_Analyst: `jewel_economics` specialist. Scope: the money model for a DTC rhodium-/gold-**plated** fashion-jewelry brand launching **six SKUs** to US women ~18–25, storefront already live. This section builds the per-piece landed COGS, turns it into a defensible retail price and contribution margin, and runs the paid-social viability arithmetic (CAC, AOV, first-order economics, LTV, payback, break-even) to a qualified go/no-go with pre-committed kill-criteria._

**Honesty contract (binds every number below).** Each figure carries one tag: **[FACT-source]** (cited public data or regulation), **[FACT]** (materials-science / accounting constant), **[ESTIMATE-basis]** (a number I derived, method shown, the literal word — this is not a claimed actual), **[METHOD]** (how to measure a number not yet obtainable), or **[UNKNOWN]** (an honest gap). **No landed cost, price, contribution, CAC, AOV or repeat rate is stated as a measured actual — this venture does not yet operate, so every dollar in the worked example is a band-centre illustration, robust in direction, soft in level.** Live gold/rhodium spot and any forward tariff surcharge are pulled at cost time and are [UNKNOWN]-forward; re-pull before any real PO.

---

### Bottom line up front (the business model in one frame)

1. **The metal is not the cost.** For a plated fashion piece the gold/rhodium consumed is a **small** line; the blank, plating labor, findings, packaging and yield dominate ex-works cost, and CAC dominates the whole P&L. So price on **perceived value inside a competitor band**, not on a cost-multiple. [FACT — fashion-price logic]
2. **Contribution margin, not gross margin, is the metric.** A 70%+ gross margin over landed COGS is a **vanity number**; what decides the business is contribution (price − *all* variable cost) minus CAC. [FACT — managerial accounting]
3. **The single-item order probably cannot absorb cold paid-social CAC.** The worked example below is first-order *negative* at a one-item basket and realistic CAC. **The model only works if AOV rises to ~2 items (bundles/sets/free-ship threshold) OR blended new-customer CAC stays low (~≤ mid-$20s).** This is the load-bearing finding. [ESTIMATE-basis: worked example §5–§6]
4. **The likely point of death is working capital, not unit margin.** Six SKUs × MOQ × landed cost + gold/rhodium inventory + ocean lead time locks up cash for months before the first sale. Per-order profit is **necessary but not sufficient**. [FACT — cash-conversion logic]
5. **Do not commit bulk MOQ before a $-capped live-ad CAC test.** CAC/AOV/repeat are the softest, most decision-critical unknowns; a verdict that leans on them is low-confidence until a live test hardens them. [METHOD]

### 1. The per-piece COGS build-up (ex-works)

**The cost stack** (bill of materials for ONE good piece), summed then yield-adjusted:

`ex-works COGS = base-metal blank + plating metal consumed + findings + direct labor + packaging`, all ÷ first-pass yield.

**The plating-metal line is the one you compute from physics, not guess:**

> **mass = plated surface area (cm²) × coating thickness (cm) × metal density**
> Gold density **19.32 g/cm³**, rhodium **~12.4 g/cm³**, troy ounce **31.1035 g** [FACT]. Thickness is the primary lever: a **2.5 µm** heavy plate consumes **~14×** the gold of a **0.175 µm** flash [FACT — geometry×thickness×density]. Price the mass at **live spot × grams**, plus a fabrication/plating-salt premium; gold spot and the volatile rhodium PGM quote are **pulled at cost time** [METHOD].

**Live inputs (re-pull before any COGS run):** gold **~$4,143–4,168/oz** (Jul 6–7 2026) [FACT-source: Kitco / CNBC / Forbes]; rhodium **~$8,000–8,700/oz** (early Jul 2026) [FACT-source: Kitco / MoneyMetals / TradingEconomics / Umicore]. Gold ≈ **$133/g**; rhodium is **~2× gold per gram and the most volatile input in the stack** — its mass per piece is tiny but its price is not, so it is a live input, never a hardcoded constant. [FACT-source]

**The FTC label class the thickness supports is a legal boundary, not a quality opinion** (carried in full by the materials section): flash **<0.175 µm** (may not be called "plate"), gold electroplate **≥10k & ≥0.175 µm**, heavy gold electroplate **≥2.5 µm**, vermeil **= gold ≥10k & ≥2.5 µm over sterling** [FACT — FTC 16 CFR Part 23]. Choose the micron against the **label/quality target**, then cost it — never thin it below the class (or the ~1–2.5 µm porosity-safe band) to back-solve a price.

### 2. Landed cost-to-stock (the true cost at your receiving door)

Yield-adjust the ex-works stack (bath efficiency, drag-out, rework → divide by first-pass yield to get cost-per-**good**-piece), then carry it through duty and freight:

> `landed = yield-adj ex-works × (1 + duty% + surcharge%) + inbound freight + MPF/HMF + broker/entry, per piece on a bulk Importer-of-Record entry`

- **Duty band is set by the substrate**, and it flips the heading [FACT-source: HTSUS]:
  - **Gold-plated over brass/base metal → imitation jewellery, heading 7117, ~11% MFN.**
  - **Rhodium-over-sterling (or any precious-metal body) → heading 7113, silver ~5% / gold-precious ~5.5–5.8% MFN.**
  - The exact 10-digit suffix and any **country tariff surcharge** stay **[UNKNOWN]** until composition, value and origin are fixed — confirm against USITC HTS / a licensed customs broker (the Importer of Record owns misclassification liability).
- **De-minimis is gone:** the sub-$800 duty-free informal entry was **suspended (Aug 29 2025)** [FACT-source: dated], so you cannot dropship duty-free — you import as IOR and pay duty + fees on a bulk entry.
- **Fees:** MPF/HMF + broker/entry spread per piece over the entry; small per unit on ocean bulk, non-trivial on air/small lots.

**So the substrate is a duty and skin-safety decision bigger than its BOM line** — a few cents of brass-vs-sterling difference moves the whole piece between an 11% and a 5% duty band and between a base-metal and a precious-metal skin story.

### 3. The working-capital trap (why per-unit profit is not enough for six SKUs)

The cash killer sits **before** the first sale:
- **MOQs stack:** per-style casting/stamping MOQ (often tens–hundreds of pieces/style), per-plating-run batch minimum, per-findings pack. Six SKUs multiplies this — you fund **6 × MOQ × landed cost** in finished-goods inventory plus any gold/rhodium the plater bills, before revenue. [FACT — MOQ reality]
- **Ocean lead time + returns settlement** stretch the cash-conversion cycle to **months** of days-inventory-outstanding.
- A per-unit-profitable brand can still **die on cash timing.** State per-order viability as **necessary but not sufficient**, and fund the cash gap explicitly. [FACT]

**Sequencing rule:** run the cheap, high-leverage tests (the poll for the SKU mix, a $-capped live-ad CAC probe) **before** committing bulk MOQ working capital. Committing MOQ ahead of the CAC test inverts the cheapest-test-first order. [METHOD]

### 4. From landed cost to a defensible price

**Method (floor ≤ price ≤ ceiling, inside a real band):**
- **Floor = contribution floor**, not keystone. Keystone (2.0× cost = 50% margin [FACT]) is a **sanity check only**, never the final price for an impulse accessory whose material cost is a small share of retail.
- **Ceiling = measured willingness-to-pay.** WTP is **[UNKNOWN] until measured** — instruments: Van Westendorp PSM, Gabor-Granger, or (strongest) a **live A/B price test** on the storefront. Do not guess it. [METHOD]
- **Band = a public-storefront competitor map**, not invented rivals. From the market section: mid-fashion rhodium/gold-electroplate pieces cluster **~$20–60**; the reported fashion-jewelry average sits near **~$35**; Etsy gold-plated listings cluster under **$25** and most under **$60** [FACT-source: market-section public scans]. Studs skew low; hoops/statement pieces higher.
- **Pick a charm price** inside floor ≤ price ≤ ceiling, within band.

**Contribution is computed against ALL variable cost, not just COGS:**
> `contribution/unit = price − (landed COGS + payment fee + pick-pack + variable outbound shipping + expected return cost)`
> Payment processing is **~2.9% + $0.30/order** on standard US Shopify Payments / Stripe [FACT-source: Shopify / Stripe published rates]. Fixed costs (site subscription, photography, your time) are **excluded here** — they enter break-even, not contribution. [FACT — accounting]

### 5. ONE worked example — cost → price → margin **[ESTIMATE-basis, NOT a claimed actual]**

**SKU:** a pair of **gold-plated brass huggie hoops**, ~1 µm gold electroplate over a nickel-free barrier, DTC value tier. *Every line is an illustrative band-centre; substitute your real quotes and a live metal pull.*

**Geometry → metal mass:** wire Ø ~2 mm, loop centreline ~1 cm → lateral area ≈ π×0.20×3.14 ≈ **~2 cm²/hoop, ~4 cm²/pair** (+coverage factor). Gold at 1 µm: 4 cm² × 1×10⁻⁴ cm × 19.32 g/cm³ ≈ **0.008 g** → ~**$1.1** deposited at spot; with drag-out/bath losses the **effective metal input ≈ $2**. (At 2.5 µm heavy plate this line is ~2.5× — still small. That is the point: **thickness swings the metal line hugely in relative terms but the metal is a small share of ex-works.**) [ESTIMATE-basis: geometry × 1 µm × density × live gold, loss-adjusted]

| Ex-works stack (per pair) | Band-centre [ESTIMATE-basis] |
|---|---|
| Brass blank + hinge finding (MOQ-amortized) | $1.50 |
| Nickel-free barrier strike + gold plating (bath + rack + labor, per-piece) | $2.00 |
| Gold metal deposited (1 µm, loss-adjusted, live spot) | $2.00 |
| Assembly + QC + polish | $0.75 |
| Packaging (box, pouch, card, polybag, hangtag) | $2.25 |
| **Ex-works subtotal** | **~$8.50** (band ~$6–11) |
| ÷ first-pass yield ~0.88 | **~$9.65** |
| × duty (7117 base-metal ~11%) | **~$10.70** |
| + freight + MPF/HMF + broker (bulk ocean, per piece) | **~$0.50** |
| **Landed COGS-to-stock** | **~$11** (band **~$9–13**; +surcharge [UNKNOWN] stress could add more) |

I use **landed ≈ $10** (round, mid-band) for the price math below.

**Price:** competitor band ~$20–60; keystone floor = 2×$10 = $20 (floor check only). Pick a charm price mid-band: **retail $34** [ESTIMATE-basis: inside band, above contribution floor; confirm the ceiling with a live A/B/WTP test — it is [UNKNOWN] until measured].

|---|---|---|
| Price | $34.00 | $34.00 |
| − Landed COGS | −$10.00 | −$10.00 |

### 6. DTC viability — CAC, AOV, first-order economics, LTV, payback, break-even

**Contribution per order** is what CAC is compared against. It scales with **AOV** and units-per-order — both **[UNKNOWN] until measured** on your live store + launch orders; read them, don't assume. The market read (low-AOV, high-frequency Gen-Z) means **basket-building is existential.** [METHOD]

**CAC benchmarks (context only — your CAC is [UNKNOWN] until a live test; platform-reported CAC runs optimistic post-ATT):**
- Meta median CPA **~$38**, TikTok CPA **~$33** (2025) [FACT-source: 2025 paid-social benchmark compilations].
- CPMs: Meta **~$11 (Q1'25), ~$23 (Q4 peak)**; TikTok **~$4.26** — cheaper reach, lower purchase intent. [FACT-source: same]
- Vertical blended CAC: apparel **~$90**, beauty **~$110** [FACT-source: eightx 2026]; US retail DTC average **~$226 (2024)** [FACT-source: Shopify data]. A low-ticket accessory should sit **below** apparel, but **cold paid-social new-customer CAC of ~$25–60 is a realistic planning band** [ESTIMATE-basis: bracketed by the CPA benchmarks above].

**First-order economics (the load-bearing test for a low-repeat impulse category):** `contribution/order − CAC`.

| CAC → | $20 | $35 | $50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Single-item order** (contrib ~$15.21) | **−$4.79** | **−$19.79** | **−$34.79** |
| **2-item order** (contrib ~$36.73) | **+$16.73** | **+$1.73** | **−$13.27** |

**Read:** at a **single-item basket the first order loses money at any realistic cold CAC** — you would be pre-paying for an [UNKNOWN] repeat tail. The launch only funds its own growth from order one if **AOV ≈ 2 items AND CAC ≲ ~$35**, or if CAC is driven to the **mid-$20s or below** via organic/UGC/referral. [ESTIMATE-basis]

**LTV (contribution basis, never revenue × a made-up multiple):** `LTV = contribution/order × expected orders/customer`. Accessories are **low-repeat**; expected orders is **[UNKNOWN]** until a cohort-retention curve exists [METHOD]. Illustratively, 20–30% reordering once ⇒ ~1.25–1.4 orders ⇒ single-item LTV ≈ **$19–21**. To clear the **LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1** heuristic [FACT-heuristic, with caveats] you would need CAC ≲ **~$7** — **not attainable on cold paid social.** Therefore **first-order economics, not LTV:CAC, is the gate**, and any path to a healthy ratio runs through **organic/referral/repeat**, not paid acquisition.

**Break-even ROAS ≈ 1 / CM ratio** → at 45% CM ≈ **2.2**, at 54% CM ≈ **1.85** (revenue-per-ad-dollar below which incremental spend loses money). Steer scaling on **marginal paid CAC**; read profitability on **blended CAC / MER** (blended is flattered by organic dilution). [FACT — BE ROAS identity]

**Break-even units** (against the fixed nut only): `fixed + launch cost / contribution per order`. A ~$150/mo platform+apps nut clears at **~10 orders/mo** at ~$15 contribution — trivial; **the real hurdle is CAC recovery and the inventory cash-out (§3), which are working capital, not fixed cost.**

### 7. Kill-criteria (pre-commit BEFORE any spend) and the qualified go/no-go

**Pre-committed kill-criteria** (numeric stops set now, so the no-go is automatic and sunk-cost can't override):
- **CAC ceiling:** blended new-customer CAC > **~$35** at target AOV for **2 consecutive weeks** → stop scaling.
- **First-order-margin floor:** `contribution/order − CAC < $0` sustained across cells → no-go.
- **AOV floor:** if AOV stays at **~1 item** (baskets don't build) → the model is structurally first-order-negative → stop.
- **Payback ceiling:** CAC not recovered within **~1–2 orders / ~3 months** → no-go for a bootstrapped DTC.
- **Return rate** > **~15%** → re-underwrite the returns reserve.
- **Learning-budget cap:** a fixed **$-capped** test window (e.g. a few $k over 4–6 weeks); if no audience/creative/price cell clears the first-order floor, **stop before the bulk PO.**

**Go/no-go (qualified, honest):** the **unit** economics are workable *if and only if* AOV builds to ~2 items and CAC lands in the ~mid-$20s–$35 range at that basket — a **real but unproven** condition. The **governing unknown is your live paid-social/organic CAC at an achievable AOV**; until a $-capped live test hardens it, the verdict is **low-confidence**. **Do not commit bulk MOQ across six SKUs before that test and before the working-capital cash gap is funded.** Lead with first-order economics, treat repeat/LTV as upside, and re-pull gold/rhodium spot and confirm the duty line at cost time.
