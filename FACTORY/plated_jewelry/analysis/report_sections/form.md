## Which Shapes to Make

_Analyst: `jewel_form` specialist. Scope: **FORM only** — which worn-location categories, silhouettes and constructions fit an 18–25 US plated-jewelry launch, which of them the owner can actually **make** on a DIY-electroplating route, which **minimize sizing-driven returns**, and a recommended **shape/category direction** for the six-SKU launch. I own which forms are *buildable, photogenic, and low-return*; I do **not** own which one *sells best* (that is the `jewel_market` demand read + the S4 poll), the final six SKUs (`jewel_assortment`), or the price/margin (`jewel_economics`)._

**Honesty contract (binds every claim below).** Each carries one tag: **[FACT-source]** (cited public figure/regulation), **[FACT-domain]** (established jewelry/plating/standards fact), **[FACT-imaging]** (a merchandising/imaging principle), **[ESTIMATE-basis]** (a judgment I derived, method shown), **[METHOD]** (the recipe to measure a number I refuse to invent), or **[UNKNOWN]**. **No shape is asserted as "best-selling," no category as "most popular," and no return rate as measured for this store** — those are outputs of the poll and your own analytics, and I give the method instead of a guess.

### 1. The frame — three axes every candidate is scored on

Every candidate piece is placed on a three-axis form frame [FACT-domain]: **(1) worn-location category** (earrings / necklaces / rings / bracelets / anklets) × **(2) construction method** (cast, stamped, wire-formed, tube-formed, chain-made, findings-assembled, stone-set) × **(3) plating/forming constraint** (rack vs barrel plating mode, geometry, substrate, wear zone). "Which type/shape do I make" is answered on axes 1×2; "which is simplest to make" folds axis 3 on. A candidate is a full coordinate — e.g. a *huggie hoop* = earring/hoop × wire-or-tube-formed × small-barrel-platable.

Two facts about your production route decide almost everything downstream [FACT-domain]:
- **Owner scale** = small-batch DTC, DIY electroplating/sputtering (limited bath, likely barrel + modest rack), **bought-in standard findings and chain**, hand assembly (jump rings, solder, simple forming/stamping), and critically **no casting foundry and no automated stone-setting line**. A category is "simple" only if its dominant method fits inside this envelope.
- **Faraday-cage / recess starvation:** in electroplating, current density is starved inside recesses, deep channels, hollows and behind protrusions, so plating there is thin or absent. **Flat and gently convex forms plate evenly; deeply recessed, filigree, hollow-interior and hinged/moving-joint forms plate unevenly** and show early wear at starved spots. This single physics fact is why intricate statement pieces are both hard to make *and* hard to keep looking good.

### 2. Which shapes you can actually MAKE — producibility ranking [METHOD/ESTIMATE, owner-scale envelope]

Scoring each form on an assembly-complexity index (components + forming ops + solder joints + plating passes + setting ops + moving parts) inside your envelope yields this ordering. **It orders categories more confidently than it spaces them, and it is a scored judgment conditional on your equipment — not a measured yield.**

| Tier | Producible forms (silhouette × construction) | Why it screens here |
|---|---|---|
| **Simplest (launch-core-safe)** | Flat **stamped studs** (face + post); **pendant-on-a-bought-chain** necklaces; **endless / huggie hoops** (wire or tube); **anklets** and **chain/link bracelets** (bought chain + clasp) | Low component count, flat/convex plating-friendly geometry, barrel-platable, mostly bought findings + a formed body. The **anklet is the structural floor** — bought chain + clasp + jump rings, no forming/casting/setting at all. |
| **Middle** | **Drop/dangle earrings**, station/charm chains, **plain or open/adjustable formed rings**, bangles | More findings or a forming/temper step, but still ownable by hand. |
| **Hardest (avoid at launch)** | **Cast-and-stone-set rings** (solitaires/halos/pavé), **tennis bracelets** (dozens of prong settings + hinge), **rigid spring-temper cuffs**, any deeply-recessed or hinged statement form | Needs a casting foundry / automated setting / heavy forming you don't have, *and* the recessed/hinged geometry is Faraday-starved so it plates badly even if you could build it. |

**The value-engineering rule when appeal and makeability collide** [FACT-domain method]: if a high-appeal shape is un-producible, first **simplify** it to a makeable form that keeps the look (reduce openwork, thicken thin sections, drop a plating pass, standardize the findings); if it can't be simplified without losing the appeal, **cut** it from the core and cap it at a small test. Never commit a plating run to a form you can't plate cleanly — a wear-through or dent-prone piece returns more than its looks earn.

**Standardization lever** [METHOD]: share one or two post styles, one chain gauge, one lobster-clasp family and a standard bail across all six SKUs. The same findings then order in volume, plate in one barrel run and assemble on one jig, so each new SKU varies only the visible body. Standardize the invisible functional findings; vary the visible form.

### 3. Which shapes PHOTOGRAPH and convert well — imaging fact vs. conversion estimate

**What is a genuine imaging fact** [FACT-imaging]: a piece reads well in a ~150px feed/PDP thumbnail when it has **an unambiguous, high-contrast silhouette**, good figure-ground contrast against skin, **controlled reflectivity** (bright rhodium/gold catching a specular highlight without blowing out detail), a clear **on-body scale reference**, and simple negative space that survives compression. On these criteria the **round/hoop, stud/point, linear/bar/paperclip and simple pendant** silhouettes are structurally strong: clean outlines, legible small, and they are exactly the plating-friendly convex/flat forms from §2. This is a real reason the producible core and the photogenic core overlap so heavily.

**Finish is a style lever, not just a material** [FACT-domain]: bright-white **rhodium over sterling** reads modern/minimalist/clean; warm **yellow-gold plating** reads classic/coastal; the same silhouette shifts style family across the two finishes. Your "rhodiumgold" grounding — **rhodium-plated silver-tone + gold-plated tone** — maps cleanly onto a two-finish, one-silhouette system, which also aids collection cohesion. Cost caveat: two-tone/masked finishes cost extra plating passes and masking, so keep launch pieces single-finish where possible.

**The hard boundary** [FACT-imaging → the rest is not a fact]: *"photographs well" is NOT "converts."* A thumbnail-optimized oversize hoop can still be returned as too heavy to wear. **Which silhouette actually converts for your buyer is [UNKNOWN] until measured**, by this method [METHOD]:
- Segment product-level analytics by **silhouette** and read the **view → add-to-cart → purchase** funnel per shape on your live site.
- Run **controlled tests** — A/B on grid placement, holdout groups, or a **pre-order/waitlist** for shapes not yet produced — instead of reading raw bestseller rank.
- **Control the confounds** that masquerade as a shape effect: price band, photo quality, grid position, seasonality, ad spend behind a SKU, and existing-catalog bias ("mindful of the old stuff").
- Weight **on-site conversion above social engagement**. Saves/shares/watch-time are a *directional, vanity-vs-intent* proxy — prefer save/share over likes, and watch the **aspirational-statement bias** where bold, un-affordable pieces over-index on engagement but under-index on purchase.
- Report every result as a **labelled [ESTIMATE] with confidence and sample-size caveat**, never "this shape sells best."

**Durable silhouette vs. fad motif** [METHOD]: route **durable silhouettes** — hoops, studs, huggies, paperclip/curb chains, simple pendants — into the launch core (broad, multi-year, cross-platform demand attaches to the *silhouette*), and **cap fad motifs** (a single viral charm) at a small, cheap, fast test. A plating run committed to an already-peaked fad becomes dead stock.

**Public context, not your number** [FACT-source]: across the US jewelry market, **earrings led the product segment at ~43.2% share in 2024** ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/us-jewelry-market-report)) and Gen Z indexes on **frequency** — **58% bought jewelry 3+ times in 2023** ([nuOrder / MVI Marketing](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/united-states-jewelry-market-report-130100432.html)). This describes the *market*, not *your 18–25 women* — it is a directional prior that the producible earring-forward core is aligned with, **not** proof your buyer prefers earrings. That is the core poll question (§6).

### 4. Sizing and returns — which categories structurally minimize sizing-driven returns [FACT-domain + METHOD]

The decisive plated-specific fact [FACT-domain]: an electroplated gold/rhodium layer is microns thin and **negligible for fit**, but you **cannot bench-resize a plated ring** — cutting/stretching/soldering cracks and abrades the plating and exposes base metal, forcing full re-plating. So a wrong ring size is handled as a **full exchange (reverse logistics)**, not a resize — materially costlier than any length adjustment. That raises the value of *getting size right the first time* far above where it sits for solid metal, and it pushes the launch toward forms that carry **no fit dimension at all.**

Partition every category by fit character [FACT-domain]:
- **SIZELESS** (no per-person fit) — **stud earrings, hoops, pendants on a standard/adjustable chain, charms**. Scale is a *look*, not a fit. Returns here come from appearance, hygiene or allergy — **never size.**
- **LENGTH-CHOICE** (a preference length, absorbed by a +1–3 in extender) — **necklaces, anklets, clasp bracelets**. Standard princess ~17–19 in default + extender covers most dissatisfaction.
- **SIZED** (hard per-person fit) — **rings** (US size adds ~2.55 mm inside circumference per full size; offer **half sizes**, and size up ~½ for wide/comfort-fit bands) and **rigid bangles** (must clear the knuckle — sized on widest hand circumference, not wrist).

**Return-minimizing ranking** [METHOD on ordering; FACT-domain on the fit-dimension basis; absolute rates UNKNOWN]:

`LOWEST → sizeless (studs, hoops, pendants on adjustable chains) → adjustable-construction (open/adjustable rings, cuffs, slider/extender chains) → fixed-length chains/clasp bracelets → rigid bangles → HIGHEST fixed-size rings`

Adjustable constructions (open rings flexing ~2–3 sizes, extender chains, oval/adjustable bangles) are the single most powerful size-return lever *after* choosing sizeless categories — one SKU covers many wearers with no size-range stocking.

**Two return drivers that a size chart does NOT fix, kept distinct from size** [FACT-domain]:
- **Hygiene:** pierced earrings are commonly sold **final-sale/non-returnable** (worn against a broken-skin site), so an earring's risk must be attacked *pre-purchase* (to-scale images, clear gauge) — you can't recover it with a return.
- **Nickel allergy:** the earring **post** is the strictest skin-contact case. Under EU REACH/EN1811, pierced-ear post assemblies must release **<0.2 µg/cm²/week** and other prolonged-contact items **<0.5 µg/cm²/week**; the US has no identical federal limit but the same allergy science applies. **Confirm the post/base-metal nickel status with `jewel_materials` (S2) before calling any earring "sell-safe"** — this is a material choice (nickel-safe/hypoallergenic post), not a sizing fix.

**Public return context — a benchmark, explicitly NOT your rate** [FACT-source]: ecommerce jewelry return rates are reported around **~8–20%**, with **rings the sizing-problem category** (one benchmark cites ~15% of rings returned for resizing) while **adjustable necklaces and earrings return lower** ([wisepim](https://wisepim.com/ecommerce-industry-insights/jewelry), [Richpanel](https://www.richpanel.com/learn/ecommerce-return-rates), [PostIndustria](https://postindustria.com/how-to-reduce-returns-in-jewelry-e-commerce-with-disruptive-technologies-ar-commerce/)). Directionally this **corroborates** the ranking above (sizeless/earrings low, rings high) but the numbers are sector benchmarks — **your rate is [UNKNOWN] until you measure it** with reason codes + size-exchange tracking (§6).

**Size-guide requirement** [METHOD]: for any sized SKU that ships, put a printable ring-sizer strip + string-wrap method, an existing-ring measurement guide, **to-scale hoop-diameter and chain-length reference images** (ruler/coin for scale or model overlay), and an explicit **band-width/size-up note** on the PDP. Measure their effect with a before/after size-coded-return-rate test. These tools are cheapest and highest-leverage exactly where resize is impossible (plated rings).

### 5. Recommended shape/category DIRECTION for the six-SKU launch

This is a **producible + low-return + photogenic + IP/culture-clear candidate direction**, not the final six (that's `jewel_assortment` combining this with the demand read) and not a claim about what sells (that's the poll). It is the field, correctly narrowed.

**Anchor the launch in the sizeless/adjustable, plating-friendly, durable-silhouette core:**

1. **Stud earrings** — flat/convex stamped face on a nickel-safe post. Simplest to make, sizeless, lowest wear zone, most photogenic small. *Gate: confirm post nickel status with S2.*
2. **Huggie / small endless hoops** — one formed wire/tube, barrel-platable, sizeless (diameter is a look), stacks for the "curated ear."
3. **Pendant on a bought chain** — form the pendant, buy chain + clasp, assemble; length-choice made adjustable with an extender; near-zero fit risk.
4. **Anklet or chain/link bracelet** — the structural production floor (bought chain + clasp), length-choice + extender.
5. **Open / adjustable ring** — gives you ring *presence* without the fixed-size return exposure (flexes ~2–3 sizes); avoids the cast-and-set trap.
6. **A second earring or a layering necklace** that shares the finding language and finish, to complete a **cohesive, stackable set** rather than six unrelated pieces.

**Design constants across all six** [ESTIMATE-basis: producibility + cohesion + return-minimization logic above]:
- **Silhouettes:** round/hoop, stud, simple pendant, bar/paperclip — durable, plating-friendly, legible at thumbnail.
- **Finish:** the two-finish rhodium-white + gold-tone system, mostly single-finish per SKU to save plating passes.
- **Scale:** small-to-mid, tied to stackability and comfort (avoid oversize/heavy pieces that photograph well but return on comfort).
- **Findings:** one shared post + one chain gauge + one clasp family + one bail (standardization lever).
- **Explicitly deferred out of the launch core:** fixed-size rings, rigid bangles, tennis bracelets, cast/pavé/stone-set pieces, and any deeply-recessed or hinged statement form — hard to make on your route, worse to plate evenly, and (for sized pieces) the highest return exposure. Any of these enters only as a small, cheap test after a positive waitlist signal.

**Two binding pre-design gates cleared before any render/sample** [FACT-legal / FACT-ethics]:
- **Cultural/religious motif gate:** generic silhouettes (hoop, stud, bar, plain pendant) are free; but religious/sacred/culturally-loaded motifs (cross, hamsa, evil-eye/nazar, Om, sacred/Indigenous imagery) are routed to human review — *"AI-designed" does not launder a sacred symbol.* Prefer secular/geometric forms when unsure.
- **IP/originality gate:** generic shapes and common motifs (heart, star, plain hoop) are free, but copying a fashion house's **signature motif/trade-dress** or a **trademarked symbol** is infringement risk and goes to counsel before any render — *"AI-designed" does not launder a copied signature.* Keep the launch forms generic/original by design and this gate is clean.
