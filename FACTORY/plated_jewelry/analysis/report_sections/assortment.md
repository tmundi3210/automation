### 1. The selection method — how the six get chosen (four gates)

The six are not "the top six by popularity." They are the output of a four-stage funnel; raw demand rank is a **prior, not a verdict** at every stage.

1. **Fuse three demand signals into one reliability-weighted score per candidate.** Every candidate is a coordinate on a shared frame — **category × form/shape × finish-tier × price-band** [FACT]. Three signals are mapped onto it and combined with weights proportional to reliability, plus shrinkage toward the market prior:
   - **S1 — public market read** (marketplace best-seller rank, search volume, retail assortment share): *revealed/proxy, sector-level, lagging.*
   - **S4 — the paid IG/TikTok poll** (stated preference of your ~18–25 women): *stated, self-selected, incentive-skewed — but the only signal actually about YOUR buyer.*
   - **Your own storefront/bestseller history** ("the old stuff"): *revealed purchase, but a small, stale, survivorship/availability-biased sample of only what you already stocked.*
   The invariant [FACT-method]: **revealed purchase outweighs a stated poll vote, which outweighs a thin engagement proxy — but no single signal ranks a candidate alone,** and store history never ranks a candidate by itself (see §5).

2. **Rank within strata, then classify ABC/Pareto.** Rank *inside* category and price-band strata so one hot category can't crowd out the list, then sort by cumulative signal share into **A (the vital few) / B / C (the long tail)** using the ~80/20 Pareto split as a *reading lens, never an invented share for your catalog* [FACT]. The A-band candidates become **hero** candidates; B/C are coverage/stacking breadth. Re-run under perturbed weights to flag any order-flips.

3. **Compose the balanced six.** Allocate the six slots across category **coverage-vs-depth**, a **good-better-best price ladder**, a **disciplined finish mix**, **collection cohesion** and **cross-sell/stacking**, name **one or two hero anchors**, and bias toward **low-sizing-return** categories when demand is otherwise comparable. Score the candidate set on a **coherence scorecard** (§4) so the pick is defensible, not taste.

4. **Prune to buildable/fundable/margin-clearing.** Intersect the balanced ideal with the feasibility set: per-SKU **MOQ**, **plating-run batching** (each distinct finish is a separate fixed-cost bath), aggregate **launch capital**, and the **contribution-margin floor** — every SKU must clear the floor, so **demand rank alone never buys a slot** (§6).

### 4. The provisional launch six + the balance logic

Filling the slot grid from the candidate pool, honoring coverage-vs-depth, the price ladder, finish batching, cohesion and cross-sell:

| Slot | Provisional SKU | Category | Finish | Price rung | Role in the set |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stud earring (pair) | Earring | Rhodium-white | **Entry** | Impulse-price first-buy converter; hero-adjacent |
| 2 | Huggie hoop (pair) | Earring | Rhodium-white | Core | **Hero anchor** — headlines paid-social; stacks with slot 1 |
| 3 | Pendant on adjustable chain | Necklace | Gold-tone (accent) | Core | Category breadth + layering base |
| 4 | Layering necklace (2nd length) | Necklace | Rhodium-white | Core | **Depth behind the hero category**; drives the stack |
| 5 | Open/adjustable ring | Ring | Rhodium-white | Core | Ring presence, no fixed-size returns |
| 6 | Chain bracelet **or** anklet | Bracelet/anklet | Gold-tone (accent) | Core→**Hero/premium** if vermeil | Wardrobe breadth; ladder top if run as vermeil |

**The balance logic, made explicit (six slots make everything compete):**

- **Coverage vs depth** [METHOD]: default is **breadth-with-one-hero** — cover the 3 highest-prior, lowest-return categories (earrings, necklaces, ring) once, then invest the *remaining slots as depth behind the single highest-conviction hero category*. Here earrings carry depth (slots 1–2) and necklaces carry depth (slots 3–4) because stacking is the AOV mechanism; rings/bracelet stay one-each for breadth. **Go deeper only if the poll gives a strong, reliable hero signal;** stay broad if preference comes back diffuse.
- **Price ladder (good-better-best)** [METHOD; prices owned by S6]: an **entry** impulse item (stud) to convert first-timers, a **core** cluster that carries the P&L, and one **hero/aspirational** top (a vermeil bracelet or statement piece) to set the ceiling. Public price context: demi-fine clusters **$50–500**, most fashion sits **under ~$30**, studs **~$50–150** [FACT-source: [MarketIntelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/demi-fine-jewelry-market) / BaubleBar–Kendra Scott listings]; observed Etsy gold-plated listings **$19–179, most under $60** [FACT-source: Etsy scan, July 2026]. A launch all at one price forgoes both the entry conversion and the aspirational anchor.
- **Finish mix (FTC-anchored, batching-disciplined)** [FACT + method]: your "rhodiumgold" grounding maps to a **two-finish system — rhodium-plated silver-tone (bright white) + gold-plated tone (warm)** [FACT]. Because **each distinct finish is a separate fixed-cost plating bath** [FACT-process], the set runs **one dominant finish (rhodium-white) + one accent (gold-tone)**, not a rainbow — the tiny launch cannot amortize many baths. Finish labels obey **16 CFR Part 23**: "vermeil" requires **sterling + ≥10k gold + ≥2.5 µm**; "gold-plated" needs **≥10k, ≥0.175 µm**; sterling = **92.5% Ag** [FACT — FTC]. Earring posts must clear **EN 1811 nickel-release (≤0.2 µg/cm²/week for pierced posts, ≤0.5 general)** after wear pre-conditioning before any piece is "sell-safe" [FACT] — an S2 gate.
- **Cohesion** [METHOD]: one shared finding language across all six (one post style, one chain gauge, one clasp family, one bail) so the pieces read as a *collection* and plate/assemble efficiently — vary the visible body, standardize the invisible findings.
- **Cross-sell / stacking** [FACT-mechanism; magnitude UNKNOWN until measured]: slots 1–2 stack on one ear; slots 3–4 layer on the neck; the set is designed for multi-item baskets, not six unrelated pieces.
- **Hero anchor** [METHOD]: **one or two** anchors only (a launch with three heroes has none) — the huggie hoop is the working anchor: high category prior, producible, photogenic for the paid-social the venture depends on.

**Coherence scorecard (this candidate six):** category coverage ✔ (4 of 5 categories), price spread ✔ (entry→hero present), cohesion ✔ (two-finish, shared findings), cross-sell ✔ (ear stack + neck layer), demand alignment ✔ *to the public prior* (weakest criterion — needs the poll to confirm), margin health **[UNKNOWN until S6/quotes]**. The two soft criteria are exactly what §5–§6 resolve.

### 5. The refinement rule — how the poll + your private data lock the six

This is the operative instruction: the set above is a **prior**; here is *exactly* how the two missing inputs move it.

**A. Your store/bestseller history ("mindful of the old stuff") — how it counts, and how it must NOT.**
- It is **revealed purchase**, so it outranks a stated poll vote *for the SKUs it actually covers* — but it is a **small, stale, survivorship/availability-biased sample of only what you already stocked** [FACT-method].
- **Rules:** (i) apply **recency decay** — weight recent sales over old; (ii) apply the **overfit guard** — a never-stocked shape's zero sales is **absence, not rejection**, and can still rank on S1/S4; (iii) **shrink** each store rank toward the market prior; (iv) **store data never ranks a candidate alone.** A bestseller in your history earns *weight*, not an automatic slot; a category you never carried is not penalized.

**B. The S4 poll — the preference gap it fills.**
- The poll answers the one thing no public source can: **which category and which finish YOUR 18–25 women prefer** ("do you like earrings, necklaces, rings…?" and "silver-tone or gold-tone?"). Keep it simple, incentive-lift acknowledged as a bias on the *stated* signal.
- **How a poll result moves the set:**
  - *Category winner* → that category gets the **depth slots** (breadth-with-one-hero collapses onto the poll's hero); a losing category drops to one slot or out.
  - *Finish winner* → sets the **dominant plating bath**; the loser becomes the single accent (or is cut to save a run).
  - *Poll and public prior agree* → the candidate is promoted to A with **higher confidence**.
  - *Poll and public prior conflict* → resolve by reliability weight (revealed store data > stated poll > public proxy), flag the candidate as an **order-flip risk**, and if the composite is still sub-threshold, park it **[UNKNOWN]** and feed it into the next poll wave rather than force a rank on noise.

**C. The lock.** The six lock only when: the poll has returned, the store history has been read through A, the finish/category depth has been set by B, and the set has cleared the §6 prune. **Until then this is a provisional, method-backed set — act on it as a build-and-test plan, not a final catalog.**

### 6. The constraint prune — the gate before you commit cash

Before any of the six is committed, each must clear the feasibility filter [METHOD]. Apply the cheap, decisive prunes first:
- **Contribution-margin floor** [FACT-definition; level owned by S6]: every SKU must clear the inherited per-unit contribution floor *net of returns/markdowns* — a popular candidate that can't clear it is cut. Rhodium is the most volatile input: **gold ≈ $4,122/oz (Kitco, 7 Jul 2026), rhodium ≈ $7,700–8,100/oz (Umicore/TradingEconomics, 2–5 Jul 2026)** — rhodium roughly **~2× gold** [FACT-source: [Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/charts/gold) / [Umicore PMM](https://pmm.umicore.com/en/prices/rhodium/) / [TradingEconomics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/rhodium)]; **re-pull spot before any COGS build** [METHOD].
- **Per-SKU MOQ × unit COGS × six** is the launch-cash driver; the MOQ and plating-run bands are **[UNKNOWN] until a real supplier/plater quote** — never fabricated.
- **Plating-run batching:** the two-finish discipline (§4) exists to keep this to a **few baths**; adding a third finish adds a fixed-cost run the launch can't amortize.
- **Aggregate capital ceiling** (owner input): sum the six against it; if over, drop the weakest *surviving* candidate and re-check — do not let SKU count creep past six.

Everything that survives is handed to S6 (unit economics) for pricing and to `kb3`'s post-launch **sell-through / return-quality / contribution** test, where no keep/cut/expand verdict is drawn before the measurement window and sample-sufficiency gates are met, and sell-through is read against **your own store baseline, never a fabricated benchmark** [FACT-method].
