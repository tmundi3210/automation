# Personal Care Sector — Supply Chain & Operations
### Turlock, CA (Stanislaus County) | Independent Businesses

**Scope:** 25 independent Personal Care businesses across Barber Shop, Hair Salon, Med Spa/Aesthetics, Men's Grooming, Nail Salon, Spa/Day Spa, and Tattoo/Piercing (per `businesses.json`). This memo covers the **input/supply chain**, **operating model**, **cost structure (labeled estimates)**, and **regulatory inputs** that gate operations.

> **Honesty note:** Per-business revenue and margins are NOT public. Every cost %, margin, or revenue number below is a **LABELED benchmark [ESTIMATE]** drawn from industry sources — never a stated fact about a named local business. Verified facts are tagged **[FACT]**; gaps are **[UNKNOWN]**.

## 1. Supply chain by sub-category

The defining feature of this sector is a **low-capex, distributor-fed, consumables-light** input model. With the partial exception of med spas (FDA-regulated injectables and six-figure laser capex) and tattoo studios (single-use sterile consumables), most operators buy from a small set of national beauty distributors with regional CA warehouses, plus Amazon/direct-to-pro e-commerce. Inventory is generally **just-in-time / low-buffer**: storefronts are small, product turns are predictable, and distributors offer fast regional delivery.

### Barber Shops & Men's Grooming
*Named: Barber Club Turlock, Get Faded Barbershop, Godspeed Barbers Turlock, Jack's Barbershop, The Fade Room, Noblemens Grooming*

- **Suppliers [FACT]:** Clipper/tool hardware (Wahl, Andis, Oster, BabylissPro, JRL) flows through national pro distributors with CA reach — **CosmoProf**, **SalonCentric**, plus barber-specialty e-tailers **BuyBarber, Vip Barber Supply, ChopMyHair, WCK Barber Supply, Palms Fashion Inc**. Consumables (blade oil, talc, capes, neck strips, disinfectant — Barbicide) come from the same channels.
- **Material flow:** Durable tools are a one-time/periodic capex; consumables are reorder-on-demand. Lead times are short (1–5 days regional). **Inventory model: minimal JIT** — a chair needs perhaps 2–4 clipper sets, guards, shears, and a few weeks of consumables.
- **Tooling capex [ESTIMATE — vendor pricing, moderate confidence]:** A fully equipped chair (clippers/trimmers/shears, station, mirror, chair) runs roughly **$1,500–$4,000 per chair**; "estimate" derived from BuyBarber/Wahl/Andis pro list pricing. Storefront fit-out (3–6 chairs) is the larger line.
- **Note:** Noblemens Grooming and Square-based shops (Get Faded, Godspeed) layer in **retail grooming product** (pomades, beard oil) — a higher-margin add-on bought wholesale from the same distributors.

### Hair Salons
*Named: Bliss Salon and Day Spa, Inspirations Salon, Salon Two Zero Nine, Inc.*

- **Suppliers [FACT]:** Professional color, lightener, and care lines are **distributor-gated to licensed pros** through **SalonCentric** (L'Oréal-affiliated) and **CosmoProf** (Sally Beauty Holdings) — the two dominant national wholesalers, both with NorCal stores/delivery. Brands: Redken, Wella, Schwarzkopf, Olaplex, etc. Extensions and lash supplies (relevant to Salon Two Zero Nine and Bliss) come via the same channels or specialty importers.
- **Material flow:** Color/developer is the core consumable (COGS driver). **Inventory: low-to-moderate buffer** — color bars hold a working stock of shades; reorder weekly/biweekly. Lead times short.
- **Capex [ESTIMATE — vendor pricing, moderate confidence]:** Styling stations, shampoo bowls, dryers, and color bar fit-out for a multi-chair salon land in the **$1,500–$3,000 per station** range plus plumbing for wash stations. Bliss is described as "one of the largest salons in the valley," implying higher absolute fit-out and inventory but the same per-unit economics.

### Nail Salons
*Named: Amazing Nails & Spa, Cathy Nails, Cong's Nails and Spa, Infinity Nail Spa, Jennifer Nail & Spa, KV Nails & Spa, Organic Nails and Spa*

- **Suppliers [FACT]:** Distinct, **largely Vietnamese-American-owned wholesale ecosystem** concentrated in CA. Acrylic (EMA monomer + powder), gel polish, and **SNS/dip powder** systems flow from distributors like **DTK Nail Supply (San Jose, CA)**, **ND Nails Supply**, **Cali/Cal Nail Supply**, **Beauty Zone Nail Supply (Westminster/Little Saigon)**, and **C8 / LA Nail Supplies**. Brands: OPI, DND, Kiara Sky, NuGenesis, SNS, Gelish, Apres. Organic Nails specifically advertises SNS dipping — a named match to this channel.
- **Material flow & inventory:** Consumables-heavy but cheap per unit; salons hold a **broad SKU stock** of polish/powder colors (slow-turn long-tail) plus fast-turn staples (monomer, files, tips). Regional CA suppliers enable **JIT reorder**, often with free/fast local delivery. Lead times 1–4 days.
- **Capex [ESTIMATE — vendor pricing, moderate confidence]:** Pedicure spa chairs are the big line at **~$1,500–$3,500 each**; manicure tables, e-file drills, UV/LED lamps, and ventilation add modest cost. A 6–10 station salon's equipment fit-out is the principal capex.

### Med Spa / Aesthetics
*Named: Advance Med Spa, Aesthetic Lab, Face&Beyond Aesthetic Lounge*

- **Suppliers [FACT]:** Fundamentally different — **medical procurement**, not beauty distribution. Injectables (Botox/Allergan-AbbVie, Dysport/Galderma, Jeuveau/Evolus, Daxxify/Revance, Xeomin/Merz; fillers Juvéderm, Restylane) flow through authorized medical distributors — **McKesson Medical-Surgical**, **Pipeline Medical**, and aesthetics-specialty vendors. Face&Beyond explicitly lists Botox/Dysport/Jeuveau/Daxxify/Xeomin — a named match to this multi-manufacturer injectable supply chain.
- **Material flow / inventory:** Injectables are **cold-chain, lot-tracked, short-dated** — refrigerated, FIFO inventory with manufacturer opening-order minimums. **[ESTIMATE — vendor/industry pricing, moderate confidence]** Allergan new-account opening orders ~$2,000–$5,000; basic injectable launch inventory/equipment ~$10,000–$25,000.
- **Capex [ESTIMATE — vendor/industry pricing, moderate confidence]:** Energy-based devices (laser hair removal, body contouring, RF microneedling — offered by Aesthetic Lab and Face&Beyond) are the heavy line: **$50,000–$150,000 to purchase or $1,500–$4,000/month to lease** per device (Alma, Sciton, Cutera class). This makes med spa **the highest-capex sub-category by an order of magnitude**, and leasing is the common cash-flow workaround.

### Tattoo / Piercing Studios
*Named: Colossal Tattoo, Main Street Tattoo, Moezart Tattoo, Rebelucio Tattoo, Third Eye Tattoo*

- **Suppliers [FACT]:** Single-use **pre-sterilized needles/cartridges and needle tubes** and **commercially manufactured inks/pigments only** are mandated by CA's Safe Body Art Act. Supply flows from tattoo-specialty distributors (machines, inks, cartridges, sharps/biohazard disposal, barrier film). A **steam autoclave is required** for any reusable instruments. [FACT — Stanislaus County / Safe Body Art Act]
- **Material flow / inventory:** Mostly single-use consumables held in modest buffer; artists frequently supply their own machines. Lead times short via e-commerce.
- **Capex [ESTIMATE — vendor pricing, low-moderate confidence]:** Per-station setup (machine/power supply, furniture, autoclave shared) is modest — roughly **$2,000–$6,000 per artist station**; autoclave + sharps/biohazard compliance is a shared fixed cost.

### Spa / Day Spa
*Named: The Spa Turlock (plus spa secondary at Bliss, Salon Two Zero Nine, Organic Nails)*

- **Suppliers [FACT]:** Professional skincare/wax/massage consumables via **SalonCentric/CosmoProf** and skincare-specialty distributors; retail product lines for resale. Massage tables, wax warmers, steamers are low-cost durables.
- **Inventory:** Low buffer, JIT reorder. Capex per treatment room is low (**[ESTIMATE — vendor pricing]** ~$1,000–$3,000/room).

## 3. Operating model

**Labor models (this is the sector's defining variable):**

- **Booth/chair-rental dominant** in barber and hair [FACT]: owners rent stations to independently-licensed pros who keep service revenue, issue a 1099 for rent, carry their own insurance, and book their own clients. Salon Two Zero Nine explicitly advertises "independent stylists" and "private suites" — a named booth-rental/suite model. This shifts COGS, scheduling, and client risk onto the renter and converts the owner's revenue to **predictable rent** rather than service margin.
- **Owner-operator / small W-2 crews** dominate single-chair shops and walk-in tattoo studios.
- **NAIL SALONS — major 2025 regulatory shift [FACT]:** CA's temporary AB 5 booth-rental carve-out for **manicurists expired Jan 1, 2025** — nail techs must now be **W-2 employees** (hair/skin/barber pros retain the Borello carve-out). This raises payroll-tax, workers'-comp, and PAGA-misclassification exposure for the seven named nail salons and disproportionately affects the Vietnamese-immigrant labor base (per CalMatters/UCLA reporting). **It is the single biggest operating-model disruptor in this sector right now.**
- **Med spa:** requires **medical-director oversight** — a physician (MD/DO) owns/supervises the medical acts; injectors are RNs/NPs/PAs under delegation. This is a structural labor/cost input unique to Advance Med Spa, Aesthetic Lab, and Face&Beyond.

**Daypart / seasonality [ESTIMATE — industry pattern, moderate confidence]:**
- Barber/hair/nail peak **evenings, weekends, and pre-event seasons** (holidays, prom, weddings); barbers skew weekend/biweekly cadence.
- Med spa and tattoo are **appointment-led** (some walk-in: Colossal, Main Street, Jack's); med spa sees gifting/event seasonality (holidays, wedding season).
- All sub-categories are **cash-heavy / tip-heavy**, with Square/Booksy/Vagaro POS common (Get Faded and Godspeed run Square sites — [FACT]).

## 4. Cost structure (labeled estimates)

> All figures below are **industry-benchmark [ESTIMATE]s** — method and source named, confidence stated, word "estimate" used. **None is a statement about a specific Turlock business's actuals.** Booth-rental shifts these line items onto renters, so owner-level economics differ from operator-level.

**Hair salon (full-service, operator basis) [ESTIMATE — Professional Beauty Association / Vagaro / industry P&L benchmarks, moderate confidence]:**
- Labor (wages + commission 30–40% + payroll tax): **~40–55% of revenue** (largest line).
- Rent / occupancy: **~6–12% of revenue**.
- Product COGS (color, care): **~5–12% of revenue**.
- Net profit margin: **~8–15% typical; 20%+ top performers** (US avg ~8% estimate, PBA-cited).
- *Booth-rental owners invert this:* revenue ≈ rent collected; labor/COGS borne by renters.

**Barber [ESTIMATE — derived from salon benchmarks + chair-rental structure, low-moderate confidence]:** Lower COGS than hair (minimal product), so under chair-rental the **owner's economics are rent-driven**; per-chair rent commonly **$150–$350/week estimate** (regional pro-forum benchmark, low confidence — local figure [UNKNOWN]).

**Nail salon (post-2025, W-2 basis) [ESTIMATE — industry + new-law impact, low-moderate confidence]:** Historically thin margins on low ticket prices; the **forced W-2 transition adds ~10–15%+ to effective labor cost** (payroll tax + workers' comp + admin) versus the prior rent model — a material 2025 margin compression. Magnitude for any named salon is [UNKNOWN].

**Med spa [ESTIMATE — aesthetics-industry benchmarks, low-moderate confidence]:** Higher gross margins on injectables but **product COGS is real and lot-tracked** (vial cost passed largely to price); dominant cost drivers are **medical-director/injector labor, device lease/depreciation ($1.5k–$4k/mo/device estimate), and malpractice/medical insurance**. Capital intensity, not COGS, is the gating economic.

**Tattoo [ESTIMATE — body-art shop norms, low confidence]:** Predominantly **commission-split or booth-rent** with artists; consumable COGS per session is small; shop's economics are rent/split-driven. Local splits [UNKNOWN].

## 5. Regulatory & licensing inputs that gate operations

- **CA Board of Barbering & Cosmetology (BarberCosmo)** [FACT]: gates barber, cosmetology (hair), manicuring, and esthetics. Requires **individual licenses** (displayed) **and an establishment license**; facilities must meet sanitation/plumbing minimums (toilets, hot/cold running water, handwashing, potable water) before opening. Booth renters must each be licensed and the establishment must verify currency.
- **Worker-classification law** [FACT]: Hair/skin/barber pros retain the **Borello professional-services carve-out** (booth rental permitted with conditions: separate business, own insurance, written agreement, 1099). **Manicurists lost the carve-out 1/1/2025 → must be W-2.** Retroactive withholding/workers'-comp and PAGA exposure for non-compliance.
- **Med spa medical licensure** [FACT]: requires **physician medical-director oversight**; injectables are FDA-regulated prescription products dispensed through authorized medical distributors (DEA/state pharmacy considerations for some scheduled topicals). Medical malpractice + corporate-practice-of-medicine structure required.
- **Safe Body Art Act + Stanislaus County Environmental Health** [FACT]: tattoo/piercing requires a **county Body Art Facility health permit** and **registered body-art practitioners**; mandates **pre-sterilized single-use needles, commercial inks only, steam autoclave**, an infection prevention & control plan, plan-check, and self-inspection. Stanislaus County Env. Resources is the local enforcement agency.
- **General business inputs** [FACT/ESTIMATE]: City of Turlock business license; CA seller's permit (retail product resale); general liability + professional liability insurance; cosmetology establishments and booth renters typically carry their own liability coverage. **No CSLB (Contractors State License Board) input applies** to ongoing operations — CSLB would only touch one-time tenant-improvement construction, not the personal-care service itself.

## 6. Key takeaways

1. **Two supply chains, not one:** beauty-distributor-fed (barber/hair/nail/spa — CosmoProf, SalonCentric, DTK/ND/Beauty Zone) vs. **medical procurement** (med spa — McKesson, Pipeline, Allergan/Galderma). The nail channel is a distinct CA Vietnamese-American wholesale ecosystem.
2. **Capex spectrum:** barber/nail/tattoo/spa are **low-capex** (~$1.5k–$6k/station); med spa is **10–50× higher** ($50k–$150k devices), driving lease-not-buy behavior.
3. **The 2025 nail W-2 mandate is the live operational shock** for the seven named nail salons — a structural cost increase, not a supply issue.
4. **Booth/chair rental converts owner economics to rent collection**, insulating owners from COGS/labor but capping upside — explicitly visible at Salon Two Zero Nine's independent-stylist/suite model.
5. **Inventory is JIT and light everywhere except med spa** (cold-chain, short-dated, lot-tracked injectables).
