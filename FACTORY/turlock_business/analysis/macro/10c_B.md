## 0. The question, framed correctly

The owner-facing question this memo answers: **"Everyone talks about booming Sunbelt cities — is Turlock one of them, and if not, what kind of market is it?"** The honest answer, defended below, is that **Turlock is not a growth market in the national sense.** It is a **slow-growth-to-flat California Central Valley city** whose recent population change has hovered between roughly **−0.2% and +0.3% per year** — i.e., essentially **stagnant** — while the nation's growth engines (Texas, Florida, the Carolinas, Arizona) post **3–25% annual gains** in their hottest submarkets.

This is not a pessimistic framing; it is a **strategic** one. A business in a flat market competes for *share of a fixed pie* and *replacement demand*, not for *new arrivals*. That changes everything about siting, marketing, and how to read "opportunity." The rest of this memo builds the spectrum and locates Turlock precisely on it.

## 1. The national backdrop: growth surged on immigration, then slowed sharply

The denominator matters before any city ranking. US population growth **accelerated to ~1.0% in 2023→2024 — the fastest since 2001 — then slowed to ~0.5% in 2024→2025**, almost entirely because **net international migration fell by more than half.**

| Year (Jul 1→Jul 1) | US population change | Growth rate | Net international migration | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023→2024 | +3.3 million | **~1.0%** (to 340.1M) | ~2.8M (84% of total increase) | [Census – 2024 estimates, ~1% to 340.1M](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2024/12/population-estimates.html) |
| 2024→2025 | +1.8 million | **~0.5%** | ~1.3M (down ~54% YoY from ~2.7M) | [Census – Population Growth Slows (2026 release)](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/population-growth-slows.html) |

**Read this as one sentence:** the 2023–24 boom was an **immigration story**; when humanitarian/immigration inflows were curtailed, national growth roughly halved, while **natural increase stayed broadly stable.** ([Census – Population Growth Slows](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/population-growth-slows.html)) This is the tide against which every metro below is measured.

## 4. WHY the Sunbelt wins (the causal drivers, cited)

The growth is not random; it is a **cost + jobs + policy + climate/lifestyle** stack, now partially eroding.

**4.1 Tax & policy.** Texas has **no state personal or corporate income tax** (one of only ~5 states with neither personal income nor business income tax), giving it the **lowest combined state+federal corporate burden tier**. ([TxEDC – why tech moves to TX](https://businessintexas.com/blog/why-more-tech-companies-are-relocating-to-texas/)) For a senior tech worker at **$200k income, the Texas-vs-California saving is ~$18,300/yr**; vs NY/NJ ~$21,650/yr. ([CountryTaxCalc – Austin tax guide 2026](https://www.countrytaxcalc.com/tax-guides/usa/austin-texas-tax-guide-2026/)) Lighter regulation and fast business formation reinforce it. ([TxEDC](https://businessintexas.com/blog/why-more-tech-companies-are-relocating-to-texas/))

**4.2 Jobs / corporate relocation.** **From 2020 to early 2025, ≥139 firms relocated HQs to Texas, ~40% from California.** ([TxEDC](https://businessintexas.com/blog/why-more-tech-companies-are-relocating-to-texas/)) Austin drew Tesla, Oracle, SpaceX, plus dense startup activity and UT-Austin talent. ([Austin Technology Council](https://www.austintechnologycouncil.org/why-move-your-tech-company-to-austin/)) **Caveat (honesty):** Austin big-tech employment actually **declined ~1.6% in 2024** as the GenAI boom re-concentrated jobs in San Francisco. ([search summary of SignalFire analysis](https://austinapartmentlocators.com/blog/companies-moving-to-austin/)) The Sunbelt thesis is strong but **no longer one-directional.**

**4.3 Cost / housing — the original magnet, now eroding.** Pandemic-era relative affordability + remote/hybrid work pulled the 25–44 cohort to TX/FL metros. ([Bridge Investment Group](https://www.bridgeig.com/the-great-reshuffling-to-the-sunbelt-not-just-a-flash-in-the-pan/)) But **home-price appreciation, property taxes, and (in FL) insurance costs have eroded the affordability edge**, and **2025 inflows to TX/FL fell well below pandemic peaks**; interstate migration hit a multi-year low as affordability bit. ([StorageCafe – interstate migration 10-yr low](https://www.storagecafe.com/blog/us-interstate-migration-trends-state-winners-losers/)) The Midwest even posted **positive net domestic migration for the first time this decade.** ([Census – state change 2026](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/01/pop-estimates-state-change.html))

**Synthesis:** the winning formula is **low tax + abundant/permissive housing supply + job creation + warm climate**, amplified by remote work. Its **durability is now in question** because the affordability gap that powered it is closing.

## 5. California vs the nation — losing domestic migration, propped up by immigration

California is the **counter-example** to the Sunbelt, and Turlock lives inside it.

| Metric (Jul 1 2024 → Jul 1 2025) | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| CA population change | **+19,200 (~+0.05%)** to ~39,529,000 | [Gov. of CA – population increases again](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/05/01/californias-population-increases-again/) |
| CA net **domestic** migration | **−216,000** (more negative YoY; ~2018–19 levels) | [CA Dept. of Finance E-2/E-6 (via summary)](https://dof.ca.gov/forecasting/demographics/estimates/E-2/) |
| CA net **international** migration | **+126,000** (≈ half of prior year, after humanitarian programs ended) | [CA DOF (via summary)](https://dof.ca.gov/forecasting/demographics/estimates/E-2/) |
| CA **net total** migration | **−89,000+** (domestic loss outweighs international gain) | [CA DOF (via summary)](https://dof.ca.gov/forecasting/demographics/estimates/E-2/) |
| CA among 5 states that **declined** 2024→25? | One framing (Census) lists **CA as declining**; CA DOF/Governor report a small **gain** — see note | [Census – state change](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/01/pop-estimates-state-change.html) · [Gov. of CA](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/05/01/californias-population-increases-again/) |

**Honesty note on the discrepancy:** Census (Vintage 2025) and the California Department of Finance use different methods and reference dates and **disagree on the sign** of California's 2024→25 change — Census frames CA among states that **declined**, while CA DOF/the Governor report a small **gain (~+0.05%)**. Both are cited above. The defensible takeaway either way: **California is essentially flat, growing far slower than the nation (~0.5%) and dramatically slower than Sunbelt leaders, and is bleeding domestic migrants while immigration partially offsets it.**

**Where California still grows:** the **Central Valley and Inland Empire** are the state's relative growth zones, with **Bakersfield (~+1.2%), San Diego (~+1.0%), Fresno County (~+0.87%)** outpacing **Los Angeles (~+0.4%)**. ([CA County News – population growing again](https://www.californiacountynews.org/news/2025/05/california%E2%80%99s-population-growing-again-can-housing-catch)) So within California, **the Valley is on the *faster* end** — which makes Turlock's near-flat reading notable even by California standards.

## 7. So what — the strategic implication for a Turlock business

This is the load-bearing conclusion the rest of the FACTORY analysis should inherit:

1. **No demographic tailwind.** Unlike a Celina or Ocala operator riding 4–25% annual population growth, a Turlock business cannot assume new-resident demand will lift it. Growth must come from **share-of-wallet, share-of-competitor, regional capture (Denair/Hilmar/Hughson/Keyes/Delhi), and replacement demand** — consistent with the saturation ceiling in `10a_B.md` and the regional-capture revenue logic in `10b_B.md`.
2. **Flat ≠ bad.** Flat markets are **stable, predictable, and lower-churn**; they reward operational excellence and incumbency, not land-grab expansion. Turlock's modest growth also means **less new competitive supply** arriving than in a boomtown.
3. **The relevant macro risk is California-specific**, not Sunbelt-specific: **net domestic out-migration, high cost structure, and policy/cost-of-doing-business** pressures (see fiscal notes `10b_A`/`10b_B`). The upside is the **Central Valley's relative resilience** within California (Valley/Inland Empire are the state's growth zones).
4. **Benchmark honestly in any owner-facing material:** do **not** position Turlock as "a growing Sunbelt-style market." Position it as a **stable Central Valley regional hub** that **captures surrounding small-town demand** — which is its actual, defensible advantage.
