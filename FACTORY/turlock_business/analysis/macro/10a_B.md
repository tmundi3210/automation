# 10a_B — Supply / Saturation: Why NOT More Businesses in Turlock

**Analyst lens:** Supply & saturation (the structural ceiling on independent-business count)
**Current month:** June 2026
**Primary input:** `FACTORY/turlock_business/catalog/turlock_independent_business_catalog.json` (197 retained independent businesses; the catalog's own meta calls this a **floor, not a ceiling**)

---

## 0. The question, framed correctly

The catalog documents what *exists* (a public-web floor of 197 independent operators across 74 primary categories). This memo asks the inverse, supply-side question: **given Turlock's market, why is the operating independent-business population roughly the size it is, and not 2x or 3x larger?**

The answer is not "the web missed them" (though it did — see the catalog's own undercount caveats). The answer is that several **structural supply constraints** cap how many independent storefronts a city of ~73,700 people with Turlock's income, rent, labor, and capital profile can support — and that a disproportionately large neighbor (Modesto) siphons the higher-margin discretionary demand that would otherwise fund *more* local independents. These constraints define a **structural ceiling**.

---

[World Population Review – Turlock](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/california/turlock) · [City of Turlock Economic Profile](https://www.cityofturlock.org/doingbusinessinturlock/turlockeconomicprofile/)

### Methodology note (important and honest)

The catalog is **Turlock-only and independent-only**, hand-built from a 6-lens public-web sweep; no equivalent hand-built census exists for the peer cities, and the U.S. Census **County Business Patterns** series only publishes at the *county* level (Stanislaus / Merced / San Joaquin), not by city. ([Census CBP](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp.html)) Therefore a literal "scrape-for-scrape" cross-city density table would be apples-to-oranges. Instead, the honest comparison is **what each peer city would host *if* it carried Turlock's independent-business density** — which isolates the population-scaling effect and lets us reason about whether Turlock is over- or under-stored relative to its size.

| City | Population | Independents implied **at Turlock's 26.7/10k floor density** |
|---|---|---|
| Turlock | 73,737 | **197** (actual catalog floor) |
| Ceres | 49,305 | ~132 |
| Merced | 98,039 | ~262 |
| Manteca | 99,274 | ~265 |
| Tracy | 99,705 | ~266 |
| Modesto | 220,048 | ~588 |

**What this shows:** independent-business count scales **roughly linearly with population**. Modesto, at ~3x Turlock's population, supports on the order of ~3x the independent base — and because larger markets cross thresholds that unlock categories Turlock cannot (regional mall, specialty/niche retail, full restaurant rows), Modesto's *effective* density is actually **higher**, not just larger. Turlock's ~26.7/10k floor is normal-to-healthy for an ag-anchored Central Valley city of its size and income; it is **not** depressed, but it is **bounded**. The ceiling is set by the demand the local population and income can fund, minus the share that leaks to Modesto.

## 2. Why NOT more businesses — the five supply constraints

### 2.1 Business-formation & turnover dynamics (the churn ceiling)

The independent base is not a static stock; it is a **flow** where roughly half of all entrants are gone within five years. Nationally, ~21.5% of new businesses fail in year one and the **cumulative five-year failure rate is ~48–50%** — only about half survive to year five. ([LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/business/small/failure-rate/) · [LLC.org startup-failure statistics](https://www.llc.org/startup-failure-rate-statistics/)) California's *first-year* survival is actually strong (~86.8% survive year one, the best in the nation), but the attrition between years one and five still removes roughly a quarter of survivors. ([Digital Silk](https://www.digitalsilk.com/digital-trends/startup-failure-rate-statistics/))

**Implication for the ceiling:** even with healthy formation, the *net* stock only grows when new openings exceed the ~50%-by-year-five death rate. For a market Turlock's size, gross formation has to run hot just to hold the line. The catalog captures survivors at a point in time; the structural reality is a treadmill that caps net count. Categories that visibly churn here — food trucks, taquerias, cafes, nail/lash studios, boutiques — are exactly the low-capital, high-competition segments where the 50% wall bites hardest, which is why those categories show many entries but high turnover rather than ever-expanding counts.

### 2.2 Commercial rent & vacancy (the storefront supply constraint)

Turlock's commercial inventory is **thin and tightening on the retail side specifically**. The city has on the order of **1.67M sq ft of commercial space across ~17 tracked properties, with only ~112,000 sq ft of *available* retail and ~5 active retail listings** at the time of search. ([CommercialCafe – Turlock](https://www.commercialcafe.com/commercial-real-estate/us/ca/turlock/)) That is a small number of physically available storefronts at any moment — a hard cap on how many *new* brick-and-mortar independents can open regardless of demand.

Compounding it, Turlock has been **building retail faster than its peers** — retail space grew ~334,000 sq ft since 2017, ~56% of all Stanislaus County retail growth, but heavily concentrated in **big-box / shopping-center formats** (Monte Vista Crossings, the Westside Industrial Specific Plan). ([City of Turlock Economic Profile](https://www.cityofturlock.org/doingbusinessinturlock/turlockeconomicprofile/)) New square footage skewed toward **national-chain anchor pads**, not the small downtown bays that independents occupy. Statewide retail asking rents rose from ~$2.90 (2022) toward ~$3.10/sq ft (2025) with vacancy leveling ~10.3%. ([Private Capital Investors – mid-2025 CA outlook](https://privatecapitalinvestors.com/mid-year-2025-california-commercial-real-estate-market-outlook/)) So the independent-relevant constraint is **format and location scarcity** (few affordable, foot-traffic downtown bays), not raw vacancy — new supply mostly serves chains.

### 2.3 Labor (the staffing & wage constraint)

Independent storefronts are labor-intensive and wage-sensitive, and Turlock sits in a **structurally high-unemployment, ag-seasonal labor market** with a **rising statewide wage floor**:

- Stanislaus County unemployment ran **6.6% (May 2025) rising to 7.6% (June 2025)**, with ~2,300 unemployed in Turlock itself — persistently above state and national averages and seasonal with the ag calendar. ([Turlock Journal – local unemployment](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/after-strong-start-to-year-local-unemployment-rates-rise/))
- California's minimum wage rose to **$16.90/hr effective Jan 1, 2026** (from $16.50), and the exempt-salary floor hit **$70,304**. ([CA DIR – 2026 minimum wage](https://www.dir.ca.gov/DIRNews/2025/2025-118.html))

High unemployment looks like *available* labor, but for a small independent the binding issue is **the cost and reliability of staff at a statutory wage floor** against thin local margins. A rising wage floor on Turlock's modest revenue base raises the break-even headcount and **suppresses the marginal new opening** — the would-be owner who can clear $16.90/hr × a viable schedule plus rent is a smaller set than the storefront count suggests.

### 2.4 Capital access (the financing constraint)

New independents are overwhelmingly self- or locally-financed, and Turlock's household balance sheets are **moderate, not affluent**:

- Median household income **~$82,995 (2024)**, per-capita income in the **$35k (Census Reporter) to ~$54k** range, poverty ~11%. ([World Population Review – Turlock](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/california/turlock) · [Census Reporter – Turlock](http://censusreporter.org/profiles/16000US0680812-turlock-ca/))
- SBA's flagship **7(a)/504** programs exist and the cumulative limit was just **doubled to $10M (May 2026)**, with Central Valley CDCs/banks active. ([SBA – 7(a)/504 limit increase](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/05/18/sba-doubles-cumulative-7a-504-loan-limit-10-million) · [SBA 504](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/504-loans))

But SBA-scale capital flows to **larger, asset-backed ventures**, not the $20–80k a micro-independent (taqueria, boutique, detail shop) needs. Those rely on personal savings and home equity — and at Turlock's income level the pool of households that can **self-fund and absorb the ~50% five-year failure risk** is finite. Capital availability is a *real* ceiling on formation density: the structural number of households able to underwrite a new independent is the true upstream cap on how many can open.

### 2.5 Modesto's larger-market pull (the demand-leakage constraint)

This is the **single most important structural ceiling**, and it is geographic. Modesto (~220,048) sits ~15 miles north on SR-99 with **Vintage Faire Mall** — a Macerich regional mall anchored by Macy's, JCPenney, Apple, Dick's, Dave & Buster's — plus the surrounding big-box and full restaurant/entertainment cluster. ([Vintage Faire Mall – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vintage_Faire_Mall) · [Vintage Faire directory](https://www.shopvintagefairemall.com/Directory))

Turlock has **no regional mall and no comparable specialty/entertainment cluster.** The predictable consequence is **retail and dining leakage**: Turlock residents drive to Modesto for apparel, electronics, specialty, big-ticket, and "destination" dining. That leaked discretionary spend is precisely the demand that would otherwise fund *additional* local independents in higher-margin categories. Turlock's recent retail build-out (Monte Vista Crossings) was partly an explicit effort to **recapture** that leakage — but it did so with **national chains**, which capture the dollars without adding to the independent count. Net effect: the larger neighbor caps the **discretionary ceiling** for Turlock independents, holding the category mix toward **convenience, ethnic-grocery, auto/trades, and personal-care/health** (locally captive demand) and away from the discretionary-retail categories a non-leaking city of 73,700 could support.

---

## 3. The structural ceiling (synthesis)

Turlock's independent-business population is bounded by a **stacked set of supply constraints**, each setting a lower lid than the last:

1. **Demand ceiling (Modesto leakage)** — the binding cap. Discretionary spend that would fund higher-margin independents leaks ~15 mi to Vintage Faire Mall / Modesto's big-box-and-dining cluster, holding Turlock's category mix toward locally-captive convenience, ethnic grocery, auto/trades, and health/personal-care.
2. **Income/capital ceiling** — moderate median income (~$83k) and an ~11% poverty rate cap both local discretionary spend *and* the pool of households able to self-fund a new venture and absorb ~50% five-year failure odds.
3. **Storefront ceiling** — only ~112k sq ft / ~5 listings of available retail at a time; new supply skews to chain-format big boxes, not the small downtown bays independents need.
4. **Labor/wage ceiling** — a $16.90/hr (2026) floor on a thin-margin, high-unemployment (7%+), ag-seasonal base raises the break-even and suppresses marginal openings.
5. **Churn ceiling** — ~50% five-year failure means net stock only grows when gross formation outruns a steady death rate; the treadmill caps net count.

**Bottom line.** At ~**26.7 independent businesses per 10,000 residents (floor)**, Turlock is **appropriately stored for its size and income, not under-served** — and the count scales roughly linearly with population across its peer set (Ceres ~132, Merced/Manteca/Tracy ~262–266, Modesto ~588 at equivalent density). The reason there are **not more** is not a missing-entrepreneur problem; it is that the **structural ceiling** — set primarily by Modesto's demand pull and secondarily by income/capital, storefront supply, wages, and churn — limits the addressable, fundable, staffable, house-able independent base. Growth above the ceiling requires changing an input: **recapturing leaked Modesto demand into independent (not chain) formats, expanding affordable downtown-bay storefront supply, and widening micro-capital access** — not simply "more founders."

*(Per the catalog's own honesty note, 197 is a public-web floor; true independent count is materially higher due to invisible home-based, mobile, and non-advertising operators. That raises the *level* of the density estimate but does not change the *ceiling* logic — the same five constraints bound the larger true number.)*
