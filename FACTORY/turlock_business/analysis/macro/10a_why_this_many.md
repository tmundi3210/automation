# 10a — Turlock's Economic Base & Why It Supports This Many Businesses (Not More)

**Topic:** Turlock, CA's economic base, and why it supports roughly the number of independent businesses observed — neither far fewer nor 2–3x more.
**Companion data:** `FACTORY/turlock_business/catalog/turlock_independent_business_catalog.json`
**Date:** 2026-06-30.

> **Honesty rule applied throughout.** Every government, tax, budget, and macro-economic figure below is either a **[FACT]** — a cited public fact with a source URL — or a clearly **[ESTIMATE]** with method and confidence. No private business's revenue is stated as fact: firm-level revenue is **not public** and appears (if ever) only as a labeled benchmark estimate. Where a figure cannot be sourced, it is marked as an estimate or as unknown. Scope tags (city / county / region / state) are attached to borrowed figures so they are not over-read as city-level.

---

## 1. The thesis in one paragraph

Turlock's *effective* demand pool is larger and more varied than its ~73,000 residents would suggest, while its business count is simultaneously **bounded well below** what a comparably sized but non-leaking, higher-capital, storefront-rich city could host. On the **demand side**, four amplifiers lift the ceiling: a low cost-of-living that raises the discretionary share of an ~$83k median income; roughly 9,700 CSU Stanislaus students plus a multi-hundred-million-dollar university footprint; a top-ranked retail power center that captures and retains regional trade; and a multi-billion-dollar agricultural hinterland that adds a rural/B2B demand stream absent in non-ag cities. On the **supply side**, five constraints cap the count: demand leakage ~15 miles north to Modesto and Vintage Faire Mall, moderate household income and capital, scarce affordable storefront supply, a rising wage floor on a high-unemployment seasonal labor base, and ~50% five-year business churn. The catalog's count is therefore best read as a **public-web floor** that sits where it does because demand amplifiers raise the ceiling above a bare residential base, while supply constraints hold it below a frictionless counterfactual.

## 4. Why this many (demand side)

Turlock's effective demand pool exceeds its ~73k residents, and the *shape* of that demand explains the *shape* of the catalog:

- **Discretionary lift.** Low cost-of-living raises the discretionary share of an $82,995 median income, supporting more personal-care, food, and service independents than the headline income implies.
- **Imported demand.** ~9,700 CSU students plus a $600.7M regional university footprint (San Joaquin Valley scope) add high-frequency, recurring demand.
- **Captured regional trade.** Monte Vista Crossings (#5 in CA / #9 nationally; >25% of city sales tax) pulls regional shoppers into Turlock instead of letting all trade leak away.
- **Ag/B2B stream.** A $3.15B county ag/dairy economy generates rural and business-to-business demand (auto, trades, ag-specialty supply) absent in non-ag cities.

**Resulting sector fingerprint:** deep, non-leakable personal-care/health and food (bifurcated value-ethnic + discretionary); deep car-dependent and homeowner-driven auto and trades; a distinctive ag-specialty segment; and thin general retail — precisely the leakable, chain- and e-commerce-captured category.

## 7. Net conclusion

The catalog's **197 independents (a floor, ≈26.7/10k)** is appropriately positioned — not depressed, but bounded. Demand amplifiers (university footprint, regional retail capture, ag hinterland, discretionary lift) raise the ceiling above a bare 73k residential base. Supply constraints (leakage to Modesto, moderate capital, scarce affordable storefronts, a rising wage floor on a weak labor base, and ~50% five-year churn) hold it below what a non-leaking, capital-rich, storefront-abundant city of the same size could host. The true independent count is **materially higher** than 197 in invisible home-based, mobile, and word-of-mouth ethnic categories — which raises the level but leaves the ceiling logic intact.
