- **`10c_A.md`** — LOCAL-TREND / macro: the long-run growth "chart" for the **City of Turlock** and **Stanislaus County** (= Modesto MSA) across **population, jobs, business counts, income**, ~2010–2026.

The two memos are **largely complementary, not duplicative** (A = local multi-dimension depth; B = national positioning). > **HONESTY RULE (applies throughout).** Every government / census / tax / budget / economic figure is either a **CITED PUBLIC FACT** (source URL) or a **clearly LABELED ESTIMATE** (method + confidence) or marked **UNKNOWN**. No private business's revenue is stated as fact anywhere. No numbers are fabricated; figures that could not be sourced are flagged rather than guessed.

## 0. Headline reconciliation (the one-paragraph answer)

Both memos agree on the **shape of the curve and the strategic conclusion**: Turlock/Stanislaus is a **slow-growth-to-flat** market — population up then plateaued post-2020, jobs recovered hard off a brutal 2010 trough but now grow <0.5%/yr, real incomes up only ~11–12% over 13 years. **National benchmark (B):** Turlock is **not** a Sunbelt-style growth market; it sits **below the US rate (~0.5–1.0%), an order of magnitude below Sunbelt leaders (3–31%), and at-or-just-below California's own faster Valley peers.** The strategic inheritance for the rest of FACTORY: **no demographic tailwind — growth must come from share-of-wallet, regional capture, and replacement demand**, not new arrivals. The memos **do not contradict each other on direction**; they differ on **exact Turlock/Stanislaus population levels** because they draw on **different data vintages (Census decennial/PEP vs CA DOF) with different reference dates** — see §1 and the Conflict Log (§7).

## 8. Strategic conclusion (reconciled — both memos agree)

1. **No demographic tailwind.** Turlock is **flat**, not growing. Growth must come from **share-of-wallet, share-of-competitor, regional capture (Denair/Hilmar/Hughson/Keyes/Delhi), and replacement demand** — not new arrivals. (B §7; consistent with A's plateau read.)
2. **Recovery story, not a boom.** The dominant local fact is the climb out of **~17% unemployment (2010)**; the decade since is **steady normalization**, now decelerating to <0.5% job growth. (A)
3. **Real income barely moved** (~11–12% real over 13 years, ~0.9%/yr) → local **purchasing power expanded slowly**; demand growth is **population × stable real wages**, not rising affluence. (A)
4. **The relevant macro risk is California-specific** (net domestic out-migration, cost structure), **not** Sunbelt-style — but the Central Valley is California's **relative growth zone**, making Turlock's near-flat reading notable even within CA. (B)
5. **Benchmark honestly in owner-facing material:** do **not** position Turlock as a "growing Sunbelt-style market." Position it as a **stable Central Valley regional hub capturing surrounding small-town demand.** (B)
6. **What macro can't resolve:** confirmed establishment counts and the **independent-vs-chain** split — that gap is the job of this project's business catalog, not macro data. (A)
