## 4. Concentration risk — a few anchors carry the City

Turlock's sales-tax base is **extremely concentrated**, tightly coupling the General Fund to a handful of retail anchors. This is a direct consequence of California's **point-of-sale Bradley-Burns 1% local tax**, which rewards high-ticket retail (autos, big-box, power centers).

| Item | Value | Year | Tag | Source / note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Top **25 firms** = share of Sales & Use Tax | **60.1%** | recent | [FACT] | [City of Turlock – Quarterly Sales Tax Reports](https://www.cityofturlock.org/doingbusinessinturlock/quarterlysalestaxreports/) |
| Monte Vista Crossings share of total city sales tax | **>25%** | recent | [FACT] | [Turlock Journal](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/monte-vista-crossings-over-16-years-of-development-brings-top-shopping-center-to-turlock/) |
| Transportation (autos) segment | **≈$862,612** (+2.7% YoY) | Q2 2025 | [FACT] | [City of Turlock – Quarterly Sales Tax Reports](https://www.cityofturlock.org/doingbusinessinturlock/quarterlysalestaxreports/) |
| Transportation (autos) segment | **≈$778,809** (−1.6% YoY) | Q1 2025 | [FACT] | [City of Turlock – Quarterly Sales Tax Reports](https://www.cityofturlock.org/doingbusinessinturlock/quarterlysalestaxreports/) |
| Bradley-Burns local rate (point-of-sale) | **1.0 pp** of base; ~30% of avg CA city general revenue | [FACT] | [Bradley-Burns – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley-Burns_Uniform_Local_Sales_and_Use_Tax_Law) |

**Implication:** the loss of a single dealer or anchor is a **measurable General Fund hit**. The City's fiscal stability is unusually exposed to the performance of a small number of retailers.

## 8. Bottom line

Turlock runs a **~$62.5M FY24-25 adopted budget** [FACT] on a General Fund reported at multiple scopes (~$52M core operating; ~$80M "ongoing GF revenues," up from $78.7M, swinging from a +$4.6M surplus to a −$5.6M deficit) [FACT]. Its fiscal engine is **sales tax** — the largest GF source at **33–37%** [FACT] — supercharged since **April 2021 by Measure A** (voter-approved 0.75% local tax, 56.74% YES, ~$11M/yr City estimate) [FACT], lifting the combined rate to **8.625%** [FACT]. That revenue is **extremely concentrated**: **25 firms = 60.1%** of sales tax and **Monte Vista Crossings alone >25%** [FACT], with the **auto segment running ~$0.78–0.86M/quarter** [FACT] — a direct result of California's **point-of-sale Bradley-Burns 1%** [FACT]. **Property tax** is a distant second (City keeps ~8.57% of the 1% levy) [FACT], and there is **no Utility Users Tax** — the $6.969M UUT figure is a City hypothetical, not collected revenue [EST]. Exact dollar lines for total sales tax (~$15–22M City share / ~$19–20M total receipts), property tax (~$8–11M), and per-capita (~$200–300/resident) remain **LABELED ESTIMATES — UNKNOWN as cited facts** pending the primary budget and AFRs. **Net:** Turlock is **heavily sales-tax-dependent and unusually concentrated** in a few anchors, creating real concentration risk — one dealer or anchor closure is a measurable General Fund hit.
