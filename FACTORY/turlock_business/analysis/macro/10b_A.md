## 5. UTILITY USERS TAX (UUT)

**Turlock does NOT levy a Utility Users Tax.** This is the strongly-supported reading of the evidence:
- The City has repeatedly *studied/considered* a UUT as a *potential new* revenue source (Council options reports; "Does Turlock need a new tax?"), which only makes sense if one is not already in place ([cityofturlock.org Council packet, via search index](https://www.cityofturlock.org/_pdf/downloadpacket.asp?dept=CityCouncil&id=621); [turlockjournal.com](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/government/does-turlock-need-new-tax/)).
- The **$6,969,000** figure that surfaces for Turlock UUT is explicitly framed as a **"very rough estimate" of what a UUT *would* generate** *if* voters approved one "similar to other communities" — i.e. a hypothetical, **not** actual collected revenue ([cityofturlock.org, via search index](https://www.cityofturlock.org/cityfinancesbudget/wherethemoneycomesfrom/)).

> **Status:** Turlock UUT actual revenue = **$0 (no UUT levied)** — MEDIUM-HIGH confidence based on the above. The **$6.969M** is a **City-published hypothetical ESTIMATE** of UUT potential, not a fact about money the City makes. (For contrast, neighboring **Modesto** does levy a UUT on electric/gas/water/telecom/cable — a useful comparative, not a Turlock figure.) A definitive confirmation would come from the AFR revenue notes, which were access-blocked here.

## 7. One-paragraph synthesis for the macro brain

The City of Turlock runs a **~$62.5M FY2024-25 adopted budget** ([Turlock Journal](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/government/council-adopts-a-status-quo-budget/)), with a General Fund that is reported two ways in public summaries — a **~$52M core operating GF** and a broader **~$80M "ongoing GF revenues"** measure for FY24-25 (up from $78.7M in FY23-24), the latter running a **~$5.6M structural deficit** in FY24-25 after a +$4.6M surplus the prior year ([Turlock Journal](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/government/council-continue-budget-talks-consider-new-general-fund-reserves-policy/)). The fiscal engine is **sales tax** — the single largest GF source at **33–37% of revenue** (39% / $14.12M as far back as FY2016-17), turbocharged since **April 2021 by voter-approved Measure A**, a **¾-cent local sales tax (passed 56.74% in Nov 2020, ~$11M/yr, rate now 8.625%)** ([cityofturlock.org](https://www.cityofturlock.org/cityfinancesbudget/wherethemoneycomesfrom/); [Turlock Journal](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/government/city-moves-forward-with-%C2%BE-cent-sales-tax-measure/)). **Property tax** is a distant second, with the City keeping only **~8.57%** of the 1% levy. There is **no Utility Users Tax** (the $6.969M UUT figure is a City hypothetical, not actual revenue). **Bottom line: Turlock is heavily sales-tax-dependent**, which ties the City's fiscal health directly to retail performance — Monte Vista Crossings, the Amazon facility, and the independent retail/food base catalogued in companion notes — and makes the local economy's consumer-spending health and the City's own balance sheet tightly coupled.
