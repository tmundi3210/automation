## 0. The question, framed correctly

`10a_A` explained *why* Turlock supports the consumer demand it does. This memo asks the **fiscal** question on top of it: **which businesses actually pay the city's bills, and how does the mix of anchor/big-box/auto retail vs. small independents determine how much municipal revenue the same population generates?**

The short thesis, defended below: **city revenue is radically more concentrated than the business population.** A city of ~73,700 with ~200+ web-visible independents and hundreds more chains funds its General Fund from a *handful* of high-ticket, point-of-sale anchors — the auto dealers, the big-box/power-center retail (Monte Vista Crossings), and fuel/grocery. Independents are economically and culturally vital (jobs, character, demand capture, resilience) but, **per storefront, they are minor sales-tax producers.** The fiscal structure rewards anchor-heavy retail; the civic/economic structure rewards a healthy independent long tail. Those are two different objectives, and conflating them is the most common mistake in this debate.

---

## 1. How a California city actually gets paid (the mechanism that makes the mix matter)

The reason "business mix" maps to "city revenue" at all is the **Bradley-Burns** local sales-and-use tax: of California's base rate, **1 percentage point is a local Bradley-Burns levy returned to the city/county**, allocated **on a point-of-sale basis** — the jurisdiction where the sale is *negotiated/closed* keeps the local 1%. ([Bradley-Burns – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley-Burns_Uniform_Local_Sales_and_Use_Tax_Law)) On average, Bradley-Burns supplies **~30% of a California city's general-purpose revenue**, though it varies widely by city. ([Bradley-Burns – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley-Burns_Uniform_Local_Sales_and_Use_Tax_Law))

Turlock's total retail rate is **8.625%** (6% state + 0.25% county + **0.75% city** + **1.625% special/district** from Measure A, effective Apr 1, 2021). ([Zamp – Turlock sales tax 2025](https://zamp.com/resources/turlock-california-sales-tax/) · [SalesTaxHandbook – Turlock 8.625%](https://www.salestaxhandbook.com/california/rates/turlock)) The city's *general-purpose* share is the Bradley-Burns 1% plus the locally-approved add-ons.

**Why point-of-sale is the whole game.** Because the local 1% follows the *location of the sale*, a **single high-ticket retailer** — a new-car dealership ringing up $40k–$70k transactions, or a big-box anchor moving high volume — concentrates far more municipal revenue per door than dozens of low-ticket independents. A $50,000 vehicle sale generates roughly **$500 of Bradley-Burns local 1%** in one transaction; a $25 haircut or a $15 taqueria lunch generates **$0** of *sales* tax at all, because **most personal services and most unprepared food are not subject to California sales tax.** (Prepared restaurant food is taxable; haircuts, gym memberships, dental, chiropractic, nail/lash, tattoo, most professional services are **not**.) This single fact — that Turlock's *deepest* independent block (personal/health services, per `10a_A` §4.1) is **largely outside the sales-tax base entirely** — is the structural key to everything below.

## 3. The auto mall / big-box / regional retail vs. small independents in sales-tax generation

### 3.1 Autos and transportation — the per-door champion

Turlock hosts a clustered new-and-used dealer base (e.g., **Turlock Auto Plaza, established 1993**, plus franchised new-car dealers). ([Manta – Turlock Auto Plaza](https://www.manta.com/c/mm2vppc/turlock-auto-plaza) · [KBB – Turlock dealers](https://www.kbb.com/dealers/turlock-ca/)) Statewide, **autos & transportation is ~19% of all local sales-tax**, and **general consumer goods ~28%**, with the remainder spread across pools (~18%), restaurants/hotels (~13%), food/drug (~9%), fuel (~5%), building (~5%), business/industrial (~2%). ([HdL – CA Sales Tax Trends & Economic Drivers, Q1 2025](https://www.hdlcompanies.com/news/california-sales-tax-trends-and-economic-drivers-report-q1-2025-data)) Turlock's reported transportation segment of **~$0.78–0.86M/quarter** (above) makes autos one of the city's single largest taxable categories — and crucially, it is generated by a **small number of dealerships**, not a broad base.

**Why a dealership beats a block of independents, fiscally (LABELED ESTIMATE — illustrative, medium confidence, arithmetic only):**
- One new-car dealer selling ~**1,000 vehicles/yr** at an avg ~**$45,000** taxable price = **~$45M taxable sales** → Bradley-Burns local 1% ≈ **$450,000/yr to the city from one store.**
- To match that, you would need independents generating **$45M in *taxable* sales.** A typical independent taqueria/boutique/cafe doing a benchmark **~$0.5–1.0M/yr** in taxable sales (LABELED ESTIMATE; firm revenue not sourced) contributes **~$5,000–$10,000/yr** in local 1%. **You would need on the order of 45–90 such independents to equal one mid-size dealership** — and that is *only* counting the independents whose sales are taxable at all.
- *Method/confidence:* vehicle counts and prices are **illustrative placeholders**, not Turlock-specific filings (dealer-level sales are not public); the 1% mechanism and the order-of-magnitude ratio are robust. Treat the **direction and ~50:1 magnitude** as reliable; the exact numbers as a worked example, not a measurement.

### 3.2 Big-box / regional retail (Monte Vista Crossings) — the volume champion

Monte Vista Crossings is the **fiscalization-of-land-use** play executed correctly: a power center of national-chain anchors that **keeps Turlock's own spending in Turlock and pulls in surrounding small-town shoppers** (Denair, Hilmar, Hughson, Delhi, Keyes), producing **>25% of the city's entire sales tax** off one footprint. ([Turlock Journal – Monte Vista Crossings](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/monte-vista-crossings-over-16-years-of-development-brings-top-shopping-center-to-turlock/)) Big-box general-merchandise and the auto segment together plausibly account for **a majority of the city's taxable retail base** (consistent with the "25 firms = 60.1%" public figure).

### 3.3 Small independents — high civic value, low *per-door* sales-tax yield

Independents are not fiscal free-riders, but their revenue contribution is **structurally capped** by three things established above and in `10a_A`:
1. **Category:** Turlock's deepest independent block is **personal/health services** (`10a_A` §4.1: dental, nail, chiro, optometry, barber, tattoo, gyms) — **mostly non-taxable**, so it contributes **~$0 in sales tax** regardless of how vibrant it is.
2. **Ticket size:** Even the taxable independents (restaurants, boutiques, specialty retail) ring **small tickets**, so per-door local 1% is small (see §3.1 estimate).
3. **Count vs. concentration:** With **25 firms producing 60.1%**, the entire independent long tail is fighting over a minority share of the remaining ~40%.

**This is not a knock on independents.** They deliver what anchors cannot: local jobs and ownership, demand capture that keeps trips in town, neighborhood character, resilience/diversification, and the *property-tax* and *business-license/utility* base that does not depend on taxable retail volume. The point is narrower and fiscal: **on the sales-tax line specifically, independents are a thin contributor per storefront.**

## 4. How independent-heavy vs. anchor-heavy retail affects municipal revenue (the trade-off)

| Dimension | Anchor-heavy (autos + big-box) | Independent-heavy (small storefronts) |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales-tax per door** | Very high (high-ticket, point-of-sale) | Low; often **zero** (services/unprepared food untaxed) |
| **Revenue concentration / risk** | High — losing **one** dealer or anchor is a measurable GF hit (Turlock's "25 firms = 60.1%" makes this concrete) | Diversified — no single closure moves the budget |
| **Volatility** | Cyclical — autos and big-ticket retail swing with rates, recessions, incentives (note the Q1-2025 transportation **−1.6% YoY** dip) | Steadier — non-taxable services are recession-resistant demand |
| **Leakage capture** | Strong — Monte Vista *recaptures* spend that would leak to Modesto's Vintage Faire (`10a_B` §2.5) | Weak on commodity retail; strong only in defensible ethnic/specialty/service niches |
| **Other city revenue (property/license/utility/jobs)** | Large pads, fewer owners | Many owners, many licenses, denser job/foot-traffic per dollar |

**The structural tension.** California's point-of-sale rule pushes cities toward the **"fiscalization of land use"** — chasing auto malls and big boxes because each new dealer/anchor is worth dozens of independents *on the sales-tax line.* Turlock has clearly done this (Monte Vista build-out, auto cluster). The cost is **concentration risk**: a revenue base where **25 firms = 60.1%** and **one center = >25%** is exposed to a single dealership relocating, an anchor going dark, or an auto downturn. An independent-heavy base is fiscally *thinner* but *more diversified and less volatile.* A resilient city wants **both**: anchors for the sales-tax engine, independents for diversification, jobs, character, and the non-sales-tax revenue lines.

## 5. Turlock's sales-tax-per-capita vs. peers (HONEST treatment)

([CDTFA – Bradley-Burns quarterly allocation dataset](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/dataportal/dataset.htm?url=LRBQtrAllocBradleyBurns)) So the per-capita comparison here is a **LABELED ESTIMATE**, not a cited fact.

**Estimate (method + confidence — MEDIUM):**
- *Method:* The city states sales tax is **33–37% of a ~$62.48M-budget General Fund.** Taking GF ≈ budget order-of-magnitude and the Bradley-Burns/general sales-tax share, the city's **own sales-tax-funded revenue is on the order of ~$15–22M/yr** (estimate; the exact GF total and the precise sales-tax dollar line were not sourced and are **UNKNOWN** here). Against **~73,700 residents**, that implies **roughly ~$200–$300 in sales-tax revenue per resident per year** (LABELED ESTIMATE, ±30%).
- *Peer read (directional, low-medium confidence):* Turlock most likely sits **at or somewhat above** the median for a Central-Valley city of its size **because** of two above-average assets — the **auto cluster** and a **top-5-in-California power center** that recaptures regional spend — while **Modesto** (Vintage Faire regional mall + far larger auto/retail base, `10a_B` §2.5) almost certainly out-produces Turlock in *absolute* sales tax but not necessarily per-capita. **A precise peer ranking is UNKNOWN without each city's published sales-tax line.**
- *Why the band is wide:* I will not state a per-capita figure as fact when the GF sales-tax dollar total is unsourced. The defensible claim is **structural, not numeric**: Turlock punches **above its residential size** on taxable retail because of anchors and trade-area capture, and its revenue is **unusually concentrated** in those anchors.

## 6. What business growth would add to city coffers (LABELED ESTIMATES)

Framed by the §1 mechanism (local 1% on *taxable* sales, point-of-sale). All figures below are **illustrative estimates** (method stated), not forecasts or sourced facts.

| Growth scenario | Local-1% added to city / yr | Confidence & method |
|---|---|---|
| **+1 mid-size new-car dealership** (~1,000 units × ~$45k) | **~$450,000/yr** | MEDIUM-LOW. Illustrative units×price; dealer sales not public. Highest-yield single move; also highest concentration risk. |
| **+1 big-box anchor / power-center pad** (~$30–60M taxable/yr) | **~$300,000–$600,000/yr** | MEDIUM-LOW. Benchmark big-box volume; recaptures Modesto leakage. |
| **+50 taxable independents** (restaurants/specialty boutiques, ~$0.5–1.0M taxable each) | **~$250,000–$500,000/yr (combined)** | LOW-MEDIUM. Benchmark per-firm taxable sales (not sourced); diversified, lower-risk, but slow to assemble. |
| **+50 personal-service independents** (salons, gyms, dental, chiro) | **~$0 in sales tax** | HIGH (mechanism-certain). Adds jobs, licenses, property/utility base, foot traffic — **but not sales-tax revenue**, because the services are untaxed. |
| **Recapturing Modesto retail leakage into Turlock taxable retail** (any format) | Scales with $ recaptured (each $1M taxable = **$10k** local 1%) | MEDIUM (mechanism-certain; magnitude depends on recapture). The single biggest *latent* upside per `10a_B` §2.5. |

**Punchline for "what grows the coffers":** on the **sales-tax line**, the city gets the most per effort from **one more auto dealer or one more big-box/anchor pad** and from **recapturing leakage** — high-ticket, point-of-sale, taxable. Independent growth is fiscally valuable mainly through the **taxable-retail/restaurant** subset and through the **non-sales-tax** channels (jobs, property tax, business licenses, utility users tax, vibrancy that lifts the whole trade area). The honest civic recommendation is the same as `10a_B`'s: **pair** anchor recruitment (the revenue engine) with independent-format support (diversification, resilience, character) — they fund the city through *different* mechanisms and a one-sided strategy under-optimizes either revenue or resilience.

## 7. Synthesis — one paragraph

Turlock's **business population is independent-rich** but its **revenue base is anchor-poor-in-number, anchor-rich-in-dollars**: a documented **25 firms produce 60.1% of the city's sales tax**, **one power center (Monte Vista Crossings) produces >25%**, and the **auto segment alone runs ~$0.78–0.86M/quarter**, all flowing through California's **point-of-sale Bradley-Burns 1%** that rewards high-ticket retail. ([City of Turlock](https://www.cityofturlock.org/doingbusinessinturlock/quarterlysalestaxreports/) · [Turlock Journal](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/monte-vista-crossings-over-16-years-of-development-brings-top-shopping-center-to-turlock/) · [Bradley-Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley-Burns_Uniform_Local_Sales_and_Use_Tax_Law)) Because Turlock's **deepest independent block is non-taxable personal/health services** (`10a_A`), the independent long tail — economically and culturally essential — is a **thin per-door sales-tax contributor**, while a single dealership can equal **45–90 independents** on that line (labeled estimate). The fiscal logic therefore favors **anchors and leakage-recapture** for raw sales-tax growth, and **independents** for diversification, jobs, property/license revenue, and resilience against the concentration risk that "25 firms = 60.1%" makes literal. A precise **sales-tax-per-capita peer ranking remains UNKNOWN** without each city's published sales-tax dollar line; the defensible, sourced claim is structural — **Turlock over-performs its residential size on taxable retail thanks to its auto cluster and a top-5-in-California power center, at the cost of unusually concentrated, more volatile revenue.**
