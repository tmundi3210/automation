## 0. The owner's question, answered in one paragraph

> *"Which cities are adopting [concepts] from others — maybe the fast ones are New York?"*

**Yes — and the direction is the important part.** Concepts overwhelmingly travel **down the urban hierarchy**: they are born in a handful of large, dense, high-income, immigrant-rich "hearth" cities — **New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco/Bay Area, and (in the newer wave) Austin** — and only *later* appear in mid-size cities, then small cities like Turlock, then rural areas. Geographers call this **hierarchical diffusion**: a trait "originates and takes hold first in large cities and then *trickles down* to smaller cities, towns, and rural areas," and "because diffusion occurs over time as well as over space, there [is] a time lag between the origin of a trait in a large city and its appearance in small towns." ([NVCC – Cultural Diffusion](https://pressbooks.nvcc.edu/nolgeo210/chapter/cultural-diffusion/)) So the honest framing for the owner is: **NYC/LA/SF/Austin are the originators; Turlock is a *late receiver*.** Turlock is almost never adopting *from* another mid-size city — it is adopting, on a lag, *from* the big four. The strategic value is that the lag is **observable and roughly predictable**, which turns "what's trendy in LA" into a usable pipeline for "what Turlock will be ready for in a few years."

---

## 1. The mechanism: hierarchical diffusion, and how it flips to "contagious" near the end

**Two diffusion modes stack on top of each other.**

1. **Hierarchical diffusion (the long-distance jump).** The concept leaps from a big originating city to *other large cities*, skipping the small towns in between — it spreads by **city size, not by adjacency**. ([NVCC – Cultural Diffusion](https://pressbooks.nvcc.edu/nolgeo210/chapter/cultural-diffusion/)) Fashion's canonical example: trends start in Paris/Milan/New York and trickle down. ([NVCC – Cultural Diffusion](https://pressbooks.nvcc.edu/nolgeo210/chapter/cultural-diffusion/))
2. **Contagious / neighborhood diffusion (the last mile).** Once a concept reaches a regional anchor, it spreads **by proximity** — from that anchor outward to the towns physically near it. Research on innovation over the urban hierarchy finds the pattern is a *mixture*: "spreading initially occurs over great distances between large cities but becomes more and more local when it reaches smaller towns." ([NVCC – Movement Across Space](https://pressbooks.nvcc.edu/nolgeo210/chapter/diffusion-cultural-hearth/); urban-hierarchy diffusion literature, [Royal Society Open Science 2022, "Urban hierarchy and spatial diffusion over the innovation life cycle"](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/9/5/211038/96688/Urban-hierarchy-and-spatial-diffusion-over-the))

**What this means concretely for Turlock.** Turlock does **not** get a concept directly from New York. The path is almost always:

```
NYC / LA / SF / Austin   (INCEPTION — fine-dining & immigrant independents)
        │  hierarchical jump (skips small towns, lands in big cities)
        ▼
Bay Area / Sacramento / Fresno   (large-metro ADOPTION — chains form, price points drop)
        │  contagious / last-mile (proximity, regional hubs)
        ▼
Modesto  (Stanislaus County anchor, ~218k metro pop — the REGIONAL gateway)
        │  short hop, ~30 min freeway
        ▼
TURLOCK  (~73k — receives once the concept is de-risked and a chain franchisee will commit)
```

**Modesto is Turlock's import dock.** Because last-mile diffusion is proximity-driven, the operationally useful "leading city" for Turlock is **not** New York at all — it is **Modesto** (and behind it, Sacramento/Bay Area). A concept is *available* to Turlock roughly once it has proven out one rung up the ladder. The owner's instinct ("the fast ones are New York") is correct about **where ideas are born**, but the **actionable signal** for a Turlock operator is **what just landed in Modesto / Sacramento**, because that is the concept entering Turlock's adoption window *now*.

## 2. The lag, quantified: ~6–8 years inception→ubiquity, and Turlock sits near the "ubiquity" end

Datassential's **Menu Adoption Cycle** is the most usable framework here. It has four stages — **Inception → Adoption → Proliferation → Ubiquity** — and explicitly models *where* each stage lives:

| Stage | Where it lives | Who carries it |
|---|---|---|
| **Inception** | Fine dining; **ethnic/immigrant independents** | Innovators in big "hearth" cities |
| **Adoption** | Lower price points, simplified prep | Fast-casual, regional players |
| **Proliferation** | "Mainstreamed" — put on burgers, pasta, etc. | Broad casual/fast-casual |
| **Ubiquity** | **Mainstream chains + supermarket shelves** | National chains, grocery |

([Datassential – Understanding the Menu Adoption Cycle](https://datassential.com/resource/understanding-trend-lifecycles/))

**The full cycle now runs ~6–8 years**, compressed from ~12 years a generation ago (social media + faster franchise rollouts shortened it). ([Datassential, via Blue Book – "Foodservice menu trends move faster than ever"](https://www.producebluebook.com/2019/05/20/foodservice-menu-trends-move-faster-than-ever/)) For comparison, fashion's analog lag is **~1–2 years for cosmetics, ~2–3 years for clothing** from trendsetters to "mainstreamers" — also compressing. ([Univ. of Minnesota – Trend Lifecycles & Adopter Categories](https://open.lib.umn.edu/communicatingfashion/chapter/chapter-6-trend-lifecycles-and-adopter-categories/))

**Where does Turlock fall on this curve?** Near the **Proliferation→Ubiquity** end. A small, flat-growth city (see `10c_B.md`) almost never gets a concept at **Inception** (no fine-dining/innovator base to incubate it) or even early **Adoption**. Turlock gets concepts when they have been **de-risked into a franchisable, mainstream-priced format** — i.e., when a national/regional chain or a local operator copying a proven format will commit capital. So the **practical lag from "trending in LA/NYC" to "openable in Turlock" is roughly the back half of that cycle — on the order of 3–8 years**, and shortest for concepts that franchise aggressively. *(LABELED ESTIMATE — derived by mapping Turlock's market position onto the Datassential cycle stages above; confidence: medium. The 6–8-year full-cycle figure and the stage definitions are cited facts; placing Turlock at the Proliferation/Ubiquity end is the analyst's inference.)*

## 3. The structural reason concepts are flowing to mid-size cities *right now*

This is not just theory — the **plumbing has shifted in Turlock's favor over 2024–2026**:

- **Retailers are physically running out of room in the big cities.** With prime retail space at record-low availability, national brands are "moving beyond major metropolitan areas… to secondary and tertiary markets." ([CBRE – Navigating Opportunities in Secondary and Tertiary Retail Markets](https://www.cbre.com/insights/articles/navigating-opportunities-in-secondary-and-tertiary-retail-markets))
- **The opening counts now tilt to smaller places.** In a Dataplor study of seven major retailers, there were **~330 store openings in smaller cities/suburbs vs. ~59 in major cities (pop. >600k)** — roughly a **5.6-to-1 tilt toward smaller markets** — because "consumers want in-person shopping experiences… in the places they live." ([Dataplor – Retail's New Frontier: Store Openings in Smaller Cities](https://www.dataplor.com/resources/blog/retails-new-frontier-the-rise-of-store-openings-in-smaller-cities-and-counties/))
- **Smaller-market shoppers are an attractive target.** Secondary/tertiary shoppers carry **~1.3% more per-capita disposable income** than primary-market shoppers (a gap forecast to widen), and small-town/rural shoppers are far more likely to shop **in-store** (~86%/83% vs. ~62% in city centers). ([CBRE – Secondary and Tertiary Markets](https://www.cbre.com/insights/articles/navigating-opportunities-in-secondary-and-tertiary-retail-markets))
- **2026 is a faster opening year.** Store openings (ex-restaurants) are forecast **+1.4% in 2026 vs. +0.7% in 2025**; including restaurants, **+1.8% vs. ~1%** — led by **discount, grocery, and convenience formats** concentrating in **suburban and secondary markets**. ([ICSC – 11 Retail Real Estate Predictions for 2026](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/11-retail-real-estate-predictions-for-2026))

**Caveat (the cost of being a receiver).** The same sources flag that serving remote/smaller stores from existing distribution networks is **logistically expensive**; chains solve it with regional hubs or local carriers. ([ICSC – 2026 predictions](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/11-retail-real-estate-predictions-for-2026)) Translation for Turlock: the *demand-side* pull (cheaper space, in-store-loyal shoppers) is real, but a concept only lands when the *supply-side* logistics pencil out — which is exactly why **format type matters** (discount/grocery/QSR arrive first; high-touch luxury essentially never does).

## 4. What Turlock is adopting RIGHT NOW (June 2026) — receipts

The diffusion model isn't abstract; it is visibly playing out on Turlock's Monte Vista corridor. Three concrete, dated examples — each a concept that **incepted in a big hearth city years earlier and reached Turlock only recently**:

| Concept (Turlock) | Hearth / origin | Stage when it reached Turlock | Turlock arrival | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dave's Hot Chicken** (Nashville-style hot chicken) | Format = **Nashville**; the *fast-casual chain* incepted in **East Hollywood, LA (2017)** | **Ubiquity** (national franchise rollout) | Soft launch + grand opening **late Oct 2025**, 2741 Countryside Dr, Monte Vista Crossings | [Turlock Journal – Dave's Hot Chicken set for grand opening](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/daves-hot-chicken-set-for-grand-opening/) |
| **Bubble tea ("Quickly")** — Asian café, board games, student-oriented | Bubble tea: Taiwan → US gateway cities (LA, SF/Bay, NYC) decades ago | **Proliferation/Ubiquity** — but *locally still novel* (owner's words below) | New shop **announced ~May 2026**, 2014 W. Monte Vista Ave, near Stanislaus State | [Turlock Journal – New bubble tea shop comes to Turlock](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/new-bubble-tea-shop-comes-to-turlock/) |
| **Crumbl Cookies; "Mango Crazy" (bubble-tea/dessert); Deli Delicious** | Crumbl: Utah; bubble-tea & sandwich formats: Bay Area/SoCal | **Ubiquity** (franchise) | Co-tenants in the **same 9,740 sq ft Countryside Dr building** as Dave's | [Turlock Journal – Dave's Hot Chicken set for grand opening](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/daves-hot-chicken-set-for-grand-opening/) |

**The single most useful data point for the owner** is the bubble-tea owner's own reasoning, because it is *textbook late-receiver behavior* — he is consciously importing a big-city-proven concept into an underserved small market:

> *"I just thought that it could be something different for the city… There aren't a lot of bubble tea shops here, so I thought that Turlock might be a good fit."* — Quickly owner Jack Wu ([Turlock Journal](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/new-bubble-tea-shop-comes-to-turlock/))

That is the diffusion lag *spoken out loud*: a concept that is **ubiquitous and saturated in SF/LA** is still "something different" and **under-supplied** in Turlock in 2026. **The lag is the opportunity.** Note also the **Stanislaus State** anchor: every one of these clusters near W. Monte Vista / the university, because the ~10k-student campus is Turlock's densest, youngest, most trend-receptive demand pocket — the local equivalent of the dense neighborhoods that incubate adoption in big cities.

**Pattern across all three rows:** Turlock receives concepts at the **franchise/ubiquity** end, **clustered on one retail corridor**, **anchored by the university**, and **routed through Stanislaus County's broader pipeline** (the Modesto Bee tracks the same wave of chains — e.g., Raising Cane's being built in nearby Riverbank — arriving across the county). ([Modesto Bee via NewsBreak – new stores/restaurants coming to Stanislaus County](https://www.newsbreak.com/the-modesto-bee-1592579/4334678260454-new-stores-restaurants-are-coming-to-stanislaus-county-check-out-10-businesses))

## 5. Which cities "lead" — a ranked, honest answer to the owner

| Tier | Cities | Role for Turlock | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Hearths (originators)** | **NYC, LA, SF/Bay Area; Austin (newer wave)** | *Watch for what's coming in 5+ years* | Inception happens in fine dining + immigrant independents; biggest, densest, highest-income, most immigrant-rich. ([Datassential](https://datassential.com/resource/understanding-trend-lifecycles/)) The owner is right that "the fast ones are New York" — for **birth** of concepts. |
| **2. Large-metro adopters** | **Sacramento, Fresno, San Jose/Bay** | *Early warning — concept is now franchisable* | Where a concept drops to mainstream price points and chains form. |
| **3. Regional gateway (THE one to watch)** | **Modesto** | *Direct upstream — Turlock is next* | Stanislaus County anchor; last-mile/contagious diffusion means Turlock gets it ~right after Modesto proves it. |
| **4. Turlock (receiver)** | **Turlock** | *Adopts at Proliferation/Ubiquity* | Flat-growth small city (`10c_B.md`); commits once a franchisee/local copycat will. |

**Blunt takeaway for the "which cities are adopting from others" question:** *Everyone below Tier 1 is an adopter.* New York is fast at **inventing**; it is not "adopting from" anyone. **Turlock is purely an adopter**, and its truest "leading indicator" is **Modesto + Sacramento**, not New York — even though New York is where the idea was born. For a Turlock operator, the right habit is: **track LA/SF for the 5-year horizon, track Modesto/Sacramento for the 1–2-year horizon.**

## 6. What Turlock could adopt NEXT — the pipeline (analyst inference, flagged)

Reading the cycle (§2), the corridor pattern (§4), and the structural pull (§3), the concepts most likely to be Turlock's *next* arrivals are those currently at **Proliferation in Sacramento/Modesto and franchising hard** — i.e., already de-risked, mainstream-priced, and university-friendly:

- **More Asian-beverage/dessert formats** (additional bubble-tea, Korean corn dogs, soft-serve/"crazy" dessert mashups) — the bubble-tea wave is *visibly* still entering Turlock in 2026 (§4), so it is **mid-adoption, not saturated** locally. **This is the clearest open window.**
- **Nashville-hot / global-fried-chicken & "swicy" (sweet-heat) QSR** — Dave's just landed (Oct 2025); the broader swicy/hot-chicken trend is at ubiquity nationally and will keep arriving via franchises. (National 2026 trend context: [National Geographic – biggest food trends for 2026](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/the-biggest-food-trends-for-2026); [LAist – LA food trends 2026](https://laist.com/news/food/los-angeles-food-trends-in-2026-what-to-expect-this-year))
- **Discount / value / convenience retail formats** — these are the *named leaders* of 2026 openings in secondary markets (discount + grocery + convenience). ([ICSC – 2026 predictions](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/11-retail-real-estate-predictions-for-2026); [Dataplor](https://www.dataplor.com/resources/blog/retails-new-frontier-the-rise-of-store-openings-in-smaller-cities-and-counties/)) Off-price apparel (Burlington/Marshalls were the most-opened banners in the Dataplor study) and value grocery fit Turlock's income/in-store-loyal profile.
- **Wellness-coded food/beverage** (functional/specialty coffee & tea, better-for-you) — specialty coffee/functional tea is projected to grow **~8–10%/yr through 2026**; this is an Inception/Adoption-stage trend in hearth cities that will reach Turlock as franchise/QSR formats over the **next several years**, not immediately. ([Accio – 2025 Foodservice Coffee & Tea Trends](https://www.accio.com/business/foodservice-coffee-and-tea-trends))

*All of §6 is a **LABELED ESTIMATE / analyst inference** — confidence: medium for the beverage/QSR/value-retail calls (each is directly supported by an observed Turlock arrival or a named 2026-secondary-market leader), lower for wellness timing. The underlying trend-stage facts are cited; the **"Turlock is next" sequencing is the analyst's judgment**, not a sourced prediction.*

**What Turlock will NOT get (the honest exclusion):** Inception-stage fine dining, luxury/high-touch retail, and concepts that require big-city density or affluence. Those never complete the last-mile diffusion to a flat-growth ~73k city. An operator betting on "be the first Turlock Michelin-style concept" is fighting the diffusion gradient; an operator betting on "import the proven Modesto/Sacramento concept before the local copycats do" is riding it.

## 7. One-screen summary for the owner

1. **Direction is real and one-way.** Concepts are *born* in NYC/LA/SF/Austin and *trickle down* the city-size ladder. Turlock is a **receiver, never an originator.** ([NVCC](https://pressbooks.nvcc.edu/nolgeo210/chapter/cultural-diffusion/))
2. **"Fast cities = New York" is right about invention, wrong about your radar.** New York invents; it doesn't adopt from anyone. **Your real leading indicator is Modesto/Sacramento**, because the last mile spreads by *proximity*, not by city size. ([Royal Society Open Science 2022](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/9/5/211038/96688/Urban-hierarchy-and-spatial-diffusion-over-the))
3. **The lag is ~6–8 years inception→ubiquity nationally; Turlock buys in near the *end* of that curve** (~back half, ~3–8 yrs from "trending in LA"). ([Datassential](https://datassential.com/resource/understanding-trend-lifecycles/); [Blue Book](https://www.producebluebook.com/2019/05/20/foodservice-menu-trends-move-faster-than-ever/))
4. **The pipe is wide open in 2026:** retailers are fleeing expensive big-city space *toward* secondary markets (~**5.6:1** openings tilt to smaller cities), small-market shoppers have *more* disposable income and shop *in-store* more. ([Dataplor](https://www.dataplor.com/resources/blog/retails-new-frontier-the-rise-of-store-openings-in-smaller-cities-and-counties/); [CBRE](https://www.cbre.com/insights/articles/navigating-opportunities-in-secondary-and-tertiary-retail-markets); [ICSC](https://www.icsc.com/news-and-views/icsc-exchange/11-retail-real-estate-predictions-for-2026))
5. **It's already happening on W. Monte Vista, anchored by Stanislaus State:** Dave's Hot Chicken (LA-incepted, arrived Oct 2025), new bubble tea (owner literally says the city is under-supplied, May 2026), Crumbl/Mango Crazy/Deli Delicious clustered together. ([Turlock Journal – Dave's](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/daves-hot-chicken-set-for-grand-opening/); [Turlock Journal – bubble tea](https://www.turlockjournal.com/news/local/new-bubble-tea-shop-comes-to-turlock/))
6. **The strategy that fits a receiver market:** don't try to *originate*; **import a concept proven one rung up (Modesto/Sacramento) into Turlock's still-underserved window before local copycats saturate it.** The bubble-tea owner is doing exactly this — and saying so out loud.
