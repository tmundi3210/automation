# 10c_A — The Growth Picture: Turlock & Stanislaus County, ~2010–2025

**Analyst lens:** LOCAL-TREND / macro. **Date:** June 2026.
**Companion notes:** `10a_A.md` (demand-side economic base), `10b_A.md` (City fiscal/budget). This note builds the **long-run growth "chart"** across four dimensions — **population, jobs/employment, business counts, and income** — for both the **City of Turlock** and **Stanislaus County** (the Modesto Metropolitan Statistical Area = Stanislaus County, exactly).

> **HONESTY RULE (applies throughout).** Every government/census/economic figure below is a **CITED PUBLIC FACT** (with source URL) or is **clearly LABELED as an ESTIMATE** (method + confidence) or marked **UNKNOWN**. No private business's revenue is stated as fact; the only revenue-style numbers here are public/aggregate. No numbers are fabricated. Where a figure could not be sourced to a verifiable page, it is flagged.

## 0. Executive read (the shape of the curve)

Over ~10–15 years the Turlock/Stanislaus story is **steady, modest growth off a deep recession floor**, not a boom:

- **Population:** County grew ~**+7–8%** (≈514k → ≈554k, 2010→2024). Turlock grew faster to 2020 (~+4.3%, 69,733 → 72,740) then **flattened/edged down** post-2020 (~72,500 in 2024). Direction: **UP, then flat.**
- **Jobs:** Devastated in the 2008–10 housing crash (county unemployment **peaked ~17.3% in Jan 2010**), then a long expansion. County nonfarm jobs reached **~191,000 (2026)**; resident employment base ~**241k (2024, Data USA basis)**. Direction: **strong recovery → expansion, now slow growth.**
- **Businesses/establishments:** Grew with the recovery; Turlock-area jobs in the city's own Lightcast report rose **+4.0% 2018→2023**. Exact establishment counts: **UNKNOWN from accessible sources** (CBP behind a blocked portal) — see §3. Direction: **UP (modest).**
- **Income:** Median household income rose in **nominal** terms substantially (county ~$72k→~$80k; Turlock ~$71k→~$83k headline) but much of the gain is **inflation, not real purchasing power** — real (2023-$) growth was only ~**11–12%** over 13 years. Direction: **UP nominally, roughly flat-to-modest in real terms.**

## 6. Narrative interpretation (LABELED INFERENCE)

1. **A recovery story, not a boom.** The dominant fact is the climb out of the **~17% unemployment** 2010 trough — one of the worst metro downturns in the U.S. The decade since has been a **long, steady normalization**: jobs up, unemployment down, population and income rising modestly. This is a **catch-up** economy, not a fast-growth one.

2. **Post-2020 plateau.** Population flattening (city flat-to-down; county growth ~0.2%/yr) and **sub-0.5% job growth by 2024** point to a **maturing, slow-growth** environment — driven by California-wide out-migration and cost-of-living pressure, not local collapse. *Inference, MEDIUM confidence.*

3. **Real income barely moved.** Despite headline income rising from ~$71k to ~$83k (Turlock), **real growth was ~11–12% over 13 years (~0.9%/yr).** For an independent business, this means local **purchasing power expanded slowly** — demand growth has come more from **population × stable real wages** than from rising affluence. *Inference grounded in cited real-$ series.*

4. **Demand base is broad and services-led.** Top employment in **health care, manufacturing, retail, education, and government** (plus restaurants leading the Turlock-area job mix) — a **diversified, locally-serving** base relatively insulated from any single industry, with a notable ag/manufacturing backbone. *CITED industry mix; insulation claim is inference.*

5. **What the macro data can't tell us.** It cannot distinguish **independent vs. chain** establishment growth, nor give a confirmed establishment count — that gap is exactly what this project's business catalog (`catalog/`, sector notes) exists to fill. The macro picture sets the **ceiling** (a slow-growing, modestly-prospering market of ~72k city / ~554k county residents with ~$80k median household income); the catalog work sizes the **independent slice** inside it.
