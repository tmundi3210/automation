## 2. Conflicts & discrepancies flagged

### 2.1 CONFLICT — sales-tax rate composition (the 1.625% attribution)
- **A** breaks 8.625% into: 6.00% state + 0.25% Stanislaus County + **0.75% Turlock (Measure A)** + **1.625% other/special**.
- **B** breaks 8.625% into: 6% state + 0.25% county + 0.75% city + **"1.625% special/district from Measure A."**
- **Reconciliation:** Both agree on the *numbers*, but **B's label is wrong**: Measure A **is** the 0.75% city add-on (both notes confirm this elsewhere), so the 1.625% **cannot** also "be Measure A." The 1.625% is the separate countywide/district transactions-and-use-tax layer. **A's attribution is correct; B's parenthetical is an internal error.** Use A's breakdown. (Confidence: HIGH that 0.75% = Measure A; the 1.625% "other/special" composition itself is only summary-level and not line-verified — see §8.)

### 2.2 DISCREPANCY (scope, not contradiction) — "General Fund" reported three ways
Only A addresses this; flagged because the three numbers look contradictory but are not:
- **~$52.0M** = core/operating General Fund (FY2023-24 adopted "expected revenues & expenditures").
- **$78.7M (FY23-24) / $80.1M (FY24-25)** = "ongoing General Fund revenues" — a **broader** measure (appears to fold in Measure A and other dedicated streams routed through the GF).
- **$62.48M** = total adopted budget headline (FY24-25); **all-funds vs GF-only scope is UNKNOWN** from snippets.
- **Reconciliation:** different scopes, not disagreement. Exact boundaries are **MEDIUM confidence** pending Resolution 2024-92 + the AFRs. **B treats GF ≈ $62.48M budget order-of-magnitude for its per-capita math — that is a simplification A's detail shows is imprecise.** Do not equate the $62.48M budget with the General Fund total.

### 2.3 DISCREPANCY — sales-tax dollar total (two independent estimates, roughly consistent)
- **A:** annualizes Q1-2023 receipts (~$4.91M ×4) → **~$19–20M/yr** total citywide sales-tax receipts (LABELED ESTIMATE, ±10–15%).
- **B:** from 33–37% of a ~$62.48M-order GF → **~$15–22M/yr** city sales-tax-funded revenue (LABELED ESTIMATE, ±30%).
- **Reconciliation:** **No conflict** — overlapping bands, different methods. **Caution:** A's figure is *total citywide receipts* (all rate layers); B's is *the city's own share*. These are **not the same scope**, so the overlap is partly coincidental. Both remain ESTIMATES; the exact dollar line is **UNKNOWN**.

### 2.4 Coverage gaps (each note has facts the other lacks — not conflicts)
- **Only A** has: property-tax share (~8.57%), UUT = $0 (with the $6.969M hypothetical), the $52M/$80M GF scopes, GF surplus/deficit (+$4.6M / −$5.6M).
- **Only B** has: concentration data (**25 firms = 60.1%** of sales tax; **Monte Vista Crossings >25%**), auto-segment quarterly figures (Q1-2025 ≈$778,809 / Q2-2025 ≈$862,612), the Bradley-Burns point-of-sale mechanism.

## 5. Reconciled synthesis (for the macro brain)

The City of Turlock runs a **~$62.5M FY24-25 adopted budget** [FACT], with a General Fund reported at multiple scopes — a **~$52M core operating GF** and a broader **~$80M "ongoing GF revenues"** measure (up from $78.7M FY23-24), the latter swinging from a **+$4.6M surplus (FY23-24)** to a **−$5.6M deficit (FY24-25)** [FACT]. The fiscal engine is **sales tax**, the largest GF source at **33–37%** [FACT], supercharged since **Apr 2021 by Measure A** (voter-approved 0.75% local tax, 56.74% YES, ~$11M/yr est.) [FACT], lifting the combined rate to **8.625%** [FACT]. That revenue is **extremely concentrated**: **25 firms = 60.1%** of sales tax and **Monte Vista Crossings alone = >25%** [FACT], with the **auto segment running ~$0.78–0.86M/quarter** [FACT] — a direct consequence of California's **point-of-sale Bradley-Burns 1%** that rewards high-ticket retail [FACT]. **Property tax** is a distant second (City keeps only **~8.57%** of the 1% levy) [FACT], and there is **no Utility Users Tax** — the **$6.969M UUT figure is a City hypothetical, not collected revenue** [EST]. Exact dollar lines for total sales tax (~$15–22M city share / ~$19–20M total receipts), property tax (~$8–11M), and per-capita (~$200–300/resident) remain **LABELED ESTIMATES — UNKNOWN as cited facts** pending the primary budget and AFRs. **Bottom line:** Turlock is **heavily sales-tax-dependent and unusually concentrated** in a few anchors, tightly coupling City fiscal health to retail performance and creating real concentration risk (one dealer/anchor closure is a measurable GF hit).
