## 5. APPEAL NOTES (who this targets · aspirational/premium framing · cite the research honesty)

### Who this ad targets
A **male-skewing buyer for a men's classic-gold piece**, plus a **female-skewing gift/partner audience** for whom a status/craftsmanship object reads as desirable. The register is **aspiration** (the audience wants to *own / gift* this object and the composure it signals), not identification — this is a luxury-object ad, so the **luxury/status-prop route** is the correct lever [aspiration-vs-identification framing: ESTIMATE; grounded in Horton & Wohl 1956 parasocial theory and Han et al. 2024's meta-analysis ranking Source Credibility Theory most robust — FACT-tier sources].

### Gaze & hero-framing that reads aspirational and premium (tasteful)
- **Face-first, then hand off to the product.** A human face is the near-certain first fixation in a short frame — Cerf, Frady & Koch (2009) raised fixation-prediction AUC from ~0.53 (saliency alone) to >0.84 by adding a face channel, replicated by ETRA 2020 [**FACT**]. So the presenter's face anchors attention in clips 2 and 5, then a **motion/gesture cue hands attention to the kada** [translation to craft rule: **ESTIMATE**]. This is exactly why the plan puts product macros (clips 3–4) as the hero beats and keeps the presenter in supporting cutaways.
- **Gaze direction is a per-shot A/B variable, NOT a fixed rule.** Whether the presenter should look *at the product* or *at the viewer* to maximize memory/purchase is **CONTESTED** — Sajjacholapunt & Ball (averted gaze aids memory) directly conflicts with the gaze-to-product / D'Ambrogio 2023 celebrity-status findings. We do **not** hard-code "always look at the product." The plan's approving-glance-down in clips 2/5 is a **defensible default to test**, not a claimed-settled convention.
- **One face / one motion / one status-signal per frame.** Bottom-up (Itti-Koch) saliency means a single moving element against a still field (the slow forearm rotation, the one specular roll across the band) is a reliable attention magnet, while competing motion cancels it [FACT substrate; single-cue ceiling: ESTIMATE]. The kada **is** the status signal — no competing luxury props, logos, or busy backgrounds. This matches the plan's quiet-luxury restraint.
- **Status-prop weighting (working hypothesis, not law).** A status/luxury object tends to lift a **male** target's rated attractiveness for a **female-skewing** audience more than the symmetric assumption predicts — Dunn & Searle (2010) [**FACT**, applied as a **working hypothesis**, revisited on replication, not settled law]. Supports letting the gold read as the single "flex" on an understated presenter.
- **Silhouette/tailoring:** the persona's V-taper/quiet-luxury tailoring reads as an **evident-but-not-extreme** premium masculinity — a qualitative tendency (moderate, not maximal — Frederick & Haselton 2007's inverted-U, itself **CONTESTED** with a supra-normal-stimulus caveat). **No precise numeric physique target is asserted.**

### Taste floor (hard ceiling)
Attraction is delivered via **styling, silhouette, status context, expression, and cinematography — never nudity, sexualized posing, or solicitation** (aspirational-not-explicit house rule). This is reinforced independently by the idealized-imagery harm evidence (Grabe, Ward & Hyde 2008, 77-study meta-analysis) [**FACT**], so it holds regardless of platform enforcement. For a men's-gold ad this is trivially clear, but the ceiling is stated so no "sex-sells" re-cut is entertained. Any borderline styling question defers to a **live re-fetch** of Meta's current nudity/suggestive thresholds — the exact verbatim thresholds are **UNKNOWN** this session (policy pages 403-blocked), never guessed.
