## 1. Micro-story structure for 15–60s vertical video

The reel format the owner specified is **3–6 second clips stitched together** into a 15–60s whole. That constraint forces a compressed 4-part shape: **Hook → Setup → Escalation → Payoff/Loop**. Map each stitched clip to a beat rather than treating the reel as one continuous scene.

### 1.1 The hook (first 1–2 seconds) — retention cliff

- [FACT] Instagram's head Adam Mosseri publicly frames the **first three seconds** of a reel as "the hook," and states that early-second retention is remarkably *consistent across videos regardless of eventual performance* — the differentiation happens **after** the ~3-second mark, where strong videos hold and weak ones drop sharply. (Mosseri public statements, reported/aggregated — see sources 8, 9. The exact wording lives in his own Instagram reels; secondary aggregation used because the native posts are not fetchable as text.)
- [FACT] The much-cited Facebook figure — "**65% of people who watch the first 3 seconds watch at least 10 seconds, and 45% continue to 30 seconds**" — traces to Facebook's own marketing communications and is repeated across the marketing literature, but the **original Facebook IQ primary page is not directly retrievable**; it survives only through secondary citations. Treat the exact percentages as **[FACT-of-attribution, ESTIMATE-of-precision]** — Facebook said it, but you cannot audit the methodology (source 1, 2).
- [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine] "Viewers decide within ~1.7 seconds whether to keep watching" and "reels with 60%+ 3-second hold rates outperform weak holds by 5–10x reach" circulate widely (source 8) but are **not traceable to a primary controlled study** — treat as directional marketing lore, not fact. Do not cite the 5–10x figure as established.

**Working takeaway (safe to build on):** the platform owner himself says the first ~3s is where retention is won or lost. That is the load-bearing fact. Build the hook to survive a sub-2-second swipe decision.

**Hook construction patterns** [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine, source 8, 11]:
- Open on the *most visually arresting* frame, not a slow build (no logo intro, no establishing pan that pays off later).
- Front-load the promise: the viewer should know within 1–2s what world they are in (luxury, travel, transformation) even muted.
- Motion in frame one: a moving subject, a reveal, a whip-in — static openings bleed viewers.
- For luxury personas specifically: the hook frame is an *aspiration snapshot* (the car door opening, the penthouse reveal, the tailored silhouette turning to camera) — status legible at a glance.

### 1.2 Setup, escalation, payoff

- **Setup (≈clip 1–2, seconds ~2–8):** establish who/where/what-tension. In a stitched reel this is 1–2 short clips that ground the persona in a place and a mood. Keep it short — the compressed form cannot afford a long runway.
- **Escalation (middle clips):** each stitched clip should *raise* something — a bigger location, a wardrobe change, a rising musical intensity, an accelerating edit. The stitch transitions themselves (match cut, whip pan, outfit-change snap) carry the escalation energy; see the camera/editing research file for transition grammar.
- **Payoff (final clip):** deliver the promise the hook made — the destination reveal, the final look, the quote punchline, the drop in the music. In silent autoplay the payoff must be *visual*, not a spoken line.

### 1.3 Loop-closing endings

- [FACT] Seamless looping — designing the final frame to flow back into the first so the restart is invisible — is a documented watch-time tactic: a clean loop multiplies watch time per view because viewers rewatch without noticing, and high completion/replay signals push distribution (source 10). This aligns with Mosseri's stated ranking signals (watch time being one).
- [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine, source 10] Practical loop mechanics: match the last frame's composition/motion/lighting to the first; end on continuing motion rather than a hard stop; if using audio, pick or edit audio that also loops cleanly. Suggested loop length for maximum replay sits around 7–10s per source 10, but that source is a marketing blog, not a platform study — treat as directional.
- **Two ending modes to prompt for:** (a) **hard loop** — last frame ≈ first frame (subject re-enters the same doorway, the same turn-to-camera); (b) **open loop / cliffhanger** — end on an unresolved beat ("part 2") that drives comments and follows rather than a rewatch. Choose per format (§8).

## 2. Classic structure, compressed — what survives at reel scale

All three classic frameworks below are **human craft doctrine**, not empirical findings. Tagged [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine] with the sourced definition.

### 2.1 Three-act structure
[ESTIMATE / craft doctrine] Setup → Confrontation → Resolution. At reel scale this collapses to **Hook+Setup (Act 1) → Escalation (Act 2) → Payoff/Loop (Act 3)**. The act-2 "confrontation/obstacle" is the thinnest survivor — a 20s reel rarely has room for genuine conflict; it survives as *tension* (curiosity gap, rising stakes, will-they-arrive) rather than plot conflict.

### 2.2 Freytag's pyramid (five parts)
[FACT — definition] Gustav Freytag, *Die Technik des Dramas* (1863), five parts: **exposition, rising action, climax, falling action, dénouement** — tension rises to a mid-point climax then falls to resolution (source 7).
[ESTIMATE / craft doctrine] At reel scale: exposition ≈ the hook frame; rising action ≈ escalation clips; climax ≈ payoff; **falling action and dénouement essentially do not survive** — a 15–30s reel cannot afford a wind-down. The compressed reel is closer to a *rising line that cuts to black (or loops) at the climax* than a full pyramid. This is the single biggest structural difference from long-form: **you cut the entire back half of Freytag.**

### 2.3 Hero's journey beats
[ESTIMATE / craft doctrine] Campbell/Vogler's monomyth (ordinary world → call → threshold → trials → reward → return) is far too long for one reel. **What survives:** (a) a single beat can *be* a reel (the "threshold crossing" = the car pulling out of the gate; the "reward" = the summit reveal); (b) the full arc can be spread **across a series** (§8) — each post is one beat, the account is the journey. Treat the monomyth as a *series-level* skeleton, not a per-reel one.

**Load-bearing craft conclusion [ESTIMATE]:** at 15–60s the only reliable survivors are **hook, escalation, and a climactic payoff/loop.** Denouement, subplot, and multi-beat arcs migrate up to the series level. Build per-reel for the rising line; build the arc across posts.

## 3. Emotional beat design

Four emotional levers matter most for aspirational luxury content: **aspiration, curiosity gap, tension–release, awe.**

### 3.1 Awe and high-arousal emotion drive sharing
- [FACT] Berger & Milkman (2012), *Journal of Marketing Research* — analyzing all NYT articles over ~3 months — found content evoking **high-arousal emotions** (positive: **awe**; negative: anger, anxiety) is **more viral**, while **low-arousal/deactivating emotions (e.g., sadness) are less viral.** Positive content is generally more viral than negative, but arousal is the mechanism. This held controlling for surprise, interest, usefulness, and placement (source 6).
- **Application:** luxury travel/architecture/nature reels are *awe machines* — sweeping reveals, scale, grandeur. Awe is both aspirational *and* high-arousal, i.e. exactly the sharable quadrant. Lean into scale and reveal.

### 3.2 Curiosity gap
- [FACT] Loewenstein (1994), "The Psychology of Curiosity," *Psychological Bulletin* 116(1):75–98 — the **information-gap theory**: curiosity is a form of cognitively induced deprivation arising from a *perceived gap* between what one knows and wants to know; intensity rises with how likely the gap feels fillable (source 5).
- **Application:** open reels on a gap ("what's behind this door / where does this road go / what happens at the end") and withhold the fill until the payoff. The open-loop ending (§1.3) is a deliberately *unfilled* gap that drives comments/follows. Do not over-tease (clickbait that never fills breeds distrust).

### 3.3 Aspiration
- [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine] Aspiration is not one of the classic "basic emotions"; it operates as *desired-self projection* — the viewer imagines themselves in the persona's position. Levers: proximity (first-person POV shots, "come with me"), attainability signaling (make the lifestyle feel reachable, not merely observed), and identity cues that match the target audience's ideal self. This is the media-manager/psychology file's domain; here it's a *beat* — the reel should contain at least one "this could be you" moment.

### 3.4 Tension–release
- [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine] The universal engine of held attention: build a small tension (a rising musical swell, an unfinished motion, an approaching reveal) and release it on the payoff. In stitched reels the *edit rhythm and score* carry this — see the soundtrack research file. A reel with no tension–release reads as a slideshow.

## 4. Facial expression & body language grounding (FACS + the emotion debate)

This section gives the specialist a **defensible public vocabulary** for writing expression directions into image/video prompts, and carries the scientific controversy honestly so the specialist never over-claims that a prompted face "is" a universal emotion.

### 4.1 FACS — the public framework for *describing* expressions
- [FACT] The **Facial Action Coding System (FACS)** categorizes human facial movement by the underlying muscle actions, called **Action Units (AUs)**. Originally rooted in Swedish anatomist Carl-Herman Hjortsjö's work and adapted by **Paul Ekman and Wallace V. Friesen (first published 1978; major update Ekman, Friesen & Hager 2002).** It defines on the order of ~44 AUs, each mapping to contraction/relaxation of specific facial muscles, with a **5-point intensity scale (A–E)**. Human coders can decompose nearly any anatomically possible expression into AUs (sources 3, 3a Wikipedia, 3b Paul Ekman Group).
- **Why it matters for prompting:** FACS gives a *muscle-level, emotion-agnostic* description language. Instead of prompting "happy," a specialist can direct the *mechanics* — which reads more photoreal and avoids the plastic "AI smile." Illustrative AU vocabulary [FACT — standard FACS labels]:
  - **AU1** inner brow raiser, **AU2** outer brow raiser, **AU4** brow lowerer (the frown/concentration muscle), **AU5** upper lid raiser, **AU6** cheek raiser (the *orbicularis oculi* — the "eye" part of a genuine smile), **AU7** lid tightener, **AU9** nose wrinkler, **AU12** lip corner puller (the smile muscle, *zygomaticus major*), **AU15** lip corner depressor, **AU23** lip tightener, **AU26** jaw drop.
  - **The Duchenne (genuine) smile = AU6 + AU12** together (cheek raiser + lip corner puller). A social/posed smile is AU12 *without* AU6. **This single distinction is the highest-leverage expression direction for anti-AI realism** — prompting for eye involvement (AU6: "eyes crinkling, cheeks lifting") prevents the dead-eyed synthetic grin. [FACT — standard FACS; the Duchenne distinction is textbook.]
  - For luxury *aloofness/coolness*: reduced AU12, slight AU4, neutral-to-lowered lids, minimal expressiveness — the "quiet" face (see §6).

### 4.2 The 6–7 basic emotions debate — carried honestly
- [FACT] The **classical view (Ekman)**: a set of biologically hardwired **basic emotions** — commonly listed as **happiness, sadness, fear, anger, surprise, disgust** (six), sometimes **+contempt** (seven) — with cross-culturally recognizable facial expressions (source 4, 3b).
- [FACT] **Challenge 1 — fewer categories:** Jack, Garrod & Schyns (2014), *Current Biology*, "Dynamic Facial Expressions of Emotion Transmit an Evolving Hierarchy of Signals over Time," argue expressions are perceptually segmented over time and that early signaling supports **four** basic categories (fear/surprise and anger/disgust initially share signals before diverging), i.e. **four, not six** at the biologically-rooted level (source 4a Glasgow, 4b Cell/Current Biology, 4c PubMed 24388852).
- [FACT] **Challenge 2 — no fixed categories at all:** Lisa Feldman Barrett's **Theory of Constructed Emotion** holds that emotions are *not* innate reflexes with distinct neural/facial/physiological signatures; what is innate is **affect** (valence: pleasant/unpleasant × arousal: activated/calm), and specific emotions are *constructed* by the brain from prediction, interoception, and learned concepts. Evidence cited: emotions lack consistent facial expressions, neural signatures, or universal physiological markers across contexts (source 4d SweetInstitute summary; primary: Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made*, 2017, and Barrett et al. 2025 *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, "Theory of Constructed Emotion").
- **Honest synthesis for the specialist:** there is genuine, unresolved scientific disagreement about whether "basic emotions" are real universal kinds. **Therefore:** use FACS AU descriptions as *mechanical direction* (what the muscles do) and the emotion labels only as *shorthand for the desired reading*, never as a scientific claim that the face is a universal signal. This keeps prompts honest and, conveniently, more photoreal — because you're specifying anatomy, not invoking a stereotype.

### 4.3 Micro-expressions as subtle acting directions
- [FACT] Ekman & Friesen termed very fast, involuntary facial movements lasting **1/25 to 1/5 of a second** *micro-expressions* — brief leakage of concealed/suppressed emotion (Paul Ekman Group; source 12; Wikipedia "Microexpression"). Note the honesty caveat: the *deception-detection* claims around micro-expressions are contested in the literature — do not build a "lie detection" claim.
- **Application for prompting:** true 1/25s micro-expressions are below what a stitched-clip edit reliably shows, but the *concept* is a useful acting direction — prompt for **subtle, restrained, "almost" expressions** (a flicker of a smile, a barely-there brow flash, a gaze that softens for a beat). For luxury personas, *restraint reads as class*; broad expressions read as trying-too-hard. Direct micro-moves: "the faintest smile beginning at the lip corners," "a brief softening of the eyes," "a near-imperceptible chin lift."

## 5. Show-not-tell in silent-autoplay context

- [FACT] Reels/feed video autoplay **muted** by default; a large share of social video is watched without sound. The most-cited figure — "**85% of Facebook video is watched without sound**" — comes from **Digiday (Sahil Patel, May 17 2016)**, and is a *publisher-reported* range (LittleThings ~85%, Mic ~85% of 30s views, MEC clients ~85–90%), **not a single platform-measured statistic** (source 13, corroborated by Nieman Lab and Marketing Dive, sources 13a/13b). **Tag it [FACT-of-attribution / ESTIMATE-of-generality]:** publishers said ~85%; it is a 2016 figure, publisher-specific, and should not be presented as a current universal platform metric.
- **Implication (the load-bearing one, independent of the exact %):** a large majority of first-exposure viewing is muted, so **the story must be fully legible with the sound off.** Design consequences:
  - **Carry meaning visually** — expression, body language, action, environment, and *on-screen text/captions* must deliver the story; the voiceover/score is an *enhancement* for the minority who unmute, never the load-bearer.
  - **Caption spoken content** — if a persona "says" something, it must appear as text (this also serves accessibility). [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine, source in 2016 Digiday piece: publishers adapted via captions, text overlays, strong visuals.]
  - **Text as a hook layer** — a bold text hook in seconds 0–2 works when audio doesn't play.
  - **Sound-on payoff** — because ~85% start muted, design a reason to unmute (the score drop, a whispered line) as a *bonus*, not a dependency.
- **Show-not-tell craft [ESTIMATE]:** replace stated emotion with depicted behavior — don't caption "she's confident," show the unhurried walk, the direct gaze (reduced blink), the relaxed shoulders. This dovetails with §4: express through AU-level mechanics and body language, not labels.

## 6. Character-making at story level (luxury-persona devices)

### 6.1 Want vs. need
- [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine, source 14] The **want** is the external, conscious, plot-driving goal; the **need** is the internal, often unconscious lesson the character must learn — want drives plot, need drives the arc. In a persona operation this maps to: the **want** = the visible pursuit (the destination, the acquisition, the achievement shown in each reel); the **need** = the *underlying value the brand stands for* (freedom, self-mastery, taste) revealed across the series. Per-reel you show the want; across posts you reveal the need. Keeps content from being pure flex — the "need" is what makes an audience *bond* rather than just envy.

### 6.2 Status signaling — quiet vs. loud
- [FACT] Han, Nunes & Drèze (2010), "Signaling Status with Luxury Goods: The Role of Brand Prominence," *Journal of Marketing* 74:15–30 — introduces **brand prominence** (how conspicuous the logo/mark is) and a **4-group taxonomy**: **Patricians** (wealthy, low need for status — pay a premium for *quiet* signals only insiders recognize), **Parvenus** (wealthy, high need for status — use *loud*, conspicuous signals to distance from have-nots), **Proletarians** (don't signal), **Poseurs** (aspirational, use counterfeits to mimic parvenus). Empirically, loud/prominently-branded status goods were *on average cheaper* than quiet ones (study of 465 Gucci/LV handbags) (source 15).
- **Application to "classic luxurious" [ESTIMATE grounded in source 15]:** the owner's chosen aesthetic — *classic, luxurious* — is the **Patrician / quiet-luxury** register: understated tailoring, no loud logos, materials-and-craft over branding, "if you know you know." This is a *deliberate* positioning choice with research backing: quiet signals read as *higher* status to in-group audiences. **Avoid** the Parvenu/Poseur register (giant logos, obvious flash) — it reads as lower status and "new money." Wardrobe/design specialists should encode this: texture, cut, and setting over visible branding.

### 6.3 Mystery / aloofness as luxury devices
- [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine] Scarcity of self-disclosure is a status signal — the persona that reveals less reads as more exclusive. Devices: restrained expression (§4.3 — micro not macro), gaze control (looking away, or a cool direct gaze rather than an eager one), *not explaining* (letting the image stand), controlled reveal (the face partially obscured, the back-to-camera contemplative shot). This also directly serves the owner's **famous-people / face-not-revealed** idea and the **quiet-luxury** register — aloofness and non-revelation are the same visual grammar. (Legal boundary on celebrity-adjacency lives in the media-manager/legal research file, not here.)

## 7. Voice delivery basics — the "phonics" clause

- [UNKNOWN — artifact] The owner's word **"phonics"** is a speech-to-text artifact under a story-levels list ("phonics, expressions, emotions, how they express…"). Most probable intended meanings: **(a) phonetics / voice delivery** for voiceover, or **(b) "phonics" as a mis-transcription of a delivery concept.** Covered here as **voice-delivery basics** for voiceover reels; flagged as an interpretation, not a certainty.
- **Pace [FACT — data]:** average conversational English is ~**150 wpm** (National Center for Voice and Speech, via source 16); most adults sit **120–150 wpm** in conversation; **audiobook/podcast narration runs ~150–160 wpm** (rehearsed, clearly enunciated) (source 16). For a short reel voiceover, aiming for the ~140–160 wpm band keeps it natural but crisp; slower (<130) reads as ponderous, faster (>170) as rushed. [ESTIMATE for the reel-specific band; the WPM anchors are FACT.]
- **Pitch [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine]:** vary pitch to avoid monotone; a slight downward inflection at phrase ends reads as *authoritative/confident* (statement), rising ends read as *uncertain/questioning*. For a luxury-authority persona, favor grounded, lower-register, downward-resolving delivery.
- **Pause [ESTIMATE / craft doctrine]:** strategic silence before a key word or the payoff creates emphasis and lets a muted-to-unmuted viewer catch up; pauses also let the visual breathe. In a stitched reel, align pauses with cut points.
- **Practical linkage:** voiceover is generated/handled via the Higgsfield audio + voice tools (see the audio/soundtrack research file and the MCP-surface enumeration for `create_voice`, `generate_audio`, `dubbing`). This section supplies the *delivery direction* those tools should be prompted with; it does not enumerate the tools.

## 8. Story-level series design

The account is the real narrative unit; individual reels are beats. [All §8 is ESTIMATE / craft doctrine unless a source is attached.]

### 8.1 Recurring formats (repeatable templates)
Design a small set of named formats the personas cycle through — recurring formats build viewer expectation and lower production cost:
- **"Come with me" (POV arrival):** first-person travel/luxury arrival, awe payoff (leverages §3.1 awe + §3.3 aspiration proximity).
- **Transformation / reveal:** before→after outfit, plain→destination, using the escalation-then-payoff shape (§1.2).
- **Quote-over-cinematic (motivational):** the owner's stated pillar — a motivational line as text over a cinematic luxury clip; carry meaning in silent autoplay (§5) with the quote as the text-hook.
- **"A day / a place":** episodic slice-of-life in the persona's world, mystery/aloofness maintained (§6.3).
- **Dual-persona interplay:** the male and female personas appear in complementary or paired formats — a recurring relational thread the audience follows.

### 8.2 Episodic hooks & open loops
- Use **open-loop endings** (§1.3, §3.2 curiosity gap) to create "to be continued" threads across posts — a numbered series ("Part 1/…", "Location 3 of 7") gives an explicit reason to follow and return, converting single-view curiosity into subscription.

### 8.3 Storyline across posts (the arc migrates up)
- Per §2.3 and §6.1: spread the hero's-journey arc and the want→need revelation **across the feed**. Early posts establish the ordinary/aspirational world and the *want*; a mid-series turn; the *need* (the value the persona stands for) surfaces over time. This gives the account a spine beyond disconnected flexes and rewards long-term followers.
- **Consistency as narrative glue:** the owner's hard requirements — consistent character (face/identity), consistent wardrobe capsule, consistent classic-luxurious genre — are what let disconnected reels read as *one ongoing story*. Recurring visual signatures (a color grade, a location type, a recurring object/wardrobe piece) function as the series' "brand," reinforcing the arc. (Consistency *mechanics* are the character/wardrobe research file's domain; here it is the *narrative* function.)

### 8.4 Cadence note
- Posting cadence, timing, and platform algorithm signals (Mosseri's watch-time / likes-per-reach / **sends-per-reach** ranking factors, source 8) are the **media-manager research file's** domain, not this one. Flagged only so the story specialist hands off cleanly: *story design should optimize for **sends/shares*** (the reach driver) — which loops back to §3.1 (awe/high-arousal is the most-shared emotional register). Story and distribution agree: make it awe-inducing and shareable.
