## 1. Zimmer-style scoring techniques (from public sources)

### 1.1 Theme simplicity — "the barest minimum"
- [FACT] In his MasterClass chapters on themes, Zimmer teaches that **simplicity is his primary tool for a memorable theme** — a tune of very few notes "played with a full heart"; for the Batman theme he says a composer "can get away with the barest minimum of harmonic and tune," famously reducing Batman to a **two-note device** (D–F rising minor third in published analyses of the Dark Knight scores — the two-note core is stated in the MasterClass chapter itself). Sources [1][2].
- [FACT] Zimmer frames themes as **conversation**: question → answer, phrase → pause; the theme should pose and resolve questions rather than merely decorate. Source [1] (MasterClass "Themes" chapter, via published summaries [3]).
- [ESTIMATE — secondhand attribution, high confidence] "Time" (Inception) is the canonical example of Zimmer minimalism: one short chord progression repeated with additive layering and a single long crescendo. This structure is audible and universally described in published analyses [4], but the "additive layering as deliberate design" framing is analyst inference, not a Zimmer quote we fetched.

### 1.2 Layered ostinato build
- [FACT] Zimmer's signature drive device is **layered ostinatos** — short repeating rhythmic figures stacked in layers, each recorded/programmed separately for rhythmic precision, creating "ever-building excitement" (his Batman MasterClass chapter describes recording ostinato patterns separately for precise rhythm). Sources [1][2][3].
- Practical translation for generated music: build energy by **adding layers on a loop** (strings 16ths → add low brass pulse → add percussion → add high tremolo), not by changing the melody. [ESTIMATE — craft translation of the sourced technique, high confidence]

### 1.3 The "braaam" — and its honest attribution
- [FACT] The bass-heavy brass blast associated with Inception appears in Zimmer's "Dream Is Collapsing" — analyses describe "bass-heavy motifs featuring staccato rhythms and throaty brass chords" built outward from the Édith Piaf "Non, je ne regrette rien" motif (the score's structural conceit). Source [5].
- [FACT] Attribution is genuinely contested/shared: the Inception **trailer** was scored by Zack Hemsey's "Mind Heist," which heavily shaped the "BWAAAAMM"-centric trailer-sound era; the film score braaam is Zimmer's. Both claims appear in published retrospectives. Source [6]. Do not credit the braaam solely to Zimmer.
- Recipe (published-analysis consensus, [ESTIMATE — high confidence]): low brass cluster (trombones/horns) + sub-octave synth doubling + slow attack + long reverb tail; used as a **punctuation hit**, not a texture.

### 1.4 Shepard tone — the Dunkirk infinite-rise illusion
- [FACT] Zimmer built the Dunkirk score around the **Shepard tone**: overlapping octave-spaced tones where the top fades out as the bottom fades in, so the ear hears an endless rise — "the idea of eternity and endlessness… playing a trick on time itself" (Zimmer). Nolan explicitly asked for "no emotional music… all suspense and tension." Sources [7][8][9].
- [FACT] Zimmer on quiet tension in the same interviews: "There was tension in NOT making a loud noise… the endlessness of a note that just bends and slithers around, and you never know when it's going to strike." Source [9].
- Reel translation: a Shepard-style riser under a 3–6 s clip stack makes the whole stitched sequence feel like one continuous escalation even across hard cuts. [ESTIMATE — craft inference from the sourced technique]

### 1.5 Hybrid orchestral–synth palette and low-end weight
- [FACT] For Dune (2021/2024) Zimmer used **no traditional orchestra at all**: an invented "orchestra" of heavily processed acoustic sources (electric cellos/guitars standing in for brass), expressive MPE synths (Expressive E Osmose / Haken ContinuuM, EaganMatrix engine — Zimmer: keyboardists want violinist-style vibrato), and physical resonators re-amping synth sounds so electronic and acoustic blur. Sources [10][11][12][13].
- [FACT] Zimmer's studios (Remote Control Santa Monica, London) house large modular synth walls, though software instruments dominate under film-editing time pressure. Source [11].
- [FACT] Instrument-as-metaphor: for Interstellar, Nolan suggested the pipe organ; Zimmer embraced it as "both mournful and spiritual… entering a church of the universe," noting a big pipe organ "looks like a rocket"; recorded at Temple Church, London with organist Roger Sayer, chosen for acoustics and silence. Sources [14][15].
- Working rule extracted: **pick one signature sound per project that IS the world** (organ = space-cathedral; processed cello = alien desert). For a classic-luxury persona brand: one signature instrument (e.g., felt piano, or a specific analog synth pad) recurring across every reel = sonic brand identity. [ESTIMATE — design inference from sourced practice, high confidence]
- Low-end weight: the sub-bass emphasis in modern Zimmer-school scores (Inception/Dune braaams, drone floors) is documented by analysts [5][10]; a numeric "Zimmer low-end EQ curve" does not exist in any fetched source — [UNKNOWN]; treat any specific Hz figures as engineering convention, not Zimmer doctrine.

## 2. Scene-mood scoring conventions (craft grammar — tagged honestly)

Most of what follows is **craft convention** taught in film-music practice, not measured fact. Where a real study exists it is tagged [FACT].

### 2.1 Tension / intense
- Devices [ESTIMATE — standard craft convention, high confidence across published pedagogy]: sustained low **drones**; **rising semitone** figures (minor-second oscillation); accelerating ostinati; string tremolo/sul ponticello; dynamic terracing (each phrase slightly louder); **silence before the hit** — cutting all sound for 0.5–1 s before an impact multiplies its perceived force.
- [FACT] The quiet-tension principle has a direct Zimmer quote behind it (Dunkirk interview, §1.4) [9].

### 2.2 Suspense
- Devices [ESTIMATE — craft convention]: steady **pulse/ticking** (clock-like percussion, muted-string spiccato at 60–90 BPM), harmonic stasis (one unresolved chord held), Shepard risers, high pedal tones with sparse low interjections, delayed downbeats.
- [FACT] Dunkirk literally used ticking (a recording of Nolan's own watch is widely reported in the making-of coverage of the score) — the ticking-pulse-as-suspense device is documented for this score. Source [8]. [ESTIMATE on the watch detail specifically — reported in secondary coverage we searched but not fetched verbatim; treat the "ticking pulse" as the load-bearing fact.]

### 2.3 Horror
- [FACT] A peer-reviewed basis exists: Blumstein, Davitian & Kaye (Biology Letters, 2010) scored soundtracks of top-ranked horror/drama/action/war films and found **horror films contain more nonlinear acoustic features — noisy screams and non-musical sidebands — than expected**, analogues of distressed animal vocalizations; drama suppressed noise and used abrupt frequency transitions instead. I.e., horror cues exploit evolved alarm responses to harsh, unpredictable, distorted sound. Sources [16][17].
- Devices [ESTIMATE — craft convention]: dissonant string clusters and glissandi (the Psycho-shriek lineage), detuned/inharmonic textures, extreme register contrast (very low + very high, empty middle), stingers after silence, reversed audio tails, whispered/breath textures.
- Infrasound (<20 Hz inducing unease) is frequently claimed for horror; the fetched sources here do not substantiate it — [UNKNOWN — popular claim, no source fetched; do not state as fact in KBs].
- For this luxury vertical, "horror" mood = brief dark-tension accents (a night-drive reveal, a storm over a villa), not genre horror. [ESTIMATE — brief-fit interpretation]

### 2.4 Luxury / aspirational calm
- Devices [ESTIMATE — craft/industry convention, medium-high confidence; no measured "luxury sound" study fetched]: **warm analog-style pads**; **minimalist felt piano** (sparse, rubato, close-miked); slow harmonic rhythm (1 chord per 2–4 bars); jazz-voiced extensions (maj7/9) for "expensive" warmth; **deep/organic house at tasteful tempo** — house as a genre conventionally sits ~118–126 BPM; "luxury edit" practice favors the lower, unhurried end with sub-bass round rather than aggressive; solo cello or muted trumpet for old-money classicism.
- Two luxury sub-moods for the two personas [ESTIMATE — design proposal]: *heritage-classic* (piano/strings/vinyl-warm, no drums) vs *modern-sleek* (deep house pulse, side-chained pads, filtered build). Both must stay **unhurried** — perceived luxury correlates with slowness and restraint in craft practice, but this is convention, not a measured finding [ESTIMATE — low-medium evidentiary weight].

## 3. Sound-design layers for stitched reels (3–6 s clips)

Standard post-production layer stack, adapted to reels [ESTIMATE — craft convention throughout, high confidence; layer taxonomy is standard film-audio practice]:

1. **Ambience bed** (continuous, -25 to -35 dB under music): one bed per scene world — coastal wind + gulls (Amalfi terrace), room tone + fireplace (library), distant traffic + reverberant chatter (Monaco street). The bed runs UNDER the cuts to glue 3–6 s clips into one place; changing the bed = changing the scene.
2. **Spot effects** (synced, foreground): the luxury-signature sounds — engine note on throttle blip, champagne cork + pour, footsteps on marble, silk/fabric movement, watch clasp, car door thunk (a heavy, damped thunk reads as expensive — convention), espresso cup on saucer, jet spool-up. Spot FX carry realism: the owner's #1 "must not look AI" rule extends to sound — clean, physically plausible sync sound is a large share of perceived realism. [ESTIMATE — craft consensus]
3. **Transitions synced to cuts**: **risers** (white-noise or Shepard sweep, 0.5–1.5 s into the cut), **whooshes** (on whip-pans/speed ramps), **sub drops** (pitch-dive + silence gap landing ON the cut frame — the standard reel impact grammar), reverse-cymbal swells into beat drops. Rule: transition SFX land exactly on the cut frame; the music downbeat lands with the new shot. [ESTIMATE — editing craft convention]
4. **Music** as covered in §1–2, ducked (side-chained) under any voice.
5. **Voice** (if used: motivational-quote VO): recorded/generated dry, de-essed, compressed, sits on top; music ducks 3–6 dB under it. [ESTIMATE — mixing convention]

Beat-sync note: cutting clips ON musical downbeats (or on the half-bar) is the core stitched-reel grammar; at 120 BPM a bar = 2.0 s, so 3–6 s clips = 1.5–3 bars — choosing track BPM so that bar length divides the clip lengths makes cuts feel intentional. [ESTIMATE — arithmetic + craft convention]

## 4. 3D / spatial audio reality check for Instagram

The owner asked for "3D sound." Honest platform reality:

- [FACT — Meta policy/help surface exists but full spec pages were not directly fetchable] Instagram's help pages document WHICH audio you may use in reels (music library, original audio, etc.) [18][19]; Meta publishes **no creator-facing spatial-audio or Dolby Atmos support for Reels**, and no loudness-normalization spec. Attempts to fetch Instagram Engineering's own post "Improving Instagram's Music Audio Quality" were **403-blocked by the egress proxy (and web.archive.org is blocked too)** — so pipeline details below are downgraded:
- [ESTIMATE — from multiple secondary technical sources, medium-high confidence] Instagram transcodes uploads to **AAC-LC, ~128 kbps stereo** for the default tier with lower mono variants (~64 kbps) on poor connections; recommended upload is AAC 256–320 kbps, 48 kHz, stereo, in an MP4/MOV container [20][21].
- [ESTIMATE — medium confidence] Loudness: Instagram has **no published normalization target**; community/vendor mastering guidance converges on **≈ -14 LUFS integrated, -1 dBTP true peak** (same as YouTube/TikTok practice) to avoid platform gain-riding and transcode clipping [21]. Treat -14 LUFS / -1 dBTP as the working master spec, tagged as convention.
- [FACT — observable playback reality] Reels play through phone speakers by default; many phone speakers are effectively mono or have negligible stereo separation, and platform re-encodes can downmix — so **any mix must survive a mono fold-down** (check for phase cancellation; keep bass and lead elements centered) [21][22].
- What "3D" CAN honestly mean here: (a) **binaural rendering baked into the stereo file** (HRTF panning of ambience/spot FX) — survives upload because it is just stereo; delivers real spatial impression **on headphones only**, collapses gracefully to mono on speakers; (b) **pseudo-3D width** (mid/side widening on pads/ambience, kept off the low end). There is no true object-based/Atmos delivery lane on Reels. [ESTIMATE — (a)/(b) are standard audio engineering; the "no Atmos on Reels" is absence-of-evidence from official surfaces, medium-high confidence]
- KB rule to carry: design 3D as *headphone bonus, mono-safe baseline*. Never let a spatial trick carry load-bearing information.

## 5. Music sourcing & licensing for IG (policy facts)

- [FACT] **Meta Sound Collection** (facebook.com/sound/collection): royalty-free tracks + sound effects; Meta's terms grant a **non-exclusive royalty-free license for commercial or non-commercial use, but ONLY within Meta products** (Facebook/Instagram) — content may not be used separately from Meta Company Products, i.e., the same track cannot be reused on TikTok/YouTube under this license. Sources [23][24].
- [FACT] Instagram's **licensed music library** (popular commercial music) has restricted availability for **business accounts**; Meta's help page governs access, and commercial-music use in ads/branded content is restricted — business/creator use should default to Sound Collection or independently licensed music. Sources [18][25].
- [ESTIMATE — high confidence from vendor + secondary coverage] Third-party subscription libraries (Epidemic Sound et al.) license tracks for social use across platforms and are the standard cross-platform solution for commercial creator accounts [24].
- [FACT] **AI-generated music rights (US)**: the US Copyright Office's Part 2 report (Jan 2025) holds that purely AI-generated works are **not copyrightable** and **prompts alone are not authorship**; human-authored contributions (arrangement, editing, selection, added performance) can be registered with AI portions disclaimed. Source [26].
- [FACT] **Suno/Udio litigation**: RIAA-backed label suits filed June 2024 [27]; by late 2025 Warner settled with Suno and Universal with Udio (moves toward licensed models), other claims still active, fair-use ruling anticipated 2026 [28]. Paid Suno plans grant commercial-use terms, but that is a contract license, not copyright ownership [29].
- [FACT] **ElevenLabs Music** positions itself as trained exclusively on licensed data with commercial clearance for outputs on paid plans (an extra license tier applies for advertising/film/enterprise); its sound-effects generations on paid plans are cleared for commercial use. Sources [30][31].
- Copyright-strike risk ranking for this operation [ESTIMATE — synthesis of the above, high confidence]: (1) safest = Meta Sound Collection (on-Meta only) and paid cross-platform libraries; (2) safe-with-contract = ElevenLabs/licensed-data AI tools on paid tier; (3) medium = Suno/Udio paid tiers (license valid but litigation-era models; platform Content-ID style matching can still flag soundalikes); (4) prohibited = commercial catalog music on a business account outside the library terms — mutes, takedowns, repeat-strike account risk.
- [UNKNOWN] Whether audio generated via the Higgsfield MCP `generate_audio` surface carries explicit commercial-use terms for third-party platform posting — must be read off Higgsfield's ToS/the MCP surface file; not asserted here.

## 7. Scene-to-sound recipe cards (per-mood template)

Template fields: **Tempo/feel · Palette · Structure over ~15–30 s reel · Layer stack · Transition grammar · Prompt-style brief**. All cards are [ESTIMATE — design synthesis of §1–3 sourced techniques + craft convention].

### Card A — INTENSE (supercar run, cliff-road drive, gym peak set)
- Tempo: 120–140 BPM driving; feel = layered ostinato build (§1.2).
- Palette: low staccato strings + sub-synth pulse + taiko/hybrid percussion + braaam-style brass punctuation (§1.3).
- Structure: bar-loop ostinato, +1 layer every 2 bars, 0.5 s silence, braaam hit on final shot.
- SFX: engine notes as pitched musical elements; whoosh on every whip-pan; sub drop on last cut.
- Brief: "dark hybrid-orchestral trailer cue, relentless string ostinato building in layers, deep sub pulses, one massive low brass hit at the climax, modern cinematic, no melody, 125 BPM."

### Card B — SUSPENSE (reveal tease: covered car, unopened case, door opening)
- Tempo: 60–90 BPM tick; feel = pulse + stasis (§2.2).
- Palette: ticking percussive pulse, high string pedal, sparse low piano notes, Shepard riser last 2 bars (§1.4).
- Structure: static unresolved harmony; riser into the reveal; resolve chord ONLY on the reveal frame.
- SFX: near-silence discipline — one or two hyper-real spot sounds (clasp click, fabric slide) against the pulse.
- Brief: "minimal suspense underscore, ticking clock pulse, sustained high strings, no melody, continuously rising tension, single warm resolution chord at the end."

### Card C — DARK ACCENT / "HORROR" TINGE (storm over villa, night city, blackout)
- Palette: detuned drone, non-musical sideband textures / harsh noisy accents (nonlinear-sound principle, §2.3 [FACT-based]), reversed tails, extreme low+high register with empty mids.
- Structure: drone floor → stinger after silence → cut to calm (contrast sells both moods).
- Keep ≤ 30% of a reel; this vertical uses darkness as seasoning, not genre.
- Brief: "dark ambient tension bed, dissonant detuned strings, distant metallic groans, harsh noisy swell into a hard cut to silence."

### Card D — LUXURY-CALM, HERITAGE (old-money: library, tailor, vineyard, yacht at dawn)
- Tempo: rubato or 60–80 BPM; no drums.
- Palette: felt piano, warm string pad, maj7/9 harmony, vinyl-warm saturation, solo cello line (§2.4).
- Structure: one 4-chord cycle, melody enters at reel midpoint, ends unresolved-soft (loopable).
- SFX: marble footsteps, page turns, pour sounds, fireplace bed — spot FX at near-music level (they ARE the luxury).
- Brief: "intimate felt piano and warm strings, slow, elegant, understated, expensive-feeling minimalism, soft room ambience, no percussion."

### Card E — LUXURY-SLEEK, MODERN (rooftop pool, night drive arrival, fashion walk)
- Tempo: 118–122 BPM deep/organic house, restrained (§2.4).
- Palette: round sub bass, side-chained warm pads, filtered chord stabs, sparse percussion, air/vocal-chop garnish.
- Structure: filtered intro (2 bars) → beat lands with persona's entrance shot → 8-bar groove → filter-close ending.
- SFX: champagne pour and heel clicks placed IN the groove grid (quantized to the beat).
- Brief: "deep house, 120 BPM, luxurious late-night rooftop mood, warm analog pads, deep round bass, minimal and tasteful, no vocals, smooth confident groove."

### Card F — MOTIVATIONAL-QUOTE VO REEL
- Music: Card D or E bed at -18 to -12 dB under VO, side-chain ducked; swell in the final 3 s after the last word.
- VO: dry, close, slow pace; the score answers the quote (question→answer grammar, §1.1).
- End: sub-drop-to-logo or piano resolution on the CTA frame.

**Master-spec footer for every card** [ESTIMATE — §4 basis]: deliver 48 kHz stereo AAC ≥256 kbps in the video container, master ≈ -14 LUFS int / -1 dBTP, mono-fold-down checked, bass centered, binaural/width tricks as headphone bonus only.
