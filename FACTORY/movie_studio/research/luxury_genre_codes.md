# Luxury Genre Codes — the CLASSIC LUXURIOUS genre bible (L1 research)

Scope: the locked genre for the two-persona (one male, one female) Instagram movie/reel operation — visual codes of classic/quiet luxury, location library, car canon, scenario bank, styling, luxury-breaking tells, fitness-accent rules, and seasonal/time-of-day rotation. Every other specialist (prompting, lighting, wardrobe, media manager, QC) obeys this file. Honesty tags: [FACT] = sourced below; [FACT-lite] = uncontroversial public geography/common knowledge stated conservatively; [ESTIMATE] = craft synthesis from the sourced codes, basis+confidence stated; [UNKNOWN] = named blocker.

## 1. The genre lock: CLASSIC LUXURIOUS = the quiet-luxury register

- [FACT] "Quiet luxury" (synonyms: stealth wealth, old money aesthetic, silent luxury) is a recognized aesthetic defined by understated design, high-quality materials, timeless styling, muted colours, craftsmanship, and the deliberate avoidance of conspicuous logos and flashy status markers (Wikipedia, "Quiet luxury"; sources 1–3).
- [FACT] The register was mass-popularized by HBO's *Succession* (unbranded expensive clothing — e.g. Loro Piana caps, Tom Ford suede — read as wealth only to a trained eye); "old money" and "stealth wealth" trended massively on TikTok in 2023 (Hypebeast, Fortune; sources 2–3).
- [FACT] Canonical quiet-luxury fashion houses (the reference "look" even though we never show their logos): Loro Piana, Brunello Cucinelli, Zegna, Hermès, Brioni, The Row, Bottega Veneta, Celine (Philo era), Valextra (source 1). Loro Piana's signature move: a ~$5,000 cashmere blazer with no visible branding beyond an interior label (source 4).
- [FACT] The theoretical backbone is Kapferer & Bastien, *The Luxury Strategy* (Kogan Page): luxury inverts normal marketing — scarcity over reach, no discounting, "it is not the price that makes luxury, but luxury that makes the price"; luxury communicates identity and heritage, not features (sources 5–6). Genre implication: our content sells a *world*, never a product pitch.
- [FACT] Trend caution: by Jan-2024 fashion press called peak "quiet luxury" outdated as runway styles regained flair (source 1). Read for us: we adopt quiet luxury as a *timeless visual grammar* (old-money codes predate and outlive the TikTok trend), not as a trend-chasing label. [ESTIMATE — medium-high confidence; basis: old-money aesthetic's persistence across decades per sources 1, 3, 12.]
- Operating definition for all specialists: **classic luxurious = quiet luxury visual grammar + cinematic execution.** Loud-luxury elements (logo walls, hypercar flash, champagne-spray, cash/watch-flex poses) are OUT of genre except as deliberate rare contrast beats (see §7).

## 2. Visual codes: quiet vs loud luxury

### 2a. Materials (what surfaces appear on camera)
- [FACT] Quiet-luxury material canon: fine natural fibers (cashmere, vicuña, baby cashmere, linen, silk, fine merino), full-grain leather, and craftsmanship-forward finishes (sources 1, 4). 
- [FACT] Interior/set materials that read high-end in luxury design coverage: marble, aged/dark woods (mahogany-class), terracotta, hand-painted ceramic tile, wrought iron, brass, natural stone, hand-poured terrazzo, hinoki (Japanese cypress), tatami, washi/shoji paper screens (sources 13, 16, 17).
- [ESTIMATE — high confidence, basis: the sourced canon above] The generator-facing materials whitelist: marble (honed > polished), mahogany/walnut, linen, full-grain leather, cashmere/wool, unlacquered brass, crystal, bone china, natural stone, aged hardwood floors, silk. Blacklist: visible plastic, chrome-everything, acrylic furniture, glossy laminate, synthetic satin sheen.
- Loud-luxury contrast: [FACT] loud luxury = vibrant palettes, eye-catching patterns, prominently displayed branding, statement/sequin pieces designed to be the centerpiece (source 7). We do not shoot this register.

### 2b. Color palettes
- [FACT] Luxury palettes rely on deep anchors (black, charcoal, navy), warm neutrals (ivory, champagne, beige, cream), and one restrained metallic accent (gold/brass); restraint itself is the luxury signal — "fewer colors equal higher perceived value" (sources 8–9).
- [FACT] Three combinations repeatedly cited as premium in branding-design literature: black + gold + ivory; navy + platinum/silver + cream; forest/deep green + gold + black (the "Rolex palette") (source 9). Deep burgundy also recurs in premium identities (source 9).
- [FACT] Quiet luxury specifically favors beige, gray, navy, black, and muted greens (sage/olive family) (sources 7–8).
- [ESTIMATE — high confidence, basis above] Locked grade palette for the operation: warm neutrals (cream/sand/ivory) + one deep anchor per scene (navy, forest green, charcoal, or burgundy) + gold/brass accents ≤5% of frame. Saturation kept low-to-moderate; skin tones natural; whites slightly warm, never clipped.

### 2c. Typography & composition (for posters, covers, quote cards)
- [FACT] Luxury branding leans on classical serif typefaces (Caslon/Baskerville lineage) evoking timeless sophistication; Monotype's research (as reported in its own resource pages) links this typography style to higher perceived quality (+13%) and reliability (+9%) — figures from Monotype's published summary, not independently verified (source 10). Refined geometric or humanist sans-serifs are the accepted alternative (source 10).
- [FACT] Luxury layout discipline: generous white space, 2–3 tones max, minimal elements; gradients and ornament-cluttered logos read cheap (sources 9, 11). Cluttered feeds and "design noise" are named luxury-branding mistakes (source 18).
- [ESTIMATE — high confidence, basis above] Text rules for our covers/quote cards: one serif family (Didone or old-style), letter-spaced small caps for kickers, no drop shadows, no gradients, no more than 2 type sizes per card, text occupying <20% of frame, cream-on-charcoal or charcoal-on-cream.
- [ESTIMATE — medium-high, basis: luxury advertising conventions in sources 9, 11, 18] Composition conventions: symmetry or strong single-subject placement, deep negative space, low prop count (3–5 significant objects max), shallow depth of field on details, wide establishing frames on places.

## 4. CAR canon

### 4a. Classic-luxurious tier (in-genre core)
- **Rolls-Royce (esp. classic Silver Cloud/Corniche era and modern Ghost/Phantom in muted colors)** — [FACT] the Spirit of Ecstasy mascot (Charles Sykes, 1911; "flying lady" leaning into the wind, gown billowing) is the brand's iconic ornament and a global symbol of luxury (sources 19–20). [FACT-lite] Signature exterior details: upright Pantheon-style grille, long bonnet, rear-hinged coach doors on modern cars; interiors: lambswool carpet, book-matched veneer, thin-rim wheel.
- **Bentley (Continental, Flying Spur, classic R-Type/S-series)** — [FACT-lite] matrix/mesh grille, winged-B badge, quilted leather and knurled metal interior details; reads gentleman-driver rather than show-off.
- **Vintage Porsche 911 (air-cooled, esp. 1960s–80s)** — [FACT-lite] unbroken fastback roofline, round headlamps, houndstooth or plain leather seats, Fuchs wheels; the understated connoisseur sports car. [ESTIMATE — high, basis: quiet-luxury press repeatedly frames vintage-analog objects as connoisseur signals (sources 1–3)].
- **Mercedes SL W113 "Pagoda" (230/250/280 SL, 1963–71)** — [FACT] designed by Paul Bracq/Béla Barényi; patented slightly concave hardtop gave the "Pagoda" nickname; aluminium bonnet/boot/door skins; widely treated as a timeless-design collectible (sources 21–22). [FACT-lite] Signature: vertical headlamp clusters, chrome bumpers, big thin steering wheel, ivory-paint-with-red-leather or silver-with-navy combos.
- **Ferrari 250-era classics (250 GT Lusso, 250 GT California Spyder class)** — [FACT] Pininfarina-designed / Scaglietti-built Lusso is widely regarded among the most beautiful cars ever made: long hood, fastback, delicate chrome, oval grille (source 23); the 250 GTO set the classic-car value record (~$70M private sale, 2018) (source 24). These read heritage-elegance, not flash.
- [ESTIMATE — medium-high, basis: same design-era logic] Acceptable supporting cast: Jaguar E-Type, Aston Martin DB5/DB6, Land Rover Defender/Series (estate context), Mercedes G-Class in matte/dark (modern, borderline).

### 4b. Flash tier (OUT of genre except rare contrast beats)
- [ESTIMATE — high confidence, basis: loud-luxury definition in §2a and quiet-luxury press] Modern hypercars and wrap-culture cars (Lamborghini in lime/orange, chrome wraps, gold Bugattis, widebody kits), rev-bombing exhaust content, dealership-flex framing. These photograph as "new money loud" and break the classic read.
- Modern Ferrari/Lamborghini in dark solid colors *can* pass in a Monaco-night beat, but default is no. [ESTIMATE — medium.]

### 4c. On-camera treatment + trademark caution
- [ESTIMATE — high, basis §2 codes] Shoot cars as *objects of craft*: detail close-ups (grille texture, hood ornament, gauge needles, leather stitching, wood wheel rim), slow orbital or tracking at golden hour, coastal/alpine roads, never parking-lot flex poses. Colors: silver, ivory, navy, British racing green, burgundy — no wraps.
- **Trademark caution [cross-reference]:** car grilles, badges, mascots (e.g., Spirit of Ecstasy) and model trade dress are protected marks; whether AI-generated commercial content may depict them, and how prominently, is a legal question answered in the likeness/rights research file — NOT here. Default operating posture until that file says otherwise: badges de-emphasized/out of focus, no brand names in captions implying endorsement. [ESTIMATE — high that this is the conservative lane; law pinned elsewhere.]

## 6. Styling: watches, accessories, table settings

- **Watches** [FACT-lite brand knowledge, conservative]: the classic-luxurious wrist = thin dress watches and heritage sports pieces — Patek Philippe Calatrava class, Cartier Tank class, Jaeger-LeCoultre Reverso class, vintage Rolex Datejust/Daytona class — on leather straps or subtle bracelets; no iced/gem-set pieces (gem-set = loud tier per §2a codes [ESTIMATE — high]). On camera: macro of dial/strap, never wrist-to-lens flex pose. Trademark treatment same as cars (§4c cross-reference).
- **Male accessories** [ESTIMATE — high, basis sources 1, 3, 12]: unbranded full-grain leather holdall/briefcase, silk knit tie, plain cufflinks, tortoiseshell glasses, suede loafers, navy/camel overcoat. No visible monograms.
- **Female accessories** [ESTIMATE — high, same basis]: structured unbranded leather bag (Valextra/Row silhouette class), silk scarf, pearl or small gold jewelry, cat-eye or oversized-but-plain sunglasses, slingbacks/ballet flats; hair polished but soft.
- **Table settings** [ESTIMATE — high, basis §2 material codes]: pressed white/cream linen, bone china (plain or thin gold rim), unlabeled decanters instead of bottle labels, polished (not ornate) silverware, low single-stem or no florals, real candles. Never: branded champagne labels to camera, plastic anything, over-styled "influencer brunch" clutter.
- **Grooming** [ESTIMATE — medium-high]: M — clean tailored grooming, matte skin, no shine jewelry; F — neutral manicure, skin-first makeup, low-shine finish. Both read "maintained, not decorated."

## 7. What BREAKS the luxury read (gauche tells → feeds realism/luxury QC)

Grounded tells ([FACT] where sourced; the rest [ESTIMATE — high, direct inversions of §2 codes]):
1. [FACT] Logo spam / prominent branding — the defining loud-luxury marker; quiet luxury hides branding (sources 1, 7).
2. [FACT] Oversaturated, too-bright color — reads cheap/commercial and off-register for high-end photography (source 25).
3. [FACT] Clutter / design noise / no negative space — a named luxury-branding failure (sources 18, 25).
4. [FACT] Harsh direct light too close, unfilled hard shadows, mixed color temperatures — "cheap light"; controlled soft purposeful light is the high-end marker (source 25).
5. [FACT] Gradient-heavy, ornament-stuffed graphics/logos on cards and covers (sources 9, 11).
6. [ESTIMATE — high] Plastic/synthetic surfaces in frame (acrylic chairs, PVC, glossy laminate, polyester sheen) — violates §2a material canon.
7. [ESTIMATE — high] Flex grammar: cash fans, bottle-service sparklers, watch-to-lens poses, "look at my car badge" framing, private-jet-door selfie cliché.
8. [ESTIMATE — high] Too-new everything: showroom-condition props with zero patina read staged; wabi-sabi patina reads owned (§3 Kyoto entry, source 17).
9. [ESTIMATE — high] Overcrowded tablescapes and prop-stuffed "luxury flat lays."
10. [ESTIMATE — high] Caption/typography tells: ALL-CAPS hype, emoji strings, more than two typefaces, drop shadows.
11. [ESTIMATE — medium-high] Wrong casting of place: Dubai-mall glitz framing, Vegas casino carpet, rented-yacht party density — loud registers even in expensive places.
12. [ESTIMATE — high] AI-specific luxury killers to hand the realism-QC specialist: waxy poreless skin against real linen, physically impossible marble veining repeats, warped serif type on signage, gold rendered as yellow plastic, candle flames without ambient warmth on nearby surfaces. ## 8. Fitness-accent integration ("fitness also sometimes")

- [FACT] The old-money sport canon: polo, golf, equestrian, sailing/yachting, tennis, skiing, rowing, hunting — sports with heritage and exclusivity associations; tennis whites and cable-knit are staple crossover garments of the aesthetic (sources 12, 26).
- [ESTIMATE — high, basis above + §2] In-genre fitness looks: tennis in whites on clay/grass, rowing on still morning water, fencing, boxing in an old brick gym, swimming in a lap/hotel pool, alpine skiing, equestrian, dawn runs on estate grounds. Styling: heritage athletic wear (whites, cream cable knit, navy shorts, plain grey sweats), no neon compression wear, no branded gym-bro tanks.
- [ESTIMATE — high] Physique presentation stays classic: capability and grace (form mid-motion, breath in cold air, towel-off silhouette) over pump-and-pose; the psychologist specialist decides attraction emphasis, but the *costume and light* must stay in this register — physique revealed by cut and backlight, not by oiled-skin flex framing.
- Cadence: fitness beats ≈ 1 in 5–7 posts, always shot in the same palette/light grammar so the feed stays one world. [ESTIMATE — medium; basis: owner said "sometimes," exact ratio is an editorial choice for the media-manager specialist.]

## 9. Seasonal & time-of-day rotation logic (content-calendar feed)

**Seasonal location wheel** [FACT-lite seasons; pairing logic ESTIMATE — high]:
- Dec–Mar: St. Moritz/Aspen (snow), Maldives (dry season), Dubai (cool season), Kyoto (winter tea/snow temples).
- Apr–May: Kyoto sakura, Paris, Lake Como opening, estate spring.
- Jun–Sep: Amalfi/Positano, Como, Monaco, yacht block, Mediterranean drives.
- Oct–Nov: Kyoto momiji, Aspen golden aspens, English estate autumn, Paris overcast, tailoring/interior craft block.
- Rule: never post a location visibly out of its season (snowless St. Moritz ski beat in July breaks the "real life" illusion) — feeds the media-manager's calendar and the realism QC. [ESTIMATE — high.]

**Time-of-day grammar** [ESTIMATE — high, basis: §2 light codes + cinematography convention]:
- Dawn (pre-sun): fitness + ritual scenes; cool, quiet, monastic.
- Golden hour AM: terraces, coffee, water scenes; warm low side light.
- Midday: only hard-sun locations that carry it (Amalfi white walls, yacht decks, snow); otherwise interiors with window light.
- Golden hour PM: drives, arrivals, sport wind-down — the genre's signature slot.
- Blue hour: penthouse, Monaco harbor, table-setting anticipation — deep navy + warm practicals = the palette in §2b, in light form.
- Night: candle/fireplace/lamp scenes only; no flash-lit party grammar.
- Weekly rhythm suggestion for the calendar specialist: anchor the week with 1 place-establishing beat, 2–3 ritual/detail beats, 1 motion (car/boat) beat, 1 evening beat, fitness beat every ~1.5 weeks. [ESTIMATE — medium; editorial starting point, to be tuned by analytics feedback loop.]

**Persona alternation** [ESTIMATE — medium-high]: M and F personas alternate scene *types* (his craft beat ↔ her culture beat) but share the same palette, locations-in-season, and light grammar so both feeds read as one world; joint MF scenes reserved for evening/travel beats (higher narrative value, per micro-story research file — cross-reference).
