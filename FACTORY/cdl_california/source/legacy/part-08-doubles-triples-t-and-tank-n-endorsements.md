# B8 — T endorsement: Doubles/Triples (20 questions, need 16)

## B8.1 Pulling doubles and triples

- **Rollover:** steer gently; go slowly around corners, curves, and ramps. A curve speed safe for a single trailer may be too fast.
- **Crack-the-whip:** rearward amplification — the last trailer is most likely to overturn.
- **Look far ahead** so you can slow or change lanes gradually.
- **Space:** you need more than other vehicles; allow large gaps before entering or crossing traffic; check that the sides are clear before lane changes.
- **Bad conditions:** greater length and more dead axles mean more chance of skids and lost traction.
- **Parking:** doubles and triples cannot practically be backed — plan the route and pull through.
- **Converter dolly ABS:** required on dollies built on/after **March 1, 1998**; the yellow malfunction lamp is on the **left side** of the dolly.
- **Converter dolly (converter gear):** a coupling device with 1 or 2 axles, a fifth wheel, and a tow bar with a pintle eye, used to couple a second semitrailer.
- **Loading order:** the **heavier trailer goes first**, directly behind the tractor; the lighter trailer at the rear.
- **CA:** triples are not legal on California roads, but the test still draws on the federal model handbook.

## B8.2 Coupling doubles

**1. Secure the rear (second) trailer.** If it has no spring brakes: drive close, connect the emergency line, charge the trailer air tank, then disconnect the emergency line — this sets the trailer's emergency brakes if the slack adjusters are in adjustment. Chock the wheels if in any doubt.

**2. Position the dolly in front of the second trailer.** Release the dolly brakes by opening the air tank petcock (or use the dolly parking brake control if it has spring brakes). For a short distance, wheel the dolly into place by hand, in line with the kingpin. Otherwise use the tractor and first trailer:
1. Couple the dolly to the rear of the first trailer.
2. Lock the pintle hook.
3. Raise and secure the dolly support.
4. Pull the dolly as close as possible to the nose of the second trailer.
5. Lower the dolly support.
6. Unhook the dolly.
7. Wheel it into line with the second trailer's kingpin.

**3. Connect the dolly to the front trailer.** Back the first trailer into position in front of the dolly tongue; hook the dolly to the pintle hook and lock it; raise and secure the dolly support.

**4. Connect the dolly to the rear trailer.**
- Confirm the trailer brakes are locked and/or the wheels chocked.
- Check the trailer height: slightly **lower** than the center of the dolly's fifth wheel, so it is raised slightly.
- Back the dolly under the rear trailer.
- Raise the landing gear slightly off the ground.
- **Tug test** against the pin of the second trailer.
- **Visual check:** no space between upper and lower fifth wheel; jaws closed on the kingpin.
- Connect safety chains, air hoses, and light cords.
- **Close** the dolly air tank petcock.
- **Close** the shut-off valves at the rear of the last trailer.
- **Open** the shut-off valves at the rear of the first trailer, and on the dolly if it has them.
- Raise the landing gear completely.
- Charge the trailer brakes (push in the air supply knob) and check for air at the rear of the second trailer by opening the emergency line shut-off. No air means something is wrong and the brakes will not work.

## B8.3 Uncoupling doubles

**Rear trailer:**
1. Park in a straight line on firm, level ground.
2. Apply the parking brakes.
3. Chock the second trailer's wheels if it has no spring brakes.
4. Lower the second trailer's landing gear enough to take some weight off the dolly.
5. Close the shut-off valves at the rear of the first trailer (and on the dolly if fitted).
6. Disconnect the dolly's air and electrical lines and secure them.
7. Release the dolly brakes.
8. Release the dolly's fifth wheel latch.
9. Slowly pull the tractor, first trailer, and dolly forward, drawing the dolly out.

**The dolly:**
1. Lower the dolly's landing gear.
2. Disconnect the safety chains.
3. Apply the dolly's spring brakes or chock its wheels.
4. Release the pintle hook.
5. Pull slowly clear.

**Never unlock the pintle hook while the dolly is still under the rear trailer** — the tow bar can fly up and cause injury, and recoupling becomes very difficult.

**Triples:** couple tractor→first trailer (B6 method), first→second, second→third (doubles method each time). Uncouple by removing the third trailer first, then treat the rest as a double.

## B8.4 Inspection and air checks

**Walk-around additions:** all B6.5 coupling items, plus —

| Item | Correct state |
|---|---|
| Shut-off valves, rear of front trailer(s) | **Open** |
| Shut-off valves, rear of last trailer | **Closed** |
| Dolly air tank drain valve (petcock) | **Closed** |
| Pintle eye / pintle hook | Seated and latched |
| Safety chains | Secured |
| Light cords | Firmly in sockets |
| Dolly spare tire | Secured |
| Air lines | Supported, glad hands properly connected |

**Air brake checks:** the same 4 tests as B6.5 — air reaches all trailers, tractor protection valve closes at 20–45 psi, trailer emergency brakes hold, trailer service brakes work.

# B9 — N endorsement: Tank vehicles (20 questions, need 16)

## B9.1 Definition and inspection

**You need the N endorsement when** the vehicle carries any liquid or gas in a tank (or tanks) permanently or temporarily attached to the vehicle or chassis, each tank rated over **119 gallons**, with a total of **1,000 gallons or more**.

**You don't** for portable tanks totaling under 1,000 gallons, or empty storage tanks not designed for transport that are temporarily attached to a flatbed.

**Leaks:** check under and around the vehicle. **Never haul liquid or gas in a leaking tank** — it is a crime; you will be cited, stopped from driving further, and may be liable for cleanup.

**Other checks:** tank shell and body for dents and leaks; intake, discharge, and cut-off valves in the correct position before loading, unloading, or moving; pipes, connections, and hoses, especially at joints; manhole covers with gaskets in place and closed tight; vents clear and working.

**Special equipment:** vapor recovery kits, grounding and bonding cables, emergency shut-off systems, built-in fire extinguishers — know how to use them.

**Never drive with a valve or manhole cover open.**

## B9.2 Tank hazards

**High center of gravity:** much of the load sits high, so rollover is easy — tankers can roll at the **posted** curve speed. Take highway curves and ramps well below posted.

**Liquid surge:** in a partly filled tank the liquid moves forward and back. When you stop, the wave pushes the truck forward and can shove you into an intersection. Side-to-side surge in a curve can roll the vehicle.

| Tank type | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Bulkheads** | Solid walls dividing the tank into smaller tanks; watch front-to-rear weight distribution when loading |
| **Baffles** | Bulkheads with holes; slow **forward-and-back** surge. They do **not** stop side-to-side surge |
| **Unbaffled (smooth-bore)** | Nothing slows the liquid; very strong forward surge. Used for food products such as milk, because sanitation rules forbid baffles |

**Outage:** never fill a tank completely — liquids expand as they warm, so room must be left. Different liquids expand differently; know your cargo's requirement.

**How much to load depends on:** expansion in transit, the weight (density) of the liquid, and legal weight limits. A full tank of a dense liquid (some acids) can exceed legal limits, so you may only partly fill it.

## B9.3 Safe driving

- Start, slow, stop, and change lanes **smoothly**.
- Control surge: keep steady pressure on the brakes, don't release too soon when stopping, brake far ahead, and increase following distance.
- **Emergency stops:** controlled or stab braking, or normal braking with ABS. Steering quickly while braking can roll the tanker.
- **Curves:** slow before, accelerate slightly through; posted speed may be too high.
- **Stopping distance:** a wet road can **double** it; an empty tanker may take longer to stop than a full one.
- **Skids:** no oversteering, overaccelerating, or overbraking.
- Hauling hazmat in a tank requires the **X** endorsement (H + N).
