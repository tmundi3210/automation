# B10 — H endorsement: Hazardous Materials (30 questions, need 24)

Requires a TSA background check and fingerprints. Required whenever the load must be **placarded**, or contains any amount of **select agents or toxins**.

## B10.1 Purpose and roles

**The three intents of the regulations:** contain the product · communicate the risk · ensure safe drivers and equipment.

| Role | Duties |
|---|---|
| **Shipper** | Decides the proper shipping name, class, ID number, packing group; packages, marks, and labels; prepares and **certifies** shipping papers; supplies placards and emergency response information |
| **Carrier** | Accepts only properly prepared shipments; transports promptly; reports incidents; trains drivers |
| **Driver** | Refuses leaking packages; placards the vehicle; keeps papers in the right place; follows route, parking, and attendance rules; keeps the shipment safe |

## B10.2 The Hazardous Materials Table

Ten columns; the ones tested are:

| Column | Contents |
|---|---|
| 1 | Symbols |
| 2 | Proper shipping name (in regular type; descriptive words in italics are not part of it) |
| 3 | Hazard class or division |
| 4 | Identification number (UN/NA), 4 digits |
| 5 | Packing group — **I = great danger, II = medium, III = minor** (Roman numerals) |
| 6 | Labels required |

**Column 1 symbols:** **+** fixes the name, class, and packing group regardless of other criteria · **A** regulated only by air (unless an RQ) · **W** regulated only by water · **D** proper shipping name for domestic shipments · **I** for international · **G** generic name, so a technical name must be added.

**Hazard classes**

| Class | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explosives | Dynamite, ammunition |
| 2 | Gases (2.1 flammable, 2.2 non-flammable, 2.3 poison gas) | Propane, helium, chlorine |
| 3 | Flammable liquids | Gasoline, acetone |
| 4 | Flammable solids (4.1, 4.2 spontaneously combustible, 4.3 dangerous when wet) | Matches, phosphorus, calcium carbide |
| 5 | Oxidizers (5.1) and organic peroxides (5.2) | Ammonium nitrate |
| 6 | Poisons (6.1) and infectious substances (6.2) | Pesticides, anthrax |
| 7 | Radioactive | Uranium |
| 8 | Corrosives | Battery acid, nitric acid |
| 9 | Miscellaneous | PCBs, molten sulfur |

## B10.3 Shipping papers

**Required entries:** page numbers when multi-page ("Page 1 of 4"); a proper shipping description for each hazmat; total quantity and unit; number and type of packages; an **emergency response telephone number monitored at all times**; the **shipper's signed certification**.

**Proper shipping description order:** proper shipping name → hazard class/division → identification number → packing group. **RQ** appears before or after the description for a reportable quantity.

**Marking hazmat on a mixed paper:** listed first, or highlighted in a contrasting color, or marked with an **X** (or **RQ**) in the HM column.

**Where the papers go:** while driving, within reach with the seat belt on or in the driver's-door holder; when out of the vehicle, on the driver's seat or in the driver's door pocket — so responders find them fast.

**Emergency response information** must accompany the papers (ERG or equivalent), including the material's risks and the actions to take.

## B10.4 Marking, labeling, placarding

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Package marking | Proper shipping name and ID number; consignor/consignee name and address |
| Package label | Diamond, about **4 in.** per side, showing the hazard class |
| Placard | Diamond, **10¾ in. square**, square-on-point, on **all 4 sides**, readable from all 4 directions, class/division number in the bottom corner, at least 3 in. from other markings, clean and secure |
| Bulk package | Over **119 gal** liquid, over **882 lb** solid, or over **1,000 lb** water capacity for gas |
| Bulk ID number display | Black **3.9 in. (100 mm)** numbers on the placard, an orange panel, or a white square-on-point |
| Cargo/portable tank ≥1,000 gal | ID number on **each side and each end** |
| Cargo/portable tank <1,000 gal | ID number on **two opposing sides**; portable tanks also carry the shipping name (2 in. letters ≥1,000 gal, 1 in. <1,000 gal) |

**Placard tables**

- **Table 1 — placard in ANY amount:** 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 explosives; 2.3 poison gas; 4.3 dangerous when wet; 6.1 PG I inhalation hazard; Class 7 (Radioactive Yellow-III).
- **Table 2 — placard at 1,001 lb or more** (total of all Table 2 materials on the vehicle, including packaging).

**DANGEROUS placard:** you may use it instead of separate Table 2 placards when you have **1,001 lb or more of two or more Table 2 hazard classes** requiring **different** placards. But if **2,205 lb (1,000 kg) or more of any one Table 2 class** is loaded **at one place**, that class's specific placard must be used. *(Some older handbook editions print 5,000 lb here; 49 CFR 172.504(b) now reads 1,000 kg / 2,205 lb — learn the CFR figure and note the handbook's.)*

**The driver placards the vehicle.** Never move a placarded vehicle unless the placards are correct and in place.

## B10.5 Loading and unloading

**General:**
- Set the parking brake and chock the wheels before loading or unloading.
- Inspect every package: **never accept a leaking package or container**.
- Brace containers so they cannot slide or fall; keep them right side up.
- **Never use hooks** or handle packages roughly.
- **No smoking, and keep flames away**, within **25 ft** of vehicles carrying Class 1 (explosives), Class 3 (flammable liquids), Class 4 (flammable solids), Class 5 (oxidizers), or placarded cargo tanks with Class 3 or Division 2.1.
- When fueling, the engine is off and someone must be at the nozzle controlling the flow.

**By class:**

| Class | Rules |
|---|---|
| **1 Explosives** | Engine off before loading/unloading; no cargo heaters for 1.1/1.2; non-metallic **floor liner** for 1.1/1.2/1.3; no sharp points in the cargo space; no hooks; no transfer on a public roadway except in an emergency; a **written route plan** is required |
| **2 Gases** | Cylinders upright and braced, or in racks or boxes; a flat floor if no racks |
| **2.1 / 3 Flammables** | Engine off during loading/unloading (run it only to operate a pump); ground a cargo tank before filling through an open hole; no cargo heaters |
| **4.3 Dangerous when wet** | Keep dry; protect from water |
| **6.1 / 2.3 Poisons** | **Never** in the driver's cab or sleeper, and **never with food** for human or animal consumption |
| **7 Radioactive** | Total transport index of all packages must not exceed **50**; keep the required separation from people, animals, and undeveloped film |
| **8 Corrosives** | Load breakable containers one at a time, upright; **no nitric acid above another product**; stack no more than **two high**; never with explosives |

**Segregation:** materials that react with each other may not be loaded together — for example, cyanides with acids or corrosives, and poisons with foodstuffs.

## B10.6 Driving and parking

**Tires:** check placarded vehicles with dual tires at the start of each trip and at each stop. **Stop and check every 2 hours or 100 miles, whichever is less.** Remove a leaking or overheated tire and place it a safe distance away; don't drive on it.

**Routes:** whenever placarded, avoid heavily populated areas, crowds, tunnels, narrow streets, and alleys. Carriers must make a **written route plan** for 1.1/1.2/1.3 explosives and choose the **safest route** for radioactive materials. Obey local permits and restrictions, and check tunnel rules.

**Fires:** never drive a placarded vehicle near an open fire unless you can pass safely within **300 ft** without stopping; never park within 300 ft of an open fire.

**Railroad crossings:** stop **15–50 ft** from the nearest rail if the vehicle is placarded, carries any amount of chlorine, or has cargo tanks (loaded or empty) used for hazmat. Never shift gears while crossing.

**Parking — Division 1.1/1.2/1.3 explosives:**
- Never within **5 ft** of the traveled part of the road.
- Not within **300 ft** of a bridge, tunnel, building, place where people gather, or open fire, except for brief operational stops.
- Someone must always **attend** the vehicle unless it is on the shipper's, carrier's, or consignee's property, or in an approved **safe haven**.

**Parking — other placarded vehicles:** within 5 ft of the road only if work requires it, and only briefly; attended when on a public roadway or shoulder; never uncouple a trailer carrying hazmat on a public street; not within 300 ft of an open fire.

**Attendance:** the attendant must be **in the vehicle, awake, and not in the sleeper**, or **within 100 ft with a clear view**; must be aware of the hazards, know the emergency procedures, and be able to move the vehicle.

**Chlorine:** carry an approved gas mask and an emergency kit for controlling leaks in dome-cover plate fittings.

## B10.7 Emergencies

**At the scene:** warn others and keep people away; call for help; give responders the shipping papers and emergency information; do not move the vehicle more than safety requires. Use the ERG guidance for the material.

**Report to the National Response Center, 1-800-424-8802,** when any of these occur:
- a person is killed;
- an injured person requires hospitalization;
- property damage exceeds **$50,000**;
- the public is evacuated for **1 hour or more**;
- a major transportation artery or facility is closed for **1 hour or more**;
- fire, breakage, spillage, or suspected radioactive contamination occurs;
- a continuing danger to life exists.

The carrier files a detailed written report within **30 days**. For a Class 7 (radioactive) incident, also notify the **shipper**.
