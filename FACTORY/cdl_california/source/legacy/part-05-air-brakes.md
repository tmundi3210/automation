# B5 — Air Brakes (25 questions, need 20)

## B5.1 The three systems

- **Service brakes:** applied with the brake pedal (foot valve) in normal driving.
- **Parking brakes:** the spring brakes, applied with the parking control.
- **Emergency brakes:** use parts of the service and parking systems to stop the vehicle if a service system fails.

## B5.2 Components

**Air compressor:** pumps air into the storage tanks; driven by the engine through gears or a V-belt; cooled by air or the engine cooling system; lubricated by its own oil or engine oil.

**Governor:** controls when the compressor pumps.
- **Cut-in** (starts pumping): about **100 psi**.
- **Cut-out** (stops pumping): about **125 psi**.

**Storage tanks (reservoirs):** hold enough compressed air for several brake applications if the compressor stops.

**Tank drains:** compressed air contains water and compressor oil, which can freeze and cause brake failure. **Drain tanks fully at the end of each working day.** Manual drains use a quarter-turn valve or pull cable; automatic drains exist but should still be checked.

**Alcohol evaporator:** adds alcohol to the air system to reduce icing in valves in cold weather. Check and fill daily in cold weather.

**Safety valve:** in the first tank the compressor fills; opens at about **150 psi**. If it releases air, the governor needs repair.

**Brake pedal (foot valve):** harder pressure = more air pressure. Releasing lets air out, lowering tank pressure. "Fanning" the pedal wastes air.

**Foundation brakes:**
- **S-cam drum brakes** (most common): brake chamber pushrod → slack adjuster → brake camshaft → the S-cam forces the shoes apart against the drum.
- **Wedge brakes:** the pushrod drives a wedge between the ends of the two shoes.
- **Disc brakes:** a power screw clamps the rotor between caliper linings.

**Drums, shoes, linings:** heat is normal, but excess heat causes brake fade. Drums must not be cracked. Linings must not be loose, oil- or grease-soaked, or dangerously thin.

**Gauges and warnings:**
- **Supply pressure gauge:** tank pressure. A dual system has two gauges or one with two needles.
- **Application pressure gauge:** how much pressure you are applying to the brakes.
- **Low air pressure warning:** required; a red light and/or buzzer comes on **before pressure falls below 60 psi** (on older vehicles, at half the governor cut-out).
- **Wig-wag:** a mechanical arm on some older vehicles that drops into view below 60 psi. Automatic ones rise out of view above 60 psi; manual ones must be reset by hand.
- **Stop light switch:** air pressure turns on the brake lights.

**Front brake limiting valve:** on some vehicles built before 1975, with "normal" and "slippery" settings; "slippery" cuts front brake pressure in half. Modern vehicles adjust automatically. **Front brakes are good in all conditions and do not cause skids, even on ice.**

**Spring brakes:** mechanical springs held back by air pressure. When air is released, the springs apply the brakes. Used for parking and emergencies. If tank pressure is lost, they apply automatically — in most vehicles at **20–45 psi**. Their power depends on the brakes being in adjustment.

**Parking brake control:** a **yellow diamond-shaped knob**. Push in to release, pull out to apply. Older vehicles may use a lever, a **modulating control valve** (gradual spring-brake application), or **dual parking control valves** (a separate tank lets you release the spring brakes once to move out of danger).

**Parking brake use:**
- Use it every time you park.
- **Don't** use it if the brakes are very hot (after a long downgrade) — let them cool.
- **Don't** use it if the brakes are wet in freezing weather — they may freeze on; use chocks.
- Never leave the vehicle unattended without the parking brake set or the wheels chocked.

**ABS:** see B3.3 — required on air-braked vehicles built on/after March 1, 1998 and tractors on/after March 1, 1997.

## B5.3 Dual air brake system

- Two separate air systems share one set of brake controls, each with its own gauge, tanks, hoses, and lines.
- **Primary** usually operates the rear axle(s); **secondary** operates the front axle and possibly one rear axle.
- If one fails, the other still gives some braking.
- Before driving, let pressure build to at least **100 psi in both systems**.

## B5.4 Inspection additions

**Engine compartment:** if the compressor is belt-driven, check the belt's condition and tightness.

**Slack adjusters (walk-around):**
1. Park on level ground and chock the wheels.
2. Release the parking brake.
3. Pull hard on each slack adjuster you can reach.
4. More than about **1 inch** of movement where the pushrod attaches means adjustment is needed.

Manual slack adjusters must be adjusted. An automatic slack adjuster out of adjustment indicates a mechanical problem needing repair.

**The 7 final air brake checks:**

| # | Test | Procedure | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low pressure warning | Engine off, key on, fan the pedal to bleed pressure | Warning comes on **before 60 psi** |
| 2 | Spring brakes set | Keep fanning with wheels chocked, parking brake released | Knob pops out at **20–45 psi** (combination: tractor protection valve closes) |
| 3 | Pressure buildup rate | Engine at operating rpm | Dual: **85→100 psi in 45 seconds**. Single: **50→90 psi in 3 minutes** at 600–900 rpm |
| 4 | Air leakage, released | Full pressure, engine off, wheels chocked, parking brake released, time 1 minute | **<2 psi/min** single, **<3 psi/min** combination |
| 4b | Air leakage, applied | Apply **90 psi or more**, time 1 minute after the initial drop | **<3 psi/min** single, **<4 psi/min** combination |
| 5 | Governor cut-in/cut-out | Fast idle, then fan the brakes | Cut-out ≈ **125 psi**; cut-in ≈ **100 psi** |
| 6 | Parking brake | Set it and pull gently in low gear | Vehicle does not move |
| 7 | Service brake | About 5 mph, brake firmly | No pulling to one side, no delay, nothing unusual |

## B5.5 Driving with air brakes

- **Normal stops:** push the pedal down smoothly, adjusting pressure for a smooth stop.
- **With ABS:** brake normally; don't pump.
- **Emergency:** controlled braking, or stab braking on non-ABS vehicles.

**Stopping distance with air brakes** adds **brake lag** — about **½ second** for air to reach the brakes.
Total = perception + reaction + brake lag + braking.
At 55 mph, dry: 142 + 61 + **32** + 216 ≈ **451 ft**.

**Brake fade:** overheating makes the drums expand away from the shoes, so more pressure gives less braking. Brakes out of adjustment fade first and overload the others. On a downgrade, **engine braking in a low gear is the main braking**; the service brakes supplement it.

**Snub braking:** at safe speed, brake firmly to about 5 mph below it (about 3 seconds), release, repeat. Never use light steady pressure all the way down — it overheats the brakes.

**Low air warning while driving:** stop and park as soon as it is safe. The brakes may soon fail and the spring brakes may come on suddenly.

**End of day:** drain the air tanks.
