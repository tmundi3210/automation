# B3 — Hazards, emergencies, skids, crashes, fitness, extreme driving (GK)

## B3.1 Hazard awareness

**Clues to a developing hazard:** blocked vision (vans, parked cars, backing trucks); work zones; children and pedestrians; cyclists with headphones; distracted drivers; drunk drivers (weaving, erratic speed, headlights off at night); delivery trucks; parked cars (door opening, driver in the seat); confused drivers (tourists, out-of-state plates); drivers in a hurry; crash scenes; shopping areas.

**Planning:** always have an escape route; watch 12–15 seconds ahead and decide in advance what you would do.

**Distraction:** a **5-second glance** away at 55 mph covers about the length of a football field. Hands-free only. Other distractions: eating, adjusting the radio or GPS, rubbernecking.

**Aggressive drivers:** don't engage, avoid eye contact, get out of their way, report to police.

## B3.2 Emergencies

**Steer or stop?** You can almost always **turn faster than you can stop**, especially at highway speed — but the high center of gravity makes rollover a risk.

**Evasive steering:**
- Don't brake while turning; it can lock the wheels.
- Turn only as much as needed; be ready to countersteer back.
- Usually steer **right**, toward the shoulder. Left risks a head-on crash.

**Leaving the road:**
- Avoid braking until below about **20 mph**.
- Keep one set of wheels on the pavement if possible.
- Stay on the shoulder until slowed; check mirrors and signal before returning.

**Controlled braking:** squeeze as hard as possible without locking the wheels; keep steering movement small.

**Stab braking (non-ABS only):** apply fully → release when the wheels lock → reapply when they roll again. Never jam and hold.

**Brake failure:** downshift; pump (hydraulic); use the parking brake (hold the release to control it); use an escape ramp or an uphill road.

**Tire failure:** signs are a bang, vibration, heavy steering, or fishtailing. Hold the wheel firmly, **stay off the brakes**, slow gradually, then pull off. Check all tires afterward.

## B3.3 ABS

**Required on:** truck tractors built on/after **March 1, 1997**; trailers and other air-braked vehicles built on/after **March 1, 1998**; hydraulic-brake trucks built on/after **March 1, 1999**.

- Prevents wheel lockup. It does **not** necessarily shorten stopping distance and does not add braking power.
- **Brake normally. Don't pump.**
- The yellow malfunction lamp (dash, and left rear of the trailer) lights briefly at start-up as a bulb check. If it stays on, ABS isn't working — normal brakes still work; get it serviced.
- ABS on the tractor only still helps you keep steering control.

## B3.4 Skids

**Causes:** overbraking, oversteering, overaccelerating, driving too fast for conditions.

**Drive-wheel skid (most common):** rear drive wheels lock or spin.
1. Stop braking or accelerating.
2. Push in the clutch (manual).
3. Countersteer — turn in the direction you want to go.
4. Countersteer back as the vehicle straightens.

**Front-wheel skid:** usually from driving too fast; the vehicle goes straight regardless of steering. Stop braking and turning, and let it slow.

## B3.5 Crashes

1. **Protect the area:** flashers and triangles.
2. **Notify authorities** (911).
3. **Care for the injured.** Don't move them unless there's danger such as fire. Give first aid only if trained.
4. **Collect information:** names, license, insurance, witnesses, plates.

**CA reporting:** file an **SR-1** with the DMV within **10 days** if anyone was injured or killed, or damage exceeded **$1,000**. Also notify your employer.

## B3.6 Fires

**Causes:** fuel leaks or spills after a crash; underinflated tires or duals touching; electrical shorts; loose fuel connections; flammable cargo improperly loaded, sealed, or ventilated.

**Response:**
- Pull off into an open area, away from buildings, trees, and fuel. **Never into a service station.**
- Call for help.
- **Keep trailer doors closed** if the fire is in the cargo — opening them feeds oxygen.
- Stay upwind. Aim the extinguisher at the **base** of the fire.
- With hazmat, identify the material; fight the fire only if safe and you are trained.

**Extinguisher classes:** A = wood/paper/cloth; B = flammable liquids and grease; C = electrical. The CMV extinguisher is usually rated **B:C**. No water on electrical or gasoline fires; water can cool a tire fire.

## B3.7 Alcohol, drugs, illness, fatigue

**Alcohol:** 12 oz beer = 5 oz wine = 1.5 oz of 80-proof liquor. **Only time sobers you up.** BAC depends on amount, speed of drinking, and body weight. No drinking within 4 hours of duty. No possession in the CMV unless it is manifested cargo.

**Drugs:** Schedule I, amphetamines, narcotics, and anything impairing are prohibited. OTC cold/allergy medicines can cause drowsiness — read the labels.

**Illness:** don't drive while ill.

**Fatigue:** get 7–8 hours of sleep. Lowest alertness is about **midnight to 6 a.m.**, with a mid-afternoon dip. **The only cure is sleep**; caffeine is brief. If drowsy, pull off safely and nap.

**Hours of service — federal (interstate property carriers):**
- 11 hours driving after 10 consecutive hours off duty.
- 14-hour on-duty window.
- 30-minute break after 8 hours of driving.
- 60 hours/7 days or 70 hours/8 days; a 34-hour restart resets it.

**CA intrastate:** 12 hours driving, 16-hour window, 80 hours in 8 consecutive days.

## B3.8 Railroad crossings

**Required stops** (15–50 ft from the nearest rail): placarded hazmat vehicles, buses, and tank vehicles loaded or empty that carried hazmat.

**At every crossing:**
- Look and listen both ways. **Never shift gears while crossing.**
- Make sure there is room to clear the whole vehicle — at least **15 ft** past the far rail.
- Never stop on the tracks. Expect a train at any time.
- A train overhangs the rails by about **3 ft** on each side, and can take **a mile or more** to stop.
- At double tracks, wait for a clear view of both.

**Passive crossings** have only crossbucks and signs — you judge. **Active crossings** have lights, gates, and bells — obey them. A **low ground clearance** sign warns that your vehicle may hang up.

**Stuck on the tracks:** get everyone out immediately, move away **toward the approaching train at an angle** so debris misses you, and call the **ENS number** on the blue sign at the crossing.

## B3.9 Mountain driving

- Choose a low gear **before** starting down. Engine braking is the main braking; the service brakes supplement it.
- Safe speed depends on total weight, grade length and steepness, road conditions, and weather.

**Snub braking (correct technique):** at your safe speed, brake firmly to about **5 mph below** it (roughly 3 seconds of application), release, and repeat when speed returns. Example: safe speed 40 → brake to 35 → release → repeat at 40.

**Brake fade:** overheating from overuse, or brakes out of adjustment. Adjusted brakes share the load; out-of-adjustment brakes fade first. Don't ride the brakes.

**Escape ramps:** use them if the brakes fail.

## B3.10 Night, fog, winter, heat, wind, work zones

**Night:** reduce speed to stop within headlight range; clean windshield, lights, mirrors; dim high beams within **500 ft** of oncoming traffic and **300 ft** when following (CA); look toward the right edge of your lane; no sunglasses.

**Fog:** best advice is not to drive. If you must: slow down, low beams and fog lights, 4-way flashers if needed, no passing, never stop in the travel lanes.

**Winter:** check coolant/antifreeze, defroster, wipers and fluid, tires, lights, chains. Clear ice from lights, windows, mirrors. **Ice on mirrors, antenna, or wipers means the road may be icy.** Dry wet brakes by braking lightly while driving.

**CA chain controls:** trucks and vehicles towing must chain up whenever controls (R1–R3) are in effect, per the posted signs. Carry chains in chain-control areas.

**Heat:** tire pressure rises with heat — check every **2 hours or 100 miles**, and **never let air out**. Let overheated tires cool. Bleeding tar makes the road slippery. Never remove the radiator cap when hot; check coolant only when cool. Check hoses and belts.

**High winds:** empty or lightly loaded trailers are most at risk of being pushed sideways or tipped. Slow down, grip firmly, and watch for gusts at overpasses, canyon exits, and open stretches. In severe wind, pull off.

**Work zones:** slow down; watch for workers, flaggers, lane shifts, uneven pavement; warn drivers behind with brake lights or flashers. **CA fines are doubled** in highway work zones when workers are present.

**Human trafficking awareness:** indicators include a person who seems controlled by someone else, gives scripted answers, lacks ID, isn't free to leave, or shows fear. Report to 911 or the National Human Trafficking Hotline, **1-888-373-7888**. Using a CMV in trafficking is a lifetime disqualification with no reinstatement.
