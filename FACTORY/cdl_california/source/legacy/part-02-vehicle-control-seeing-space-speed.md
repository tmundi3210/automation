# B2 — Control, shifting, seeing, communicating, space, speed (GK)

## B2.1 Basic control

**Starting and stopping:**
- Accelerate smoothly; jerky starts can damage the coupling.
- To avoid rolling back on an upgrade, partially engage the clutch before releasing the parking brake.
- Accelerate gently on slippery roads so the drive wheels don't spin.
- Brake gradually, easing off near the stop. On a manual, push the clutch in only when the engine nears idle.

**Steering:** both hands on the wheel, on opposite sides (about 9 and 3 o'clock).

**Backing:**
- Always dangerous. Avoid it; park so you can pull forward.
- **GOAL — Get Out And Look.** Check the path and clearance above and to the sides.
- Back slowly, in the lowest reverse gear.
- Use both mirrors; use a helper when possible and agree on hand signals first. If you lose sight of the helper, stop.
- Back toward the driver's side when possible, for visibility.
- Back only as far as necessary. You are responsible when you back.

**Backing a trailer:**
- Turn the wheel opposite to the way you want the trailer to go: put the **bottom of the wheel toward the desired direction**.
- Once the trailer starts turning, turn the wheel back to follow it.
- Correct drift immediately by turning the wheel toward the drift.
- Pull forward to reposition as needed.

## B2.2 Shifting

**Double clutching — upshift:** release the accelerator, clutch in, shift to neutral, clutch out. Let rpm drop to the right speed, clutch in, shift up, clutch out.
**Downshift:** same steps, but **raise the rpm** while in neutral before shifting down.

**Downshifting rules:**
- Downshift before starting down a hill.
- Downshift before entering a curve, not in it.
- Select the low gear before the downgrade. The gear you'd use to climb the hill is usually right for descending it.

**Automatics:** use a lower range on grades for engine braking.

**Retarders** (engine, exhaust, hydraulic, electric):
- Reduce brake wear.
- Can cause a skid when drive wheels have poor traction. **Turn them off on wet, icy, or snowy roads.**
- Some areas prohibit them because of noise.

## B2.3 Seeing

- Look **12–15 seconds** ahead: about 1 block in town, about ¼ mile on the highway.
- Adjust mirrors during the pre-trip; for a trailer, adjust with the trailer straight.
- Check mirrors regularly, and more often in heavy traffic, at intersections, and before lane changes, turns, and merges.
- **Convex mirrors** show a wider area, but objects look **smaller and farther away** than they are.
- Blind spots are largest on the right side and directly behind. Before a lane change, check mirrors: before signaling, after signaling, when the change begins, and after it is complete.

## B2.4 Communicating

- Signal well before turns; keep the signal on until the turn is complete, then cancel it.
- **Tap the brake pedal** a few times to flash the brake lights when slowing unexpectedly, before a toll booth, or for a stalled car.
- **Don't direct traffic.** Don't wave others on to pass or cross; you may be liable.
- Use the horn only when needed.
- Headlights at dawn, dusk, in rain, and in low visibility.

**Stopped on the road or shoulder:**
- Turn on the 4-way flashers **immediately**.
- Place warning triangles within **10 minutes**.
- Carry the triangle held out in front of you, facing oncoming traffic.

**Triangle placement:**
- Two-lane or undivided road: **10 ft** behind on the traffic side, **100 ft** behind, **100 ft** ahead.
- One-way or divided road: **10 ft, 100 ft, 200 ft**, all behind.
- Hill, curve, or obstruction: move the rear triangle back as far as **500 ft**.

## B2.5 Space management

**Following distance:**
- Under 40 mph: **1 second per 10 ft** of vehicle length.
- Over 40 mph: **add 1 second**.
- 40-ft truck: 4 s under 40 mph, 5 s over. 70-ft combination: 7 s under, 8 s over.
- Add time in bad weather.

**Tailgaters:** increase your own following distance, avoid sudden changes, don't speed up, don't flash lights, signal early.

**Sides:** stay centered; avoid driving alongside others; strong winds make lane-keeping hard.

**Overhead:** posted clearances may be wrong (repaving, packed snow). An **empty trailer rides higher** than a loaded one. If unsure, slow down or reroute.

**Below:** watch undercarriage clearance at railroad tracks, dirt roads, and yards.

**Right turns:** turn slowly; keep the rear close to the curb so cars can't squeeze in on the right; swing wide only as you **complete** the turn, never before.

**Left turns:** reach the center of the intersection before turning (off-tracking). With two left-turn lanes, use the **rightmost** one.

**Crossing/entering traffic:** you need a much larger gap, especially loaded, because acceleration is slow.

## B2.6 Speed and stopping

**Stopping distance** = perception + reaction + braking.
- Perception time ≈ 1.75 s; reaction time ≈ 0.75 s.
- At 55 mph, dry: 142 ft perception + 61 ft reaction + 216 ft braking ≈ **419 ft** — longer than a football field.

**Speed effects:** doubling speed makes stopping distance about **4×** longer; impact force also rises about 4×.

**Empty trucks** need **more** stopping distance — less weight means less traction, and brakes, springs, and tires are built for a full load.

**Slippery roads:**
- Wet: reduce speed by about **one-third** (55 → about 35–40 mph).
- Packed snow: reduce by **half or more**.
- Ice: crawl, and stop driving as soon as it is safe.
- Slippery spots: shaded areas, bridges and overpasses (freeze first), the first minutes of rain (oil lifts off the road). **Black ice looks like a wet road.**

**Hydroplaning:**
- Possible at speeds as low as about **30 mph**; more likely with low tire pressure or worn tread.
- Response: release the accelerator, push in the clutch, **do not brake**.

**Curves:** posted curve speeds may be too fast for a truck (rollover). Slow down **before** the curve, then accelerate slightly through it. Don't brake in the curve.

**Sight distance:** drive slowly enough to stop within the distance you can see. Low beams show about **250 ft**; high beams about **350–500 ft**.

**CA rule:** trucks with 3+ axles and any vehicle towing are limited to **55 mph** (CVC 22406), and must use the right lane, or either of the two rightmost lanes where there are 4+ lanes in your direction, except to pass or turn (CVC 21655).
