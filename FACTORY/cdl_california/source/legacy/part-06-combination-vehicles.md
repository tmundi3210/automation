# B6 — Combination Vehicles (20 questions, need 16)

## B6.1 Driving safely

**Rollover:** more than half of truck driver deaths in crashes are rollovers. A **fully loaded rig is 10× more likely** to roll over than an empty one. Keep cargo low, go slowly around turns, curves, and ramps. A rig can roll at the posted curve speed.

**Rearward amplification ("crack-the-whip"):** a quick steering move is amplified toward the rear. The **last trailer** is most likely to roll over. An amplification of **2.0** means the rear trailer is twice as likely to turn over as the tractor. Triples amplify most, then doubles, then a 5-axle tractor-semitrailer. Steer gently; avoid sudden lane changes.

**Braking:** brake early and gently, and allow plenty of following distance. **Empty rigs take longer to stop** (less traction). A **bobtail** tractor is hard to stop smoothly and takes longer than expected.

**Railroad crossings — low clearance units that can hang up:** car carriers, moving vans, lowboys; possum-belly livestock trailers; a single-axle tractor pulling a long trailer whose landing gear is set for a tandem-axle tractor.

**Trailer skid (trailer jackknife):** the trailer wheels lock and the trailer swings out.
- Spot it early in your mirrors.
- **Release the brakes** to regain traction; the trailer then straightens and follows the tractor.
- **Never use the trailer hand valve to straighten out the rig** — it locks the trailer wheels and worsens the skid.

**Tractor jackknife:** the drive wheels lock (a drive-wheel skid). Release the brakes and countersteer.

**Off-tracking:** the rear wheels follow a shorter path than the front wheels in a turn. The longer the vehicle, the more it off-tracks; the trailer off-tracks more than the tractor. Steer the front wide enough for the rear to clear.

**Right turns:** keep the rear close to the curb; swing wide only as you complete the turn.

**Backing a trailer:** steer opposite, follow the trailer once it turns, correct drift immediately, back slowly using both mirrors, pull forward to reposition, and back toward the driver's side when possible.

## B6.2 Combination air brakes

**Trailer hand valve** (trolley valve, Johnson bar): applies the **trailer brakes only**. Use it only to test the trailer brakes. Don't use it while driving (trailer skid). **Never use it for parking** — air leaks off and releases the brakes.

**Tractor protection valve:** keeps air in the tractor if the trailer breaks away or develops a bad leak. Controlled by the trailer air supply control; closes automatically at **20–45 psi**.

**Trailer air supply control:** a **red 8-sided (octagon) knob**. Push in to supply air; pull out to shut off air and apply the trailer emergency brakes. Pops out on its own at 20–45 psi. Older vehicles may use a "normal/emergency" lever.

**Service line** (control/signal line): **blue**. Carries air controlled by the foot brake or trailer hand valve; pressure varies with braking effort.

**Emergency line** (supply line): **red**. Supplies the trailer air tanks and controls the trailer emergency brakes. Loss of emergency line pressure (breakaway, rupture) applies the trailer emergency brakes.

**If the service line comes apart while driving:** nothing happens immediately; when you brake, air escapes, pressure drops, and the trailer emergency brakes may come on.

**Glad hands:** couplers joining tractor and trailer air lines, sealed with rubber grommets, color-coded **blue = service, red = emergency**.

**Dummy couplers:** blank glad hands for the tractor's lines when no trailer is attached, to keep out water and dirt.

**Crossed air lines:**
- Old trailer without spring brakes: you can drive away with **no trailer brakes**.
- Trailer with spring brakes: the **spring brakes won't release**.
- Always test before driving.

**Trailer air tanks:** filled through the emergency line; drain daily.

**Shut-off valves (cut-out cocks):** on the service and emergency lines at the rear of trailers used to tow others. **Closed on the last trailer; open between trailers.**

**Trailer brakes:** newer trailers have spring brakes. Trailers built before 1975 may have none — their emergency brakes rely on trailer tank air, which leaks away, so don't rely on them for parking.

## B6.3 Coupling a tractor-semitrailer

1. **Inspect the fifth wheel:** no damaged or missing parts; securely mounted, no cracks; properly greased (a dry plate causes steering problems); tilted down toward the rear; jaws open; safety unlocking handle in the automatic lock position; sliding fifth wheel locked; kingpin not bent or broken.
2. **Check the area and chock the trailer wheels:** area clear, trailer wheels chocked or spring brakes on, cargo secured.
3. **Position the tractor** directly in front of the trailer. **Never back under at an angle** — it can push the trailer sideways and break the landing gear. Check alignment in the outside mirrors.
4. **Back slowly** until the fifth wheel just touches the trailer. Don't hit it.
5. **Secure the tractor:** parking brake set, transmission in neutral.
6. **Check the trailer height:** low enough that the tractor raises it slightly. Too high → may not couple (kingpin rides over the jaws). Too low → the tractor may damage the trailer nose. Confirm kingpin/fifth wheel alignment.
7. **Connect the air lines:** check the glad hand seals; emergency to emergency, service to service; support the lines.
8. **Supply air and check the brakes:** push in the trailer air supply knob, wait for normal pressure, shut off the engine so you can hear, apply and release the trailer brakes and listen (this catches crossed lines), watch the gauges for major loss, then restart and rebuild pressure.
9. **Lock the trailer brakes:** pull out the trailer air supply knob.
10. **Back under the trailer:** lowest reverse gear, slowly, stopping when the kingpin locks into the fifth wheel.
11. **Tug test:** raise the landing gear slightly off the ground and, with the trailer brakes locked, pull gently forward in low gear.
12. **Secure the vehicle:** neutral, parking brake on, engine off, take the key.
13. **Inspect the coupling with a flashlight:** **no space between the upper and lower fifth wheel** (a gap means the kingpin may be on top of closed jaws); jaws closed around the **shank** of the kingpin, not the head; locking lever in the lock position; safety catch over the locking lever. If anything is wrong, don't drive.
14. **Connect the electrical cord:** plug in, fasten the safety catch, check air and electrical lines for damage and clear of moving parts.
15. **Raise the landing gear:** start in low gear range, switch to high once free of weight; raise it **all the way** (partly raised gear can catch on tracks or bumps); secure the crank handle; with full weight on the tractor, check that the tractor frame clears the landing gear in sharp turns and the tires clear the trailer nose.
16. **Remove and store the wheel chocks.**

## B6.4 Uncoupling

1. **Position the rig** in a straight line on firm, level ground.
2. **Ease pressure on the locking jaws:** pull out the trailer air supply knob, back up gently, then set the parking brake while still pushing against the kingpin.
3. **Chock the trailer wheels** if it has no spring brakes or you aren't sure.
4. **Lower the landing gear:** empty trailer — until firm contact; loaded trailer — a few extra turns in low gear to take some weight off the tractor, without lifting the trailer off the fifth wheel.
5. **Disconnect the air and electrical lines:** connect the air lines to the dummy couplers or each other; hang the electrical cable **plug down** so moisture can't enter; support the lines.
6. **Unlock the fifth wheel:** raise the release handle lock, pull the handle to open, and keep your legs and feet clear of the rear tractor wheels.
7. **Pull partly clear:** drive forward until the fifth wheel is out from under the trailer, but stop with the **tractor frame still under the trailer** so it can't fall if the landing gear collapses.
8. **Secure the tractor:** parking brake on, neutral.
9. **Inspect the trailer supports:** ground holding, landing gear undamaged.
10. **Pull clear:** release the parking brake, check the area, drive clear.

## B6.5 Inspecting a combination

**Lower fifth wheel:** securely mounted, no missing or damaged parts, enough grease, **no visible space** between upper and lower fifth wheel, jaws around the kingpin shank, release arm seated, safety latch engaged.
**Upper fifth wheel:** glide plate securely mounted to the trailer frame, kingpin undamaged.
**Air and electrical lines:** cord firmly plugged in and secured; air lines properly connected, no leaks, enough slack for turns; no damage.
**Sliding fifth wheel:** undamaged, greased, all locking pins present and locked, no air leaks if air-powered, not so far forward that the tractor frame hits the landing gear or the cab hits the trailer in a turn.
**Landing gear:** fully raised, no bends or missing parts, crank handle secured, no air or hydraulic leaks.

**The 4 combination brake checks:**

| # | Test | Procedure | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Air flows to all trailers | Charge, push in the supply knob, apply the hand valve; at the rear of the **last** trailer open the emergency valve, then the service valve | Air escapes audibly from both; close both. No air → a shut-off valve is closed |
| 2 | Tractor protection valve | Charge, engine off, fan the pedal down | Supply knob pops out (or goes to emergency) at **20–45 psi** |
| 3 | Trailer emergency brakes | Charge, confirm the trailer rolls free, stop, pull out the supply knob, pull gently | The trailer's emergency brakes hold |
| 4 | Trailer service brakes | Normal pressure, parking brakes released, move forward slowly, apply the hand valve | You feel the trailer brakes engage |
