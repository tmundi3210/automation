# SB-02 · School bus emergencies, railroad crossings and special situations

> **Handbook:** Section 10.3–10.7, pages 10-7–10-13 · **Test:** School Bus · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~45 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- How to decide whether to evacuate the bus, and the five situations where you **must** evacuate.
- How to plan an evacuation, where the "safe place" is (**100 ft**, **300 ft**, upwind), and the steps of an evacuation.
- The school bus procedure at railroad crossings: hazard lights at **200 ft**, stopping **15–50 ft** from the nearest rail, gates, multiple tracks, and the **+15 ft** storage rule.
- What to do if the bus stalls on the tracks, the signal seems broken, or you cannot see down the tracks.
- How to handle behavior problems on the bus, and when you may (and may not) put a student off the bus.
- How ABS works on a school bus, and the rules for **[CA]** strobe lights, high winds, **[CA]** backing and tail swing.

## Learn it

An emergency can happen anywhere: a crash, a bus stalled on railroad tracks or in a busy intersection, an electrical fire in the engine, or a student who gets sick or hurt. Knowing what to do **before, during and after** an evacuation can decide whether children live or die. This lesson also covers railroad crossings, student behavior, ABS brakes and a few special driving situations. (p. 10-7)

**Key words**
- **Evacuate** = get everyone off the bus quickly and move them to a safe place.
- **Dispatcher** = the person at your base (dispatch office) you talk to by radio or phone.
- **Upwind** = on the side the wind is blowing **from**, so smoke and fumes blow **away** from you.
- **HazMat** = hazardous materials: fuel, chemicals and other dangerous goods.
- **Culvert** = a large pipe or channel that carries water under a road.
- **Emergency warning devices** = reflective triangles or similar items you set out to warn traffic.
- **Railroad-highway grade crossing** = a place where a road crosses train tracks at the same level.
- **Crossbuck** = the X-shaped sign at a railroad crossing.
- **Containment (storage) area** = the space on the far side of the tracks where the whole bus can wait clear of the tracks.
- **ABS** = anti-lock braking system; it keeps the wheels from locking when you brake hard.
- **GVWR** = gross vehicle weight rating, the maker's maximum safe loaded weight.
- **Strobe light** = a bright, flashing white light on the bus roof.
- **Tail swing** = the rear of the bus swinging out to the side when you turn.
- **Lookout** = a person outside the bus who warns you of danger while you back up.

### 10.3.1 Planning for emergencies (p. 10-7)
**Step 1 is to recognize the hazard.** If there is time, **call your dispatcher** and explain the situation **before** you decide to evacuate.

**General rule:** students are usually **safest and easiest to control when they stay on the bus**, as long as staying does not put them at needless risk. But if you do decide to evacuate, decide **in time**.

Things to think about before evacuating:
- Is there a **fire**, or a danger of fire?
- Do you smell **raw or leaking fuel**?
- Could other vehicles **hit the bus**?
- Is the bus in the path of a **tornado** you can see, or **rising water**?
- Are there **downed power lines**?
- Would getting off put students near **fast traffic**, **bad weather**, or another danger such as downed power lines?
- Would moving students make injuries worse (**neck or back injuries**, broken bones)?
- Is there a **hazardous spill**? Sometimes staying on the bus is safer than touching the material.

**Mandatory evacuations — you must evacuate when:**
1. The bus is **on fire** or there is a **threat of fire**.
2. The bus is **stalled on or next to a railroad crossing**.
3. The bus's **position may change** and make things more dangerous.
4. A crash is about to happen (**imminent danger** of an accident).
5. A **HazMat spill** means you need to get out fast.

### 10.3.2 Evacuation procedures (pp. 10-7–10-8)
**Plan ahead.** When you can, assign **2 older, responsible students** to **each emergency exit** and teach them to help others off. Assign **another** student to lead everyone to a **"safe place"**. But older students may not be on board when trouble comes, so **explain the evacuation plan to all students**, including how the emergency exits work and why they must follow your instructions. (p. 10-7)

**Choosing a safe place** (p. 10-7):

| Situation | Where to take the students |
|---|---|
| Any roadside evacuation | At least **100 ft** off the road, **in the direction of oncoming traffic** (so debris does not hit them if a car strikes the bus) |
| Fire | **Upwind** of the bus |
| Railroad tracks | As **far from the tracks** as possible, **in the direction of any oncoming train** |
| Spilled HazMat | **Upwind**, at least **300 ft** from the bus |
| Tornado (and evacuation ordered, no building nearby) | A nearby **ditch or culvert**; students **lie face down, hands over their heads**, far enough away that the bus **cannot tip onto them**. Avoid places that can **flash flood**. |

**General procedures** (p. 10-8):
1. Decide if evacuating is the safest choice.
2. Pick the best type: **front, rear or side door** (or a mix), or **roof or window**.
3. **Secure the bus:** Park (or Neutral if there is no Park), **set the parking brakes**, **shut off the engine**, **remove the key**, turn on the **hazard-warning lights**, and — if there is time — tell dispatch the location, conditions and kind of help you need.
4. **Hang the radio microphone or phone out the driver's window**, and take your cell phone along if it works.
5. No working radio or phone? Send a **passing driver or nearby resident** to call for help. **Last resort:** send **2 older, responsible students** for help.
6. **Order** the evacuation, and get the students off.
   - **Do not move** a student who may have a **neck or spine injury** unless their **life is in immediate danger**. Special methods are needed to move them.
7. Have a student assistant lead the others to the nearest safe place.
8. **Walk through the bus** to make sure no one is left. Take the emergency equipment.
9. Join the students, **count them**, and check that they are safe.
10. **Protect the scene:** set out emergency warning devices as needed.
11. Get information ready for emergency responders.

### 10.4.1 Types of crossings and warning signs (pp. 10-8–10-9)
**Passive crossing** = **no** traffic control device. A school bus **must stop** here and follow the proper steps, but the decision to go is **entirely yours**: spot the crossing, look for trains, and decide if there is room to cross safely. Passive crossings have **yellow round advance warning signs**, **pavement markings** and **crossbucks** to help you notice them. (p. 10-8)

**Active crossing** = has a traffic control device: **flashing red lights** (with or without bells), or **flashing red lights with bells and gates**. (p. 10-8)

Signs and devices (10.4.2):
- **Advance warning sign** — **round, black on yellow**, placed **before** a public crossing. It means slow down, look and listen for a train, and be ready to stop. (Figure 10.5, p. 10-8)
- **Pavement markings** — mean the **same** as the advance warning sign: an **"X" with "RR"**, and a no-passing marking on **2-lane** roads. A **no passing zone sign** may also be posted on 2-lane roads. (pp. 10-8–10-9)
- **White stop line** — may be painted before the tracks. The **front of the school bus must stay behind** it while stopped. (p. 10-9)
- **Crossbuck** — marks the crossing; you must **yield** to the train. If there is **no white line**, stop the bus **before the crossbuck**. With **more than one track**, a sign **under the crossbuck** shows how many. (p. 10-9)
- **Flashing red lights** — when they start flashing, **stop**: a train is coming. Yield. With more than 1 track, make sure **all** tracks are clear. (p. 10-9)
- **Gates** — stop when the lights start flashing and **before the gate comes down**. Stay stopped until the gates **go up** and the lights **stop flashing**. Gate still down after the train has gone by? Never go around it — call your dispatcher. (p. 10-9)

### 10.4.3 Recommended crossing procedures (pp. 10-9–10-10)
Each state has its own school bus crossing laws; obey them. **In general, school buses stop at all crossings**, and the driver makes sure it is safe before crossing. A school bus has **no chance** in a crash with a train: a train is too big and heavy to stop quickly, and it has **no escape route**. (p. 10-9)

**Approaching the crossing** (p. 10-10):
- Slow down (in a manual bus, **shift to a lower gear**) and **test your brakes**.
- Turn on the **hazard lights about 200 ft** before the crossing so others know what you will do.
- Scan around you and **check traffic behind** you.
- Stay to the **right** if you can.
- Pick an **escape route** in case your brakes fail or there is trouble behind you.

**At the crossing:**
- Stop **no closer than 15 ft** and **no farther than 50 ft** from the **nearest rail**, where you can see the tracks best.
- Put it in **Park** (or **Neutral** if no Park) and **press the service brake or set the parking brakes**.
- Turn off **radios and noisy equipment**, and **quiet the passengers**.
- **Open the service door and the driver's window.** Look and listen for a train.

**Crossing the tracks:**
- **Check the crossing signals again** before going.
- At a **multiple-track** crossing, stop **only before the first track**. When you are sure no train is coming on **any** track, cross **all** of them without stopping until you are completely clear.
- Cross in a **low gear**. **Do not shift** while crossing.
- Gate drops once you are already crossing? Keep going and drive through it, even if the gate breaks.

*Worked example:* no white line is painted. You stop 10 ft from the nearest rail → too close. You stop 60 ft back → too far. Anywhere from **15 to 50 ft**, before the crossbuck, with the best view, is right.

### 10.4.4 Special crossing situations (p. 10-10)
- **Bus stalls or is trapped on the tracks:** get **everyone off and away** from the tracks **at once**. Move them far from the bus **at an angle** that is **away from the tracks and toward the train**.
- **Police officer at the crossing:** obey the officer. **No officer and the signal seems broken:** **call your dispatcher**, report it, and ask how to proceed.
- **View of the tracks is blocked:** plan routes with the **best sight distance**. Do not cross unless you can see far enough down the track to be **certain** no train is coming. Be **especially careful at passive crossings**. Even when **active signals** say the tracks are clear, you must still **look and listen**.
- **Containment or storage areas:** **"If it will not fit, do not commit!"** Know your bus length and the space past each crossing on your route and on activity trips. If there is a signal or stop sign on the far side, make sure the whole bus can stop **clear of the tracks**. Rule of thumb: **bus length + 15 ft**.
- *Worked example:* say your bus is 40 ft long. You need at least 40 + 15 = **55 ft** of clear space past the tracks before the next stop sign.

### 10.5 Student management (p. 10-11)
**Don't handle on-bus problems while loading or unloading.** Loading and unloading take **all** your attention — keep your eyes **outside** the bus. If there is a behavior problem, wait until the unloading students are **safely off and away**. If needed, **pull over** to deal with it.

**Serious problems:**
- Follow your **school's procedures** for discipline or taking away riding privileges.
- **Stop the bus** in a safe place **off the road** (a parking lot or driveway).
- **Secure the bus.** **Take the key** if you leave your seat.
- **Stand up** and speak **respectfully**, courteously and **firmly**. Remind the student of the expected behavior. Don't show anger, but show you mean business.
- If a seat change is needed, have the student move to a seat **near you**.
- **Never put a student off the bus** except **at school or at their own bus stop**. If the problem is so serious you cannot drive safely, call a **school administrator or the police** to remove the student.
- Always follow state or local procedures for getting help.

### 10.6 Anti-lock braking systems (ABS) (pp. 10-11–10-12)
**Which vehicles must have ABS** (U.S. DOT):
- **Air-brake** vehicles (trucks, buses, trailers, converter dollies) built **on or after March 1, 1998**.
- **Hydraulic-brake** trucks and buses with a GVWR of **10,000 lb or more**, built **on or after March 1, 1999**.
- Many older buses have ABS added voluntarily. A bus with ABS has a **yellow ABS malfunction lamp** on the **instrument panel**.

**How ABS helps:** without ABS, hard braking on a slippery road can lock the wheels. Locked **steering** wheels = **no steering**. Other wheels locked = **skid or spin**. ABS keeps wheels from locking so you **keep control**. You may or may not stop faster, but you should be able to **steer around** something while braking, and avoid skids from over-braking.

**Braking with ABS:** brake **as you always have**. Use only the force you need to stop safely and stay in control. In an **emergency stop, do not pump** the brakes on an ABS bus. As you slow, watch the bus and **ease off the brakes** (if safe) to stay in control.

**If ABS is not working** (p. 10-12): you **still have normal brakes** — drive and brake normally, and **get it serviced soon**.
- On **newer** buses the lamp comes on at start-up as a **bulb check** and goes out quickly. On **older** systems it may stay on until you are going **over 5 mph**.
- If the lamp **stays on** after the bulb check, or **comes on while driving**, you may have lost ABS at **1 or more wheels**.

**ABS will NOT:** let you drive faster, follow closer or drive less carefully; prevent **power or turning skids** (from spinning the drive wheels or taking a turn too fast); **necessarily shorten** stopping distance; **increase or decrease** ultimate stopping power (it is an **add-on**, not a replacement); change how you normally brake (it only acts when a wheel would lock); or make up for **bad brakes or poor maintenance**. The best safety feature is still **a safe driver** — drive so you never need ABS. (p. 10-12)

### 10.7.1 [CA] Strobe lights (p. 10-12)
- Some school buses have a **white strobe light on the roof**. If yours does, use it when **visibility is limited** — when you cannot easily see around the bus (front, back or sides).
- **[CA]** Use the strobe **only** when visibility is **500 ft or less** because of weather or air conditions, such as **fog, rain, snow, smoke or dust** (CVC §25257.7).

### 10.7.2 Driving in high winds (p. 10-12)
The side of a school bus works like a **sail**. Strong wind can push the bus sideways, off the road, or in extreme cases **tip it over**. If you are caught in strong winds:
- **Grip the wheel firmly** and try to **expect gusts**.
- **Slow down** to reduce the wind's effect, or **pull off the road and wait**.
- **Call your dispatcher** for more information on what to do.

### 10.7.3 [CA] Backing and tail swing (p. 10-13)
**Backing a school bus is strongly discouraged.** Back up **only when there is no other safe way** to move the bus, and **never** when **students are outside** the bus. If you must back:
- **[CA]** You **may flash the hazard lights** to warn drivers and people on foot (CVC §25257.5).
- **Post a lookout** to warn you of obstacles, people and vehicles. The lookout should **not** tell you how to back.
- Signal for **quiet** on the bus.
- Keep **checking all mirrors and rear windows**.
- Back **slowly and smoothly**.
- **No lookout?** Set the parking brake, **turn off the motor and take the keys**, and **walk to the rear** to see if the way is clear.
- At a **pick-up point**: **pick up students first**, then back, and watch for **late comers**.
- Make sure **all students are on the bus** before backing.
- At a **drop-off point**: **back first**, then unload the students.

**Tail swing (10.7.4):** a school bus can have up to **3 ft** of tail swing. **Check your mirrors before and during every turn** to watch it.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** In any emergency, get the students off the bus right away. → **Correct:** Students are usually safest **on the bus** unless staying exposes them to needless risk; evacuate for the **5 mandatory** reasons, and decide in time. (p. 10-7)
- **Trap:** Lead students downwind of a burning bus. → **Correct:** Lead them **upwind**, so smoke blows away from them; for HazMat, upwind **at least 300 ft**. (p. 10-7)
- **Trap:** The safe place is 100 ft off the road, away from oncoming traffic. → **Correct:** **In the direction of oncoming traffic**, so debris from a crash does not hit them. (p. 10-7)
- **Trap:** Move students away from a stalled bus in the direction the train is going. → **Correct:** Move them away from the tracks at an angle **toward the train**. (p. 10-10)
- **Trap:** Carry an injured student with a possible neck injury off the bus first. → **Correct:** **Do not move** a possible neck or spine injury unless the student's **life is in immediate danger**. (p. 10-8)
- **Trap:** At a crossing with several tracks, stop before each track. → **Correct:** Stop **only before the first** track, then cross them all. (p. 10-10)
- **Trap:** The gate came down while you were on the tracks — stop and wait. → **Correct:** **Drive through the gate**, even if it breaks. (p. 10-10)
- **Trap:** The gate stays down after the train passed, so drive around it. → **Correct:** **Do not drive around** it; **call your dispatcher**. (p. 10-9)
- **Trap:** Active signals show clear, so no need to look. → **Correct:** You must still **look and listen**. (p. 10-10)
- **Trap:** You need only as much space past the tracks as your bus is long. → **Correct:** Bus length **+ 15 ft**. (p. 10-10)
- **Trap:** Put an unruly student off the bus at the next corner. → **Correct:** **Never**, except at **school or the student's own stop**; call an administrator or police if needed. (p. 10-11)
- **Trap:** Pump the brakes in an emergency stop on an ABS bus. → **Correct:** **Do not pump**; brake normally and ease off as needed to stay in control. (p. 10-11)
- **Trap:** If the ABS fails, you have no brakes. → **Correct:** You still have **normal brakes**; get ABS serviced soon. (p. 10-12)
- **Trap:** Use the strobe light any time it is cloudy or dark. → **Correct:** **[CA]** Only when visibility is **500 ft or less** from fog, rain, snow, smoke, dust and similar conditions. (p. 10-12)
- **Trap:** The lookout should direct you as you back. → **Correct:** The lookout **only warns** you; they do **not** give backing directions. (p. 10-13)
- **Trap:** At a drop-off point, unload the students and then back up. → **Correct:** **Back first**, then unload. At a pick-up point, **load first**, then back. (p. 10-13)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 10 #7** When must you evacuate the bus?
→ When the bus is on fire or may catch fire; when it is stalled on or next to a railroad crossing; when the bus's position may change and make things more dangerous; when a crash is about to happen; and when a HazMat spill means you must get out quickly. (p. 10-7)

**TYK 10 #8** How far from the nearest rail should you stop at a railroad crossing?
→ No closer than 15 ft and no farther than 50 ft, at the spot where you can see the tracks best. (If a white stop line is painted, the front of the bus must stay behind it; if there is none, stop before the crossbuck.) (pp. 10-9–10-10)

**TYK 10 #9** What is a passive railroad crossing, and why should you be extra careful at one?
→ A crossing with no traffic control device (no lights, bells or gates). Nothing warns you that a train is coming, so the decision to cross rests entirely on you: you must recognize the crossing, search for trains, and decide whether there is enough room to cross safely. Do not cross unless you can see far enough down the track to be sure no train is coming. (pp. 10-8, 10-10)

**TYK 10 #10** How should you use your brakes if your bus has ABS?
→ Brake the way you always have: use only the force needed to stop safely and stay in control, do not pump the brakes in an emergency stop, and ease off the brakes as you slow (if safe) to stay in control. (p. 10-11)

## One-minute recap
- Recognize the hazard; call dispatch if time allows. Usually keep students **on the bus** — but **must** evacuate for fire/fire threat, stalled on or next to RR tracks, bus may shift, imminent crash, HazMat spill.
- Plan: **2** helpers per exit, 1 leader, teach everyone. Safe place: **100 ft** off road toward oncoming traffic; **upwind** of fire; HazMat **upwind 300 ft**; far from tracks toward the train; tornado → ditch, face down.
- Secure the bus (Park, brakes, engine off, key, hazards), hang the mike out the window, order evacuation, don't move neck/spine injuries, walk through, count, protect the scene.
- Crossings: school buses stop at all. Hazards on **200 ft** before; stop **15–50 ft** from the nearest rail (behind the white line / before the crossbuck); door and window open, radios off, look and listen.
- Multiple tracks: stop once, before the first. Low gear, no shifting. Gate drops while crossing → **drive through**. Gate stuck down after train → **call dispatch**. Storage space = bus **+ 15 ft**.
- Stalled on the tracks: everyone out, away at an angle **toward the train**. Blocked view or passive crossing: don't cross unless sure.
- Students: don't deal with problems while loading/unloading; stop off the road, secure, speak firmly; put students off **only at school or their stop**.
- ABS: air **3/1/1998**, hydraulic 10,000 lb+ **3/1/1999**; yellow lamp; brake normally, **don't pump**; failed ABS = normal brakes. **[CA]** Strobe at **500 ft** visibility or less; high wind = slow down or pull off; backing discouraged, lookout warns only, **[CA]** hazards allowed; tail swing up to **3 ft**.
