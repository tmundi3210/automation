# DT-01 · Doubles and triples

> **Handbook:** Section 7.1–7.4, pages 7-1–7-6 · **Test:** Doubles and Triples · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~40 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- Why doubles and triples tip over more easily, and which trailer is most likely to roll over.
- How to drive, space, and park a rig that pulls more than one trailer.
- The steps to couple and uncouple a double, including the converter dolly, and the one thing you must never do.
- Which trailer goes in front, and how high the second trailer must sit before you back the dolly under it.
- The extra things to inspect on doubles: shut-off valves, pintle hook, safety chains, light cords.
- How to check that air reaches every trailer, and what it means if no air comes out at the back.

## Learn it

A **double** is a tractor pulling **2** trailers. A **triple** pulls **3**. They are less steady than other trucks, and more things can go wrong, so you must drive and inspect with extra care. You should also study Sections 2, 5, and 6 (Driving Safely, Air Brakes, Combination Vehicles). (p. 7-1)

**[CA]** **Triples are not legal in California.** The handbook still covers them because many other states allow them, so triples questions can still be on the test. (p. 7-1)

**[CA]** The doubles/triples endorsement is earned by **knowledge test only**. Do **not** bring a set of doubles to your Class A skills test. (p. 7-1)

**Key words**
- **Doubles / double bottoms** = a tractor pulling 2 trailers. **Triples** = a tractor pulling 3 trailers.
- **Semitrailer** = a trailer with no front axle; its front rests on the tractor (or on a dolly).
- **Converter dolly (converter gear)** = a short unit with **1 or 2 axles** and a fifth wheel. It hooks onto the back of the first trailer, and the second trailer's front rests on it.
- **Fifth wheel** = the flat, round coupling plate that the front of a semitrailer sits on and locks to.
- **Kingpin** = the steel pin under the front of a semitrailer that the fifth wheel's jaws lock around. The **shank** is the pin's neck; the **head** is its wider end.
- **Pintle hook** = the hook on the back of a trailer that the dolly's tow bar hooks into. **Pintle eye** (ring hitch) = the ring on the end of the dolly's tow bar.
- **Landing gear** = the legs that hold up the front of a trailer when it is not coupled.
- **Petcock** = a small valve used to let air out of a tank (here, the dolly's air tank).
- **Shut-off valves** = valves in the service and emergency air lines at the back of a trailer. They close the lines so air does not escape.
- **Glad hands** = the couplers that join the air lines between units.
- **Dead axle** = an axle that is pulled along and has no engine power. Only the tractor's **drive axles** push the rig.
- **Rollover** = the rig tips onto its side. **Jackknife** = the rig folds at a hitch into a "V".

**What a double looks like (Figure 7.1, p. 7-2)**
```
Tractor ─ fifth wheel ─ LEAD trailer ─ pintle hook ◄ ring (pintle eye) ─ CONVERTER DOLLY (1-2 axles + fifth wheel) ─ kingpin ─ REAR trailer
                                        air hoses + light cord + safety chains run across this gap
```

### 7.1.1 Prevent rollover (p. 7-1)
- **Steer gently** and **go slowly** around corners, on-ramps, off-ramps, and curves.
- A speed that is safe on a curve for a straight truck or a single-trailer rig **may be too fast** for doubles or triples. *Why:* doubles and triples are **less stable** than other trucks.

### 7.1.2 Crack-the-whip effect (p. 7-1)
- When you make a quick lane change, the rear of the rig swings harder than the front ("rearward amplification"), like the tip of a whip. (p. 6-1)
- Because of this, doubles and triples are **more likely to turn over** than other combinations.
- The **last trailer** is the one **most likely to turn over**.
- Fix: **steer gently**. (Crack-the-whip is explained in more detail in Section 6.1.2 — see CV-01.)

### 7.1.3 Driving doubles day to day (pp. 7-1–7-2)
- **Inspect completely (7.1.3):** 2 or 3 trailers means more critical parts. **Check them all.**
- **Look far ahead (7.1.4):** you must drive **very smoothly** to avoid rollover or jackknife. Looking far ahead lets you slow down or change lanes **gradually**.
- **Manage space (7.1.5):** doubles are longer, and they **cannot be turned or stopped suddenly**. So:
  - leave **more following distance**,
  - make sure gaps are **big enough** before you enter or cross traffic,
  - be sure the **sides are clear** before changing lanes.
- **Adverse conditions (7.1.6):** in bad weather, on slippery roads, and in mountains, be **especially careful**. *Why:* you have **more length** and **more dead axles** for your drive axles to pull, so **skids and loss of traction** are more likely.
- **Parking (7.1.7):** don't get into a spot you **cannot pull straight through**. Know how the parking lot is laid out so you avoid a long, hard escape.

### 7.1.8 ABS on converter dollies (p. 7-2)
- Converter dollies built **on or after March 1, 1998** must have **anti-lock brakes (ABS)**.
- These dollies have a **yellow lamp** on the **left side**.

### 7.2.1 Coupling double trailers (pp. 7-2–7-3)
**Order of trailers:** put the **heavier** trailer **first**, right behind the tractor. The **lighter** trailer goes in the **rear**. *Why:* it gives the safest handling on the road. (p. 7-2)

**Step A — Secure the second (rear) trailer.** If it has **no spring brakes**: drive the tractor close, **connect the emergency line**, **charge** the trailer air tank, then **disconnect** the emergency line. This sets the trailer's emergency brakes (if the slack adjusters are adjusted right). **Chock the wheels** if you have any doubt about the brakes. (p. 7-2)

**Step B — Put the converter dolly in front of the rear trailer.** (p. 7-2)
- **Release the dolly brakes**: open the dolly air tank **petcock** — or, if the dolly has spring brakes, use the dolly parking brake control.
- If the distance is short, **roll the dolly by hand** so it lines up with the kingpin.
- Or use the tractor and first trailer to move it: back up close, couple the dolly to the first trailer, **lock the pintle hook**, raise the dolly support, pull the dolly as close as you can to the nose of the second trailer, lower the dolly support, unhook the dolly, and roll it into line with the kingpin.

**Step C — Connect the dolly to the front trailer.** Back the first trailer into position in front of the dolly tongue, hook the dolly on, **lock the pintle hook**, and secure the converter gear support in the **raised** position. (p. 7-3)

**Step D — Connect the dolly to the rear trailer.** (p. 7-3)
1. Make sure the rear trailer's **brakes are locked and/or wheels chocked**.
2. Check trailer height: The trailer should sit **a little below the middle of the dolly's fifth wheel**, so the dolly lifts it **a bit** as you back under.
3. **Back the dolly under** the rear trailer.
4. Raise the landing gear **slightly** off the ground. *Why:* prevents damage if the trailer moves.
5. **Test the coupling** by pulling against the pin of the second trailer.
6. **Look** at the coupling: **no space** between upper and lower fifth wheel, and the **locking jaws closed on the kingpin**.
7. Connect the **safety chains, air hoses, and light cords**.
8. **Close** the dolly air tank **petcock**, and **close** the shut-off valves at the **rear of the second trailer** (service and emergency).
9. **Open** the shut-off valves at the **rear of the first trailer** (and on the dolly, if it has them).
10. Raise the landing gear **completely**.
11. **Charge the trailer brakes** (push the "air supply" knob in). Then open the emergency line shut-off at the back of the second trailer to **make sure air comes out**. **No air = something is wrong, and the brakes will not work.**

### 7.2.2 Uncoupling double trailers (p. 7-3)
**Uncouple the rear trailer:**
1. Park in a **straight line** on **firm, level ground**.
2. Put on the **parking brakes** so the rig cannot move.
3. **Chock** the second trailer's wheels if it has **no spring brakes**.
4. Lower the second trailer's landing gear enough to **take some weight off the dolly**.
5. **Close** the air shut-offs at the rear of the **first** trailer (and on the dolly, if it has them).
6. Disconnect and secure all dolly **air and electric lines**.
7. **Release the dolly brakes.**
8. **Release the dolly's fifth-wheel latch.**
9. **Slowly** pull the tractor, first trailer, and dolly forward to pull the dolly out from under the rear trailer.

**Uncouple the converter dolly:**
1. Lower the dolly landing gear.
2. Disconnect the safety chains.
3. Put on the dolly (converter gear) **spring brakes** or **chock** its wheels.
4. Release the **pintle hook** on the first trailer.
5. Slowly pull clear of the dolly.

**Never unlock the pintle hook while the dolly is still under the rear trailer.** *Why:* the dolly **tow bar may fly up**, which can **injure** someone, and it makes the dolly **very hard to recouple**. (p. 7-3)

### 7.2.3 [CA] Triple trailers (p. 7-4)
- **Couple:** hook the tractor to the first trailer the normal tractor-semitrailer way. Then move the dolly into place and couple the **first trailer to the second** using the doubles method. The triple is now complete. Delete the parenthetical sentence.
- **Uncouple:** take off the **third** trailer first — pull the dolly out, then unhitch the dolly, the doubles way. Then uncouple the rest like any double.
- **[CA] Remember: operating triples is not allowed in California.**

### 7.2.4 Other combinations (p. 7-4)
There are too many kinds of truck-trailer and tractor-trailer rigs to cover. Learn the correct way to couple and uncouple **your** vehicle, and follow the **manufacturer's and/or owner's** specifications.

### 7.3.1 Extra inspection checks for doubles (pp. 7-4–7-5)
Use the **7-step inspection** from Section 2. Combinations have more of the same items (tires, wheels, lights, reflectors) plus some new ones. Do these checks **in addition to Step 5, the walk-around** (p. 7-4):

| Area | Check |
|---|---|
| Lower fifth wheel | Securely mounted to frame; no missing or damaged parts; **enough grease**; **no visible space** between upper and lower fifth wheel; locking jaws around the **shank, not the head**, of the kingpin; release arm seated and safety latch/lock engaged |
| Upper fifth wheel | Glide plate securely mounted to trailer frame; kingpin not damaged |
| Air and electric lines | Electric cord firmly plugged in and secured; air lines properly connected to glad hands, **no leaks**, secured with **enough slack for turns**; no damaged lines |
| Sliding fifth wheel | Slide not damaged, no parts missing; greased; **all locking pins present and locked**; no air leaks if air-powered; not so far **forward** that the tractor frame hits the landing gear or the cab hits the trailer in turns |
| Landing gear (p. 7-5) | **Fully raised**; nothing missing, bent, or damaged; crank handle in place and secured; no air or hydraulic leaks if power-operated |

**Doubles and triples only (p. 7-5):**
- **Shut-off valves** (at the rear of trailers, in service and emergency lines):
  - rear of the **front** trailers: **OPEN**
  - rear of the **last** trailer: **CLOSED**
  - **converter dolly air tank drain valve: CLOSED**
- Air lines supported; glad hands properly connected.
- A **spare tire** carried on the dolly is **secured**.
- The dolly's **pintle eye** is in place in the trailer's **pintle hook**, and the **pintle hook is latched**.
- **Safety chains** are secured to the trailer(s).
- **Light cords** are firmly plugged into the sockets on the trailers.

### 7.4.1 Doubles air brake check — does air reach every trailer? (p. 7-5)
First do the normal combination air brake checks from Section 6 (see CV-04). Then:
1. Hold the rig with the **tractor parking brake and/or wheel chocks**.
2. Wait for air pressure to reach **normal**.
3. **Push in the red "trailer air supply" knob.** This sends air to the **emergency (supply)** lines.
4. Use the **trailer handbrake** to send air to the **service** line.
5. Walk to the **back of the rig**. At the **last** trailer's rear, **open the emergency line shut-off valve**. You should **hear air escaping** — the whole system is charged. **Close** it.
6. **Open the service line valve** to confirm service air reaches every trailer (the trailer handbrake or brake pedal must be on for this). Then **close** it.
7. **No air from one or both lines?** Check that the shut-off valves on the trailers and dollies are **OPEN**.

*Why:* you **must have air all the way to the back** for all the brakes to work.

**Also on the doubles check (p. 7-6):** (a) **Tractor protection valve:** charge the system, shut the engine off, and step on and off the brake pedal to lower tank pressure. The trailer air supply knob should **pop out** (normal → emergency) at the maker's pressure, **usually 20–45 psi**. If it does not, a hose or trailer brake leak could drain all the tractor's air and set the emergency brakes, with possible loss of control. (b) **Trailer emergency brakes:** charge, check the trailer rolls freely, pull out the trailer air supply knob, then pull gently with the tractor — the trailer brakes should hold. (c) **Trailer service brakes:** normal pressure, release parking brakes, move slowly forward, apply the trailer brakes with the **hand control (trolley valve)** — you should feel them come on. Test with the hand valve, but in normal driving control them with the foot pedal.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** The first trailer, right behind the tractor, is the one most likely to roll over. → **Correct:** The **last** trailer is most likely to turn over, because of the crack-the-whip effect. (p. 7-1)
- **Trap:** A speed that is safe on a curve for a single-trailer rig is safe for doubles too. → **Correct:** It **may be too fast** for doubles or triples; they are less stable. (p. 7-1)
- **Trap:** Put the lighter trailer up front so the tractor pulls less weight. → **Correct:** The **heavier** trailer goes **first**, right behind the tractor; the lighter one goes in the rear. (p. 7-2)
- **Trap:** The rear trailer should sit a little higher than the fifth wheel so the dolly slides under easily. → **Correct:** It should be **slightly lower** than the center of the fifth wheel, so it is raised slightly when the dolly is pushed under. (p. 7-3)
- **Trap:** When coupling, open the shut-off valves at the rear of the second (last) trailer. → **Correct:** **Close** the valves at the rear of the **last** trailer and **open** those at the rear of the **first** trailer. (p. 7-3)
- **Trap:** You can unlock the pintle hook first, then pull the dolly out from under the rear trailer. → **Correct:** **Never** unlock the pintle hook while the dolly is still under the rear trailer — the tow bar may fly up and hurt someone. (p. 7-3)
- **Trap:** The converter dolly air tank drain valve should be left open. → **Correct:** It must be **CLOSED**. (Opening the petcock is how you *release* the dolly brakes while moving it by hand, before coupling.) (pp. 7-2, 7-5)
- **Trap:** Locking jaws closed around the head of the kingpin are fine. → **Correct:** The jaws must be around the **shank**, not the head. (p. 7-4)
- **Trap:** Triples are legal in California if you have the endorsement. → **Correct:** **[CA]** Triples are **not legal in California** at all. (pp. 7-1, 7-4)
- **Trap:** You take a skills test in a set of doubles to get the endorsement. → **Correct:** **[CA]** It is given by **knowledge test only**; do not bring doubles to the Class A skills test. (p. 7-1)
- **Trap:** To check air flow, open the shut-off valves at the rear of the first trailer. → **Correct:** Open the valves at the rear of the **last** trailer (one at a time), listen for air, then close them. (p. 7-5)
- **Trap:** If no air comes out at the back, the tractor compressor has failed. → **Correct:** First check that the shut-off valves on the trailers and dollies are **OPEN**. (p. 7-5)
- **Trap:** Doubles skid less on slippery roads because they have more axles. → **Correct:** The extra axles are **dead axles** your drive axles must pull, so **skids and loss of traction** are **more** likely. (p. 7-1)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 7 #1** What is a converter dolly?
→ A coupling device with 1 or 2 axles and a fifth wheel that lets a semitrailer be coupled to the rear of a tractor-trailer, making a double. (p. 7-2)

**TYK 7 #2** Do converter dollies have spring brakes?
→ Some do. If the dolly has spring brakes, use the dolly parking brake control (and set them when you uncouple the dolly); if not, release its brakes by opening the air tank petcock, and chock its wheels. (pp. 7-2, 7-3)

**TYK 7 #3** What 3 methods can you use to secure a second trailer before coupling?
→ Its spring brakes (if it has them); if no spring brakes, connect the emergency line, charge the trailer air tank, and disconnect the line to set the emergency brakes; and chock the wheels. (p. 7-2)

**TYK 7 #4** How do you check trailer height before coupling?
→ It must be slightly lower than the center of the dolly's fifth wheel, so the trailer is raised slightly when the dolly goes under. (p. 7-3)

**TYK 7 #5** What do you check in a visual check of the coupling?
→ No space between upper and lower fifth wheel, and the locking jaws closed on the kingpin (around the shank, not the head). (pp. 7-3, 7-4)

**TYK 7 #6** Why pull the dolly out from under a trailer before disconnecting it from the trailer in front?
→ If you unlock the pintle hook while the dolly is under the rear trailer, the tow bar may fly up, possibly injuring someone, and recoupling becomes very hard. (p. 7-3)

**TYK 7 #7** What do you check on the converter dolly? The pintle hook?
→ Dolly: air tank drain valve closed, spare tire (if carried) secured, pintle eye in place in the pintle hook, safety chains secured, air lines supported and glad hands connected, light cords firmly in sockets. Pintle hook: latched, with the pintle eye in place. (p. 7-5)

**TYK 7 #8** Shut-off valves at the rear of the last trailer? First trailer of doubles? Middle trailer of triples?
→ Last trailer: CLOSED. First trailer of doubles: OPEN. Middle trailer of triples: OPEN (all front trailers open). (p. 7-5)

**TYK 7 #9** How can you test that air flows to all trailers?
→ Hold the rig, build normal pressure, push in the red trailer air supply knob, apply the trailer handbrake; at the rear of the last trailer open the emergency line shut-off (air should escape), close it, then open the service line valve, then close it. No air → check that shut-offs are OPEN. (p. 7-5)

**TYK 7 #10** How do you know a converter dolly has anti-lock brakes?
→ It has a yellow ABS lamp on the left side; dollies built on or after March 1, 1998 must have ABS. (p. 7-2)

## One-minute recap
- **[CA]** Triples are **not legal** in California, but they are on the test. The endorsement is **knowledge test only**.
- Doubles/triples are **less stable**; the **last trailer** is most likely to roll over (**crack-the-whip**). **Steer gently**, go **slowly** on curves and ramps — a single-trailer speed may be too fast.
- Look **far ahead**, leave **more space**, be extra careful in bad weather (**dead axles** → skids), and park where you can **pull straight through**.
- Dollies built on or after **March 1, 1998** need ABS — **yellow lamp, left side**.
- **Heavier trailer first.** Rear trailer **slightly lower** than the fifth wheel center before backing the dolly under. **Lock the pintle hook**; test by pulling against the pin; check jaws and gap.
- Coupled: dolly **petcock closed**, rear-of-**last**-trailer valves **closed**, rear-of-**first** valves **open**. Then charge and check for air at the back — **no air = no brakes**.
- **Never unlock the pintle hook** with the dolly still under the rear trailer — the **tow bar may fly up**.
- Air flow check: push in the **red** knob, set the **trailer handbrake**, open each shut-off at the **back of the last trailer** and **listen**; no air → check shut-offs are **open**.
