# PV-01 · Passenger vehicle inspection and loading

> **Handbook:** Section 4–4.2, pages 4-1–4-3 · **Test:** Passenger Transport · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~40 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- Who needs a **"P" (passenger) endorsement**, what the **15-passenger van** restriction is, and which tests you must pass.
- What to check on a bus before you drive: the systems, the doors and panels, the inside of the bus, roof hatches and seat belts.
- How to load carry-on baggage so the aisle stays clear and riders can get out in an emergency.
- Which hazardous materials a bus **may** carry, which it must **never** carry, and the **100 lb** and **500 lb** limits.
- What the **standee line** is, what to announce at a stop, and the rules for charter riders and animals.

## Learn it

This lesson is about the **bus itself and the people who ride it**. A bus carries people, not freight, so the rules are about keeping riders safe: a safe bus, a clear aisle, no dangerous cargo, and calm loading and unloading. (p. 4-1)

**Key words**
- **"P" endorsement** = the passenger mark added to your CDL. You need it to drive a bus or other vehicle built to carry a lot of people.
- **CLP** = commercial learner permit, the permit you hold while you learn, before you have your CDL.
- **Farm labor vehicle** = a vehicle used to carry farm workers.
- **General public paratransit vehicle** = a bus or van that gives flexible or door-to-door rides to the general public.
- **Defect** = something broken or not working right on the vehicle.
- **Recapped / regrooved tire** = a used tire that has had new tread glued on (recapped) or new grooves cut into the old tread (regrooved).
- **Circuit breaker** = a switch that cuts off electric power when there is a problem and can be reset. A **fuse** does the same job but must be replaced once it blows.
- **Access panel** = a small door on the outside of the bus, for example over the baggage bay, the restroom service hookup, or the engine.
- **Emergency exit** = a door, window or roof hatch that riders can use to get out in an emergency.
- **Roof hatch** = an emergency exit in the roof of the bus.
- **HazMat** = hazardous materials: things that can hurt people, health or property while they are moved (fuel, poisons, explosives and so on).
- **ORM-D** = "Other Regulated Material – Domestic", a low-risk HazMat group (the handbook's examples are hair spray and charcoal).
- **Standee** = a rider who stands while the bus moves.
- **Charter bus** = a bus hired by a group for a trip.
- **Service dog / guide dog / signal dog** = a trained dog that helps a person with a disability.

### 4 Who needs the "P" endorsement (p. 4-1) [CA]
- You need a CDL with a **"P" endorsement** to drive a vehicle **designed to transport more than 10 persons, including the driver**.
- **[CA]** California's list of passenger transportation vehicles includes (but is not limited to) a **bus**, a **farm labor vehicle**, and a **general public paratransit vehicle**, when it is designed, used, or maintained to carry **more than 10 passengers including the driver** — whether it is for hire, for profit, or run by a **nonprofit** group.
- **15-passenger restriction:** if your driving test is taken in a **van** built or used for **15 people or fewer (counting the driver)**, your license is **restricted** to a **15-passenger-or-less small-size bus**. *Why:* you showed your skill only in a small vehicle, so you are licensed only for that size.
- **Tests you must pass:** written tests covering **Sections 2 and 4**; add the **Section 5** (air brakes) test if your bus has air brakes; then pass the **skills tests** for your license class.
- **CLP holders:** a CLP holder with a "P" and/or "S" (school bus) endorsement **may not carry passengers**. The only people allowed on board are **federal/state auditors and inspectors, test examiners, other trainees, and the CDL holder riding along** with the learner.

*Worked example:* a van is built for 10 persons including the driver. That is **not more than 10**, so the "P" rule in this section is not triggered. A bus built for 12 persons including the driver **is** more than 10, so you need the "P" endorsement.

### 4.1 Checking the bus before you drive (p. 4-1)
- **Read the last driver's inspection report first.** Sign it **only if** the defects on it were **certified as repaired**, or certified as **not needing repair**. Your signature says that the earlier defects have been taken care of. *Why:* you must not drive a bus with a known problem that was never fixed.
- The full inspection steps are in Section 11. At the DMV vehicle inspection test you may use **only one** memory aid (the ones shown at the end of Section 11), and it **cannot include instructions** on how to do the inspection.
- **Vehicle systems (4.1.1)** — make sure these work before driving:
  - **Service brakes**, including the air hose couplings if the bus pulls a trailer or semitrailer.
  - **Parking brake.**
  - **Steering** mechanism.
  - **Lights and reflectors.**
  - **Tires** — the **front wheels must not have recapped or regrooved tires**. *Why:* a front tire failure makes the bus very hard to steer.
  - **Horn**, **windshield wiper(s)**, **rear-vision mirror(s)**.
  - **Coupling devices** (if present), **wheels and rims**.
  - **Emergency equipment:** the **fire extinguishers** and **emergency reflectors** the law requires, and **spare electrical fuses** — unless the bus has **circuit breakers**.

### 4.1.2 Doors, panels and the bus interior (p. 4-2)
- **Outside (4.1.2):** as you walk around, **close any open emergency exits** and **any open access panels** (baggage, restroom service, engine, and so on) **before driving**.
- **Inside (4.1.3):** **people sometimes damage buses that are left alone**, so **always check the inside** before you drive. *Why:* a broken seat or rail can hurt a rider.
- **Aisles and stairwells** must always be **clear**.
- These must be in safe working order:
  - each **handhold and railing**;
  - the **floor covering**;
  - **signaling devices**, including the **restroom emergency buzzer** if the bus has a restroom;
  - **emergency exit handles**.
- **Seats** must be safe, and **every seat must be securely fastened** to the bus.
- **Emergency exits:** check that they **open easily**, are **marked correctly**, and that any required **buzzers or devices work**.
- **Never drive with an emergency exit door or window open.**
- The **"Emergency Exit"** sign on an emergency door must be **clearly visible**.
- A **red emergency door light**, if the bus has one, must work. **Turn it on at night or any other time you use your outside lights.**
- **Farm labor vehicles:** in the passenger area, **cutting tools and tools with sharp edges** go in a **covered container**. All **other tools, equipment, or materials** must be **secured to the body** of the vehicle. *Why:* loose tools fly around in a sudden stop.
- The **driver and all passengers must wear seat belts** (the handbook states this right after the farm labor rules).

### 4.1.4 Roof hatches and your seat belt (p. 4-2)
- You **may lock some** emergency roof hatches **partly open** for fresh air.
- **Do not** leave them open as a **regular practice**.
- With a hatch open, remember the bus is **taller** — its **clearance is higher**. *Why:* an open hatch can hit a low bridge, sign or branch.
- **4.1.5 Seat belt:** the driver's seat should have a seat belt. **Always use it.**

### 4.2 Loading and trip start — baggage (p. 4-2)
- **Do not let riders leave carry-on bags in a doorway or the aisle.** Nothing should be in the aisle that could **trip** someone.
- Secure baggage and freight so that it does not get damaged and so that it:
  - lets the **driver move freely and easily**;
  - lets riders **get out by any window or door** in an emergency;
  - **protects riders** from injury if carry-ons **fall or shift**.

### 4.2.1 Spotting hazardous materials (pp. 4-2–4-3)
- Watch for cargo or baggage that contains HazMat. **Most HazMat cannot be carried on a bus.**
- Shippers must mark HazMat containers with the material's **name**, **ID number**, and **hazard label**.
- There are **9 different** hazard labels. Each is **4 inches** and **diamond-shaped**. **Watch for the diamond-shaped labels.**
- **Do not carry any HazMat unless you are sure the rules allow it.**
- The handbook's hazard class chart (Figure 4.1), in short:

| Class | Name | Handbook examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explosives | ammunition, dynamite, fireworks |
| 2 | Gases | propane, oxygen, helium |
| 3 | Flammable (liquids) | gasoline, acetone |
| 4 | Flammable solids | matches, fuses |
| 5 | Oxidizers | ammonium nitrate, hydrogen peroxide |
| 6 | Poisons | pesticides, arsenic |
| 7 | Radioactive | uranium, plutonium |
| 8 | Corrosives | hydrochloric acid, battery acid |
| 9 | Miscellaneous hazardous materials | formaldehyde, asbestos |
| None | ORM-D | hair spray or charcoal |
| None | Combustible liquids | fuel oils, lighter fluid |

### 4.2.2 HazMat a bus may carry — and must never carry (p. 4-3)
**A bus MAY carry:**
- **small-arms ammunition labeled ORM-D**;
- **emergency hospital supplies**;
- **drugs**;
- **small amounts of other HazMat**, but **only if the shipper cannot send them any other way**.

**A bus must NEVER carry:**
- **Division 2.3 poison gas**, **liquid Class 6 poison**, **tear gas**, or **irritating material**;
- **more than 100 pounds of solid Class 6 poisons**;
- **explosives in the space where people ride** — except small-arms ammunition;
- **labeled radioactive materials in the space where people ride**;
- allowed HazMat weighing **over 500 lb in all**, or **over 100 lb of any single class**.

*Worked example:* a load has 90 lb of one allowed class and 90 lb of another. Each class is under 100 lb and the total (180 lb) is under 500 lb, so the weight limits are met. If one class were 120 lb, it would break the **100 lb per class** rule, even though the total is under 500 lb.

**Riders and HazMat:**
- Riders sometimes bring **unlabeled** HazMat on board. **Do not let riders carry on common hazards such as car batteries or gasoline.**
- **Oxygen is allowed** when it is **medically prescribed** for the rider, **in the rider's possession**, and in a **container made for personal use**.

**Wheelchairs** carried on buses (except school buses):
- must have **brakes** or another mechanical way to **hold still while being raised or lowered** on the wheelchair platform;
- batteries must be **spill resistant** and **securely attached** to the wheelchair;
- may **not use flammable fuel**.
- (School bus wheelchair rules are in CCR, Title 13 §1293.)

### 4.2.3 Standee line (p. 4-3)
- **No rider may stand forward of the rear of the driver's seat.**
- Buses built to allow standing must have a **2-inch line on the floor**, or some other way to show riders where they cannot stand. This is the **standee line**.
- **All standing riders must stay behind it.** *Why:* riders up front block the driver's view and can fall into the driver or the windshield.

### 4.2.4 At your destination, loading and unloading (p. 4-3)
- At the destination or at stops along the way, **announce**:
  1. the **location**;
  2. the **reason for stopping**;
  3. the **next departure time**;
  4. the **bus number**.
- **Remind riders to take their carry-ons** when they get off.
- If the aisle is **lower than the seats**, warn riders about the **step-down**, ideally **before** the bus has fully stopped.
- **Charter bus drivers** should **not let riders on until departure time**. *Why:* it helps **prevent theft or vandalism** of the bus.
- **Loading and unloading (4.2.5):** make sure riders are **safely on the bus before you close the door(s) and pull away**. Give them **time to sit down or brace themselves** before you leave. **Start and stop as smoothly as possible** so no one gets hurt.

### 4.2.6 Animals (p. 4-3) [CA]
- **Carrying animals is prohibited**, except **certified service, guide, or signal dogs** used by passengers with physical disabilities (California Civil Code §54.2).

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** You need a "P" endorsement for a vehicle built for 10 or more people. → **Correct:** **more than 10** persons, **including the driver**. Exactly 10 is not "more than 10". (p. 4-1)
- **Trap:** Passing the skills test in a 15-passenger van lets you drive any bus. → **Correct:** you are **restricted to a 15-passenger-or-less** small-size bus. (p. 4-1)
- **Trap:** A CLP holder with a "P" endorsement may carry passengers if a CDL holder rides along. → **Correct:** a CLP holder may carry **no passengers** except auditors/inspectors, test examiners, other trainees, and the accompanying CDL holder. (p. 4-1)
- **Trap:** Sign the previous driver's report as soon as you read it. → **Correct:** sign **only if** the defects were **certified as repaired** or as not needing repair. Your signature certifies that. (p. 4-1)
- **Trap:** Regrooved tires are fine on the front of a bus if the tread is deep. → **Correct:** front wheels must have **no recapped or regrooved** tires. (p. 4-1)
- **Trap:** Only emergency exits must be closed before driving. → **Correct:** close open emergency exits **and** open access panels (baggage, restroom service, engine). (p. 4-2)
- **Trap:** You may leave roof hatches locked partly open all the time for fresh air. → **Correct:** you **may** lock some partly open, but **not as a regular practice** — and remember the higher clearance. (p. 4-2)
- **Trap:** The limit is 500 lb of each HazMat class. → **Correct:** **500 lb total**, and **no more than 100 lb of any one class**. (p. 4-3)
- **Trap:** A bus may carry tear gas if it is labeled. → **Correct:** tear gas, Division 2.3 poison gas, liquid Class 6 poison and irritating material are **never** allowed. (p. 4-3)
- **Trap:** No oxygen tanks may ever be carried on a bus. → **Correct:** a rider's **medically prescribed** oxygen, in their possession, in a **personal-use container**, is allowed. (p. 4-3)
- **Trap:** Riders may stand anywhere in the aisle. → **Correct:** no one may stand **forward of the rear of the driver's seat** — stay behind the **standee line**. (p. 4-3)
- **Trap:** Tell riders about the step-down after the bus has stopped. → **Correct:** it is best to tell them **before** coming to a complete stop. (p. 4-3)
- **Trap:** Let charter riders board early so they can settle in. → **Correct:** do **not** let them on **until departure time**, to prevent theft or vandalism. (p. 4-3)
- **Trap:** Small pets in carriers may ride. → **Correct:** animals are **prohibited** except **certified service, guide, or signal dogs**. (p. 4-3)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 4 #1** Name some things to check inside a bus during a vehicle inspection.
→ Each **handhold and railing**; the **floor covering**; **signaling devices**, including the **restroom emergency buzzer** if there is a restroom; **emergency exit handles**; that **all seats are securely fastened**; that **emergency exits** open easily, are correctly marked and their buzzers work; that the **"Emergency Exit" sign** is clearly visible and any **red emergency door light** works; and that **aisles and stairwells are clear**. (p. 4-2)

**TYK 4 #2** What are some HazMat you can transport by bus?
→ **Small-arms ammunition labeled ORM-D**, **emergency hospital supplies**, and **drugs**. Small quantities of other HazMat are also allowed, but only when the shipper has no other way to ship them. A rider's medically prescribed oxygen in a personal-use container is also allowed. (p. 4-3)

**TYK 4 #3** What are some HazMat you cannot transport by bus?
→ **Division 2.3 poison gas**, **liquid Class 6 poison**, **tear gas**, **irritating material**; **more than 100 lb of solid Class 6 poisons**; **explosives** in the space where people ride (except small-arms ammunition); **labeled radioactive materials** in the space where people ride; and **more than 500 lb total** of allowed HazMat or **more than 100 lb of any one class**. Also, do not let riders carry on car batteries or gasoline. (p. 4-3)

**TYK 4 #4** What is a standee line?
→ A **2-inch line on the floor** (or another way of marking) on buses built to allow standing. It shows riders where they may **not** stand. **No rider may stand forward of the rear of the driver's seat**; all standing riders must stay behind the line. (p. 4-3)

## One-minute recap
- **"P" endorsement** = vehicle designed for **more than 10 persons including the driver**. [CA] Includes buses, farm labor vehicles and paratransit vehicles, for hire, profit or nonprofit.
- Tested in a **15-or-less van** → restricted to a **15-passenger-or-less** small bus. Knowledge tests: **Sections 2 and 4** (+ **5** for air brakes). **CLP holders carry no passengers** (only examiners, inspectors, trainees, the CDL holder).
- Sign the last driver's report **only if** defects were certified repaired or not needing repair. **No recapped or regrooved front tires**; carry **fire extinguishers**, **reflectors**, **spare fuses** (unless circuit breakers).
- Close **emergency exits and access panels**. Check the **interior** every time: handholds, floor, signaling devices, exit handles, **seats fastened**, **aisles clear**. Red emergency door light on **at night / with outside lights**.
- Roof hatches may be locked **partly open**, but not routinely. Always wear your **seat belt**.
- **Nothing in the aisle.** Baggage must let the driver move and riders escape by **any window or door**.
- HazMat: **9 labels, 4-inch diamonds**. Allowed: **ORM-D small-arms ammo, hospital supplies, drugs**. Never: **Div 2.3 poison gas, liquid Class 6, tear gas, irritants**; **> 100 lb solid Class 6**; **> 500 lb total / > 100 lb of any class**; explosives or radioactive where people ride. No **car batteries or gasoline**; prescribed **oxygen OK**.
- **Standee line** (**2-inch**): no one forward of the rear of the driver's seat. Announce **location, reason, departure time, bus number**. Charter riders board **at departure time**. Animals: **service, guide or signal dogs only**.
