# SK-01 · The vehicle inspection test

> **Handbook:** Section 11, pages 11-1–11-11 · **Test:** Skills Tests · **Exam weight:** Medium · **Study time:** ~50 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- The **English-only** rule, the **2 warnings / 3rd = fail** rule, and the **3 attempts** you get for all three skills tests.
- What you must **point to, touch or name, and say out loud** for each part of the truck — and why the examiner wants the "why", not just the "what".
- The numbers the examiner expects you to say: belt play, governor cut-out, tread depth, pushrod travel, leaf springs, DEF level.
- The brake checks you must do in the cab, and which mistakes are an **automatic failure** of the whole inspection.
- The extra items for a **school bus**, a **trailer** and a **coach/transit bus**.
- How the test is given: **1 of 4** versions for Class A, **1 of 3** for Class B and C.

## Learn it

The vehicle inspection test is the **first** of your three skills tests. It is not a written test. You stand next to the vehicle you brought, and you show the examiner that you know **what to check** and **why** before you drive it. You walk around the vehicle and, for each item, you **name it, point to it or touch it**, and **explain out loud** what you are looking for. (p. 11-1)

You must **pass this test first**. If you fail it, the other two tests (basic control skills and road test) are put off to another day. (pp. 11-1, 11-11)

**Key words**
- **Examiner** = the DMV person who gives and scores your skills tests.
- **Verbalize** = say out loud. On this test, a check you do but do not explain may not earn credit.
- **Governor cut-out** = the air pressure at which the air compressor stops pumping because the tanks are full.
- **Sight glass** = a small clear window on a tank or part that lets you see the fluid level inside.
- **Dipstick** = a long metal stick you pull out to read an oil or fluid level.
- **Slack adjuster** = the arm that connects an air brake chamber's pushrod to the brake; it keeps the brake in adjustment.
- **Pushrod** = the rod that comes out of a brake chamber and moves the slack adjuster.
- **Brake chamber** = the round can that turns air pressure into a push on the brake.
- **Tread depth** = how deep the grooves in a tire are, measured in 32nds of an inch.
- **Lug nuts** = the nuts that hold a wheel on the hub.
- **Budd spacing / spacers** = the parts that keep dual (side-by-side) wheels evenly apart.
- **DEF (diesel exhaust fluid)** = a fluid used by some diesel engines' clean-exhaust (after-treatment) system; it has its own tank and a dash gauge.
- **Fifth wheel** = the flat, round coupling plate on the tractor that the trailer rests on.
- **Kingpin** = the heavy steel pin under the front of the trailer that the fifth wheel's **locking jaws** grab.
- **Apron** = the flat plate on the underside of the trailer around the kingpin.
- **Pintle hook** = a hook-type hitch used on some trucks and trailers instead of a fifth wheel.
- **Glad hands** = the couplers that join the air lines between the tractor and trailer.
- **Landing gear** = the legs that hold up the front of a trailer when it is not coupled.
- **Hydro-Boost** = a type of power-assisted hydraulic brake system found on some vehicles; it has its own check.

### 11.1 Test rules everyone must know (p. 11-1)
- **No interpreters.** The whole test is done **in English** (a federal rule cited in the handbook). (p. 11-1)
- If you speak another language or do not understand English instructions during any skills test, you get a **verbal warning** the first **2** times on that test date. The **3rd** time, the test ends as an **automatic failure**. This rule covers all three skills tests. (p. 11-1)
- A vehicle whose parts are **marked or labeled** (for example, stickers naming each part) **cannot be used** for the test. *Why:* you must know the parts yourself. (p. 11-1)
- The **brake lights, emergency flashers, turn signals and horn** must be checked. If any of them **do not work**, the skills and road tests are **postponed**. (p. 11-1)
- You may use the handbook's **inspection guide** (the memory aid page), but you **may not write notes** or instructions on it. (p. 11-1)
- You get **3 tries in all** to pass the three skills tests (vehicle inspection, basic control skills and road test). (p. 11-1)
- *Why do it the same way every time:* a fixed order helps you learn every step and makes you less likely to skip one under test pressure. (p. 11-1)

### 11.2.1 Walking up and the engine compartment (pp. 11-1–11-2)
**As you walk up** (engine off): look at the vehicle's general condition — damage, or leaning to one side. Look **under** it for fresh **oil, coolant, grease or fuel** leaks. Look around it for hazards to moving: people, other vehicles, objects, **low wires or tree limbs**. Make sure the **parking brakes are set and/or the wheels are chocked**. (p. 11-1)

**Engine compartment** — what you check and say (p. 11-2):
| Item | What you say you are checking |
|---|---|
| Leaks / hoses | No puddles under the vehicle, no drips under the engine or transmission, hoses in good shape and not leaking |
| Water pump, alternator, air compressor | **Identify** each one; mounted securely; not leaking; alternator wires fastened |
| Oil level | Engine **off**; show where the **dipstick** is; level in the safe range, **above the refill mark** |
| Coolant level | Level shows in the **sight glass** (radiator or reservoir); if there is no sight glass, **describe** what you would look for after taking off the radiator cap |
| Power steering fluid | Dipstick or sight glass; **above the refill mark** |
| Windshield washer fluid | Level OK and **cap secure** |
| Belts (power steering, water pump, alternator, air compressor) | Snug — **1/2 to 3/4 inch** of play at the **center** of the belt — and no cracks, frays, loose fibers or wear |
| Hydraulic brake master cylinder | Securely attached, not leaking; brake fluid **between the add and full marks** |

- If one of those parts is **not belt-driven**, you must **tell the examiner** which one, and check that it works, is not damaged or leaking, and is mounted securely. (p. 11-2)
- *Worked example:* you press the middle of the alternator belt and it moves about 5/8 inch. That is inside the 1/2–3/4 inch range, so it is snug enough — then you still check it for cracks and frays.

**Safe start** (p. 11-2): shift lever in **Neutral** (or **Park** for an automatic), **press the clutch** before starting, start the engine, keep the clutch down until the engine reaches **idle speed**, then let the clutch out **slowly**.

### 11.2.2 Cab check after the engine starts (pp. 11-3–11-4)
| Gauge / item | What should happen |
|---|---|
| Oil pressure gauge | Works; pressure **rises or is normal**, or the warning light **goes off**; oil temperature gauge (if any) slowly rises to normal |
| Temperature gauge | Works; temperature starts to **climb to normal**, or the light is off |
| Air gauge | Works; build air to **governor cut-out**, about **120–140 psi** (or the maker's number) |
| Ammeter / voltmeter | Shows the alternator/generator is **charging**, or the warning light is off |
| Mirrors and windshield | Clean, adjusted from inside; windshield clean, **no illegal stickers**, nothing blocking the view, no glass damage |
| Wipers / washers | Arms and blades secure, not damaged, move smoothly; washers work if equipped |
| Horn | Air and/or electric horn works |
| Heater / defroster | Both work |
| Safety belt | Securely mounted, adjusts, latches, not ripped or frayed |

**Emergency equipment** (p. 11-3): **spare electrical fuses**; **3 red reflective triangles**, **or 6 fuses**, **or 3 liquid-burning flares**; a **fire extinguisher** that is **charged** and **securely mounted**. If the vehicle has no electrical fuses, you must **say so** to the examiner.
**Optional equipment** you may mention: **tire chains** (where winter needs them), **tire-changing equipment**, a **list of emergency phone numbers**, an **accident reporting kit**. (p. 11-3)

**Lights** (p. 11-3):
- Dash indicators come on for the **left and right turn signals**, **4-way flashers**, **high beams** and the **ABS** light.
- Outside, all lights and reflectors are clean, working, not broken or missing, and the **right color**: **clearance lights red on the rear, amber everywhere else**; **red reflectors on the rear, amber elsewhere**. Also check headlights (high and low), taillights, backing lights, turn signals, flashers, brake lights, reflector tape and license plate light(s).
- Check the **brake lights, turn signals and 4-way flashers separately**. (p. 11-3)

### 11.2.2 Brake checks in the cab (p. 11-4)
These are where people fail. **Several mistakes here fail the whole inspection.**
- **Parking brake check:** fasten your **seat belt**. With air at governor cut-out and the parking brake on (trailer brakes **released** on a combination), **gently** try to pull forward — the parking brake should hold. On a combination, also set only the **trailer parking brake** and gently pull against it. (p. 11-4)
- **Hydraulic brake check:** **pump the pedal 3 times, then hold it down for 5 seconds.** The pedal should **not sink** during those 5 seconds. If there is a **reserve (backup) system**, turn the key off, press the pedal and **listen for the reserve pump motor**. Make sure the warning buzzer or light is **off**. **Not doing both parts of the hydraulic check is an automatic failure** of the inspection. (p. 11-4)
- **Hydro-Boost check** (if equipped): engine off, parking brake released, press and release the pedal several times to use up the pressure. Hold the pedal with **light pressure (15–25 pounds)** and start the engine at idle. If it works, the pedal **gives a little and then holds**, and it takes less force to hold it. (p. 11-4)
- **Air brake check** (air brakes only): do the checks marked with an **asterisk (*)** in Section 5. You must **do each one AND say the numbers**. The order is up to you, as long as you do each check correctly. Doing them wrong = **automatic failure of the entire vehicle inspection**. (p. 11-4) The asterisk checks are (pp. 5-8–5-9):
  | Check | What you do and say |
  |---|---|
  | **Applied leakage** (1-minute hold) | Air at cut-out (120–140 psi) — say when cut-out happened — then engine off, wheels chocked if needed, parking brake (and tractor protection valve) released, foot brake fully on and held **1 minute** after the gauge settles. Maximum loss: **3 psi** single vehicle, **4 psi** combination of 2, **6 psi** combination of 3 or more (**3 psi** if the towed units have no air brakes). Say how much it lost and the limit for your vehicle. |
  | **Low air warning** | Key on; fan off air by pumping the foot brake. The buzzer/light/flag must come on **before the pressure drops below 55 psi** (or the maker's level). Say when it came on and the limit. On a **large bus** the warning often comes on at **80–85 psi**: say the **55–75 psi** range and tell the examiner your bus is built to warn at a higher pressure. **Farm labor vehicles** and **Type I school buses** must have **both a sound and a visible** warning. |
  | **Spring brake test** | First **release the parking brake** (and the **tractor protection valve** on a combination), engine running or not, then keep fanning the air down. The parking brake knob (and tractor protection valve on a tractor-trailer) should **pop out**, normally at **20–45 psi**. Say the pressure where it happened. |
- **Bus with an emergency park brake air tank (triple reservoir):** the parking brake knob **will not pop out**, so you prove the spring brakes work another way. When the air is down to about **20 psi**, take out the wheel chocks if needed, **leave the parking brake released**, and with the engine running put the bus in a **forward gear** and try to drive ahead. The spring brakes should **drag and keep the bus from moving easily**. If they don't, your **road test is postponed**. Only do this on **single vehicles** with a separate parking brake tank — **never on a combination**. (p. 5-9)
- **Service brake check:** roll ahead at about **5 mph** and press the service brake to stop. The vehicle should **stop** and **not pull to either side**. (p. 11-4)

> Note: the handbook also describes a rate-of-air-buildup check in Section 5 (p. 5-9), but it is not one of the asterisk items. It is taught in the Air Brakes lessons.

### 11.3.1–11.3.3 Outside: steering, suspension and brakes (p. 11-5)
- **Steering box and hoses:** box mounted securely, not leaking, **no missing nuts or bolts**; no power steering fluid leaks or hose damage.
- **Steering linkage:** the links, arms and rods from the box to the wheel are not worn or cracked; joints and sockets not worn or loose; **no missing nuts, bolts or cotter keys**. (A cotter key is a small split pin that keeps a nut from backing off.)
- **Springs:** check the **leaf springs** for leaves that are gone, out of place, cracked or broken. When **1/4 or more** of the leaves are gone or broken, the vehicle is **"out of service"**. Also check coil springs, torsion bars, torque arms and **air ride** parts (damage and leaks).
  - *Worked example:* a spring pack has 12 leaves. 1/4 of 12 = 3. If 3 or more are broken or missing, the vehicle is out of service.
- **Mounts:** cracked or broken spring hangers, missing or damaged bushings, broken/loose/missing bolts and **u-bolts** — check where they attach to the **frame and axle**.
- **Shock absorbers:** secure and not leaking.
- **Slack adjusters and pushrods:** mounted securely, no broken/loose/missing parts. On **manual** slack adjusters, the pushrod should **not move more than 1 inch** when you pull it by hand (brakes **released**).
- **Brake chambers:** not leaking, cracked or dented; mounted securely; no loose or missing clamps.
- **Brake hoses/lines:** can deliver air or fluid; not cracked, worn or frayed; fittings tight and not leaking.
- **Drums:** no cracks, dents or holes; no loose or missing bolts; no debris, oil or grease on them.
- **Linings:** where you can see them, **not dangerously thin**. Some drums have openings; there, some lining must be visible.
- Be ready to inspect suspension and brakes on **every axle** — power unit and trailer. (p. 11-5)

### 11.3.4–11.3.6 Outside: wheels, side and rear (pp. 11-6–11-7)
- **Rims:** not damaged or bent; **no welding repairs**; **rust trails** may mean the rim is loose. (p. 11-6)
- **Tires** — check **every** tire (p. 11-6):
  - **Tread depth:** at least **4/32 inch on steering-axle tires** and **2/32 inch on all other tires**.
  - Condition: even wear; no cuts or damage to tread or sidewalls; valve caps and stems present and undamaged.
  - **No mixing** radial and bias-ply tires. **Bus front tires may not be recapped, retreaded or regrooved.**
  - **Inflation: use a tire gauge.** You get **no credit** for kicking a tire or hitting it with a mallet.
- **Hub oil / axle seals:** not leaking; if there is a sight glass, the oil level is OK. (p. 11-6)
- **Lug nuts:** all there, no cracks or bending, no signs of looseness such as **rust trails or shiny threads**; bolt holes not cracked. (p. 11-6)
- **Spacers (Budd spacing):** not bent, damaged or rusted through; evenly centered so the **dual tires are evenly apart**; **nothing stuck between the duals**. (p. 11-6)
- **Side of vehicle:** doors, hinges and seals; mirrors and brackets secure and not too dirty; windows clean and working; **fuel tank** secure, **cap tight**, no leaks; **drive shaft** not bent or cracked, **U-joints** secure. (p. 11-6)
- **Exhaust:** no damage or leak signs such as **rust or carbon soot**; no cracks, holes or bad dents; tightly connected and mounted. If the truck has after-treatment, the **DEF tank is more than 1/8 full** and the DEF dash indicator works. (p. 11-7)
- **Frame:** no cracks, broken welds, holes or other damage to the frame rails, cross members, box and floor. (p. 11-7)
- **Rear:** splash guards (mud flaps) secure; cargo doors open, close and **latch** from outside; **ties, straps, chains and binders secure**; a **cargo lift** has no leaking/damaged/missing parts (and you **explain how to test it**), and it is **fully retracted and latched**. (p. 11-7)

### 11.3.7 Tractor and coupling (pp. 11-7–11-8)
- **Air/electric connectors:** trailer air connectors sealed and in good shape; fittings and all truck-to-trailer connections secure; trailer **electrical plug firmly seated and locked** on both ends. (p. 11-7)
- **Air/electric lines:** **listen for air leaks**; lines not cut, chafed, spliced or worn (**steel braid should not show**); not tangled, pinched or dragging on tractor parts. (p. 11-7)
- **Catwalk and steps:** solid, clear of objects, bolted to the frame. (p. 11-7)
- **Mounting bolts:** no loose or missing brackets, clamps, bolts or nuts; fifth wheel and slide mounting solidly attached. For a ball hitch or pintle hook, no missing/broken parts and **no broken welds**. (p. 11-8)
- **Hitch release lever** in place and secure. (p. 11-8)
- **Locking jaws:** look into the fifth-wheel gap — the jaws are **fully closed around the kingpin**. Other hitches: locking part secure; **safety chains/cables** secure, no kinks, not too much slack. (p. 11-8)
- **Fifth-wheel skid plate:** properly **greased**, securely mounted, bolts and pins in place. **Platform** under it not cracked or broken; pintle hook not cracked or worn too much. (p. 11-8)
- **Release arm** (if equipped) in the **engaged** position with the **safety latch** in place. (p. 11-8)
- **Kingpin/apron/gap:** explain that the jaws hold the kingpin and that the kingpin is not bent or damaged; apron not bent, cracked or broken; the trailer lies **flat on the skid plate — no gap**; check the **kingpin lock**. (p. 11-8)
- **Sliding fifth wheel locking pins:** no loose or missing pins, no air leaks if air-powered, pins **fully engaged**; fifth wheel positioned so the **tractor frame clears the landing gear** in turns. (p. 11-8)
- **Sliding pintle, tongue/draw-bar, tongue storage:** not bent or twisted, no broken welds or stress cracks, draw-bar eye not badly worn, cotter pin in place, storage area and its cargo secure. (p. 11-8)

### 11.4 School bus only (p. 11-9)
On top of the normal checks, a school bus driver also checks:
- **Emergency equipment:** an **emergency kit** and a **body fluid cleanup kit** (plus the usual fuses, triangles/fuses/flares and a **charged and rated** fire extinguisher).
- **Inside panel lights:** **alternately flashing amber** light indicator (if equipped), **alternately flashing red** light indicator, **strobe** light indicator (if equipped).
- **Outside lights:** strobe light (if equipped), **stop arm light** (if equipped), alternately flashing amber lights (if equipped), and alternately flashing **red lights on the front and back**.
- **Student mirrors:** the inside and outside mirrors used to watch students — adjusted right, not damaged, mounted securely, not dirty.
- **Stop arm** (if equipped): mounted securely to the frame, no loose fittings or damage, **extends fully**.
- **Passenger entry/lift:** door not damaged, works smoothly, **closes securely from the inside**; handrails secure; step light works (if equipped); steps clear, treads not loose or badly worn; a **handicap lift** has no leaks or damaged parts, you explain how to test it, it is **fully retracted and latched**, and the **lift interlock** works.
- **Emergency exits:** **demonstrate at least 1** exit — not damaged, works smoothly, closes securely from inside; the **release handle works from inside and outside**; **point out and describe** all the other exits; exit **warning devices** work.
- **Seats:** no broken seat frames; frames firmly attached to the floor; cushions attached to the frames.

### 11.5–11.6 Trailer and coach/transit bus (pp. 11-10–11-11)
**Trailer front** (p. 11-10): air connectors sealed and in good shape; **glad hands locked**, undamaged, not leaking; electrical plug seated and locked. **Header board** (if any) secure, undamaged and strong enough to hold cargo; tarp carrier fastened; on enclosed trailers, no cracks, bulges, holes or **missing rivets** at the front.
**Trailer side** (p. 11-10): **landing gear fully raised**, no missing parts, crank handle secure, frame and pads undamaged, no air/hydraulic leaks if powered; doors, ties and lifts as for a truck; frame; **tandem release arm and locking pins** locked and secured.
**Rest of the trailer:** wheels, suspension, brakes, doors/ties/lift and splash guards — same as Section 11.3. (p. 11-10)

**Coach/transit bus** (pp. 11-10–11-11):
- Passenger entry/lift, emergency exits (**demonstrate at least 1**) and seating — same ideas as the school bus.
- **Entry/exit doors** not damaged, work smoothly from the outside, hinges secure, seals intact; **passenger exit mirrors** and all outside mirrors secure. (p. 11-11)
- **Level/air leaks:** the bus **sits level** front and rear; if air-equipped, **listen for air leaks** from the suspension. (p. 11-11)
- Fuel tanks secure and not leaking; **baggage and other outside compartment doors** work and latch. (p. 11-11)
- **Battery/box:** batteries secure, connections tight, **cell caps present**, no heavy **corrosion**; box and cover secure and undamaged. (p. 11-11)
- Rest of the bus: same as Section 11.3. (p. 11-11)

### 11.7 How the test is given (p. 11-11)
| | Class A | Class B or C |
|---|---|---|
| Versions | **1 of 4**, all equal | **1 of 3**, all equal |
| When you learn which one | Just **before** the test starts | Just **before** the test starts |
| Always includes | **Engine start**, **in-cab** inspection and the **coupling system** | **Engine start** and **in-cab** inspection, plus any **special features** (for example a school or transit bus) |
| How much of the vehicle | The whole vehicle **or only part** of it — the examiner explains | The whole vehicle **or only part** of it — the examiner explains |

- **The memory aid** (the page right after p. 11-11) shows a combination vehicle and a straight truck or bus seen from above, with the inspection areas boxed: front/engine compartment/steering, steering axle, engine start, driver door and fuel area, under the vehicle, drive axles, coupling devices (combination), trailer parts and trailer axles, side and rear lights/reflectors, and passenger and school bus items (buses). Boxes with different borders show which areas go with which test form; **coupling devices** and the **passenger/school bus items** are marked to be covered on **every** form for vehicles that have them.
- **Remember:** you must **pass the inspection before** you can move on to the basic control skills test. (p. 11-11)

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** You can bring a friend to translate during the inspection. → **Correct:** **interpreters are prohibited**; the whole test is in English, and a 3rd language offense on the same date fails you. (p. 11-1)
- **Trap:** Doing the checks silently is enough if you do them right. → **Correct:** you must **explain what you are checking and why**, and for air brake checks you must **say the numbers** to get credit. (pp. 11-1, 11-4)
- **Trap:** Labels on the parts will help you on test day. → **Correct:** a vehicle with **marked or labeled parts cannot be used** for the test. (p. 11-1)
- **Trap:** You can write reminders on the inspection guide. → **Correct:** you may use the guide, but **no notes or instructions** may be written on it. (p. 11-1)
- **Trap:** Tapping a tire with a mallet shows it is properly inflated. → **Correct:** use a **tire gauge**; kicking or striking a tire earns **no credit**. (p. 11-6)
- **Trap:** Steering tires need 2/32 inch of tread. → **Correct:** **4/32 inch** on the steering axle; 2/32 is for all **other** tires. (p. 11-6)
- **Trap:** A belt should be tight with no play at all. → **Correct:** a snug belt has **1/2 to 3/4 inch** of play at its center. (p. 11-2)
- **Trap:** Missing one part of the hydraulic brake check just costs a point. → **Correct:** skipping **either part** of the hydraulic brake check is an **automatic failure** of the inspection. (p. 11-4)
- **Trap:** An air brake mistake only fails the brake part of the test. → **Correct:** doing the air brake checks wrong fails the **entire vehicle inspection**. (p. 11-4)
- **Trap:** A combination of 2 vehicles may lose up to 3 psi in the 1-minute hold. → **Correct:** **4 psi** for 2 vehicles (3 psi is for a single vehicle, 6 psi for 3 or more). (p. 5-8)
- **Trap:** Tire chains are required emergency equipment. → **Correct:** chains are **optional** (where winter needs them); required items are spare fuses, **3 triangles** (or 6 fuses or 3 flares) and a charged, mounted **fire extinguisher**. (p. 11-3)
- **Trap:** Check brake lights, turn signals and flashers all at once. → **Correct:** these functions must be checked **separately**. (p. 11-3)
- **Trap:** If the horn does not work you just lose points. → **Correct:** broken **brake lights, flashers, turn signals or horn** mean the skills and road tests are **postponed**. (p. 11-1)
- **Trap:** You get to pick which version of the inspection test you take. → **Correct:** you **do not know** which version (1 of 4 for Class A, 1 of 3 for B/C) until **just before** the test. (p. 11-11)
- **Trap:** Push the pushrod to test a manual slack adjuster with brakes on. → **Correct:** with brakes **released**, pull it by hand; it should move **no more than 1 inch**. (p. 11-5)

## One-minute recap
- The inspection is **first**: you walk around, **name/point/touch** each item, and **say what and why**. Fail it and the other tests are postponed.
- **English only**, no interpreters: **2** warnings, the **3rd** = fail. **3 attempts** total for all three skills tests. No **labeled parts**, no **notes** on the guide.
- Broken **brake lights, flashers, turn signals or horn** = tests postponed.
- Engine compartment: leaks, hoses, pumps, fluid levels (above refill marks), belts with **1/2–3/4 inch** play.
- Cab: gauges, air to **120–140 psi**, **3 triangles** (or 6 fuses / 3 flares), charged extinguisher, lights with the right colors, checked separately.
- Brakes: hydraulic **pump 3, hold 5** (skip a part = fail); air brake asterisk checks — leakage **3/4/6 psi**, warning **before 55 psi**, knob pops **20–45 psi** — done wrong = **fail the whole inspection**; service brake at **5 mph**.
- Outside: leaf springs **1/4**, pushrod **1 inch**, tread **4/32 steer / 2/32 others**, **tire gauge**, DEF **> 1/8**, coupling jaws closed on the kingpin with **no gap**.
- School bus adds emergency kit, body fluid kit, flashing lights, stop arm, student mirrors, **at least 1 exit demonstrated**. Class A = **1 of 4** versions; B/C = **1 of 3**.
