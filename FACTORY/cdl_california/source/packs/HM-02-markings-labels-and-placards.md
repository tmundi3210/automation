# HM-02 · Markings, labels and placards

> **Handbook:** Section 9.3.7–9.3.11, pages 9-9–9-11 · **Test:** Hazardous Materials · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~45 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- What markings and labels a shipper puts on a HazMat package, and how you check them against the shipping paper.
- How to spot a HazMat shipment from the paperwork and from other clues.
- The rules for the Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifest: who signs, who you may hand the waste to, and what copy you keep.
- Where placards go, how big they must be, and the 6 rules for mounting each one.
- Which materials need placards in any amount (Table 1) and which only at 1,001 pounds or more (Table 2), including when you may use DANGEROUS placards.
- When bulk packages and empty tanks with residue must be placarded.

## Learn it

In the last lesson you learned how the danger is written on the **shipping paper**. This lesson covers the warnings you can **see**: the **markings** and **labels** on each package, and the **placards** on the outside of the truck. Placards let police and firefighters know what you carry from a distance, even if you cannot tell them. Most placard questions come down to two tables and one number: **1,001 pounds**. (pp. 9-9–9-10)

**Key words**
- **Marking** = the words and numbers required on the outside of a HazMat package, such as the name, ID number, instructions, weight or UN marks. (pp. 9-9, 9-25)
- **Name of contents** = the proper shipping name of the material, the same name used on the shipping paper. (pp. 9-9, 9-25)
- **Package orientation markings** = arrows on a package holding liquid containers that point to the correct upright side. (p. 9-9)
- **Hazardous waste** = any material covered by the EPA's hazardous waste manifest rules. (p. 9-25)
- **Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifest** = the shipping paper used for hazardous waste; every shipper and carrier signs it by hand. (p. 9-9)
- **EPA** = U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. (p. 9-24)
- **Placard table** = one of the 2 charts that tell you which placard to use: Table 1 (any amount) or Table 2 (1,001 pounds or more). (p. 9-10)
- **DANGEROUS placard** = a general placard you may use in place of separate placards for 2 or more Table 2 hazard classes, when the rules allow. (p. 9-10)
- **Subsidiary hazard** = a second, extra hazard a material has besides its main hazard class. (p. 9-10)
- **Gross weight** = the weight of the packaging plus the weight of what is inside. (p. 9-24)
- **Bulk packaging** = a single container with a capacity greater than 119 gallons. (p. 9-11)
- **Nonbulk packaging** = smaller packaging, such as a container of 119 gallons or less for a liquid. (p. 9-25)
- **Cargo tank** = a tank for liquids or gases that is permanently attached to a vehicle, or loaded and unloaded without being taken off it. (p. 9-24)
- **Portable tank** = a bulk tank built to be put on or attached to a vehicle for a while and handled by machine, with skids or mountings. (p. 9-26)
- **Transport vehicle** = any cargo-carrying vehicle, such as a van, truck or semi-trailer. Each cargo-carrying body (like each trailer) counts as a separate transport vehicle. (p. 9-26)
- **Residue** = the small amount of HazMat left inside a package or tank after it is emptied. (p. 9-11)

### 9.3.7 Package markings and labels (p. 9-9)
- The shipper prints required **markings** right on the package, on an attached label, or on a **tag**.
- The most important marking is the **name of the HazMat** — the **same name** as on the shipping paper.
- Marking rules depend on the package size and the material. When required, the shipper puts on the package:
  - the **name and address of the shipper or consignee**;
  - the **HazMat shipping name and ID number**;
  - the **required labels**.
- When the HMR require it, the package also says **RQ**, **MARINE POLLUTANT**, **BIOHAZARD**, **HOT**, or **INHALATION HAZARD**.
- Packages with **liquid containers** inside have **orientation arrows** pointing to the correct **upright** direction.
- Labels **always reflect the hazard class** of the product. If a package needs **more than 1 label**, the labels must be **close together, near the proper shipping name**.
- **Your check:** compare the **shipping paper** with the **markings and labels**. Make sure the paper shows the correct basic description and that the packages carry the proper labels. *Why:* the driver is responsible for making sure the shipper identified, marked and labeled the HazMat properly (p. 9-3).
- **Not familiar with the material?** Ask the shipper to **contact your office**.

### 9.3.8 Recognizing hazardous materials (p. 9-9)
**First, read the shipping paper.** Does it have:
- an entry with a **proper shipping name, hazard class and ID number**?
- a **highlighted** entry, or one with an **"X" or "RQ"** in the HazMat (HM) column?

**Other clues that a load may be HazMat:**
- **The shipper's business:** paint dealer, chemical supply, scientific supply house, pest control or farm supplier, or explosives, munitions or fireworks dealer.
- **Tanks** with **diamond labels or placards** at the shipper's site.
- **The type of package:** **cylinders and drums** are often used for HazMat.
- A **hazard class label, proper shipping name or ID number** on the package.
- Any **handling precautions** printed on the package.

*Worked example:* you pick up "supplies" in drums at a pest control company, and the paper has no HM entries. The drums and the type of business are both clues — check the packages for labels, names or ID numbers before you haul.

### 9.3.9 Hazardous waste manifest (pp. 9-8–9-9)
When you haul **hazardous waste**, you must **sign by hand** and **carry** a **Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifest**.
- It must show the **name and EPA registration number** of the **shippers, carriers and destination**.
- The **shipper** prepares, dates and **signs it by hand**.
- **Treat the manifest as a shipping paper** while hauling the waste.
- Hand the waste **only** to **another registered carrier** or a **disposal/treatment facility**.
- **Each carrier** that hauls the load must **sign the manifest by hand**.
- After delivery, **keep your copy**. Each copy must have **all needed signatures and dates**, including the signature of the person you delivered the waste to.
- On the paper, the shipper puts the word **WASTE** before the proper shipping name, for example **UN1090, Waste Acetone, 3, PG II**. The shipper keeps hazardous waste papers for **3 years**. (p. 9-8)

### 9.3.10 Placarding (p. 9-9)
- **Put the placards on before you drive.** You may move an **improperly placarded** vehicle **only in an emergency, to protect life or property**.
- Placards go on **both sides and both ends**. The **front** placard may be on the front of the **tractor or the trailer**.
- From 9.3.3: at least **4 identical** placards, readable from **all 4 directions**, each at least **9.84 inches (250 mm) square**, turned **square-on-point** (like a diamond). (p. 9-5)

**Each placard must be:**
1. **Easily seen** from the direction it faces.
2. Placed so the words or numbers are **level** and read **left to right**.
3. At least **3 inches** away from any other markings.
4. Kept **clear of attachments** such as ladders, doors and tarps.
5. Kept **clean and undamaged** so the color, format and message are easy to see.
6. Put on a **background of contrasting color**.

**Slogans** such as "Drive Safely" are **not allowed** on placards.

**To choose placards you need 3 facts:** the **hazard class** of the materials, the **amount** of HazMat shipped, and the **total weight of all classes** of HazMat on your vehicle.

### 9.3.11 Placard Table 1 — placard any amount (p. 9-10)
There are **2 placard tables**. **Table 1** materials must be placarded **whenever any amount** is carried (Figure 9.7).

| If your vehicle has any amount of… | Placard as… |
|---|---|
| **1.1** Mass explosives | Explosives 1.1 |
| **1.2** Projection hazards | Explosives 1.2 |
| **1.3** Mass fire hazards | Explosives 1.3 |
| **2.3** Poisonous/toxic gases | Poison Gas |
| **4.3** Dangerous when wet | Dangerous When Wet |
| **5.2** Organic peroxide, Type B, liquid or solid, temperature controlled | Organic Peroxide |
| **6.1** Inhalation hazard, zone A and B only | Poison/toxic inhalation |
| **7** Radioactive — **Yellow III label only** | Radioactive |

*Memory hook:* the most dangerous explosives (**1.1–1.3**), **poison gas**, **dangerous when wet**, the temperature-controlled **Type B organic peroxide**, **inhalation hazard zone A/B**, and **Radioactive Yellow III**.

### 9.3.11 Placard Table 2 — the 1,001-pound rule (p. 9-10)
Except for **bulk packaging**, Table 2 classes need placards **only if the total is 1,001 pounds or more**, **counting the package**. **Add up** the amounts from **all shipping papers** for the Table 2 products on board (Figure 9.8).

| Class or division | Placard name |
|---|---|
| 1.4 Minor explosion · 1.5 Very insensitive · 1.6 Extremely insensitive | Explosives 1.4 · Explosives 1.5 · Explosives 1.6 |
| 2.1 Flammable gases | Flammable Gas |
| 2.2 Non-flammable gases | Non-Flammable Gas |
| 3 Flammable liquids | Flammable |
| Combustible liquid | Combustible (FLAMMABLE may be used instead on a cargo tank or portable tank) |
| 4.1 Flammable solids | Flammable Solid |
| 4.2 Spontaneously combustible | Spontaneously Combustible |
| 5.1 Oxidizers | Oxidizer |
| 5.2 Organic peroxide (other than Type B temperature controlled) | Organic Peroxide |
| 6.1 Poison (other than inhalation hazard zone A or B) | Poison |
| 6.2 Infectious substances | **None** |
| 8 Corrosives | Corrosive |
| 9 Miscellaneous HazMat | Class 9 (**not required** for domestic transportation) |
| ORM-D | **None** |

**DANGEROUS placards.** You **may** use DANGEROUS placards instead of separate placards for each Table 2 class when **both** are true:
- you have **1,001 pounds or more** of **2 or more** Table 2 hazard classes that need **different** placards, **and**
- you have **not** loaded **2,205 pounds or more** of any **one** Table 2 class **at any one place**. (If you have, use that material's **specific** placard.)

The DANGEROUS placard is an **option, not a requirement**. You can always placard for each material.

*Worked example:* you carry 600 lb of Class 3 flammable liquid and 500 lb of Class 8 corrosive, weights including packaging. Neither is 1,001 lb alone, but you **add them**: 600 + 500 = **1,100 lb**, which is 1,001 or more → **placards are required**. Use FLAMMABLE and CORROSIVE placards, **or** DANGEROUS placards (2 Table 2 classes, and less than 2,205 lb of either was loaded at one place).

*Worked example:* 900 lb of Class 8 (package included) is your only HazMat. 900 is less than 1,001 → no placard is required for it.

### 9.3.11 Special placard rules (p. 9-10)
- **INHALATION HAZARD** on the paper or package → show **POISON GAS** or **POISON INHALATION** placards **in addition to** the class placards. The **1,000-pound exception does not apply**, so this is required for any amount.
- A **secondary hazard of dangerous when wet** → show the **DANGEROUS WHEN WET** placard **in addition to** the class placards. The **1,000-pound exception does not apply**.
- A placard for a primary or subsidiary hazard class must show the **hazard class or division number** in its **lower corner**. **Permanently attached subsidiary hazard placards** without the number may be used if they stay within the color specifications.
- You **may** display placards even when they are **not required**, as long as they **correctly identify the hazard** of the material you carry.

### 9.3.11 Bulk packaging (p. 9-11)
- **Bulk packaging** means a **single container** with a capacity **greater than 119 gallons**.
- A bulk package, and a vehicle carrying one, **must be placarded even if it holds only residue** of a HazMat. *Why:* even a small amount left inside is still HazMat, so others must still be warned.
- **Certain** bulk packages need placards on only the **2 opposite sides**, or may show labels instead. **All other** bulk packages must be placarded on **all 4 sides**.
- Remember that the **1,001-pound** rule for Table 2 does **not** apply to bulk packaging. (p. 9-10)

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** Labels on a package can show any hazard the shipper chooses. → **Correct:** Labels **always reflect the hazard class** of the product; if more than 1 is needed, they go **close together near the proper shipping name**. (p. 9-9)
- **Trap:** If you don't recognize the material, just haul it as described. → **Correct:** Compare the paper with the markings and labels, and **ask the shipper to contact your office**. (p. 9-9)
- **Trap:** Only the shipper signs the hazardous waste manifest. → **Correct:** The shipper **and each carrier** sign it **by hand**. (p. 9-9)
- **Trap:** You may hand hazardous waste to any carrier who will take it. → **Correct:** Only to **another registered carrier** or a **disposal/treatment facility**. (p. 9-9)
- **Trap:** You may drive a short way to a shop to get proper placards. → **Correct:** Placard **before** you drive; an improperly placarded vehicle may move **only in an emergency** to protect life or property. (p. 9-9)
- **Trap:** A "Drive Safely" slogan may be added to a placard. → **Correct:** Slogans are **prohibited**. (p. 9-9)
- **Trap:** Table 2 materials need placards when any single shipment reaches 1,001 lb. → **Correct:** **Add** the Table 2 amounts from **all** shipping papers on board, **counting packaging**. (p. 9-10)
- **Trap:** DANGEROUS placards are required when you carry 2 Table 2 classes. → **Correct:** They are **optional**; and not allowed for a class when **2,205 lb or more** of it was loaded at one place. (p. 9-10)
- **Trap:** Under 1,001 lb of an INHALATION HAZARD material needs no placards. → **Correct:** The 1,000-pound exception **does not apply**; POISON GAS or POISON INHALATION placards are required. (p. 9-10)
- **Trap:** A small amount of a material with a dangerous-when-wet secondary hazard needs only its class placard. → **Correct:** It also needs the **DANGEROUS WHEN WET** placard, and the 1,000-pound exception **does not apply**. (p. 9-10)
- **Trap:** All radioactive loads need placards in any amount. → **Correct:** Table 1 covers radioactive with a **Yellow III label only**. (p. 9-10)
- **Trap:** 6.2 infectious substances need a placard. → **Correct:** Table 2 lists **no placard** for 6.2 (or for ORM-D). (p. 9-10)
- **Trap:** An empty tank needs no placard. → **Correct:** A bulk package with **only residue** must **still be placarded**. (p. 9-11)
- **Trap:** You may never show a placard that is not required. → **Correct:** You **may**, if it correctly identifies the hazard of the material carried. (p. 9-10)

## One-minute recap
- Package **name marking** = the shipping-paper name. More than 1 label → **close together near the shipping name**. Liquids inside → **upright arrows**. Unsure → **ask the shipper to call your office**.
- Spot HazMat: shipping name + class + ID number, highlighted or **X/RQ** entries; clues like **cylinders and drums** and chemical, paint, pest control or fireworks shippers.
- Hazardous waste: **Uniform Hazardous Waste Manifest**, signed **by hand** by shipper and **each carrier**; give it only to a **registered carrier or disposal/treatment facility**; keep your signed copy.
- Placard **before** driving; move a wrongly placarded vehicle **only in an emergency**. **4** placards, **both sides and ends**, **9.84 in (250 mm)** on point, **3 in** from other markings, level, clean, contrasting background, **no slogans**.
- **Table 1 = any amount:** 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 2.3 poison gas, 4.3 dangerous when wet, 5.2 Type B temp-controlled, 6.1 inhalation zone A/B, 7 Yellow III.
- **Table 2 = 1,001 lb or more**, **counting packages**, **added across all papers** (not for bulk). No placard for **6.2** and **ORM-D**; Class 9 not required domestically.
- **DANGEROUS** placard is **optional** for 2+ Table 2 classes — but not for a class with **2,205 lb or more** loaded at one place.
- INHALATION HAZARD and dangerous-when-wet secondary hazard: extra placards at **any amount**. **Bulk = over 119 gallons**; placard it **even with residue**.
