# SB-01 · School bus danger zones, mirrors, loading and unloading

> **Handbook:** Section 10.1–10.2, pages 10-1–10-6 · **Test:** School Bus · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~45 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- What the danger zones are and how far they reach in front, at the sides and behind the bus (**30 ft**, **12 ft**).
- The four kinds of school bus mirrors, where each one is mounted, and what you must be able to see in each one.
- How to approach a bus stop: when to turn on the amber lights and the turn signal, and where to stop.
- The loading and unloading steps on the route and at school, including what to do if a student is missing.
- **[CA]** The California rules for students who must cross the road, and the California seat belt rule.
- The special dangers of loading and unloading, and what a post-trip inspection looks for.

## Learn it

More students die **getting on and off** a school bus each year than die **riding inside** one. So most of this lesson is about the few minutes at each bus stop. You will learn where children are hardest to see (the danger zones), how to set up your mirrors to see them, and the safe order of steps at every stop. (pp. 10-1, 10-3)

The handbook also says you must know California law and your own school district's rules well. (p. 10-1)

- A **CLP** holder with a **P** (passenger) and/or **S** (school bus) endorsement may **not** drive a CMV with passengers. The only people allowed on board are federal/state auditors and inspectors, test examiners, other trainees, and the CDL holder who rides along. (p. 10-1)
- **[CA]** Since **July 1, 2018**, every passenger on a bus with seat belts must be buckled. Parents, guardians and chartering parties may **not** put a child who is **at least 8 but under 16 years old** on a bus unless the child is properly buckled. (p. 10-1)

**Key words**
- **CLP** = commercial learner's permit, the permit you drive on while learning, before you have your CDL.
- **S endorsement** = the mark on a CDL that lets you drive a school bus. **P endorsement** = the mark for driving passenger vehicles.
- **Danger zone** = the area on every side of the bus where a child is most likely to be hit, by another car or by the bus itself.
- **Flat mirror** = an ordinary mirror; things look their true size and distance.
- **Convex mirror** = a mirror that curves outward. It shows a wider area, but people and objects do not appear at their true size and distance.
- **Crossview mirror** = a curved mirror on the front corner of the bus that shows the ground right in front of the bumper and beside the front wheels.
- **Blind spot** = an area around the bus you cannot see, either directly or in a mirror.
- **Alternating flashing amber lights** = yellow warning lights that flash **before** the stop to warn drivers that the bus is about to stop.
- **Alternating flashing red lights** = red lights that flash **while** the bus is stopped for students; traffic must stop.
- **Stop arm** (stop signal arm) = the moveable STOP signal arm on the bus, extended when the red lights are on.
- **Crossing control arm** = a moveable arm some buses have; follow state and local rules for using it.
- **Service door** = the door students use to get on and off.
- **Secure the bus** = make it safe to leave: stop it, set the brake, and take control of the key.
- **Post-trip inspection** = the check you do when your route or trip is over.

### 10.1.1 Danger zones (p. 10-1)
The danger zones surround the whole bus. **Figure 10.1** (a drawing of the bus from above) shows them:

| Where | How far the danger zone may reach |
|---|---|
| In front of the bumper | Up to **30 ft**. The **first 12 ft** is the **most dangerous** part. |
| Left side | **12 ft** from the side |
| Right side | **12 ft** from the side |
| Behind the rear bumper | **12 ft**. The drawing marks this as **most dangerous** too. |
| The whole area to the **left** of the bus | **Always dangerous**, because of **passing cars** |

- The drawing also shows a **walking area** that leads away from the right front of the bus at an angle, and labels the strip down the left side "danger from passing cars". (p. 10-1)
- *Why:* these are the places where children are in the most danger of being hit — by passing traffic or by their own bus.

### 10.1.3 Outside flat mirrors (pp. 10-1–10-2)
First, the general rule (10.1.2): **check every mirror before you drive** the bus so you have the biggest possible view. If a mirror is wrong, **get it adjusted**. *Why:* the mirrors are how you watch the danger zones for students, traffic and objects. (p. 10-1)

- **Where:** on both front corners of the bus, beside or in front of the windshield. (p. 10-2)
- **Used to:** watch traffic, check clearances, and check for students **along the sides and behind** the bus.
- **Blind spots:** right **below and in front of** each mirror, and **directly behind the rear bumper**. The blind spot behind the bus runs **50 to 150 ft** back, and can reach **400 ft** on a long, wide bus.
- **Adjust them so you can see:**
  - **200 ft**, or **4 bus lengths**, behind the bus.
  - Along the sides of the bus.
  - The **rear tires touching the ground**.
- *Worked example:* your flat mirror shows the road only 100 ft behind you. That is short of the **200 ft (4 bus lengths)** you need, so the mirror must be adjusted before you drive.

### 10.1.4 Outside convex mirrors (p. 10-2)
- **Where:** **below** the outside flat mirrors.
- **Used to:** watch the left and right sides at a **wide angle**: traffic, clearances, and students beside the bus.
- **Warning:** people and objects do **not** look their true size or distance in these mirrors.
- **Adjust them so you can see:**
  - The **entire side** of the bus, up to the **mirror mounts**.
  - The **front of the rear tires** touching the ground.
  - **At least 1 traffic lane** on each side of the bus.
- Figure 10.3 shows the view as a wide wedge along each side of the bus; the drawing labels the wedges **32 ft** and **12 ft**.

### 10.1.5 [CA] Crossview mirrors (pp. 10-2–10-3)
- **Where:** on the left and right **front corners** of the bus.
- **Used to:** see the **front bumper danger zone** — the area right in front of the bus that you cannot see directly — and the danger zones on the left and right, including the **service door** and **front wheel** area.
- Like convex mirrors, they do **not** show true size and distance. The **driver** must make sure they are adjusted.
- **[CA]** California regulations (CCR Title 13 §1258) require **every school bus** to have a crossview mirror on the **front outside** of the bus, so the seated driver can see the area directly in front. (p. 10-3)
- **Adjust them so you can see:**
  - The whole area in front of the bus, from the **front bumper at ground level** up to where you can see directly. Your **direct view and mirror view should overlap**.
  - The **right and left front tires** touching the ground.
  - The area from the front of the bus to the **service door**.
- Check the crossview, convex and flat mirrors **in a logical order** every time, so no child or object is missed in any danger zone. (p. 10-3)

### 10.1.6 Overhead inside rearview mirror (p. 10-3)
- **Where:** right **above the windshield**, on the driver's side.
- **Used to:** watch the **students inside** the bus. It may give a little view behind the bus if the rear emergency door has a glass bottom.
- **Blind spots:** right **behind the driver's seat**, and a large one that starts at the rear bumper and can reach **400 ft or more** behind the bus. Use the **outside side mirrors** to watch traffic coming into that area.
- **Adjust it so you can see:**
  - The **top of the rear window** in the **top** of the mirror.
  - **All the students**, including the **heads** of the students right behind you.

**Mirror summary**

| Mirror | Mounted | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| Flat | Front corners, side or front of windshield | Traffic, clearances, students on sides and rear; **200 ft / 4 bus lengths** behind |
| Convex | Below the flat mirrors | Wide view of the sides; **at least 1 lane** each side |
| Crossview **[CA]** required | Front corners / front outside | Area in front of the bumper, service door, front wheels |
| Overhead inside | Above windshield, driver's side | Students inside the bus |

### 10.2.1 Approaching the stop (pp. 10-3–10-4)
- The **school district** sets the routes and bus stops. **Never move a bus stop** without **written approval** from the right district official. (p. 10-3)
- Follow all state and local laws for approaching a stop, including how you use mirrors, flashing lights, the stop arm and the crossing control arm.

**As you approach** (p. 10-4):
1. Come in **slowly** and carefully.
2. Look for people on foot, traffic and objects **before, during and after** you stop. Keep **checking all mirrors**.
3. If the bus has them, turn on the **alternating flashing amber lights** at least **200 ft** or about **5–10 seconds** before the stop (or as state law says).
4. Turn on the **right turn signal** about **100–300 ft** or about **3–5 seconds** before you pull over.
5. Move as **far right** as you can on the traveled part of the road.

**When you stop** (p. 10-4):
1. Stop with the **front bumper at least 10 ft away** from the students at the stop. *Why:* it makes the students walk to the bus, so you can see them move.
2. Put the transmission in **Park** — or **Neutral** if there is no Park — and **set the parking brake** at **every** stop.
3. Turn on the **alternating red lights** when traffic is a safe distance from the bus, and make sure the **stop arm is out**.
4. Make a **final check that all traffic has stopped** before you fully open the door and signal students to come.

*Worked example:* you are going to stop at a corner. At least 200 ft (about 5–10 s) before the stop, the amber lights go on. About 100–300 ft (3–5 s) before you pull over, the right signal goes on. You stop with the bumper at least 10 ft from the waiting children, go to Park, set the parking brake, then turn on the red lights once traffic is a safe distance away and make sure the stop arm is out.

### 10.2.2 Loading procedures (p. 10-4)
- Students wait at a set place, **facing the bus** as it comes.
- Students get on **only when you signal** them.
- **Watch all mirrors** the whole time.
- **Count** the students at the stop and make sure **all** get on. Learn their names if you can. If one is missing, **ask the other students** where the student is.
- Students board **slowly, in single file**, using the **handrail**. Turn on the **dome light** when loading in the dark.
- **Do not move** until students are **seated and facing forward**.
- Check all mirrors. Make sure **no one is running** to catch the bus.
- **A student is missing?** Secure the bus, take the key with you, and look all around the bus and underneath it.

**Pull-away steps (on the route)** — when every student is accounted for:
1. Close the door.
2. Put the transmission in gear.
3. Release the parking brake.
4. Turn off the alternating flashing red lights.
5. Turn on the **left** turn signal.
6. Check all the mirrors again.
7. Let heavy traffic clear.

Then, when it is safe, move out into traffic and go on with the route.

**Loading at the school campus** is almost the same, but you also: **turn off the ignition**, **take the key** if you leave the driver's seat area, and stand where you can **supervise** the loading.

### 10.2.3 [CA] Unloading on the route, and students who cross (p. 10-5)
Make a safe stop (as in 10.2.1), then:
- Students **stay seated until you tell them** to get off.
- Check all mirrors.
- **Count** the students as they get off, so you know where every student is before you leave.
- Tell them to get off and walk **at least 10 ft away from the side** of the bus, to a spot where you can **plainly see** all of them.
- Check all mirrors again. Make sure **no one is near the bus or coming back** to it.
- Can't account for a student? **Secure the bus, take the key**, and check **around and under** the bus.
- Then use the same **7 pull-away steps** as for loading.
- **Missed a student's stop? Do not back up.** Follow local procedures. (p. 10-5)

**[CA] Students who must cross the road.** When the bus stops on a highway or private road to load or unload, and **no traffic officer or traffic signal** controls traffic there, the driver **must**:
1. **Escort** every student in **prekindergarten, kindergarten, or grades 1 through 8** who needs to cross the road, using an approved **hand-held "STOP" sign**.
2. Make **every** student who crosses walk **in front of the bus**.
3. Make sure every crossing student has **crossed safely**, and all other students and people on foot are a **safe distance** from the bus, **before** the bus moves.

*Why:* the driver must be sure every crossing student is safely across before the bus moves. Also enforce any state or local rules about how students act outside the bus. (p. 10-5)

### 10.2.4 [CA] Unloading at school (pp. 10-5–10-6)
Rules at schools (for example, when things are happening in the parking lot) are often **different** from rules on the route. Know and obey them. General steps:
1. Make a safe stop at the set unloading area.
2. **Secure the bus:** turn **off the ignition**; **take the key** if you leave the driver's area.
3. Students **stay seated** until told to get off.
4. Stand where you can **supervise** the unloading.
5. **[CA]** **Escort** prekindergarten, kindergarten and grade 1–8 students who need to cross the road, using the hand-held **STOP** sign.
6. Students get off in an **orderly** way. Watch each one step off and **move promptly away**.
7. **Walk through the bus** to check for **hiding or sleeping students** and **items left behind**.
8. Check all mirrors; make sure no students are **coming back** to the bus.
9. If the bus is secure and a student outside is missing, **take the key** and check **around and under** the bus.

**Pull-away steps at school** — the route list plus two extra steps, because the engine was off:
1. Close the door. 2. **Fasten your safety belt.** 3. **Start the engine.** 4. Put it in gear. 5. Release the parking brake. 6. Turn off the flashing red lights. 7. Turn on the left signal. 8. Check all mirrors again. 9. Let heavy traffic clear. Then pull away when safe. (p. 10-6)

### 10.2.5 Special dangers and the post-trip inspection (p. 10-6)
**Dropped or forgotten objects.** Keep your eyes on students as they come to the bus, and watch for any who **drop out of sight**. A child who stops to pick up something they dropped — or runs back for it — may vanish from your view at the worst moment.
- Teach students: **leave the dropped object**, move to a safe spot **out of the danger zones**, and **get the driver's attention** so the driver can get the object.

**Handrail hang-ups.** Children have been hurt or killed when **clothing, accessories, or body parts** got caught in the **handrail or door** as they got off. Watch every student leave and make sure each is in a safe place **before you move** the bus.

**10.2.6 Post-trip inspection.** When the route or activity trip ends, **walk through and around** the bus and look for:
- Articles left on the bus.
- **Sleeping students.**
- Open windows and doors.
- Mechanical or operating problems — especially school-bus-only items: **mirror systems, flashing warning lamps, and stop signal arms**.
- Damage or vandalism.

Report any problem **right away** to your supervisor or school officials. (p. 10-6)

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** The danger zone reaches 12 ft in front of the bus. → **Correct:** It can reach **30 ft** in front; the **first 12 ft** is the most dangerous part. Sides and rear are 12 ft. (p. 10-1)
- **Trap:** The right side is the most dangerous side because students get off there. → **Correct:** The **left** side is **always** dangerous because of **passing cars**. (p. 10-1)
- **Trap:** Convex mirrors show objects at their true size and distance. → **Correct:** Convex and crossview mirrors do **not** show people and objects at their true size and distance. (p. 10-2)
- **Trap:** The flat mirrors should show 400 ft behind the bus. → **Correct:** Flat mirrors should show **200 ft (4 bus lengths)**. 400 ft is how far the **blind spot** behind the bus can reach. (p. 10-2)
- **Trap:** The inside overhead mirror is for watching traffic behind the bus. → **Correct:** It is for watching **students inside**. Use the outside side mirrors for traffic in the big blind spot behind. (p. 10-3)
- **Trap:** Turn on the amber lights when you come to a stop. → **Correct:** Amber lights go on **before** the stop, at least **200 ft / 5–10 s** out. The **red** lights go on once you are stopped and traffic is a safe distance away. (p. 10-4)
- **Trap:** Pull right up to the waiting students so they have a short walk. → **Correct:** Stop at least **10 ft** away, so they must walk to the bus and you can watch them. (p. 10-4)
- **Trap:** A student is missing, so drive off and report it later. → **Correct:** **Secure the bus, take the key**, and check **around and underneath** the bus. (pp. 10-4, 10-5)
- **Trap:** You missed a student's stop — back up to it carefully. → **Correct:** **Do not back up**; follow local procedures. (p. 10-5)
- **Trap:** Students who cross the road should walk behind the bus. → **Correct:** They must cross **in front of** the bus. (p. 10-5)
- **Trap:** You must escort only kindergarten students across the road. → **Correct:** **[CA]** Escort **prekindergarten, kindergarten and grades 1–8**, with a hand-held STOP sign, where no officer or signal controls traffic. (p. 10-5)
- **Trap:** A child who drops a book near the bus should pick it up quickly. → **Correct:** Leave it, get **out of the danger zone**, and get the **driver's attention**. (p. 10-6)
- **Trap:** You may move a bus stop if the new spot is safer. → **Correct:** Never without **written approval** from the school district official. (p. 10-3)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 10 #1** What is the danger zone, and how far does it reach around the bus?
→ It is the area on every side of the bus where children are most likely to be hit, by another vehicle or by their own bus. It can reach up to 30 ft in front of the front bumper (the first 12 ft is the most dangerous), 12 ft from the left and right sides, and 12 ft behind the rear bumper. The area to the left of the bus is always dangerous because of passing cars. (p. 10-1)

**TYK 10 #2** What should you be able to see in the outside flat mirrors, the outside convex mirrors, and the crossview mirrors?
→ Flat mirrors: 200 ft (4 bus lengths) behind the bus, along the sides, and the rear tires touching the ground. Convex mirrors: the full side of the bus as far as the mirror mounts, where the front of each rear tire meets the road, and at least 1 traffic lane on each side. Crossview mirrors: the whole area in front of the bus from the bumper at ground level to where direct vision starts (the views should overlap), the left and right front tires touching the ground, and the area from the front of the bus to the service door. (pp. 10-2–10-3)

**TYK 10 #3** You are loading students along the route. When should you turn on the alternating flashing amber warning lights?
→ At least 200 ft, or about 5–10 seconds, before the bus stop (or as state law requires), if the bus has them. (p. 10-4)

**TYK 10 #4** You are unloading along your route. Where should students walk after they get off?
→ At least 10 ft away from the side of the bus, to a place where the driver can plainly see all of them. (p. 10-5)

**TYK 10 #5** After unloading at school, why should you walk through the bus?
→ To check for students who are hiding or asleep, and for things students left behind. (p. 10-6)

**TYK 10 #6** Where should students be, in relation to the front of the bus, before they cross the road?
→ Students who cross must walk **in front of the bus**, where the driver can see them. They first move at least 10 ft from the side of the bus to where the driver can plainly see them. **[CA]** Students in prekindergarten, kindergarten and grades 1–8 must be escorted across by the driver with a hand-held STOP sign (where no officer or signal controls traffic), and the bus does not move until all have crossed safely and everyone is a safe distance away. (p. 10-5)

## One-minute recap
- Danger zones: up to **30 ft** in front (first **12 ft** most dangerous), **12 ft** at each side and behind. The **left** side is always dangerous (passing cars).
- **Flat** mirrors (front corners): **200 ft / 4 bus lengths** behind, sides, rear tires. Blind spot behind: **50–150 ft**, up to **400 ft**.
- **Convex** (below flat): whole side to mounts, front of rear tires, **1 lane** each side. Not true size or distance.
- **Crossview** (front corners): area in front of the bumper, service door, front tires. **[CA]** required on every school bus. **Overhead inside** mirror: watch students.
- Approach: amber lights **200 ft / 5–10 s** before; right signal **100–300 ft / 3–5 s** before; far right. Stop **10 ft** from students, **Park + parking brake**, then red lights and stop arm.
- Count students. Missing one → **secure the bus, take the key, look around and under**. Pull away: door, gear, brake off, reds off, **left** signal, mirrors, let traffic clear.
- Unloading: students walk **10 ft** from the side. **Never back up** for a missed stop. **[CA]** Escort **preK–grade 8** with a hand-held STOP sign; crossers walk **in front of** the bus.
- At school: ignition off, take key, supervise, **walk through** for sleeping students and items. Post-trip: articles, sleeping students, open windows/doors, mirrors/lamps/stop arms, damage.
