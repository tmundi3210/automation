# AB-02 · Dual air brakes and inspecting air brakes

> **Handbook:** Section 5.2–5.3, pages 5-6–5-10 · **Test:** Air Brakes · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~50 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- What a dual air brake system is, how much pressure to build before driving (**100 psi**), and what to do if one half loses air.
- What the one-way check valve does and where it must be.
- How to check the compressor belt, the slack adjusters (**about 1 inch** of play), drums, linings and hoses during the walk-around.
- How to do the applied and static leakage tests, and the maximum air loss for each (**3/4/6** and **2/3/5 psi**).
- How to test the low-air warning (**before 55 psi**) and the spring brakes (**20–45 psi**), and the proper rate of pressure buildup (**85 → 100 psi in 45 seconds**).
- How to test the parking brake and the service brakes before you drive.

## Learn it

Most heavy-duty vehicles use a **dual air brake system** for safety — two separate air systems worked by one set of controls. Before any trip you inspect the air brakes using the **7-step inspection** from Section 2, with extra checks added for air brakes. The in-cab checks in Step 7 **replace** the hydraulic brake check you learned for trucks without air brakes. (pp. 5-6–5-8)

**Key words**
- **Dual air brake system** = two separate air brake systems, each with its own tanks, hoses and lines, controlled by one brake pedal.
- **Primary system** = the first of the two systems in a dual air system; it usually works the rear axle brakes.
- **Secondary system** = the other system; it usually works the front axle brakes (and possibly one rear axle).
- **One-way check valve** = a valve that lets air flow in only one direction.
- **Slack** = how far the slack adjuster can move before the brake starts to work; too much slack means weak brakes.
- **Automatic slack adjuster** = a slack adjuster that adjusts itself during full brake applications.
- **Pushrod stroke** = how far the brake chamber push rod travels when the brakes are applied.
- **Friction area** = the part of the drum or disc that the linings rub against.
- **Chock** = a wedge placed against a tire to keep the vehicle from rolling.
- **Fanning off the air** = quickly pressing and releasing the brake pedal again and again to lower air pressure on purpose.
- **Tractor protection valve** = the valve that keeps the tractor's air in if the trailer loses air (controlled by the red trailer air supply knob).
- **Triple reservoir vehicle** = a single vehicle (often a bus) with a separate, isolated air tank for the parking brakes.
- **Static leakage** = air lost over time with the brakes released and the engine off.
- **Applied leakage** = air lost over time while the brake pedal is held fully down.
- **rpm** = revolutions per minute, the engine speed.

### 5.2 Dual air brake systems (p. 5-6)
- A dual system has **2 separate air brake systems** that share **one set of brake controls**. **Each** has its own **air tanks, hoses and lines**.
- **Primary** system: usually works the brakes on the **rear axle or axles**.
- **Secondary** system: usually works the brakes on the **front axle** (and possibly one rear axle).
- **Both** systems supply air to the **trailer**, if there is one.
- Figure 5.4 (described): a single-circuit system map — compressor, one-way check valve, wet and dry main reservoirs, safety valve, low pressure warning buzzer and switch, foot valve, hand valve, tractor protection valve, blue tractor parking brake valve, yellow parking/emergency brake valve, spring brakes, and on the trailer the glad hands, relay emergency valve, trailer reservoir and brake chambers.

**Before you drive a dual system:**
- Let the compressor build **at least 100 psi** in **both** the primary and secondary systems.
- Watch both gauges (or both needles on a 2-needle gauge).
- The low-air warning light and buzzer should **shut off** once both systems pass a value set by the maker. That value must be **more than 55 psi**.
- The warning should **come on before** pressure falls **below 55 psi in either system**.

**If the warning comes on while driving:** **stop right away** and park safely. *Why:* if one system is very low, **either the front or the rear brakes are not working fully**, so it will **take longer to stop**. Get the system fixed.

**[CA] One-way check valve (p. 5-6):**
- Lets air flow in **one direction only**.
- California law requires a check valve **between the air compressor and the first reservoir** (CVC §26507).
- *Why:* if the **compressor** springs a leak, the check valve **keeps the tank air from escaping** back through it.

### 5.3.1 Step 2 — Engine compartment: compressor drive belt (p. 5-7)
- Use the basic **7-step inspection** from Section 2. Air brakes add checks in **Step 2**, **Step 5** and **Step 7**.
- In **Step 2 (engine compartment)**: if the air compressor is **belt-driven**, check the belt's **condition and tightness**. It should be in good condition.

### 5.3.2 Step 5 — Walk-around: slack adjusters (p. 5-7)
**How to check slack adjusters on S-cam brakes:**
1. Park on **level ground** and **chock the wheels** so the vehicle cannot move.
2. **Release the parking brakes** — *why:* so the slack adjusters can move.
3. Wearing **gloves**, **pull hard** on each slack adjuster you can reach.
4. If it moves **more than about 1 inch** at the point where the push rod connects, it **likely needs adjusting** — do it yourself or have a shop do it.

*Why it matters:* too much brake slack makes a vehicle **very hard to stop**. At roadside inspections, **brakes out of adjustment are the problem officers find most often.**

**Automatic slack adjusters:**
- **All vehicles built since 1994** have them.
- They adjust themselves during **full** brake applications — but **you must still check them**.
- They should need manual adjustment **only** during brake maintenance or when the adjusters are being installed.
- If the pushrod stroke goes past the **legal adjustment limit**, it points to a **mechanical problem** (in the adjuster or the related foundation brake parts) or a **bad installation**.
- Adjusting an automatic adjuster by hand usually **hides the problem** instead of fixing it, and routine hand adjustment can **wear out** the adjuster early. It can also give you a **false sense of security**.
- **What to do:** take the vehicle to a **repair facility as soon as possible**. Hand-adjusting is only a **temporary fix in an emergency** — the brake will likely go out of adjustment again soon.
- Adjusters from different makers work differently, so check the **maker's service manual** before troubleshooting.

### 5.3.2 Step 5 — Walk-around: drums, linings and hoses (p. 5-7)
| Part | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| **Brake drums (or discs)** | Cracks **longer than ½ the width of the friction area** |
| **Linings** (friction material) | **Loose**, **soaked with oil or grease**, or worn **thinner than ¼ inch** |
| **Mechanical parts** | Missing, broken or out of place |
| **Air hoses** to the brake chambers | **Cut** or **worn from rubbing** |

*Worked example:* the friction area of a drum is 4 inches wide. Half of that is 2 inches, so a crack longer than 2 inches is not allowed.

### 5.3.3.1 Step 7 — Applied leakage test (p. 5-8)
**Step 7 rules for the skills test:** items marked with an asterisk (★ here) must be shown during the **vehicle inspection** part of the CDL skills test. You may do them **in any order**, as long as you do them correctly. If you **don't demonstrate them and say the correct limits out loud**, it is an **automatic failure** of the vehicle inspection. The ★ items are: applied leakage, low air warning and spring brake test.

**Applied leakage test (1-minute hold)★:**
1. Engine running, build air pressure to **governor cut-out** (**120–140 psi**, or the maker's level). Say when cut-out happened.
2. **Shut off the engine**. Chock the wheels if needed.
3. **Release the parking brake** (all vehicles) and the **tractor protection valve** (combination vehicles).
4. **Fully apply the foot brake.**
5. Hold it for **1 minute after the gauge settles**. Watch the gauge and **listen for leaks**.
6. Say how much air was lost and the **maximum allowed** for your vehicle.

| Vehicle | Max drop in 1 minute (applied) |
|---|---|
| Single vehicle | **3 psi** |
| Combination of **2** vehicles | **4 psi** |
| Combination of **3 or more** | **6 psi** |
| Combination of 2 or more where the **towed vehicles have no air brakes** (for example, a trailer with electric or surge brakes) | **3 psi** |

- Losing more than this means the brake system has a problem. **Repair it before driving.** Look for leaks and fix them.
- The governor (from 5.1.2, p. 5-1) stops the compressor at about **125 psi** and restarts it at about **100 psi**; this test starts at cut-out.

### 5.3.3.2 Step 7 — Low air warning device test (pp. 5-8–5-9)
**Low air warning device test★:**
1. Start with enough pressure that the warning is **off**.
2. Engine **on or off**, but the key must be in **"on"** or **"battery charge."**
3. **Fan off** the air: quickly press and release the foot brake again and again.
4. The warning (**buzzer, light or flag**) must come on **before pressure drops below 55 psi** (or the maker's level).
5. Say the pressure where it came on, and that the limit is **no lower than 55 psi**.

- **Large buses:** warnings often come on at **80–85 psi**. On a bus, state the normal **55–75 psi** range and tell the examiner your bus is built to warn at a higher pressure.
- *Why the test matters:* if the warning doesn't work, you could lose air and not know it. In a **single-circuit** system that could mean **sudden emergency braking**. In a **dual** system, your **stopping distance will be longer**, and you get only limited braking before the spring brakes come on.
- Figure 5.5 (described): low-air warning devices — a warning light and a drop-arm "wig wag" that falls into the driver's view and won't stay up until pressure is restored.
- Note: **farm labor vehicles** and **Type I school buses** must have **both** an **audible** (sound) and a **visible** warning device. (p. 5-8)

### 5.3.3.3 Step 7 — Spring brake test (p. 5-9)
**Spring brake test (automatic application)★:**
1. **Release** the parking brake (all vehicles) and the **tractor protection valve** (combinations). Engine running or not.
2. **Fan off** the air pressure.
3. At normally **20–45 psi** (or the maker's level):
   - **Tractor-trailer:** the **tractor protection valve** and the **parking brake valve** should **close (pop out)**.
   - **Other combinations and single vehicles:** the **parking brake valve** should pop out.
4. Say the approximate pressure at which they came on.

**Buses with an emergency park brake air tank (triple reservoir vehicles):** the parking brake valve **will not pop out**. If it hasn't popped out by about **20 psi**, show that the spring brakes came on:
- Remove the chocks if needed.
- Leave the parking brake valve **released (open)**.
- With the engine running, put it in a **forward gear** and try to drive forward.
- The spring brakes should **drag** and keep the vehicle from moving easily. If they don't, your **road test is postponed**.
- Do this **only on single vehicles** with an **isolated parking brake tank** — **never on combination vehicles**.

### 5.3.3 Step 7 — Rate of air pressure buildup (p. 5-9)
- Engine at **normal operating idle** (typically **600–900 rpm**).
- Watch the gauge to see if pressure builds fast enough:

| System | Should build | Within |
|---|---|---|
| **Dual** air system | From about **85 to 100 psi** | **45 seconds** |
| **Single** air system (**pre-1975** vehicles) | From about **50 to 90 psi** | **3 minutes** |

- For the test, say the limits and whether the vehicle met them.

### 5.3.3 Step 7 — Static leakage, parking brake and service brake tests (p. 5-10)
**Static leakage test** (one of the air leakage rate tests):
1. Air system basically **fully charged** (within the compressor's operating range).
2. **Engine off**, **release all brakes**, let the gauge needle **stop moving**.
3. Time **1 minute**. The pressure should drop no more than:

| Vehicle | Max drop in 1 minute (static, brakes released) |
|---|---|
| Single vehicle | **2 psi** |
| Combination of **2** vehicles | **3 psi** |
| Combination of **3 or more** | **5 psi** |
| Combination of 2 or more where the **towed vehicles have no air brakes** | **2 psi** |

- More loss than this = a problem; **repair it before driving**.
- *Memory hook:* **applied** limits are **1 psi higher** than static in every row (static 2/3/5, applied 3/4/6; towed units without air brakes: static 2, applied 3).

**Parking brake test:** with your **seat belt** on and the **parking brake set**, put the vehicle in a **low gear** and **pull gently** against the brake to confirm it holds.

**Service brake test:** wait for **normal air pressure**, **release the parking brake**, move forward **slowly (about 5 mph)**, and **apply the brakes firmly** with the pedal. Note any **pulling to one side**, **unusual feel** or **delayed stopping**. *Why:* this can show problems you would otherwise find only when you need the brakes on the road.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** In a dual system, both halves share the same air tanks. → **Correct:** Each system has its **own** tanks, hoses and lines; they share only the brake controls. (p. 5-6)
- **Trap:** You can drive a dual system once the warning buzzer stops. → **Correct:** Wait until **both** primary and secondary systems reach **at least 100 psi**. (p. 5-6)
- **Trap:** If one half of a dual system loses air, you can keep driving normally. → **Correct:** Either front or rear brakes won't work fully, so stops take longer — **stop right away**, park safely and get it fixed. (p. 5-6)
- **Trap:** Check slack adjusters with the parking brakes set. → **Correct:** **Chock the wheels** and **release** the parking brakes, or the adjusters can't move. (p. 5-7)
- **Trap:** A slack adjuster that moves 2 inches is fine. → **Correct:** More than **about 1 inch** at the push rod means it probably needs adjustment. (p. 5-7)
- **Trap:** Automatic slack adjusters never need checking. → **Correct:** They **must still be checked**. If one is out of adjustment, get to a **repair shop** soon; hand-adjusting only hides the problem. (p. 5-7)
- **Trap:** Linings are OK until they are 1/8 inch thick. → **Correct:** Linings must **not** be worn thinner than **¼ inch**. (p. 5-7)
- **Trap:** The applied leakage limit for a single vehicle is 2 psi. → **Correct:** Applied = **3 psi** single (4 for 2 vehicles, 6 for 3+). **2 psi** is the **static** limit. (pp. 5-8, 5-10)
- **Trap:** A tractor pulling a trailer with electric brakes may lose 4 psi in the applied test. → **Correct:** If the towed vehicle has **no air brakes**, the limit is **3 psi** (applied) — **2 psi** static. (pp. 5-8, 5-10)
- **Trap:** The low air warning may come on as low as 45 psi. → **Correct:** It must come on **before 55 psi**. 20–45 psi is the **spring brake** range. (pp. 5-8–5-9)
- **Trap:** Do the triple-reservoir drive-forward test on any vehicle. → **Correct:** **Only** on **single vehicles** with an **isolated parking brake tank** — **never** on combinations. (p. 5-9)
- **Trap:** Dual systems should build from 50 to 90 psi within 3 minutes. → **Correct:** Dual: **85 → 100 psi in 45 seconds**. 50 → 90 in 3 minutes is for **pre-1975 single** systems. (p. 5-9)
- **Trap:** Test the parking brake by pulling hard against it in a high gear. → **Correct:** Pull **gently** against it in a **low** gear. (p. 5-10)
- **Trap:** Skipping or not stating the limits for a ★ air brake check just costs points. → **Correct:** It is an **automatic failure** of the vehicle inspection. (p. 5-8)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 5.2–5.3 #1** What is a dual air brake system?
→ Two separate air brake systems worked by one set of brake controls. Each has its own tanks, hoses and lines. The primary system usually brakes the rear axle(s); the secondary usually brakes the front axle (and maybe one rear axle). Both supply air to a trailer. (p. 5-6)

**TYK 5.2–5.3 #2** What are the slack adjusters?
→ The arms that connect each brake chamber's push rod to the brake camshaft on S-cam brakes. When you brake, the push rod moves the slack adjuster, which twists the camshaft and turns the S-cam to press the shoes against the drum. Their adjustment sets how much "slack" the brake has before it works; vehicles built since 1994 have automatic slack adjusters. (pp. 5-2, 5-7)

**TYK 5.2–5.3 #3** How can you check the slack adjusters?
→ Park on level ground, chock the wheels, and release the parking brakes. With gloves on, pull hard on each slack adjuster you can reach. If one moves more than about 1 inch where the push rod attaches, it probably needs adjustment. (p. 5-7)

**TYK 5.2–5.3 #4** How can you test the low pressure warning signal?
→ Start with enough air that the warning is off. Key in "on" or "battery charge" (engine on or off). Fan off the air by quickly pressing and releasing the foot brake. The warning must come on before pressure drops below 55 psi (or the maker's level; large buses often warn at 80–85 psi). (p. 5-8)

**TYK 5.2–5.3 #5** How can you check that the spring brakes come on automatically?
→ Release the parking brake (and tractor protection valve on a combination), then fan off the air. At normally 20–45 psi the parking brake valve (and on a tractor-trailer, the tractor protection valve) should pop out. On a single vehicle with an isolated parking brake tank, if the valve hasn't popped out by about 20 psi, leave it released, put it in a forward gear and try to drive — the spring brakes should drag and hold it back. (p. 5-9)

**TYK 5.2–5.3 #6** What are the maximum leakage rates?
→ Applied (brake held 1 minute after the gauge settles): 3 psi single vehicle, 4 psi for 2 vehicles, 6 psi for 3 or more (3 psi if the towed units have no air brakes). Static (brakes released, engine off, 1 minute): 2 psi single, 3 psi for 2 vehicles, 5 psi for 3 or more (2 psi if the towed units have no air brakes). (pp. 5-8, 5-10)

## One-minute recap
- **Dual system** = 2 separate air systems (own tanks, hoses, lines), **1 set of controls**; primary usually **rear**, secondary usually **front**.
- Build **at least 100 psi in both** before driving; warning must come on **before 55 psi** in either. Low half = longer stops → stop right away.
- **[CA]** One-way **check valve** between compressor and first tank keeps air in if the compressor leaks.
- **Step 2:** compressor belt. **Step 5:** chock, release parking brakes, pull slack adjusters — **more than ~1 inch** = adjust. Automatic adjusters (since **1994**) still need checking; out of adjustment → repair shop.
- Drum cracks under **½ the friction area width**; linings at least **¼ inch**, not loose or oily; hoses not cut or rubbed.
- **Applied leakage** (1 min, pedal down): **3 / 4 / 6 psi**. **Static** (brakes off): **2 / 3 / 5 psi**. Towed units without air brakes: **3** applied, **2** static.
- **Low-air warning** before **55 psi** (buses often 80–85). **Spring brakes / valves pop out** at **20–45 psi**. **Buildup**: dual **85 → 100 in 45 s**; pre-1975 single **50 → 90 in 3 min**.
- **Parking brake**: gently pull against it in low gear. **Service brakes**: ~**5 mph**, brake firmly, watch for pulling or delay. Missing a ★ check = automatic failure.
