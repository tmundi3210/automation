# AB-03 · Using air brakes

> **Handbook:** Section 5.4, pages 5-11–5-13 · **Test:** Air Brakes · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~40 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- How to make a normal stop, and when to push in the clutch.
- How to brake with ABS — on the tractor only, the trailer only, or both — and the one time you may brake fully.
- The difference between **controlled braking** and **stab braking** in an emergency.
- What **brake lag** is (**½ second or more**), the **4** parts of stopping distance, and why a truck at 55 mph needs **over 450 feet** to stop.
- What causes brake fade, and the proper downhill braking technique (**5 mph** below safe speed, about **3 seconds**).
- What to do when the low-air warning comes on, and when **not** to use the parking brakes.

## Learn it

Knowing the parts of air brakes (AB-01) and how to inspect them (AB-02) is only half the job. This lesson covers how to **use** air brakes on the road: normal stops, stops with ABS, emergency stops, how far it really takes to stop, how to avoid brake fade on hills, and how to park. (pp. 5-11–5-13)

**Key words**
- **ABS** = anti-lock braking system; a computer system that keeps wheels from locking in hard braking.
- **Lock up** = a wheel stops turning while the vehicle is still moving, so the tire slides.
- **Jackknife** = the trailer swings around so the tractor and trailer fold toward each other like a pocket knife.
- **Controlled braking** = braking as hard as you can without locking the wheels.
- **Stab braking** = braking all the way, releasing when wheels lock, and braking fully again when they roll.
- **Perception distance** = how far you travel from seeing a hazard until your brain knows it is a hazard.
- **Reaction distance** = how far you travel from knowing about the hazard until your foot hits the brake.
- **Brake lag** = the delay between pushing the pedal and the air brakes actually working, while air flows through the lines.
- **Braking distance** = how far you travel once the brakes are working until you stop.
- **Hydraulic brakes** = brakes worked by fluid, used on cars and light/medium trucks; they work instantly.
- **Brake fade** = brakes losing stopping power because they got too hot.
- **Engine braking effect** = the slowing you get from the engine when you are in a low gear and off the gas.
- **"Safe" speed** = the top speed you have chosen as safe for a downgrade.
- **Wheel chock** = a wedge put against a tire to keep the vehicle from rolling.

### 5.4.1 Normal stops (p. 5-11)
- Push the brake pedal down. **Control the pressure** so you come to a **smooth, safe stop**.
- **Manual transmission:** do **not** push in the clutch until the engine rpm is **down close to idle**.
- When stopped, **pick a starting gear**.

### 5.4.2 Braking with anti-lock brakes (p. 5-11)
**Why ABS matters:** without ABS, hard braking on a slippery road can **lock the wheels**.
- **Steering wheels** lock → you **lose steering control**.
- **Other wheels** lock → you may **skid, jackknife** or even **spin** the vehicle.

**What ABS does:** the computer senses a wheel is **about to lock**, **lowers the brake pressure** to a safe level, and helps you **keep control**.
- You **may or may not stop faster** with ABS.
- You **should be able to steer around an obstacle while braking**, and avoid skids from braking too hard.

**ABS on part of the rig still helps.** ABS on only the tractor, only the trailer, or even **just 1 axle** still gives more control. **Brake normally.**

| Where ABS is | What it gives you | What to watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Tractor only** | You keep **steering control**; **less chance of jackknifing** | Watch the **trailer**; if it starts to **swing out**, let up on the brakes (if safe) |
| **Trailer only** | Trailer is **less likely to swing out** | If you **lose steering** or a **tractor jackknife** starts, let up on the brakes (if safe) until you regain control |

**How to brake a tractor-trailer with ABS — the same as always:**
- Use **only the braking force you need** to stop safely and stay in control.
- Brake **the same way** whether ABS is on the tractor, the trailer, or both.
- As you slow down, **watch the tractor and trailer** and **back off the brakes** (if safe) to stay in control.
- **The only exception:** if you **always** drive a **straight truck or combination with working ABS on all axles**, you **can fully apply the brakes in an emergency stop**.
- **Without ABS** you still have normal brakes. Drive and brake as you always have.
- **If your ABS fails**, you **still have regular brakes**. Drive normally, but **get it serviced soon**.

### 5.4.3 Emergency stops (p. 5-11)
If someone suddenly pulls out in front of you, hitting the brakes is a good response — **if** there is room to stop and you brake the right way. Brake so the vehicle **stays in a straight line** and you can **still turn** if you need to. Use one of two methods:

| Method | How to do it |
|---|---|
| **Controlled braking** | Brake **as hard as you can without locking the wheels**. Keep steering movements **very small**. If you need to steer more, or the wheels lock, **release** the brakes. **Reapply** as soon as you can. |
| **Stab braking** | Brake **all the way**. **Release** when the wheels **lock up**. As soon as the wheels **start rolling**, brake **fully** again. |

- **Stab braking timing:** it can take **up to 1 second** for the wheels to start rolling after you let off. If you brake again **before** they roll, the vehicle **will not straighten out**.

### 5.4.4 Stopping distance and brake lag (p. 5-12)
- **Brake lag** is the time it takes the brakes to work after you push the pedal. *Why:* the air has to flow through the lines to the brakes.
- **Hydraulic brakes** (cars, light/medium trucks) work **instantly**. **Air brakes** take a little time: **½ second or more**.
- So with air brakes, total stopping distance has **4 parts**:

**Perception distance + Reaction distance + Brake lag distance + Braking distance = Total stopping distance**

- At **55 mph** on dry pavement, brake lag adds about **32 feet**. For an average driver with good traction and brakes, the total stopping distance at 55 mph is **over 450 feet**.

Figure 5.6 (described) — distance in feet by speed:

| Speed | Perception | Reaction | Brake lag | Braking | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 mph | 39 | 16 | 9 | 17 | **81** |
| 25 mph | 65 | 28 | 15 | 47 | **155** |
| 35 mph | 91 | 39 | 21 | 92 | **243** |
| 45 mph | 117 | 50 | 27 | 152 | **346** |
| 55 mph | 142 | 61 | 32 | 216 | **451** |

*Worked example:* at 55 mph, 142 + 61 + 32 + 216 = **451 feet**. Of that, **32 feet** is brake lag — distance a car with hydraulic brakes would not need. For the test, remember **32 ft** of lag and **over 450 ft** total.

### 5.4.5 Brake fading or failure (p. 5-12)
- Brakes slow you when shoes or pads **rub** against drums or discs. That makes **heat**. Brakes are built to take a lot of heat, but they can **fade or fail** from **too much heat** — caused by **using them too much** and **not relying on engine braking**.
- **How fade happens:** too much heat causes **chemical changes in the lining** that **reduce friction**, and it makes the **drums expand**. The shoes then have to move **farther** to reach the drum, and they press with **less force**. Keep overusing them and fade can grow until you **cannot slow down or stop**.
- **Adjustment matters too:** every brake must do its share. Brakes **out of adjustment** stop doing their share **first**. The others then **overheat and fade**, and you may not have enough braking to control the vehicle.
- Brakes can go out of adjustment **quickly**, especially when **hot**. **Check brake adjustment often.**

### 5.4.6 Proper braking technique on downgrades (p. 5-12)
- On a long and/or steep downgrade, the brakes are **only a supplement to the engine's braking effect**. First get into the **correct low gear**.
- Then:
  1. Apply the brakes **just hard enough to feel a definite slowdown**.
  2. When you are about **5 mph below your "safe" speed**, **release** the brakes. (This application should last about **3 seconds**.)
  3. When your speed climbs back up to your "safe" speed, **repeat** steps 1 and 2.
- *Worked example:* your "safe" speed is **40 mph**. Don't brake until you reach 40. Then brake hard enough to slow gradually to **35 mph**, and release. Repeat as often as needed until the bottom of the grade.

### 5.4.7 Low air pressure (p. 5-13)
- If the low-air warning comes on, **stop and safely park as soon as possible**. There may be an **air leak**.
- **Controlled braking** works **only while enough air remains** in the tanks.
- The **spring brakes** come on when pressure drops into **20–45 psi**.
  - A **heavily loaded** vehicle will take a **long distance** to stop, because **spring brakes don't work on all axles**.
  - If the vehicle is **lightly loaded** or the road is **slippery**, the spring brakes coming on can send you into an **uncontrolled skid**.
- **It is much safer to stop while you still have enough air to use the foot brake.**

### 5.4.8 Parking brakes (p. 5-13)
- **Use the parking brakes any time you park**, except in the cases below.
- **Pull** the control out to apply; **push** in to release. On **newer** vehicles it is a **yellow, diamond-shaped knob** labeled "parking brakes." On **older** vehicles it may be a **round blue knob** or another shape — even a **lever** that swings side to side or up and down.

**Do NOT use the parking brakes when:**
| Condition | Why not | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Brakes are **very hot** (for example, just after a steep grade) | The heat can **damage** them | **Chock the wheels** on a level surface; let the brakes **cool** first |
| Brakes are **very wet** in **freezing** temperatures | They can **freeze** so the vehicle **cannot move** | **Chock the wheels** on a level surface. To dry wet brakes, **brake lightly while driving in a low gear** to heat and dry them |

- **No automatic tank drains?** Drain the air tanks **every working day, at the end of the day**, to clear out water and oil — left in, they can make the brakes fail.
- **Never leave your vehicle unattended** without **setting the parking brakes or chocking the wheels**. It could **roll away** and cause injury and damage.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** Push in the clutch as soon as you start braking. → **Correct:** Wait until engine rpm is **down close to idle**. (p. 5-11)
- **Trap:** ABS always lets you stop in a shorter distance. → **Correct:** You **may or may not** stop faster; ABS helps you **steer** and avoid skids. (p. 5-11)
- **Trap:** ABS on only one axle is useless. → **Correct:** ABS on the tractor only, trailer only, or even **1 axle** still gives more control. (p. 5-11)
- **Trap:** With ABS on a tractor-trailer, stomp the brakes hard in any stop. → **Correct:** Brake **as you always have**. Full application is allowed only if you **always** drive a vehicle with working ABS on **all axles**, in an emergency. (p. 5-11)
- **Trap:** If the ABS fails, you have no brakes. → **Correct:** You **still have regular brakes**; drive normally and get ABS serviced soon. (p. 5-11)
- **Trap:** In stab braking, reapply the instant you let off. → **Correct:** Wait until the wheels **start rolling** (up to **1 second**), or the vehicle won't straighten out. (p. 5-11)
- **Trap:** In controlled braking, use big steering moves while braking hard. → **Correct:** Keep steering movements **very small**; for a bigger steering move, **release** the brakes first. (p. 5-11)
- **Trap:** Air brakes work instantly, like car brakes. → **Correct:** Air brakes have **brake lag** of **½ second or more**; **hydraulic** brakes work instantly. (p. 5-12)
- **Trap:** Total stopping distance = perception + reaction + braking. → **Correct:** With air brakes add **brake lag**: **4** parts. (p. 5-12)
- **Trap:** On a long downgrade, the brakes should do most of the slowing. → **Correct:** Brakes are **only a supplement** to the **engine braking effect**; get in the right low gear first. (p. 5-12)
- **Trap:** On a downgrade, brake lightly all the way down. → **Correct:** Brake to a **definite slowdown**, down to **5 mph below** safe speed (about **3 seconds**), then release; repeat. (p. 5-12)
- **Trap:** Keep driving when the low-air warning comes on; the spring brakes will stop you safely. → **Correct:** **Stop and park as soon as possible.** Spring brakes may take a long distance (heavy load) or skid you (light load or slippery road). (p. 5-13)
- **Trap:** Always set the parking brakes, even when the brakes are very hot. → **Correct:** Don't use them if the brakes are **very hot** or **very wet in freezing temps**; **chock** the wheels instead. (p. 5-13)
- **Trap:** If you'll be gone only a minute, you don't need to set the parking brake. → **Correct:** **Never** leave the vehicle unattended without the **parking brakes** or **wheel chocks**. (p. 5-13)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 5.4 #1** Why should you be in the right gear before you start down a hill?
→ On a long or steep downgrade the brakes are only a supplement to the engine's braking effect. You need the correct low gear so the engine does most of the slowing; if you rely on the brakes instead, they overheat and can fade or fail. (p. 5-12)

**TYK 5.4 #2** What can cause brakes to fade or fail?
→ Too much heat from using the brakes too much and not relying on engine braking. Heat changes the lining so it has less friction and makes the drums expand, so the shoes press with less force. Brakes out of adjustment also cause fade, because the other brakes must do extra work and overheat. (p. 5-12)

**TYK 5.4 #3** True or false: on a long, steep downgrade, the brakes only supplement the engine's braking effect.
→ True. Get into the correct low gear first, then use the brakes in short applications (brake to 5 mph below your safe speed, about 3 seconds, then release). (p. 5-12)

**TYK 5.4 #4** True or false: if you will be away from your vehicle only a short time, you don't need to use the parking brake.
→ False. Never leave the vehicle unattended without setting the parking brakes or chocking the wheels; it could roll away and cause injury and damage. (p. 5-13)

**TYK 5.4 #5** How often should you drain the air tanks?
→ At the end of each working day (each day of driving) if the vehicle does not have automatic drains, to get rid of moisture and oil. (pp. 5-2, 5-13)

**TYK 5.4 #6** How do you brake a tractor-trailer that has ABS?
→ The same way you always have: use only the braking force you need to stop safely and stay in control, brake the same way whether ABS is on the tractor, the trailer or both, and watch the tractor and trailer as you slow, backing off the brakes (if safe) to stay in control. (p. 5-11)

**TYK 5.4 #7** True or false: you still have normal brakes if your ABS is not working.
→ True. Drive normally, but get the ABS serviced soon. (p. 5-11)

## One-minute recap
- **Normal stop:** smooth pressure; clutch in only when rpm is **near idle**; pick a starting gear.
- **ABS** lowers pressure when a wheel is about to lock — you can **steer while braking**, but stops aren't always shorter. ABS on **any** part of the rig helps.
- **Brake with ABS as you always have.** Only if you **always** drive with working ABS on **all axles** may you brake fully in an emergency. ABS fails → you still have normal brakes.
- **Controlled braking:** hard without locking, tiny steering. **Stab braking:** full on, off when locked, full on again once rolling (**up to 1 second**).
- **Brake lag ½ second+**; stopping distance = **perception + reaction + brake lag + braking**. At **55 mph**: lag ≈ **32 ft**, total **over 450 ft**.
- **Fade** = too much heat → less lining friction + expanded drums. Out-of-adjustment brakes make the others fade — **check adjustment often**.
- **Downhill:** right low gear first; brakes are only a **supplement**. Brake to **5 mph below** safe speed (~**3 seconds**), release, repeat.
- **Low air** → stop now, while the foot brake still works (spring brakes come on at **20–45 psi**, but may take a long distance to stop or cause a skid). **Park** with parking brakes — **not** if brakes are very hot or wet-and-freezing (chock instead). Never leave the vehicle unattended without brakes or chocks.
