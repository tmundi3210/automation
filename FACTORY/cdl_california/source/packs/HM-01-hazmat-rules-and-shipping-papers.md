# HM-01 · HazMat rules: who does what and shipping papers

> **Handbook:** Section 9.1–9.3.6, pages 9-1–9-8 · **Test:** Hazardous Materials · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~50 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- Who needs an "H" endorsement, who may never haul HazMat, and what training, security check and California license rules apply.
- The 3 goals of the HazMat rules (contain, communicate, assure safe drivers) and the separate jobs of the shipper, the carrier and the driver.
- The 9 hazard classes, where to keep shipping papers, and what package labels and placards are.
- How to read the Hazardous Materials Table: the 6 Column 1 symbols, "Forbidden", ID number prefixes, packing groups and the Column 7 poison inhalation codes.
- What RQ, INHALATION HAZARD and MARINE POLLUTANT mean on a shipping paper.
- How a HazMat shipping paper must be filled out: page numbers, the order of the basic description, the emergency phone number, emergency response information, how long copies are kept, and the shipper's certification.

## Learn it

**Hazardous materials** are products that can hurt people, harm health, or damage property while they are being moved. The short name is **HazMat** (on road signs) or **HM** (in government rules). Because the risks are serious, every level of government regulates them. This lesson covers the first part of Section 9: why the rules exist, who does what, and how the danger is written down on the **shipping paper**. The next lesson covers package markings and placards. (pp. 9-1, 9-3)

**Key words**
- **HazMat** = a material the U.S. Secretary of Transportation has decided can pose an unreasonable risk to health, safety and property when shipped. It includes hazardous substances, hazardous wastes, marine pollutants and materials in the Hazardous Materials Table. (p. 9-24)
- **HMR** = Hazardous Materials Regulations, the federal HazMat rules found in CFR, Title 49, Parts 171–180. (p. 9-1)
- **CFR** = Code of Federal Regulations, the book of federal rules. (p. 9-1)
- **"H" endorsement** = the mark on your CDL that lets you haul HazMat. You get it by passing a HazMat knowledge test. (p. 9-1)
- **Shipper** = the business that sends the product and prepares it for shipping. (p. 9-3)
- **Carrier** = a person or company that moves passengers or property by rail, car, aircraft, motor vehicle or vessel. (p. 9-24)
- **Consignee** = the business or person the shipment is delivered to. (p. 9-24)
- **Hazard class** = the category of danger a material is given. A material may fit more than one class but is assigned only one. (p. 9-24)
- **Division** = a smaller group inside a hazard class, like 2.1 inside Class 2. (p. 9-24)
- **Label** = a small diamond-shaped hazard warning sign the shipper puts on a package. (p. 9-4)
- **Placard** = a large diamond-shaped warning sign on the outside of a vehicle or bulk package that shows the hazard class of the cargo. (p. 9-5)
- **Shipping paper** = a paper that describes the HazMat being shipped. Shipping orders, bills of lading and manifests are all shipping papers. (pp. 9-4, 9-26)
- **Proper shipping name** = the official name of a HazMat, printed in regular (not italic) type in the Hazardous Materials Table. (p. 9-26)
- **Technical name** = the recognized chemical or germ name of the thing that makes a product hazardous. (pp. 9-5, 9-26)
- **ID number** = a 4-digit number that first responders use to identify a HazMat, shown after "UN", "NA" or "ID". (pp. 9-5–9-6)
- **Packing group (PG)** = a group number, written in Roman numerals (I, II, III), assigned to a material. (p. 9-6)
- **N.O.S.** = "not otherwise specified". (p. 9-25)
- **Reportable quantity (RQ)** = the amount of a listed hazardous substance that, if in one package, must be marked "RQ", and whose spill must be reported. (pp. 9-6, 9-26)
- **Hazardous substance** = a material on the List of Hazardous Substances (Appendix A) carried in one package at or above its reportable quantity. (p. 9-25)
- **Marine pollutant** = a chemical that is toxic to sea life, listed in Appendix B. (p. 9-7)
- **ERG** = the U.S. DOT Emergency Response Guidebook, which lists chemicals and their ID numbers. (p. 9-5)
- **Emergency response information (ERI)** = written facts on how to handle a leak, spill, fire or injury involving the material. (p. 9-8)
- **Shipper's certification** = a signed statement on the shipping paper saying the shipper prepared the shipment by the rules. (pp. 9-8, 9-26)
- **CLP** = commercial learner's permit. (p. 9-1)

### 9 HazMat and the "H" endorsement (p. 9-1)
- You need a **CDL with an "H" endorsement** before you drive a vehicle **of any size** used to haul HazMat (as defined in CFR, Title 49 §383.5). You must pass a knowledge test to get it.
- **A CLP holder may not haul HazMat.**
- The HazMat test is based on **federal** rules. Text marked "California" is a state rule that also applies in California, and it is strictly enforced.
- The HazMat rules (HMR) are in **CFR, Title 49, Parts 171–180**.
- The Hazardous Materials Table lists HazMat, but the list is **not complete**. Whether something is HazMat depends on its properties and on the **shipper's decision** that it meets a HazMat definition.
- Vehicles carrying certain types or amounts of HazMat must show **placards**: diamond-shaped ("square on point") warning signs.
- **[CA]** DMV examiners will **not** give CDL skills tests in a vehicle showing placards (CVC §27903). That includes vehicles carrying HazMat or wastes and vehicles not yet purged (cleaned out) of hazardous cargo. CVC §15278(a)(4) requires the "H" endorsement to drive a vehicle that needs placards.
- Rules change often, so keep an up-to-date copy of the regulations. Employers, colleges and associations offer HazMat courses; union or company offices often have copies of the rules.

### 9 Training, security check and permits (p. 9-2)
- **Training and testing are required** for every driver who hauls HazMat. Your **employer** (or its representative) must provide them.
- HazMat employees must be trained and tested **at least once every 3 years**.
- The employer keeps each employee's training record for as long as the person works with HazMat, **plus 90 days**.
- All drivers must be trained in **security risks**: how to spot and respond to possible security threats.
- **Special training** is needed before hauling certain **flammable gas** materials or **highway-route controlled quantities of radioactive** materials. Drivers of **cargo tanks and portable tanks** also need special training.
- **Permits and routes:** some places require permits for certain explosives or bulk hazardous wastes. States and counties may require special HazMat routes. The federal government may require permits or exemptions for special cargo such as **rocket fuel**.
- **[CA]** For permits or route limits, contact **CHP and DOT**. For hazardous waste permits contact the **Department of Toxic Substances Control**; for medical waste contact the **Department of Health Services**.
- **TSA security threat assessment:** when you apply for a new "H" endorsement **or renew** one, you must pass a **TSA** background records check. You start it after you apply for your CDL at DMV, pass all needed knowledge tests, and turn in a valid medical form. You give a TSA agent your **fingerprints**, a **fee**, any other information asked for, a copy of your CDL permit, and **1** of these IDs: a California DL/ID card, an out-of-state DL, or your CLP with a DMV photo receipt.
- **[CA] California HazMat transportation license:** every **motor carrier** hauling these in California needs a license from **CHP** (CVC §32000.5):
  - HazMat loads that must show placards (unless excepted), and
  - HazMat loads **over 500 pounds**, hauled **for a fee**, that would need placards if shipped in larger amounts the same way.
  - A clear, valid copy of the license must be **carried in the vehicle** and shown to any peace officer or CHP employee who asks. This is **in addition to** any federal HazMat registration.

### 9.1 Intent of the regulations (p. 9-3)
The rules have 3 goals:

| Goal | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Contain the material** (9.1.1) | Shippers are told how to **package** it safely; drivers how to **load, transport and unload** it. These are called **containment rules**. |
| **Communicate the risk** (9.1.2) | Shippers warn drivers and others with **labels** on packages, **shipping papers**, **emergency response information** and **placards**. |
| **Assure safe drivers and equipment** (9.1.3) | You must pass a knowledge test for the "H" endorsement. |

- To pass, you must know how to: **identify** HazMat, **safely load** shipments, **placard** your vehicle correctly, and **safely transport** shipments.
- Breaking the rules to save time is unsafe. Not following the rules can bring **fines and jail**.
- **Inspect your vehicle before and during each trip.** Officers may stop you and check your **shipping papers, placards, "H" endorsement**, and your **HazMat knowledge**.

### 9.2 Who does what: shipper, carrier, driver (p. 9-3)
| Who | Main duties |
|---|---|
| **Shipper** (9.2.1) | Sends products by truck, rail, ship or plane. Uses the rules to find each product's **ID number, proper shipping name, hazard class, packing group, correct packaging, labels, markings and placards**. **Packages, marks and labels** the materials; **prepares shipping papers**; gives **emergency response information**; **supplies placards**. **Certifies** on the shipping paper that the load was prepared by the rules (not needed when you pull cargo tanks supplied by you or your employer). |
| **Carrier** (9.2.2) | Takes the load from the shipper to where it is going. **Before** moving it, checks that the shipper described, marked, labeled and prepared it correctly. **Refuses improper shipments.** |
| **Driver** (9.2.3) | Checks that the shipper named, marked and labeled the HazMat correctly. **Turns down leaking packages** and loads. **Puts on placards at loading** when needed. Delivers the load safely and **with no delays**. Obeys every special HazMat rule. Keeps the HazMat papers and emergency response information **where they belong**. **Reports any HazMat crash or incident** to the right government agency if it happens while the driver controls the load. they happen while the driver is in control of the load. |

*Worked example:* a drum is dripping at the dock. The driver's job is to **refuse** it — not to tape it, and not to haul it and report it later.

### 9.3.1–9.3.2 Hazard classes, shipping papers and labels (p. 9-4)
**Hazard classes (Figure 9.1).** There are **9 hazard classes**. The class shows what kind of risk a material has.

| Class | Divisions and names | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Explosives | 1.1 Mass explosion · 1.2 Projection hazard · 1.3 Fire hazard · 1.4 Minor explosion · 1.5 Very insensitive · 1.6 Extremely insensitive | Dynamite · flares · display fireworks · ammunition · blasting agents · explosive devices |
| 2 Gases | 2.1 Flammable gases · 2.2 Non-flammable gases · 2.3 Poisonous/toxic gases | Propane · helium · fluorine, compressed |
| 3 | Flammable liquids | Gasoline |
| 4 | 4.1 Flammable solids · 4.2 Spontaneously combustible · 4.3 Dangerous when wet | Ammonium picrate, wetted · white phosphorus · sodium |
| 5 | 5.1 Oxidizers · 5.2 Organic peroxides | Ammonium nitrate · methyl ethyl ketone peroxide |
| 6 | 6.1 Poison (toxic material) · 6.2 Infectious substances | Potassium cyanide · anthrax virus |
| 7 | Radioactive | Uranium |
| 8 | Corrosives | Battery fluid |
| 9 | Miscellaneous HazMat | Polychlorinated biphenyls (PCB) |
| None | ORM-D (Other Regulated Material–Domestic) | Food flavorings, medicines |
| None | Combustible liquids | Fuel oil |

**Shipping papers (9.3.1).** After a crash or spill you may be hurt and unable to tell anyone what you carry. Firefighters and police can limit harm if they know what the HazMat is — so they must be able to **find the shipping papers fast**. The rules require:
- **Shippers** to describe HazMat correctly and put an **emergency response telephone number** on the shipping papers.
- **Carriers and drivers** to make HazMat shipping papers easy to spot, **or keep them on top** of other shipping papers, with the emergency response information kept with them.
- **Drivers** to keep HazMat shipping papers:
  - in a **pouch on the driver's door**, or
  - in **clear view, within immediate reach** while your **seat belt is fastened** while driving, or
  - **on the driver's seat** when you are **out of the vehicle**.

**Package labels (9.3.2).** Shippers put **diamond-shaped** hazard warning labels on most HazMat packages. If the label will not fit, the shipper may put it on a **tag** firmly attached to the package. For example, compressed gas cylinders that cannot hold a label have **tags or decals**. (Figure 9.2 shows sample labels such as Inhalation Hazard, Poison, Infectious Substance, Oxidizer and Organic Peroxide.)

### 9.3.3 Placards, ID numbers and the 3 lists (p. 9-5)
- **Placards** warn others of HazMat. They go on the **outside of the vehicle** and on **bulk packages**, and they show the **hazard class** of the cargo.
- A placarded vehicle needs **at least 4 identical placards**: **front, rear, and both sides**. They must be readable from **all 4 directions**.
- Size: at least **9.84 inches (250 mm) square**, turned **square-on-point** (a diamond).
- **Cargo tanks and other bulk packaging** show the **ID number** of their contents on placards, **orange panels**, or **white square-on-point displays** the same size as placards.
- **ID numbers** are a **4-digit** code. One ID number may cover **more than 1 chemical**. "NA" or "UN" comes before it. The **Emergency Response Guidebook (ERG)** lists chemicals and their ID numbers.
- **Check 3 lists** before you haul a material. Some materials are on all 3, some on only one:
  1. The **Hazardous Materials Table** (CFR, Title 49 §172.101).
  2. **Appendix A**: List of Hazardous Substances and Reportable Quantities.
  3. **Appendix B**: List of Marine Pollutants.

### 9.3.3 The Hazardous Materials Table, column by column (pp. 9-5–9-6)
Figure 9.4 shows one row of the table (Acetaldehyde ammonia: Class 9, UN1841, PG III, label 9).

**Column 1 — symbols.** **6** symbols may appear here:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **+** | Use this proper shipping name, hazard class and packing group **even if** the material does not meet the class definition. |
| **A** | Covered by the HMR only when shipped by **air** (unless it is a hazardous substance or waste). |
| **W** | Covered by the HMR only when shipped by **water** (unless it is a hazardous substance, waste or marine pollutant). |
| **D** | The shipping name is right for **domestic** shipping but may not be right for international. |
| **I** | The shipping name is used for **international** shipping; another name may be used for domestic-only trips. |
| **G** | A **generic** shipping name. It must be followed by a **technical name** on the shipping paper. |

**Columns 2–8:**
- **Column 2 — proper shipping names**, in **alphabetical order**. Proper shipping names are in **regular type**; names in **italics are not** proper shipping names. The shipping paper must use proper shipping names.
- **Column 3 — hazard class or division**, or the word **"Forbidden"**. **Never haul a "Forbidden" material.** To choose placards you need 3 facts: the material's **hazard class**, the **amount shipped**, and the **amount of all HazMat of all classes** on your vehicle.
- **Column 4 — ID number**, with **"UN"**, **"NA"** or **"ID"** in front.
  - **NA** marks names used only **inside the U.S. and to and from Canada**.
  - **ID** marks names recognized by the International Civil Aviation Organization for **air** shipping.
  - The ID number must be on the **shipping paper** (in the description) and on the **package**, and also on **cargo tanks and other bulk packaging**. *Why:* police and firefighters use it to identify the material quickly.
- **Column 5 — packing group**, in **Roman numerals**.
- **Column 6 — required labels.** Some products need **more than 1 label** because they have more than one hazard.
- **Column 7 — special provisions.** If there is an entry, look up the details in the CFR. The numbers **1–6** here mean the material is a **POISON INHALATION HAZARD**, which has special rules for shipping papers, markings and placards.
- **Column 8** — a **3-part** column giving the section numbers for **packaging** rules.
- **Columns 9 and 10 do not apply to highway** shipping.

### 9.3.3 RQ, inhalation hazard and marine pollutants (pp. 9-6–9-7)
**Appendix A — Hazardous Substances and Reportable Quantities.** DOT and EPA want to know about spills of these substances.
- The list shows each product's **reportable quantity (RQ)** in pounds (kilograms). Examples from Figure 9.5: phosgene **10 lb**, phosphine **100 lb**, phosphoric acid **5,000 lb**.
- When **1 package** holds the RQ **or more**, the shipper shows **"RQ"** on the **shipping paper and the package**. "RQ" may come **before or after** the basic description.
- **You or your employer must report** any spill of these materials that reaches the RQ.

*Worked example:* phosgene's RQ is 10 lb. A single cylinder holding 25 lb of phosgene is more than 10 lb, so the paper and cylinder show "RQ" (this is the Figure 9.6 example).

**INHALATION HAZARD.** If these words are on the shipping paper or package, you must show **POISON INHALATION HAZARD** or **POISON GAS** placards (as the case requires), **in addition to** any other placards for the hazard class — **even for small amounts**. (p. 9-6)

**Appendix B — Marine Pollutants.** Chemicals that are toxic to sea life. (p. 9-7)
- For highway shipping, this list is used only for chemicals in a container holding **119 gallons or more** that has no placard or label as the HMR specifies.
- **Bulk packages** of a marine pollutant must show the **MARINE POLLUTANT marking**: a **white triangle with a fish and an "X" through the fish**. It is a **marking, not a placard**, and it must also be shown on the **outside of the vehicle**.
- The shipping paper must say **MARINE POLLUTANT** near the material's description.

### 9.3.4–9.3.5 The shipping paper and the item description (pp. 9-7–9-8)
**Every HazMat shipping paper must have:**
- **Page numbers** if it has more than 1 page. The **first page** must show the **total** number of pages, for example "Page 1 of 4".
- A **proper shipping description** for each HazMat.
- A **shipper's certification**, signed by the shipper.

**HazMat mixed with non-HazMat on one paper.** The HazMat must be **either**:
- **listed first**, **or**
- **highlighted in a contrasting color**, **or**
- marked with an **"X"** in a column titled **"HM"** in front of its description. **"RQ"** may be used in place of the "X" when a reportable quantity must be shown.

**The basic description** is always in this order:

```
ID number  →  proper shipping name  →  hazard class or division  →  packing group (if any)
UN1263        Paint                    3                            PG II
```
- The packing group is in Roman numerals and may start with "PG".
- The ID number, shipping name and hazard class may **not be abbreviated** unless the HMR allows it.

**The description must also show:**
- the **total quantity and unit** of measure;
- the **number and type of packages** (like "6 Drums");
- **"RQ"** if it is a reportable quantity, and then the **name of the hazardous substance** if the shipping name does not already include it;
- the **technical name** for any material with **"G"** (generic) in Column 1.

Quantity, number and type of packages may go **before or after** the basic description, and the package type and unit may be abbreviated. Example: **10 ctns., UN1263, Paint, 3, PG II, 500 pounds.**

**Hazardous waste:** the shipper puts the word **WASTE** before the proper shipping name on the paper (the hazardous waste manifest). Example: **UN1090, Waste Acetone, 3, PG II.**

A **non-hazardous** material may **not** be described with a hazard class or ID number.

**Emergency response telephone number.** Required on the paper (unless excepted). It is the **shipper's** responsibility. Responders call it to get facts about the HazMat in a spill or fire. It must be the number of:
- the person offering the HazMat for shipping, if that person is the emergency response information (ERI) provider, **or**
- an agency or organization able and willing to give the detailed information. Then the paper must identify the person registered with that provider by **name, contract number, or other unique ID**.

**Emergency response information (ERI).** The shipper must give it to the motor carrier for each HazMat. It must be usable **away from the vehicle** and tell how to handle incidents safely. At minimum it covers:
1. the basic description and technical name;
2. immediate hazards to health;
3. risks of fire or explosion;
4. immediate precautions in an accident or incident;
5. immediate ways to handle fires;
6. first ways to handle spills or leaks when there is no fire;
7. basic first aid steps.

It can be on the shipping paper, on another paper that has the basic description and technical name, or in a guide book such as the **ERG**. Carriers may help by keeping an **ERG on each HazMat vehicle**. **The driver must give the ERI** to any federal, state or local official who responds to or investigates a HazMat incident.

**Keeping copies:**
- **Shipper:** a copy (or electronic image) for **2 years** — **3 years for hazardous waste** — after the first carrier accepts the material.
- **Carrier:** when the shipper is only providing carrier service and did not start the shipment, the carrier keeps a copy for **1 year**.

### 9.3.6 Shipper's certification (p. 9-8)
- When the shipper packages HazMat, the shipper **certifies** it was prepared by the HMR. The signed certification is on the **original shipping paper**.
- **Exceptions:** when the shipper is a **private carrier hauling its own product**, and when the **carrier supplies the package** (for example, a **cargo tank**).
- **You may accept the shipper's certification** about packaging **unless** a package is **clearly unsafe** or does not follow the HMR.
- Some carriers have extra HazMat rules. **Follow your employer's rules** when accepting loads.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** A small pickup hauling HazMat does not need the "H" endorsement. → **Correct:** You need a CDL with "H" to drive a HazMat vehicle of **any size**. (p. 9-1)
- **Trap:** A CLP holder may haul HazMat if a licensed driver rides along. → **Correct:** A CLP holder is **prohibited** from hauling HazMat. (p. 9-1)
- **Trap:** If a material is not in the Hazardous Materials Table, it is not HazMat. → **Correct:** The table is **not all-inclusive**; it depends on the material's properties and the shipper's decision. (p. 9-1)
- **Trap:** The carrier packages, labels and supplies placards. → **Correct:** The **shipper** packages, marks, labels, prepares shipping papers and supplies placards; the carrier checks and refuses improper loads. (p. 9-3)
- **Trap:** A driver may accept a leaking package if it is taped and reported. → **Correct:** The driver **refuses leaking packages and shipments**. (p. 9-3)
- **Trap:** Keep HazMat papers in the glove box or under other papers. → **Correct:** On **top** of other papers (or clearly marked), in the **door pouch** or in **clear view within reach while belted**, and **on the seat** when you leave the cab. (p. 9-4)
- **Trap:** Proper shipping names are the ones in italics. → **Correct:** Proper shipping names are in **regular type**; italic names are **not** proper shipping names. (p. 9-6)
- **Trap:** A "Forbidden" material may move with a special permit. → **Correct:** **Never** transport a "Forbidden" material. (p. 9-6)
- **Trap:** Put the shipping name first, then the ID number. → **Correct:** Order is **ID number, proper shipping name, hazard class, packing group**. (p. 9-7)
- **Trap:** "RQ" means restricted quantity. → **Correct:** It means **reportable quantity**; spills of that amount must be reported. (p. 9-6)
- **Trap:** Small amounts of an INHALATION HAZARD material need no special placard. → **Correct:** Show POISON INHALATION HAZARD or POISON GAS placards **even for small amounts**, plus the class placards. (p. 9-6)
- **Trap:** The MARINE POLLUTANT sign is a placard. → **Correct:** It is a **marking**, not a placard, shown on bulk packages and the outside of the vehicle. (p. 9-7)
- **Trap:** The emergency response phone number is the driver's job. → **Correct:** It is the **shipper's** responsibility. (p. 9-8)
- **Trap:** Shippers keep shipping papers 1 year. → **Correct:** **2 years**, or **3 years** for hazardous waste. The 1-year rule is for the carrier in the carrier-service-only case. (p. 9-8)
- **Trap:** You must open packages to check the shipper's certification. → **Correct:** You may **accept the certification** unless a package is clearly unsafe or does not follow the HMR. (p. 9-8)
- **Trap:** A generic ("G") shipping name is enough by itself. → **Correct:** It must be followed by the **technical name** on the shipping paper. (pp. 9-5, 9-8)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 9.1–9.3 #1** Shippers package in order to ___ the material.
→ Contain. Packaging rules are called containment rules; they keep the material safely inside during shipping. (p. 9-3)

**TYK 9.1–9.3 #2** Drivers placard their vehicle to ___ the risk.
→ Communicate. Placards, like labels, shipping papers and emergency response information, warn others about the hazard. (p. 9-3)

**TYK 9.1–9.3 #4** A HazMat ID number must appear on the ___ and on the ___. The ID number must also appear on cargo tanks and other bulk packaging.
→ On the shipping paper (as part of the shipping description) and on the package. (p. 9-6)

**TYK 9.1–9.3 #5** Where must you keep shipping papers describing HazMat?
→ In a pouch on the driver's door, or in clear view within immediate reach while you drive with your seat belt fastened, or on the driver's seat when you are out of the vehicle. Keep them marked as HazMat papers or on top of other papers, with the emergency response information. (p. 9-4)

## One-minute recap
- "H" endorsement for HazMat in a vehicle of **any size**; **CLP holders may not haul HazMat**; TSA check for new or renewed "H"; training at least every **3 years**.
- **[CA]** CHP HazMat transportation license for placarded loads or loads **over 500 lb** hauled for a fee; carry a copy in the vehicle.
- Rules aim to **contain** the material, **communicate** the risk, and **assure safe drivers and equipment**.
- **Shipper** packages, labels, writes the papers, supplies placards and certifies; **carrier** checks and refuses bad loads; **driver** checks, refuses leaks, placards, keeps papers in place, reports incidents.
- **9 hazard classes**. Papers: **door pouch** or **in clear view within reach while belted**; **on the seat** when you leave.
- Check **3 lists**. Column 1 symbols **+ A W D I G**; "Forbidden" = **never haul**; ID number after **UN/NA/ID**; Column 7 **1–6** = poison inhalation hazard; Columns 9–10 not for highway.
- Basic description: **ID number, proper shipping name, hazard class, packing group**. HazMat listed first, highlighted, or "X"/"RQ" in the HM column. "Page 1 of X". WASTE before a waste's name.
- Shipper gives the **emergency phone number** and **ERI**; shipper keeps papers **2 years** (**3** for waste). Accept the shipper's certification unless the package is clearly unsafe.
