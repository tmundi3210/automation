# HM-04 · HazMat driving, parking and emergencies

> **Handbook:** Section 9.6–9.7, pages 9-16–9-23 · **Test:** Hazardous Materials · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~45 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- Where you may and may not park with Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives (the **5 ft** and **300 ft** rules), what a safe haven is, and who may watch the vehicle.
- What the person attending a placarded vehicle must do, why flares are banned, and how route rules (including California's CHP routes) work.
- The on-the-road rules: the **25 ft** no-smoking rule (and which loads it covers), engine off to refuel, a **10 B:C** extinguisher, tire checks, where shipping papers go, chlorine gear, and stopping at railroad crossings.
- What the Emergency Response Guidebook is, and your four jobs at an accident or incident.
- What to do about fires, leaks and spills, and how to respond to each hazard class.
- When the National Response Center must be called, what to tell them, and California's immediate spill reporting rule.

## Learn it

This lesson covers the driving and parking rules that keep a loaded HazMat vehicle away from people and fire, and what to do when something goes wrong. (pp. 9-16–9-23)

**Key words**
- **Placarded vehicle** = a vehicle that must show HazMat warning placards because of what it carries.
- **Division 1.1, 1.2, 1.3** = the explosives divisions with mass explosion (1.1), projection (1.2) and mass fire (1.3) hazards.
- **Traveled part of the road** = the lanes where traffic actually drives (not the shoulder beyond it).
- **Safe haven** = a place approved (usually by local authorities) for parking unattended vehicles loaded with explosives.
- **Shipper** = the business that sends the HazMat. **Carrier** = the company or person that transports it. **Consignee** = the business or person it is delivered to.
- **Route plan** = a written plan of the roads you will use; required for Division 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 explosives.
- **FMCSR** = Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations. Drivers hauling Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives must carry a copy of Part 397.
- **CHP** = California Highway Patrol.
- **10 B:C fire extinguisher** = the minimum Underwriters Laboratories (UL) rating for the fire extinguisher on a placarded power unit.
- **ERG** = Emergency Response Guidebook, the DOT guide emergency crews use to protect themselves and the public from HazMat.
- **Upwind** = the side the wind is blowing from, so fumes blow away from you.
- **Survey meter** = an instrument that measures radiation.
- **National Response Center** = a federal center with a 24-hour toll-free line that helps coordinate responses to chemical hazards.
- **CHEMTREC** = Chemical Transportation Emergency Center, a 24-hour line that gives emergency crews technical facts about HazMat.
- **Reportable quantity (RQ)** = an amount of a hazardous substance listed in the federal rules (Appendix A to 49 CFR §172.101). If that much is involved in an incident, extra details must be reported.

### 9.6.1 Parking with Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives (p. 9-16)
- **Never park within 5 feet** of the **traveled part of the road**.
- **Don't park within 300 feet** of:
  - a **bridge, tunnel or building**;
  - a place where **people gather**;
  - an **open fire**.
- Exception: **short stops needed to operate the vehicle**, such as **fueling**.
- If you must park to do your job, keep it **brief**. **Don't park on private property** unless the owner **knows the danger**.
- The parked vehicle must be **watched at all times**. A person **other than you** may do the watching **only** on the **shipper's**, **carrier's** or **consignee's** property.
- You may leave it **unattended** only in a **safe haven**. Local authorities usually decide which places are safe havens.

*Why:* an explosion near a bridge, a crowd or a building would multiply the harm.

### 9.6.2–9.6.4 Other placarded vehicles, attending, and no flares (p. 9-16)
**Placarded vehicle *not* carrying Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives (9.6.2)**
- You may park **within 5 feet** of the traveled part of the road **only if your work requires it**, and only **briefly**.
- **Someone must always watch** it when it is parked on a **public road or shoulder**.
- **Don't uncouple a trailer** and leave it with HazMat on a **public street**.
- **Don't park within 300 feet of an open fire.**

**Attending a placarded vehicle (9.6.3).** The person attending must:
- be **in the vehicle, awake, and not in the sleeper berth**, **or** be **within 100 feet** of the vehicle with it in **clear view**;
- **know the hazards** of the materials;
- **know what to do** in an emergency;
- be **able to move the vehicle** if needed.

**No flares! (9.6.4)** If you break down, use **reflective triangles or red electric lights**. **Never use burning signals** (flares or fuses) around:
- a **tank** used for **Class 3 (Flammable Liquids)** or **Division 2.1 (Flammable Gas)** — **loaded or empty**;
- a vehicle loaded with **Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives**.

*Why:* an "empty" tank still holds vapor that a flare can ignite.

### 9.6.5 Route restrictions (pp. 9-16–9-17)
- Some states and counties require **permits** and limit **routes**. These local rules **change often**. It is **your job as the driver** to find out whether you need permits or special routes, and to have all papers **before starting**.
- Work for a carrier? **Ask your dispatcher.** Independent trucker on a new route? **Check with state agencies** where you will travel.
- Some places ban HazMat in **tunnels, over bridges** or on other roads. **Always check before you start.**
- When placarded, **avoid** heavily populated areas, crowds, tunnels, narrow streets and alleys — take other routes **even if inconvenient**, unless there is no other way.
- **Never drive near open fires** unless you can **safely pass without stopping**.
- **Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives** need a **written route plan**, and you must follow it. The **carrier** prepares it in advance and gives you a copy. You may plan it yourself if you pick up the explosives somewhere **other than your employer's terminal** — write it out in advance and keep a copy with you. Deliver explosives **only to authorized persons**, or leave them in **locked rooms** built for storing explosives.
- **Placarded radioactive materials:** the **carrier** must choose the **safest route**, then tell the driver about the materials and show the route plan.

### 9.6.5 [CA] California routing and parking rules (pp. 9-17–9-18)
- **Explosives:** carrying **any amount** of Division **1.1, 1.2, 1.3 or 1.6** explosives (or any of these together with **Division 1.5** blasting agents) **for hire or as a delivery service** → you must use the **special routes, safe stopping places, safe parking places and mandatory inspection locations** set by **CHP**. The same applies to **more than 1,000 pounds** of these explosives in **private carriage**.
- **Inhalation hazards:** POISON INHALATION HAZARD, TOXIC INHALATION HAZARD or INHALATION HAZARD materials in **bulk packaging** also must use CHP's special routes, safe stopping places and inspection locations.
- **Radioactive:** CHP sets routes for **Highway-Route Controlled Quantity (HRCQ)** and **Radioactive Materials (RAM)** shipments.
- You must **carry a copy of the routes** the carrier gives you for your shipment. Carriers can request the CHP route publications on the **CHP 361M** form (Application for Hazardous Materials Transportation License).

**General California rules** for any shipment that needs placards or markings (unless it follows special routing):
- Use **state or interstate highways** with the **least transit time** whenever possible (unless restricted or prohibited).
- When practical, **avoid congested highways, crowds and residence districts**.
- **Operating convenience is no excuse** to leave a designated route.
- **Don't leave a loaded vehicle unattended or parked overnight in a residence district.**
- Other highways may be used when needed for **pickup or delivery**, and to reach **fuel, repairs, rest or food** at facilities built for commercial vehicle parking, if the facility is within **1/2 mile** of the exit or entry point of the designated route.
- **Restricted or prohibited routes** may be used **only when no other lawful choice exists**. CHP publishes the list.

### 9.6.6–9.6.9 No smoking, refueling, fire extinguisher and tires (p. 9-18)
**No smoking (9.6.6)**
- No smoking **closer than 25 ft** to a placarded **Class 3 or Division 2.1** cargo tank.
- Within **25 ft** of any vehicle carrying HazMat of these kinds, no smoking and no **lit cigarette, cigar or pipe**: **Class 1** (Explosives), **Class 3** (Flammable Liquids), **4.1** (Flammable Solids), **4.2** (Spontaneously Combustible) or **Class 5** (Oxidizers).

**Refuel with the engine off (9.6.7)**
- **Turn off the engine** before fueling a vehicle containing HazMat.
- **Someone must always be at the nozzle**, controlling the fuel flow.

**Fire extinguisher (9.6.8)**
- The **power unit** of a placarded vehicle must have a fire extinguisher with a **UL rating of 10 B:C or more**.

**Check tires (9.6.9)**
- Keep tires **properly inflated**.
- Look at **every tire** **when a trip starts** and **whenever you park**.
- A **tire pressure gauge** is the **only accepted way** to check pressure. (Kicking or thumping a tire doesn't count.)
- Don't drive on a **leaking or flat** tire except to the **nearest safe place** to fix it.
- **Remove any overheated tire** and put it a **safe distance** from the vehicle. Don't drive until you **fix the cause** of the overheating.
- The **parking and attending rules still apply** while you check, repair or replace tires.

> Handbook vs other sources: many practice tests say to check the tires of a placarded vehicle "every 2 hours or 100 miles." In this handbook, "every 2 hours or every 100 miles" is the very-hot-weather tire rule (p. 2-34). For HazMat, the handbook says **at the start of each trip and each time you park** (p. 9-18) — use that for HazMat questions.

### 9.6.10–9.6.12 Shipping papers, chlorine and railroad crossings (pp. 9-18–9-19)
**Where to keep shipping papers and emergency response information (9.6.10)**
- **Don't accept** a HazMat shipment **without a properly prepared shipping paper**.
- HazMat papers must be **easy to recognize**, so others can find them fast after a crash: **tab them** or keep them **on top of the stack**.
- **Behind the wheel:** within **your reach with your seat belt on**, or in a **pouch on the driver's door**. They must be easy to see for someone entering the cab.
- **Out of the vehicle (not behind the wheel):** in the **driver's door pouch** or **on the driver's seat**.
- **Emergency response information** goes in the **same place** as the shipping paper.

**Division 1.1, 1.2 or 1.3 explosives papers.** The carrier must give you a copy of **FMCSR Part 397** and **written instructions** for delays and accidents. The instructions list: **names and phone numbers** of contacts (including carrier agents or shippers), the **nature of the explosives**, and **precautions** for fires, accidents or leaks. You **sign a receipt** for these. While driving you must have, and know:
- shipping papers;
- written emergency instructions;
- a written route plan;
- a copy of FMCSR Part 397.

**Chlorine (9.6.11).** A driver hauling chlorine in **cargo tanks** must have an **approved gas mask** in the vehicle and an **emergency kit** for controlling leaks in **dome cover plate fittings**.

**Railroad crossings (9.6.12).** **Stop before a crossing** if your vehicle:
- is **placarded**;
- carries **any amount of chlorine**; or
- has **cargo tanks used for HazMat**, whether **loaded or empty**.

Stop **15 to 50 feet** before the **nearest rail**. Cross only once you know **no train is approaching** and you can get **all the way across without stopping**. **Don't shift gears** while crossing.

### 9.7.1–9.7.2 Emergency Response Guidebook and accidents (p. 9-19)
**ERG (9.7.1).** DOT's **Emergency Response Guidebook** is for **firefighters, police and industry workers**. It is **indexed by proper shipping name and HazMat ID number**. Emergency crews look for these on the shipping paper — that is why the **shipping name, ID number, labels and placards must be correct**.

**Your job at an accident or incident (9.7.2):**
- **Keep people away** from the scene.
- **Limit the spread** of material — **only if you can do it safely**.
- **Tell emergency responders about the danger** of the HazMat.
- **Give responders the shipping papers** and emergency response information.

**Checklist:**
1. Check that your **driving partner** is OK.
2. **Keep the shipping papers with you.**
3. Keep people **far away and upwind**.
4. **Warn others** of the danger.
5. **Call for help.**
6. Follow your **employer's instructions**.

### 9.7.3 Fires, leaks and spills (p. 9-20)
**Fires**
- You may have to handle **minor truck fires**, but **don't fight HazMat fires** unless you have the **training and equipment**.
- If you find a fire, **call for help**. You may use the extinguisher to keep a **minor truck fire** from spreading to the cargo until firefighters arrive.
- **Feel the trailer doors** before opening them. If they are **hot**, you may have a **cargo fire** — **don't open the doors**. *Why:* opening lets in air and can make the fire **flare up**; without air, many fires only **smolder**.
- If the cargo is **already burning**, it is **not safe** to fight it. Keep the shipping papers to hand to responders, and warn people away.

**Leaks**
- **Call for help.** Identify the HazMat from **shipping papers, labels or package location**.
- **Don't touch** leaking material. **Don't smell** it to identify it or find the leak — toxic gases can **destroy your sense of smell** and injure or kill you, even with **no odor**.
- **Never eat, drink or smoke** around a leak or spill.

**Spills from your vehicle**
- **Call for help.** Don't move the vehicle **more than safety requires**. You may move **off the road** and away from places where people gather if that is safer, and only if you can do it without danger to anyone.
- **Never keep driving** with HazMat leaking to find a phone, truck stop or help. The **carrier pays for cleanup** of roads, lots and ditches, and the cost is **enormous**.
- **Park it. Secure the area. Stay there.** Call on your **cell phone or CB**. If they don't work, **send someone else** for help.
- Give that person (best **written down**, or let them photograph your papers):
  - a **description** of the emergency;
  - your **exact location and direction of travel**;
  - **your name, the carrier's name**, and the **community or city** where your terminal is;
  - the **proper shipping name, hazard class and ID number**, if you know them.
- **Never move the vehicle** if that would cause contamination or damage it. Stay **upwind** and away from rest stops, truck stops, cafes and businesses.
- **Never repack leaking containers.** Don't try to repair leaks unless trained and equipped. Call your **dispatcher or supervisor**, and emergency personnel if needed.

### 9.7.4 Responses to specific hazards (p. 9-21)
| Class | What to do |
|---|---|
| **1 Explosives** | Warn others, keep bystanders away, **no smoking or open fire**. If there is a fire, warn everyone of **explosion** danger. **Remove all explosives before separating** wrecked vehicles, and place them **at least 200 feet** from vehicles and occupied buildings. |
| **2 Compressed gases** | Warn others; let only people removing the hazard or wreckage get close. **Notify the shipper** if compressed gas is in **any accident**. Don't transfer flammable compressed gas between tanks on a public road, **except to fuel road construction or maintenance machinery**. |
| **3 Flammable liquids** | Keep bystanders from gathering, warn them, **no smoking**. Never haul a **leaking cargo tank** farther than needed to reach a **safe place**; get off the road if safe. No transfers between vehicles on a public road **except in an emergency**. |
| **4 Flammable solids / 5 Oxidizers** | Warn of **fire** hazard. **Don't open smoldering packages** of flammable solids; remove them if you safely can. Also remove **unbroken packages** if that lowers the fire hazard. |
| **6 Poisons / infectious substances** | Protect yourself, others and property. Many poisons are **also flammable** — take flammable precautions: **no smoking, open flame or welding**. A vehicle in a **2.3 or 6.1 leak** must be **checked for stray poison** before reuse. Damaged **6.2** package → **contact your supervisor** at once; don't accept damaged or leaking packages. |
| **7 Radioactive** | Leak or broken package → tell your **dispatcher or supervisor** ASAP. **Don't touch or inhale** spilled material. Don't use the vehicle until it is **cleaned and checked with a survey meter**. |
| **8 Corrosives** | Handle containers carefully. **Wash** vehicle parts that touched the liquid **thoroughly with water**; wash out the inside **after unloading, before reloading**. If driving on would be unsafe, get off the road; contain the leak if safe; keep people away from liquid and fumes. |

### 9.7.5 Required notification (pp. 9-22–9-23)
You or your employer must phone the **National Response Center** (**1-800-424-8802**, 24 hours, toll-free) when any of these happens as a **direct result** of a HazMat incident:
- a person is **killed**;
- an injured person needs **hospitalization**;
- estimated property damage is **more than $50,000**;
- the general public is **evacuated for more than 1 hour**;
- a major transportation artery or facility is **closed for 1 hour or more**;
- **fire, breakage, spillage** or suspected **radioactive contamination** occurs;
- fire, breakage, spillage or suspected contamination involving **infectious substances** (bacteria or toxins);
- a **marine pollutant** is released in a quantity **more than 119 gallons** (liquid) or **882 pounds** (solid); or the carrier judges a situation (such as a **continuing danger to life**) should be reported.

**Be ready to give:** your **name**; the carrier's **name and address**; a **phone number** where you can be reached; the **date, time and location**; the extent of **injuries**; the **classification, name and quantity** of HazMat, if known; the **type of incident**, how the HazMat is involved, and whether there is a **continuing danger to life**. If a **reportable quantity** of a hazardous substance was involved, also the **shipper's name** and the **quantity discharged**.

- Give your **employer** the same information.
- Carriers must send **detailed written reports within 30 days** of an incident **to CHEMTREC** (**1-800-424-9300**).
- **CHEMTREC** (the Chemical Transportation Emergency Center in Washington, DC) also runs a **24-hour toll-free line**. It was set up to give emergency crews **technical information** about the physical properties of HazMat. The National Response Center and CHEMTREC **talk to each other** — call either one and they will tell the other when appropriate. (p. 9-22)

**[CA] California immediate spill reporting (p. 9-23).** A HazMat spill on a California highway must be reported **immediately** to the **CHP** office or **police department** with traffic jurisdiction.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** You may park with Division 1.1 explosives right on the shoulder if you stay with the truck. → **Correct:** **never within 5 ft** of the traveled part of the road, and not within **300 ft** of a bridge, tunnel, building, crowd or open fire. (p. 9-16)
- **Trap:** A friend can watch your explosives load at a truck stop while you eat. → **Correct:** someone else may watch it **only on the shipper's, carrier's or consignee's property**; otherwise use a **safe haven**. (p. 9-16)
- **Trap:** Resting in the sleeper berth counts as attending the vehicle. → **Correct:** you must be **awake, not in the sleeper**, or within **100 ft** with a clear view. (p. 9-16)
- **Trap:** Flares are fine next to an empty gasoline tank. → **Correct:** never use flares near a Class 3 or Division 2.1 tank, **loaded or empty**; use triangles or red electric lights. (p. 9-16)
- **Trap:** Your dispatcher is responsible for knowing route permits, so you don't need to check. → **Correct:** it is **your job as the driver** to find out about permits and routes before you start. (p. 9-16)
- **Trap:** Smoking is fine 5 feet from a placarded gasoline (Class 3) cargo tank if you are outside. → **Correct:** no smoking within **25 ft** of a placarded Class 3 or Division 2.1 cargo tank. (p. 9-18)
- **Trap:** You may leave the engine running while refueling a HazMat load if you stay at the pump. → **Correct:** **engine off**, and someone must be **at the nozzle** the whole time. (p. 9-18)
- **Trap:** You can check tire pressure by kicking the tires. → **Correct:** the **only acceptable way** is a **tire pressure gauge**. (p. 9-18)
- **Trap:** When you leave the cab, take the shipping papers with you. → **Correct:** leave them in the **driver's door pouch** or **on the driver's seat** so responders can find them. (At an accident scene, though, you keep them with you to hand over.) (pp. 9-18–9-19)
- **Trap:** An empty HazMat cargo tank doesn't have to stop at railroad crossings. → **Correct:** cargo tanks used for HazMat must stop **whether loaded or empty**. (p. 9-19)
- **Trap:** Open the trailer doors to see what is burning. → **Correct:** **feel the doors**; if hot, **don't open them** — air can make the fire flare up. (p. 9-20)
- **Trap:** Sniff carefully to find where a leak is coming from. → **Correct:** **never use smell**; toxic gases can destroy your sense of smell and kill you, even with no odor. (p. 9-20)
- **Trap:** With a slow leak and no phone, drive on to the next town to get help. → **Correct:** **park, secure the area, stay there**, and **send someone** for help with written details. (p. 9-20)
- **Trap:** After a crash, separate the wrecked vehicles first, then unload the explosives. → **Correct:** **remove all explosives before separating** vehicles, and place them **at least 200 ft** away. (p. 9-21)
- **Trap:** The National Response Center must be called for any property damage. → **Correct:** the property-damage trigger is **more than $50,000** (plus the other listed events). (p. 9-22)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 9.6 & 9.7 #1** If your placarded trailer has dual tires, how often should you check the tires?
→ Examine **each tire** (including both tires of each dual) at the **beginning of each trip** and **each time you park**, using a **tire pressure gauge**. (p. 9-18)

**TYK 9.6 & 9.7 #2** What is a safe haven?
→ An **approved place for parking unattended vehicles loaded with explosives**. Local authorities usually designate safe havens. (p. 9-16)

**TYK 9.6 & 9.7 #3** How close to the traveled part of the road can you park with Division 1.2 or 1.3 materials?
→ **No closer than 5 feet.** Never park within 5 ft of the traveled part of the road. (p. 9-16)

**TYK 9.6 & 9.7 #4** How close can you park to a bridge, tunnel or building with the same load?
→ **Not within 300 feet**, except for brief stops needed to operate the vehicle, such as fueling. (p. 9-16)

**TYK 9.6 & 9.7 #5** What type of fire extinguisher must placarded vehicles carry?
→ One with a UL rating of **10 B:C or more**, on the power unit. (p. 9-18)

**TYK 9.6 & 9.7 #6** You are hauling 100 pounds of Division 4.3 (DANGEROUS WHEN WET) materials. Do you need to stop before a railroad-highway crossing?
→ **Yes.** Division 4.3 is on Placard Table 1, so **any amount** must be placarded, and every **placarded** vehicle must stop 15–50 ft before the nearest rail. (pp. 9-10, 9-19)

**TYK 9.6 & 9.7 #7** At a rest area you find your HazMat shipment slowly leaking. There is no phone around. What should you do?
→ **Don't drive on** to find a phone. **Park, secure the area and stay there**, keeping people away and upwind. Try your cell phone or CB; if they don't work, **send someone for help** with written details: what happened, your exact location and direction of travel, your name, the carrier's name and your terminal's city, and the shipping name, hazard class and ID number if you know them. (p. 9-20)

**TYK 9.6 & 9.7 #8** What is the Emergency Response Guide (ERG)?
→ A **DOT guidebook** for firefighters, police and industry workers on how to protect themselves and the public from HazMat. It is indexed by **proper shipping name** and **HazMat ID number**. (p. 9-19)

## One-minute recap
- Explosives (1.1–1.3): never park within **5 ft** of the road or within **300 ft** of bridges, tunnels, buildings, crowds or open fire. Unattended only in a **safe haven**; others may watch only on shipper, carrier or consignee property.
- Attending: **in the vehicle, awake, not in the sleeper**, or within **100 ft** with a clear view. **No flares** near flammable tanks (even empty) or explosives.
- Routes: **you** check permits; avoid crowds, tunnels, narrow streets; explosives need a **written route plan**. [CA] CHP sets routes for explosives, inhalation hazards and radioactive loads.
- No smoking within **25 ft** of a placarded Class 3/Div. 2.1 cargo tank or a vehicle with Class 1, 3, 4.1, 4.2 or 5. Refuel with **engine off** and someone at the nozzle. **10 B:C** extinguisher. Check tires at **the start of each trip and each time you park**, with a **gauge**.
- Papers: within reach or in the **door pouch** while driving; **door pouch or driver's seat** when out. Chlorine: **gas mask + leak kit**.
- Stop at crossings if **placarded, any chlorine, or HazMat cargo tank (even empty)**: **15–50 ft**, no shifting.
- Emergencies: keep people **away and upwind**, give responders the papers, don't open hot doors, **never smell or touch**, **park-secure-stay**, explosives **200 ft** away.
- Call the National Response Center for deaths, hospitalization, damage **over $50,000**, evacuation **over 1 hour**; written report in **30 days**. [CA] Report highway spills to CHP **immediately**.
