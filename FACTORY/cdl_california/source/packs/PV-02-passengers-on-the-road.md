# PV-02 · Passengers on the road

> **Handbook:** Section 4.3–4.6, pages 4-4–4-6 · **Test:** Passenger Transport · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~35 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- How to supervise riders, and when to explain the trip rules.
- How to start and stop safely at bus stops, and where (not) to let a drunk or disruptive rider off.
- Where bus crashes most often happen, and why buses must slow down more than cars on curves.
- Where to stop at railroad crossings (**15–50 ft**) and drawbridges (**at least 50 ft**), and when you do **not** have to stop.
- What to check and report after each shift, the three **prohibited practices**, and why the **brake-door interlock** is not a parking brake.

## Learn it

Once the bus is loaded and moving, you are responsible for the riders as well as the road. This lesson covers riding rules, stops, the crashes buses have most often, railroad crossings and drawbridges, the end-of-shift check, and a few things a bus driver must never do. (p. 4-4)

**Key words**
- **Charter carrier** = a company that rents out a bus and driver to a group. **Intercity carrier** = a company whose buses run between cities.
- **Mass transit bus** = a city bus that runs a regular route with many stops.
- **Disruptive rider** = a rider who causes trouble, such as a drunk or angry person.
- **Intersection** = a place where two or more roads cross.
- **Banked curve** = a curve where the road is tilted, higher on the outside edge, to help vehicles turn.
- **Design speed** = the safe speed a banked curve was built for.
- **Traction** = the grip of the tires on the road.
- **Convex mirror** = a mirror that curves outward. It shows a wider area than a flat mirror.
- **Flagman** = a person who directs traffic with a flag or sign.
- **Drawbridge** = a bridge that lifts or swings open to let boats pass. The **draw** is the part that opens.
- **Interstate carrier** = a company that drives across state lines.
- **Brake-door interlock** = a system on some transit buses that holds the bus still while the rear door is open.

### 4.3.1 Supervising passengers (p. 4-4)
- Many charter and intercity carriers have rules for rider comfort and safety.
- **Explain the rules about smoking, drinking, or electronic devices at the start of the trip.** *Why:* telling riders early helps avoid trouble later.
- While driving, **scan the inside of the bus** as well as the road **ahead, to the sides, and to the rear**.
- You may need to remind riders about the rules, or to **keep their arms and heads inside** the bus.

### 4.3.2 At stops, and disruptive riders (p. 4-4)
- Riders can **stumble** getting on or off, and when the bus **starts or stops**.
- **Tell riders to watch their step** when they leave the bus.
- **Wait for riders to sit down or brace themselves before you start.**
- **Start and stop as smoothly as possible** so no one gets hurt.
- **Drunk or disruptive rider:** you must keep **this rider safe as well as the others**.
  - **Do not let such a rider off where it would be unsafe for them.**
  - It may be safer to let them off at the **next scheduled stop**, or in a **well-lit area where there are other people**.
  - Many carriers have their own guidelines for disruptive riders.

### 4.3.3 Common bus accidents (p. 4-4)
- **Bus accidents often happen at intersections.** Be careful **even when a signal or stop sign controls the other traffic**. *Why:* other drivers may not obey it.
- **School and mass transit buses** sometimes **scrape off mirrors or hit passing vehicles** when **pulling out from a bus stop**.
- Remember how much **clearance** your bus needs, and watch for **poles and tree limbs** at stops.
- Know the size of the **gap** your bus needs to speed up and merge with traffic. **Wait for the gap to open** before you leave the stop.
- **Never assume other drivers will brake to make room** when you signal or start to pull out.

### 4.3.4 Speed on curves and using your mirrors (p. 4-4)
- Deadly bus crashes on curves come from **too much speed**, often when **rain or snow** has made the road slippery.
- Every banked curve has a safe **"design speed"**. In good weather, the **posted speed is safe for cars but may be too fast for many buses**.
- Going too fast:
  - with **good traction**, the bus may **roll over**;
  - with **poor traction**, it may **slide off** the curve.
- **Slow down for curves!** **If your bus leans toward the outside on a banked curve, you are going too fast.**
- **Mirrors (4.3.4(a)):**
  - **Check mirrors quickly**, looking back and forth regularly as part of your scan.
  - **Don't look at them too long.** *Why:* the bus travels a long way while you are not watching what is ahead.
  - Many buses have **convex mirrors**, which show a **wider area** than flat mirrors. But they make things look **smaller and farther away** than they really are.

### 4.3.5 Railroad crossings (p. 4-5)
- **Stop your bus between 15 and 50 feet before a railroad crossing.**
- **Listen and look both ways** for trains. **Open your forward door** if that helps you **see or hear** a train coming.
- After a train passes, make sure **no other train is coming the other way** on other tracks before you cross.
- With a **manual transmission**, **never change gears while crossing the tracks**. *Why:* if you miss the shift, the bus can stall on the tracks.
- **You do not have to stop — but you must slow down and carefully check for other vehicles —** in these cases:
  - tracks that run **alongside and on the roadway** inside a **business or residence district**;
  - **streetcar crossings**;
  - where a **peace officer or flagman** is directing traffic;
  - when a **traffic signal is green**;
  - crossings marked **"exempt"** or **"abandoned"**.
- The handbook's Figure 4-A compares crossings. Where the tracks pass **through an intersection run by an official traffic control signal**, the crossing is **exempt**. Where the tracks are **outside that intersection** and have their **own railroad crossing warning device**, it is **not exempt** — you must stop.

### 4.3.6 Drawbridges (p. 4-5)
- **Stop at a drawbridge that has no signal light and no traffic control attendant.**
- **Stop at least 50 feet before the draw** of the bridge.
- **Look to make sure the draw is completely closed** before you cross.
- **You do not have to stop — but you must slow down and make sure it is safe —** when:
  - a **traffic light is showing green**; or
  - the bridge has an **attendant or traffic officer** who controls traffic whenever the bridge opens.

*Worked example:* railroad crossing → stop **between 15 and 50 ft** (a range). Drawbridge with no light or attendant → stop **at least 50 ft** (a minimum, so 60 ft is fine, 40 ft is not).

### 4.4 After-trip vehicle inspection (p. 4-5)
- **Inspect your bus at the end of each shift.**
- If you work for an **interstate carrier**, you must fill out a **written inspection report for each bus** you drove.
  - The report must **name each bus** and **list any defect** that would **affect safety or cause a breakdown**.
  - **If there are no defects, the report must say so.**
- Riders sometimes damage safety parts such as **handholds, seats, emergency exits, and windows**. Report this damage at the end of your shift. *Why:* mechanics can fix it **before the bus goes out again**.
- **Mass transit drivers** should also check that **passenger signaling devices** and **brake-door interlocks** work properly.

### 4.5 Prohibited practices (p. 4-6)
1. **Fueling:** avoid fueling with riders on board **unless absolutely necessary**. **Never refuel in a closed building with riders on board.** *Why:* fuel fumes can build up indoors and catch fire.
2. **Distractions:** **do not talk with riders**, or do anything else that distracts you, **while driving**.
3. **Towing or pushing:** **do not tow or push a disabled bus with riders aboard**, **unless getting off would be unsafe**. Then tow or push it **only to the nearest safe spot** to let the riders off. Follow your employer's rules for towing or pushing disabled buses.

### 4.6 Brake-door interlocks (p. 4-6)
- **Urban mass transit** buses may have a **brake and accelerator interlock**.
- When the **rear door is open**, the interlock **puts on the brakes** and **holds the throttle (gas pedal) at idle**.
- The interlock **releases when you close the rear door**.
- **Do not use the interlock instead of the parking brake.** *Why:* it lets go as soon as the rear door closes; it is a safety backup, not a way to park.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** Wait to explain the no-smoking and no-drinking rules until someone breaks them. → **Correct:** explain them **at the start of the trip** to avoid trouble later. (p. 4-4)
- **Trap:** Put a disruptive rider off the bus right away, wherever you are. → **Correct:** **never** where it is unsafe for them; the **next scheduled stop** or a **well-lit place with other people** may be safer. (p. 4-4)
- **Trap:** If the cross traffic has a stop sign, you can go through the intersection without extra care. → **Correct:** bus accidents often happen at **intersections** — use caution **even if** a signal or stop sign controls the other traffic. (p. 4-4)
- **Trap:** Signal and start to pull out; other drivers will make room. → **Correct:** **never assume** they will brake; **wait for the gap** to open. (p. 4-4)
- **Trap:** The posted speed on a curve is always safe for a bus. → **Correct:** it is safe for **cars** but may be **too fast for many buses**. (p. 4-4)
- **Trap:** With good traction a speeding bus will just slide on a curve. → **Correct:** good traction → it may **roll over**; poor traction → it may **slide off**. (p. 4-4)
- **Trap:** Objects in a convex mirror are closer than they look, so you can relax. → **Correct:** convex mirrors make things look **smaller and farther away** than they are — they are really **closer**. (p. 4-4)
- **Trap:** At a railroad crossing, stop at least 50 feet back. → **Correct:** stop **between 15 and 50 feet**. "At least 50 feet" is the **drawbridge** rule. (p. 4-5)
- **Trap:** Keep the forward door shut at railroad crossings. → **Correct:** **open** the forward door if it helps you **see or hear** a train. (p. 4-5)
- **Trap:** You must stop at every crossing, even with a green signal or a flagman. → **Correct:** at a **green** signal, a **peace officer or flagman**, **streetcar** crossings, tracks along the road in a business/residence district, and **"exempt"/"abandoned"** crossings, you **slow down and check** instead of stopping. (p. 4-5)
- **Trap:** You must stop at every drawbridge. → **Correct:** stop only when there is **no signal light and no traffic control attendant**; with a **green light** or an **attendant**, slow down and check. (p. 4-5)
- **Trap:** An after-trip report is needed only when you find a defect. → **Correct:** interstate drivers write one **for each bus**; if there are **no defects, the report says so**. (p. 4-5)
- **Trap:** Never tow a disabled bus with riders aboard — no exceptions. → **Correct:** don't, **unless getting off would be unsafe**; then only to the **nearest safe spot** to let them off. (p. 4-6)
- **Trap:** You may refuel with riders aboard any time you need to. → **Correct:** **avoid** it unless absolutely necessary, and **never** in a **closed building** with riders aboard. (p. 4-6)
- **Trap:** The rear door must be open to set the brakes on a transit bus, so the interlock is your parking brake. → **Correct:** **false** — the interlock releases when the rear door closes; **never use it in place of the parking brake**. (p. 4-6)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 4 #5** Does it matter where you make a disruptive passenger get off the bus?
→ **Yes.** You must keep that rider safe as well as the others. Do not let them off where it would be unsafe for them. The **next scheduled stop**, or a **well-lit area with other people around**, may be safer. Follow your carrier's guidelines. (p. 4-4)

**TYK 4 #6** How far from a railroad crossing should you stop?
→ **Between 15 and 50 feet** before the crossing. (p. 4-5)

**TYK 4 #7** When must you stop before crossing a drawbridge?
→ When the drawbridge has **no signal light and no traffic control attendant**. Stop **at least 50 feet** before the draw and make sure the draw is **completely closed** before crossing. With a **green** light, or an attendant/traffic officer who controls traffic when the bridge opens, you just slow down and make sure it is safe. (p. 4-5)

**TYK 4 #8** Describe from memory the "prohibited practices" listed in the handbook.
→ (1) **Fueling:** avoid fueling with riders on board unless absolutely necessary, and **never refuel in a closed building** with riders on board. (2) **Distraction:** do not **talk with riders** or do anything else distracting while driving. (3) **Towing/pushing:** do not tow or push a disabled bus with riders aboard **unless getting off would be unsafe**, and then only to the **nearest safe spot** to let them off; follow your employer's guidelines. (p. 4-6)

**TYK 4 #9** True or false: the rear door of a transit bus has to be open to put on the parking brake.
→ **False.** The brake-door interlock puts on the brakes (and holds the throttle at idle) when the rear door is open, and it **releases when the door closes**. It is **not** a parking brake — **never use it in place of the parking brake**. (p. 4-6)

## One-minute recap
- Explain rules (smoking, drinking, electronic devices) **at the start** of the trip. Scan the **interior** as well as the road.
- At stops: "watch your step"; wait for riders to **sit or brace**; start and stop **smoothly**. Never let a disruptive rider off where it is **unsafe** — next scheduled stop or a **well-lit, busy** place.
- Bus crashes often happen at **intersections**. Pulling out of stops: mind **mirrors, poles, tree limbs**; **wait for the gap** — don't expect others to brake.
- Curves: posted speed is for **cars**. Good traction → **rollover**; poor traction → **slide off**. Leaning outward = **too fast**.
- Mirrors: quick glances. **Convex** mirrors make things look **smaller and farther away**.
- Railroad: stop **15–50 ft**, look and listen both ways, **open the forward door** if it helps, check for a second train, **no gear changes**. No stop (slow + check): business/residence street tracks, **streetcar**, **officer/flagman**, **green signal**, **"exempt"/"abandoned"**.
- Drawbridge with no light or attendant: stop **at least 50 ft** before the draw; make sure it is **fully closed**.
- After each shift inspect and report (interstate: **each bus**, "no defects" if none). Never refuel **indoors** with riders; don't **talk** while driving; tow with riders only if leaving is unsafe, to the **nearest safe spot**. **Interlock ≠ parking brake**.
