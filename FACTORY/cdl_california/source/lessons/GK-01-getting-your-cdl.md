# GK-01 · Getting your CDL: classes, tests, medical rules

> **Handbook:** Section 1 intro pages and 1.1–1.2, pages 1-1–1-13 · **Test:** General Knowledge · **Exam weight:** Medium · **Study time:** ~40 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- Which vehicles need a CDL, and whether a vehicle needs a Class A, B or C license.
- The learner's permit (CLP) rules: minimum age, how long it lasts, the 14-day wait, and what a permit holder may not do.
- What each endorsement letter (H, N, P, S, T, X, F) allows, and what the restriction codes (E, K, L, M, N, O, P, V, X, Z and 88) mean.
- How many tries you get on the knowledge and skills tests, and what makes DMV mark a test as failed.
- Who you must tell, and how fast, after a conviction or a license suspension.
- The medical paperwork rules (MER, MEC, National Registry, 2-year limit) and interstate vs intrastate self-certification.

## Learn it

### Key words first
- **CMV** = commercial motor vehicle. **CDL** = commercial driver license. **CLP** = commercial learner's permit, the learning stage before the CDL (it used to be called the "commercial instruction permit").
- **GVWR** (gross vehicle weight rating) = the loaded weight the manufacturer sets for **one** vehicle. (p. 1-8)
- **GCWR** (gross combination weight rating) = the loaded weight the manufacturer sets for a **combination** (power unit plus towed unit). If there is no maker's figure, add the power unit's GVWR to the weight of the towed unit and its load. (p. 1-8)
- **Endorsement** = an extra letter that allows a special kind of driving. **Restriction** = a code that limits what you may drive.
- **Placarded load** = HazMat in an amount that requires diamond-shaped warning signs (placards) on the vehicle.

### Who needs a CDL (p. 1-1)
Why: big, heavy or passenger-filled vehicles do far more harm in a crash, so their drivers must prove more skill. California's standards equal or go beyond the federal minimum.

You need a CDL to drive:
- A single vehicle with a GVWR of **26,001 lb or more**.
- A combination with a GCWR of **26,001 lb or more**.
- A vehicle designed, used or maintained to carry **more than 10 people, counting the driver**.
- **[CA]** A **3-axle** vehicle that weighs **more than 6,000 lb**.

You also need a CDL when you:
- Tow a vehicle or trailer with a GVWR of **10,001 lb or more**.
- Haul HazMat that requires placards, or **[CA]** HazMat as defined in the California Health and Safety Code (§§25115, 25117).
- Tow two units at once (two trailers, or a vehicle plus a trailer).

Memory hook: the lines sit **one pound above** a round number: **26,001** and **10,001**. "26,000" or "10,000" is the trap answer.

> Note: federal rules use "16 or more people" for passenger vehicles. The California test follows the handbook: **more than 10, driver included**.

### Passenger vans: CDL or not? (p. 1-1) [CA]
The handbook's van chart asks, in this order:
1. GCWR more than 26,000 lb? → **CDL required**.
2. Used as a **vanpool**? → **No CDL**.
3. Carries **more than 15** people incl. driver? → **CDL required**.
4. Carries **more than 10** people incl. driver? No → no CDL. Yes → CDL **only if** it carries people **for pay or profit, or a nonprofit group uses it**.

Example: a church (nonprofit) van built for 12 people including the driver → not a vanpool, over 10, used by a nonprofit → **CDL required**.

### Horse trailers and Restriction 88 (pp. 1-1 to 1-2) [CA]
- A horse trailer carries property. "Living quarters" in it are only a side feature and do not change that.
- A horse trailer with living quarters and a GVWR **over 10,000 lb** needs a **commercial Class A** license.
- If the truck-and-trailer GCWR is **under 26,001 lb**, DMV adds **Restriction 88**: you may drive only combinations with a GCWR **under 26,001 lb** whose towed vehicle has a GVWR **over 10,000 lb**.

### Which class do you need? (Figure 1.1, p. 1-8)
Figure 1.1 is a yes/no chart. It leads to these answers:

| Your vehicle | License |
|---|---|
| Combination: GCWR 26,001 lb or more, towed unit GVWR 10,001 lb or more | **Class A** |
| Combination: GCWR under 26,001 lb, towed unit GVWR 10,001 lb or more | **Class A with Restriction 88** [CA] |
| Power unit GVWR 26,001 lb or more, alone or towing a unit under 10,001 lb | **Class B** |
| Single vehicle (not a combination) with 3 axles, weighing over 6,000 lb [CA] | **Class B** |
| None of the above, but carrying placarded HazMat | **Class C** |
| None of the above, but a farm labor vehicle for **10 or more** people incl. driver, or a vehicle for **more than 10** people incl. driver used for profit, pay, or by a nonprofit | **Class B** |
| None of the above | No CDL |

Worked examples:
- Tractor (GVWR 35,000 lb) + semitrailer (GVWR 45,000 lb) → **Class A**.
- Box truck (GVWR 30,000 lb) towing a trailer with GVWR 7,000 lb → trailer under 10,001 → **Class B**.
- Pickup (GVWR 11,000 lb) + horse trailer (GVWR 14,000 lb) that weighs 12,000 lb with its load, no maker's GCWR: pickup GVWR + loaded trailer weight = 11,000 + 12,000 = 23,000 lb. Trailer GVWR over 10,000 but total under 26,001 → **Class A with Restriction 88**.

### Getting a CLP (p. 1-2)
Why: the CLP lets you practice only under supervision, in lower-risk ways, until you prove your skill.
- Age at least **18**.
- You must already hold a valid California license that covers at least a noncommercial **Class C** vehicle (a temporary one is fine). The CLP is valid **only with** that license, so carry both.
- It lasts up to **180 days** from issue. You may renew it for up to **180 more days**, but never past **1 year** from the date you first applied.
- Only three endorsements can go on a CLP: **N (tank), P (passenger), S (school bus)**.
- CLP with **N**: tank vehicles only when the tank is **empty**; a tank that held HazMat must first be **purged** (cleaned of all residue).
- CLP with **P or S**: no passengers. The only people allowed on board are the CDL holder riding with you, other trainees, test examiners, and federal or state inspectors and auditors.
- Wait at least **14 days** after the CLP is **first issued** before the skills test. The same wait applies to class upgrades and to endorsement or restriction changes that need a skills test.
- A **CDL holder** with the right class and endorsements **must ride with you** whenever you drive a CMV.
- You hand in your CLP and your regular license before DMV issues your CDL.

Timeline: Day 0 CLP issued → Day 14 earliest skills test → Day 180 CLP ends unless renewed → renewal stops at 1 year after the application date.

### Age rules for a CDL (p. 1-2) [CA]
- Minimum **18**, after first getting a California noncommercial Class C license.
- From **18** you may drive **for hire inside California**, as long as you do no interstate commerce.
- You must be **21** to drive a CMV in **interstate** commerce, or to haul **HazMat or hazardous wastes**, even on a trip that stays inside California (CVC §12515).

### What to bring when you apply (pp. 1-2 to 1-4)
- **Form DL 44C** (eDL 44C can be done online first). Signing it means you agree to a chemical test of your blood for alcohol or drugs when a peace officer asks. **Refuse to sign, and DMV will not issue or renew your license.**
- Your **true full name** and approved **medical forms** (see 1.2).
- An **identity document** proving birth date and legal presence in the U.S., for an original, renewal, upgrade or transfer. It must be a **certified copy** from the issuing office with a raised seal or original stamp; DMV gives it back; a photocopy of it is **not accepted**. If its name differs from your application, bring a true-full-name document too. The name on it is the name printed on your license.
- **[CA]** **One residency document** for an original and each renewal, transfer or upgrade. Its address must match your DL 44C.
- Proof of your **Social Security number**, checked with the Social Security Administration while you wait. For an **original CDL**, only the **Social Security card** is accepted.
- A **Certificate of Driving Skill (DL 170 ETP)**, when DMV has authorized your employer to issue one; you and the employer both sign it.
- The **application fee**. Fees can change every **January 1** (the handbook's 2020 table lists, for example, $82 for an original Class A or B). Fee amounts are unlikely test questions.

### Time limits and number of tries (p. 1-4)
- The application fee stays valid for **12 months** after the date you apply.
- Renewing the CLP does not require retaking the knowledge tests. But skills test parts you pass count only during the **first 180 days** of the CLP; after a renewal you must retake **all** skills parts.
- **Knowledge tests: 3 attempts** per application, **no waiting period** between them.
- **Skills tests: 3 attempts in total** per application. **Each failed part counts as 1.** Failing the vehicle inspection, then basic control, then the road test = **3 failures**, none left.
- Testing in two kinds of vehicles (for example Class A and a passenger vehicle)? You get 3 attempts for each kind.
- Failing basic control skills or the road test means a **retest fee** when you return. No waiting period to retake basic control skills.

### Duties of every CDL driver (p. 1-4)
Why: DMV and employers must see your whole record. One driver, one license, one record.
- Be a **California resident** before you apply.
- List every state that licensed you in the past **10 years**, and hand in all out-of-state licenses (current or expired).
- Certify you do **not** hold a license from more than one state or country.
- Self-certify your type of commerce (see 1.2.1).
- Tell your **home state DMV** about any out-of-state conviction within **30 days**.
- Tell your **employer** about any conviction within **30 days**, on form **DL 535**.
- Tell your **employer** about a license **suspension, revocation, cancellation or disqualification** before the **end of the next business day**.
- When applying for a driving job, give the employer your **10-year** commercial driving work history.

> Note: Section 1.4 (p. 1-16) says to tell your employer about a suspension **within 2 business days**. Both mean "right away." Pick whichever appears; never 30 days.

### Endorsements (pp. 1-4 to 1-5)
Each endorsement is one letter on your license and needs a special knowledge test (for X, you pass both the tank and the HazMat tests).

| Letter | For | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **H** | HazMat or wastes in placarded or marked vehicles | Any class. Also any amount of a listed select agent or toxin. Requires a **TSA background check** (criminal, immigration, FBI records); a security threat → endorsement denied. Original Class C CDL with H → skills test too. |
| **N** | Tank vehicles (cement trucks count) | Liquid or gas; **each tank more than 119 gal** and **all tanks together 1,000 gal or more**, attached permanently or temporarily. Any class. Original Class C CDL with N → skills test too. |
| **P** | Passenger vehicles | Built for **10 or more** people incl. driver. Any class. Knowledge **and** skills tests in a passenger vehicle. |
| **S** | School buses | Class **A or B** only. Knowledge **and** skills tests in a school bus. |
| **T** | Double or triple trailers | Class A. **[CA] Triple trailers are not legal in California.** |
| **X** | Tank vehicles with placarded HazMat or waste | Shows you passed **both** the tank and HazMat tests. |
| **F** | **[CA]** Firefighting equipment | Required to drive it; **optional** for Class A or B CDL holders. |

- Why the N endorsement: bulk liquid is heavy, shifts, and has a high center of gravity, so the vehicle is harder to control.
- Why the T endorsement: pulling more than one trailer safely takes a lot of extra knowledge and skill.
- **Not** a tank vehicle: an **empty storage tank** (1,000 gal or more) not built for transport, temporarily attached to a flatbed trailer.

> Note: the P paragraph says "10 or more" people; p. 1-1 says a CDL is needed for "more than 10." Both count the driver. Match the wording of the question.

### Restrictions (pp. 1-6 to 1-7, 1-11)
Why: your CDL covers only what you proved on the skills test. Test in a simpler vehicle and your license is limited to that kind. There are **10** standard codes.

| Code | Meaning | You get it when… |
|---|---|---|
| **E** | No manual transmission | You tested in an automatic (any transmission not fully gearshift-and-clutch). |
| **K** | Intrastate only | You certify as intrastate (NA or EA), or the state adds it for another reason. |
| **L** | No air-brake CMV | You skipped or failed the air brake knowledge test, **or** tested in a vehicle without air brakes. |
| **M** | No Class A passenger vehicle | P applicant tested in a Class B passenger vehicle. |
| **N** | No Class A or B passenger vehicle | P applicant tested in a Class C passenger vehicle. |
| **O** | No tractor-trailer | Class A test vehicle joined by a **pintle hook** or other non-fifth-wheel hitch, or one with a fifth wheel that is not a truck-tractor/semitrailer. |
| **P** | No passengers in a CMV bus | CLP with P (same rule for a school bus with S): only auditors, inspectors, examiners, trainees and the CDL holder may ride. |
| **V** | Medical variance | DMV is told you have a medical variance; shown on your CDL and CDLIS record (the national CDL database). |
| **X** | No cargo in a CMV tank vehicle | CLP with N: empty tanks only; a tank that held HazMat must be purged. |
| **Z** | No full air-brake CMV | You tested with **air-over-hydraulic** brakes (partly air, partly hydraulic). |

- A CLP with **P or S** always has **restriction P**; a CLP with **N** always has **restriction X**.
- **Same letter, different meaning:** endorsement N = tank, restriction N = no Class A/B passenger vehicle. Endorsement P = passengers, restriction P = no passengers. Endorsement X = tank + HazMat, restriction X = no cargo in a tank.
- From p. 1-11: no air brakes on your test vehicle → you may drive only vehicles without air brakes. Passenger test vehicle for **15 or fewer** people incl. driver → small buses only.
- Tip: you passed Air Brakes. To avoid L, Z, E and O, take the Class A skills test in a tractor-trailer with a fifth wheel, **full air brakes** and a **manual transmission**.

### Who does not need a CDL (p. 1-7)
- Drivers exempted under Health and Safety Code §25163.
- Anyone driving in an emergency **at a peace officer's direction**.
- People towing, **not for pay**, a fifth-wheel travel trailer over **15,000 lb GVWR** or a trailer coach over **10,000 lb GVWR** (they need a **noncommercial Class A**).
- Housecar (motorhome) drivers, **over 40 ft but not over 45 ft**, with an endorsement.
- Non-civilian military personnel driving military vehicles.
- Implement-of-husbandry (farm equipment) operators who need no license.
- **Vanpool** drivers.

### Special certificates (pp. 1-7, 1-9) [CA]
Some jobs need a certificate **in addition to** a CDL. A * means the driver faces commercial-driver penalties (sanctions).

Phone rule: using a cell phone for non-work reasons while driving a **school bus or transit vehicle** is **illegal**. Emergency calls (police, health care provider, fire department, other emergency services) are allowed.

| Certificate | Apply at | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| Ambulance Driver | DMV | Ambulance used commercially in emergencies; MER and MEC every **2 years**. |
| Hazardous Agricultural Materials (HAM)* | DMV | Farm worker may haul hazardous waste or placarded loads **without a CDL** if: age **21+**, works in agriculture, not for pay, farmer owns/leases the vehicle, finished a CHP-approved HAM program, medical (MER/MEC or DL 546) every **2 years**, and drives an implement of husbandry or a vehicle needing only a noncommercial Class C license, **no more than 50 miles** point to point. |
| Verification of Transit Training (VTT) | DMV | Transit bus drivers (regular routes with a fare, not paratransit). Not needed with a school bus or SPAB certificate. |
| General Public Paratransit Vehicle (GPPV)* | CHP | Up to **24** people incl. driver for local public service (e.g. Dial-A-Ride); pupils (grade 12 or below) to school/activities; vehicles only for disabled persons. |
| School Bus Driver* | CHP | Also needs the **S** endorsement. Drivers **65+** send MER and MEC **every year**. |
| School Pupil Activity Bus (SPAB)* | CHP | Buses taking pupils to school-related activities. |
| Farm Labor Vehicle* | CHP | Farm labor trucks and buses. Driver **and all passengers** wear seat belts. |
| Youth Bus* | CHP | Non-school bus with up to **16 children** plus driver; extra CHP training. |
| Tow Truck Driver* | CHP | Freeway service patrol tow drivers under an agreement or contract with a traffic commission. |
| Vehicle for Developmentally Disabled Persons (VDDP)* | CHP | Driving for an organization whose main job is carrying people with developmental disabilities for hire. |

### General (p. 1-9)
You must pass **both** the knowledge and skills tests. The handbook prepares you for the tests but does not replace a truck-driving school; DMV calls formal training the most dependable way to learn the skills.

### 1.1.1 Knowledge tests (p. 1-10)
- **[CA]** Offered in **English, Arabic, Chinese, Punjabi, Russian and Spanish**, on DMV's **Automated Knowledge Testing equipment (AKTE)**.
- Which tests: **General knowledge** (everyone); **passenger transport** (all bus drivers); **air brakes** (air brakes, **including air-over-hydraulic**); **combination vehicles**; **HazMat** (plus a **TSA background check**); **tank vehicle**; **doubles/triples**; **school bus**; **firefighter** (optional for Class A/B).
- Adding an endorsement **without** moving up a class? DMV **waives** knowledge tests (HazMat included) you passed or had waived on your last application, if the new one starts within **12 months** of your last renewal or original CDL issue date.
- Allow **2–3 hours** to take all tests. Tests are not returned to you. Take them at any DMV field office (book at dmv.ca.gov or 1-800-777-0133).

> **Beyond the handbook — not on the test:** the GK test has 50 questions (40 correct to pass), each with 3 answer choices. In 2026 federal officials announced English-only CDL testing, so ask DMV which languages are offered before test day.

### Testing rules: no aids, no helpers, no recording (pp. 1-10 to 1-11)
Why: the test must show what **you** know and can do.
- **Knowledge test:** no handbook, notes, cheat sheets, phone, hand-held computer or watch. Using any aid, or a stand-in test taker → test **failed**, and DMV may act against your license and the helper's.
- **Vehicle inspection test** (once called "pre-trip"): the **only** aid allowed is the handbook's **Section 11 vehicle inspection guide**. Anything else → skills test failed.
- **No electronic devices** in any skills test (phone, Bluetooth, CB radio, smart watch).
- Bystanders may **not** signal or shout help → test stopped and failed.
- **No helper marks** (writing, tape or odd paint on the vehicle; marks on curbs, walls or trees) → test stopped and failed.
- **No recording, including dash cams.** Switch it off or disable video and audio; if it can't be switched off (common in CMVs), **block** it during the road test.

### 1.1.2 Skills tests (p. 1-11)
Only **after** passing the knowledge tests. Three tests, all in the **type of vehicle** you want to be licensed for. **All in English; no interpreter.** A vehicle with **marked or labeled parts** can't be used for the inspection test.

| Test | What you do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Vehicle inspection** | Inspect and explain to the examiner what you check and why. Fail it → other tests postponed. Free retake on the same application. (Section 11) | ~**40 min** |
| **Basic control skills** | Drive forward, back up and turn inside a marked area (lines, cones, barriers). Failing any skills test ends testing. Retest fee each time. (Section 12) | ~**30 min** |
| **Road test** (once "drive test") | DMV route with turns, intersections, railroad crossings, curves, grades, streets and highways. Retest fee each time. (Section 13) | ~**45–60 min** |

A road test is **required** to:
- Get an **original** CDL.
- **Remove a restriction** added because of vehicle size or equipment.
- Add a **P or S** endorsement.
- Renew a CDL **expired more than 2 years**.

**Troops to Trucks:** DMV can **waive the skills test** for qualified military members who drive (or did within the last **year**) a military vehicle like a CMV on public roads. Forms **DL 963** and **DL 964**; active duty show military ID, veterans a **DD 214** with discharge within the last year. **Not** waived for **S or P**.

### What to study (Figure 1.2, p. 1-12)
- **Class A:** Sections **1, 2, 3, 6** and **11–13**, plus **5** if your vehicle has air brakes. Class B or C: the same without 6.
- Endorsements add: passenger → 4, doubles/triples → 7, tank → 8 and 9 (plus 6 for Class B or C), HazMat → 9, school bus → 10.

### 1.2 Medical documentation (pp. 1-12 to 1-13)
Why: a medical emergency at the wheel of a large CMV endangers everyone, so a trained examiner checks you meet federal fitness standards.

- **MER** = Medical Examination Report (the examiner's full report). **MEC** = Medical Examiner's Certificate (the certificate you carry). **National Registry** = the federal list of trained, certified examiners for CMV driver physicals.
- **Who must use a National Registry examiner:** CDL drivers in **interstate** commerce, and anyone applying for or holding a school bus, SPAB, youth bus, GPPV or farm labor vehicle certificate.
- **Who can examine you:** a U.S.-licensed **MD, DO, physician assistant, advanced practice registered nurse or chiropractor** listed on the National Registry.
- **[CA] Who pays:** if your job requires a CDL, your **employer**, unless you had the exam **before** applying for the job (Labor Code §231).
- You can **open an application and take the law and vision tests** first and hand in the medical report later, but DMV issues **no commercial license document** until you submit one that qualifies.
- Every CDL driver (interstate or intrastate) gives DMV a valid MER and MEC (original or copy) for an original CLP/CDL or to update the medical expiration date. Both must be dated within the last **2 years**.
- Submit only **pages 1–4** of the MER. Page 5 covers state variances — and **[CA] California has no variances or exemptions** from the federal physical standards.
- The examiner signs the **MEC**; you **carry it** whenever you drive commercially. Drive a CMV after it **expires** → you can be **cited for driving out of class**.
- **[CA]** School bus drivers **65 or older**: new medical report **every year**.
- **[CA]** Noncommercial drivers who used to file form **DL 51**, plus ambulance, vanpool and Freeway Service Patrol tow truck drivers, now submit an MER and MEC. **DL 51 is no longer accepted.**
- **[CA]** Failed the medical? You may qualify for an **intrastate restricted Medical Certificate (DL 51B)** by sending DMV the MER showing you did not pass.
- To update: take the MER and MEC to a DMV office, or **mail them at least 4 weeks** before your old medical expires (DMV CDL Unit, Sacramento).

### 1.2.1 Interstate or intrastate commerce (p. 1-13)
Federal rule 49 CFR §383.71 makes you declare (**self-certify**) your type of commercial driving.

**Interstate commerce** = trade or transport that goes:
- from a place in a state to a place outside it (including outside the U.S.);
- between two places in the same state but **through** another state or country; or
- between two places in the same state as **part of a shipment that began or will end outside** the state or the U.S.

**Intrastate commerce** = between two places in the state, as part of a shipment that begins or ends inside the state.

| Code | Meaning | In California |
|---|---|---|
| **NI** Non-Excepted Interstate | Interstate driver who meets the federal qualifications (49 CFR Part 391). | Issued; no limit on where loads start or end. |
| **NA** Non-Excepted Intrastate | In-state only, meets Part 391. Cargo and passengers must start and end in CA; no crossing state or national borders while driving commercially. | Issued, with the **Intrastate Only restriction (40/K)** on card and record. |
| **EI / EA** Excepted Interstate / Intrastate | "Excepted" = exempt from the federal qualifications. | **Not issued.** All CA CMV drivers are non-excepted. |

- **You can be in interstate commerce even if your truck never leaves California** (for example, the goods you carry between two CA cities came from another state or country).
- DMV **strongly recommends NI** if you meet the federal qualifications and might ever drive interstate.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** a CDL starts at 26,000 lb → **Correct:** **26,001 lb** or more. Round numbers feel right, but the line is one pound higher. (p. 1-1)
- **Trap:** only weight decides if you need a CDL → **Correct:** [CA] a **3-axle** vehicle over **6,000 lb** also needs one. (p. 1-1)
- **Trap:** a CLP lasts 1 year → **Correct:** **180 days**, renewable for 180 more; 1 year from application is only the outer limit. (p. 1-2)
- **Trap:** you may take the skills test right after getting a CLP → **Correct:** wait **14 days**. "No waiting period" is the rule for **retaking knowledge tests**. (pp. 1-2, 1-4)
- **Trap:** a CLP can carry H or T → **Correct:** only **N, P, S**. (p. 1-2)
- **Trap:** at 18 you may haul HazMat if you stay in California → **Correct:** HazMat needs age **21**, even intrastate. (p. 1-2)
- **Trap:** each skills test part has its own 3 tries → **Correct:** **3 failures total** across all parts. (p. 1-4)
- **Trap:** tell your employer about a suspension within 30 days → **Correct:** by the end of the **next business day** (§1.4: 2 business days). 30 days is for **convictions**. (pp. 1-4, 1-16)
- **Trap:** endorsement X means "empty tanks only" → **Correct:** endorsement **X** = tank + HazMat; "no cargo in tank" is **restriction** X on a CLP. (pp. 1-5, 1-6)
- **Trap:** passing the Air Brakes knowledge test prevents any air brake restriction → **Correct:** testing without air brakes gives **L**; testing with air-over-hydraulic brakes gives **Z**. (pp. 1-6, 1-7)
- **Trap:** a T endorsement lets you pull triples in California → **Correct:** [CA] triples are **illegal** here. (p. 1-5)
- **Trap:** interpreters are allowed on the skills test → **Correct:** skills tests are **English only**; only knowledge tests come in other languages. (pp. 1-10, 1-11)
- **Trap:** a truck that never leaves California is always intrastate → **Correct:** it can be **interstate** if the shipment began or ends outside the state. (p. 1-13)
- **Trap:** California can grant a medical variance → **Correct:** [CA] **no** variances or exemptions from federal physical standards. (p. 1-13)

## One-minute recap
- CDL needed at **26,001 lb** (vehicle or combination), when towing **10,001 lb** or more, for **more than 10** people incl. driver, for placarded HazMat, and **[CA]** for a **3-axle vehicle over 6,000 lb**.
- Class A = heavy combination (towed unit **10,001+ lb**), with **Restriction 88** if GCWR is under 26,001 lb. Class B = heavy single vehicle, 3-axle over 6,000 lb, or bus. Class C = smaller vehicle with placarded HazMat.
- CLP: age **18**, needs a Class C license, lasts **180 days** (renewable, max **1 year**), **14-day** wait before skills test, only **N/P/S**, CDL holder must ride along.
- Age **21** for interstate or any HazMat; **18** for hire inside California only.
- Knowledge tests: **3 tries**, no wait. Skills tests: **3 failures total**, English only, only the **Section 11** guide as an aid, no dash cam recording.
- Out-of-state conviction → home DMV within **30 days**; any conviction → employer within **30 days**; suspension → employer by the **next business day**.
- Endorsements: **H** HazMat (TSA check), **N** tank (**119/1,000 gal**), **P** passenger, **S** school bus (A or B), **T** doubles (triples illegal in CA), **X** tank + HazMat, **F** firefighter.
- Medical: National Registry examiner, MER/MEC within **2 years**, carry the MEC, employer pays, no CA variances. Self-certify **NI** or **NA** (NA = **40/K** intrastate-only).
