# GK-03 · Other CDL rules and California laws

> **Handbook:** Section 1.4–1.5, pages 1-16–1-30 · **Test:** General Knowledge · **Exam weight:** Medium · **Study time:** ~40 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- Who you must tell, and how fast, after an out-of-state ticket, a license suspension, or a crash.
- The California speed limit and lane rules for big trucks and for any vehicle that tows, and when you must use a turnout.
- California size limits: length, width, height, load overhang, and the main axle-weight numbers.
- How California (intrastate) hours of service differ from federal (interstate) hours of service.
- When you must send an SR 1 accident report, and how CHP handles an overweight truck at the scale.
- Basic clean-air (CARB) rules such as the idling limit, and what motor carrier permits, IRP, IFTA and trip records are.

## Learn it

This module is law, not driving skill. Most questions are "what is the number?" questions, so memorize the **bold** numbers. Rules marked **[CA]** are California rules.

Words used a lot:
- **CMV** = commercial motor vehicle. **CDL** = commercial driver license.
- **CVC** = California Vehicle Code (the state traffic law). **CFR** = Code of Federal Regulations (federal rules).
- **CHP** = California Highway Patrol. **Caltrans** = California Department of Transportation (runs state highways).
- **GVWR** = gross vehicle weight rating: the maker's maximum safe weight for the loaded vehicle.

> **Other California rules live in other modules.** They are printed in other parts of the handbook, so learn them there: negligent-operator point counts → GK-02 (p. 1-15); two mirrors with a 200-ft rear view → GK-06 (p. 2-13); service-brake stopping from 20 mph → GK-07 (p. 2-16); passing slow vehicles on grades and the 300-ft following rule → GK-07 (p. 2-18); when headlights must be on → GK-10 (p. 2-31); flags and lights on projecting loads → GK-14 (p. 3-5). Triple trailers are not legal in California (p. 7-1).

### 1.4 Other CDL rules (p. 1-16)

These federal and state rules apply to CMV drivers in every state.

**Who to tell, and how fast**

| What happened | Who you tell | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| You are convicted of any traffic violation (except parking) in another state or jurisdiction — in any kind of vehicle | Your licensing agency (in California, the DMV) | within **30 days** |
| Your license is suspended, revoked or cancelled, or you are disqualified | Your employer | within **2 business days** |
| You hold a HazMat (H) endorsement and you are convicted of, indicted for, or found not guilty by reason of insanity of a disqualifying crime; or a court finds you lack mental capacity; or you are involuntarily committed to a mental health facility; or you give up your U.S. citizenship | The state that issued your CDL — you must also **surrender** the H endorsement | within **24 hours** |

Why: your employer and the state must know right away when you are no longer safe or legal to drive.

> **Handbook conflict:** An earlier page of Section 1 (p. 1-4, taught in GK-01) says to tell your employer before the end of the business day after the action. Section 1.4 says within 2 business days. If a question uses the 1.4 wording, answer **2 business days**. Telling your employer by the end of the next business day satisfies both.

**Other rules in 1.4**
- **No CDL, no CMV.** Nobody may drive a CMV without a CDL. A court can fine you or send you to jail.
- **Only one license.** If you hold **more than 1 license**, or your CDL has been suspended or revoked, your employer must not allow you to drive a CMV. A court can fine the employer or send the employer to jail. Why: all states share one computer system of CDL driver records, and they check it so no driver holds more than 1 CDL.
- **Phones while driving:**
  - You may **not hold** a phone to talk.
  - You may **not dial** by pressing more than **one button**.
  - You may **not send or read** text messages.
  - Why: your hands and eyes must stay on driving. (The license penalties for phone violations are in 1.3.9 — see GK-02.)
- **Seat belt at all times** in a CMV. Why: the belt keeps you in the seat during a crash, so you can keep steering, and it lowers the chance of serious injury or death. If you are thrown from the vehicle because you had no belt, you are **4 times** more likely to be killed.

### Disqualification summary tables (pp. 1-17 to 1-19)

These pages hold four summary tables from the federal rule 49 CFR §383.51. "Disqualified" = you may not drive a CMV for that time. GK-02 teaches the offenses and main periods. The tables add these details:

- **Major offenses** (alcohol, drugs, BAC **0.04%**+ in a CMV, test refusal, leaving the scene, felony, driving a CMV while revoked or disqualified, negligent CMV death) (p. 1-17): 1st = **1 year** (**3 years** if hauling HazMat); 2nd = **life**. Most of these also count when you are in your **personal car** (1 year, then life). Three are CMV-only: 0.04 BAC in a CMV, driving a CMV while revoked or disqualified, and negligent CMV death. A felony involving a controlled substance = **life** on the 1st conviction, in any vehicle.
- **Serious traffic offenses** (p. 1-18): 2nd within **3 years** = **60 days**; 3rd within 3 years = **120 days**. In a personal car they count only if the conviction takes away all your driving privileges; two of them (driving a CMV without carrying your CDL, or without the proper class or endorsement) count only in a CMV.
- **Railroad crossing offenses** (p. 1-19): at least **60 days**, then **120 days**, then **1 year** (2nd and 3rd within 3 years).
- **Out-of-service (OOS) order violations** (p. 1-19). An OOS order officially stops a driver or vehicle from operating for a set time.

| Violating an OOS order while… | 1st | 2nd within 10 years | 3rd+ within 10 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not hauling HazMat | **90 days** to 1 year | 1 to 5 years | 3 to 5 years |
| Hauling HazMat, or **16 or more** passengers including the driver | **180 days** to 2 years | 3 to 5 years | 3 to 5 years |

> **Note:** Current federal law gives different OOS periods. The test follows the handbook: first OOS violation = at least **90 days**.

### 1.4.1 State laws and rules (p. 1-16) [CA]

- Every CDL driver must know the state's size and weight limits.
- **Every CMV must stop at each posted CHP inspection and testing site** (CVC §§2802–2805 and 2813). Why: scales and inspection sites find unsafe or overweight trucks.
- An officer who has reason to think your load is unsafe, or that your vehicle and load are too high, wide, long or heavy, may **stop you** and inspect, measure or weigh the vehicle. The officer can make you park in a suitable place and **reload or remove** part of the load.
- **You pay for the damage.** If you drive a CMV illegally over a highway or bridge (for example, too heavy), you are liable for all damage to it. If you drive someone else's vehicle with the owner's permission, the **owner and the driver** may both have to pay. Why: overweight vehicles crack pavement and weaken bridges.

### State air emissions rules — CARB (pp. 1-20 to 1-21) [CA]

**CARB** = California Air Resources Board. Its rules cover every commercial diesel vehicle that runs in California — **even ones based in another state or country**. CARB can inspect for too much smoke, tampering, and fleet-rule compliance, and can write citations with large fines. Inspections happen at **border crossings, CHP weigh stations, fleet facilities and random roadside spots**.

New words: a **DPF** (diesel particulate filter) is an exhaust filter that traps soot. **Opacity** is how much light the exhaust smoke blocks, as a percent. **PM** = particulate matter (soot).

| Rule | Who it covers | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| **Commercial idling** | Diesel CMVs over **10,001 lb** GVWR | At most **5 minutes** of non-essential idling anywhere in CA. At a **school**: no idling **at all** (CMVs and school buses). |
| **Truck and Bus Regulation** | Nearly all diesel trucks and buses over **14,000 lb** GVWR | DPF since **Jan 1, 2012**; **2010** model-year engines (or equal) phased in from **2015**, nearly all by **Jan 1, 2023**. Since **2020**, DMV won't register a truck that doesn't comply. |
| **Emission Control Label (ECL)** | Diesel trucks/buses over **6,000 lb**, **1974** or newer engine | Readable maker's label on the engine as proof it meets standards. |
| **Periodic Smoke Inspection Program (PSIP)** | CA fleets with **2+** diesel trucks over 6,000 lb, engines **4+ years** old | Smoke test **every year**; keep records at least **2 years**. DPF trucks: **5%** opacity limit; others **20%, 30% or 40%**, by engine year and technology. |
| **Greenhouse gas (GHG)** | 2011–2013 tractors; box trailers **53 ft**+ | SmartWay tractors; trailers SmartWay certified or aero-retrofitted. |
| **Garbage trucks / public agency & utility vehicles** | Diesel over 14,000 lb with older engines (1960–2006 / 1960–2007) | PM-reduction technology. |
| **Transit bus** | Transit and urban buses over **8,500 lb** | DPF retrofit, cleaner equipment, or alternative fuel. |
| **Transport refrigeration unit (TRU)** — the diesel "reefer" cooling unit | CA-based TRU trailers and generator sets | Registered with CARB, CARB ID number (IDN), meets standards for its engine year. |
| **Port (drayage) trucks** — short hauls to and from ports and rail yards | Class 7–8 diesel trucks (over **26,000 lb**), any origin | In the CARB **Drayage Truck Registry (DTR)**; **2007** or newer engine. |

More information: arb.ca.gov/truckstop.

> **Beyond the handbook — not on the test:** CARB rules and deadlines change often. Check arb.ca.gov before you buy or register a truck.

### Length — single vehicle (p. 1-21) [CA]

Why length limits: long vehicles need more room to turn and can block intersections and lanes.

- A single vehicle may be at most **40 ft** long. Parts that follow the CVC fender and mudguard rules may stick out past this.
- Some vehicles are exempt under certain conditions — for example **semitrailers, buses and housecars** (motor homes).
- The **front bumper** may stick out no more than **2 ft** ahead of the fenders, cab or radiator — whichever is farthest forward.
- **Buses:** a front or rear safety bumper may add **1 ft**; a wheelchair lift may stick out up to **18 in** in front; some buses may carry bicycle racks up to **36 in** in front or **10 ft** in the rear.
- An **articulated bus** (a bus that bends in the middle) or a trolley coach: at most **60 ft**.

### Length — combination vehicles (p. 1-21) [CA]

- Extra parts that do not carry load and do not support the vehicle may go past the single-vehicle limit, but the whole combination must stay within the combination limit.
- A semitrailer pulled by a motor truck or truck tractor may be longer than 40 ft if it meets the conditions in CVC §35400(b)(4).
- **Truck tractor + one trailer:** total length at most **65 ft** (exceptions in CVC §§35401 and 35401.5).
- **Truck tractor + semitrailer + trailer (doubles):** at most **75 ft**, and **neither trailer** may be longer than **28 ft 6 in**.
- **Cities and counties** may post signs that ban combinations longer than **60 ft** on roads they control.
- Extension devices are allowed, with limits (CVC §35402).
- **Load length:** at most **75 ft**, measured from the front of the vehicle or load (whichever is farther forward) to the back of the vehicle or load (whichever is farther back).

### Length exceptions and overhang (p. 1-22) [CA]

**Long one-piece loads.** A load made only of poles, timbers, pipes, structural materials, or single-unit parts (such as missile parts, aircraft assemblies, drilling equipment, or tanks) may be up to **80 ft** long if it rides on one of these (CVC §35414):
- a pole or pipe dolly (or a legal trailer used as one), pulled by a motor vehicle;
- a semitrailer;
- a semitrailer plus a pole or pipe dolly, pulled by a truck tractor, for flexible structural material.

Public utilities have their own load exceptions (CVC §35414(b)).

**Front overhang.** A load may extend no more than **3 ft** past the front bumper or front tires (whichever is farther forward). Exceptions: booms, masts of shovels and cranes, and water-well drilling or service equipment (CVC §35407). A load made **only of vehicles** may extend **4 ft**.

**Rear overhang.** On a single vehicle, the load may extend behind the **last point of support** by no more than **2/3 of the wheelbase**. The **wheelbase** is the distance between the centers of the front and rear axles. On a semitrailer, measure it from the center of the towing vehicle's last axle to the center of the semitrailer's last axle.
- Worked example: wheelbase **18 ft** → the load may hang out at most 2/3 × 18 = **12 ft** behind the last support.
- Why: a long tail swings wide in turns and can hit people or vehicles.

### Width (p. 1-22) [CA]

Why width limits: lanes have a fixed width, and a wide vehicle hits mirrors, signs and other vehicles.

| Item | Maximum |
|---|---|
| Body of the vehicle, or the load | **102 in (8½ ft)** |
| Wheel to wheel (outside of one wheel to outside of the opposite wheel), with pneumatic (air-filled) tires | **108 in (9 ft)** |
| "Permitted devices": door handles, hinges, cable cinchers, chain binders, placard holders | **3 in** past each side (recreational vehicles: **6 in** on one side) |
| "Required devices": lights, mirrors and similar | **10 in** past each side |
| Special mobile equipment; special construction and highway maintenance equipment | **120 in (10 ft)** |
| Buses and motor coaches | **102 in** (for-hire common-carrier buses in city or suburban service: **104 in**) |
| Loosely piled farm products in bulk (hay, straw, legumes — not baled, boxed, crated or stacked): load plus racks | **120 in** |
| Trusses and similar one-piece building parts, with a Caltrans special trip permit | **12 ft** (CVC §35780.5) |

**Local roads:** cities and counties may post signs that allow wider vehicles, or that ban vehicles wider than **96 in (8 ft)**.

### Farm equipment (p. 1-23) [CA]

**Implements of husbandry** = farm equipment. It is generally exempt from the width and length limits when it is moved on a highway as part of normal farm work. A Caltrans transportation permit may still be needed (CVC §§36000 and 36600).

### Height (p. 1-23) [CA]

- The vehicle and load may be at most **14 ft** high, measured from the road surface the vehicle stands on.
- Exceptions: **double-deck buses** up to **14 ft 3 in**; farm equipment moved over a highway as part of farm work.
- Why: bridges, overpasses and wires.

### Weight limits — general (p. 1-23) [CA]

- **Caltrans** may post maximum weights on bridges and state highways. A posted limit can be **higher or lower** than the normal CVC limits (§§35550–35557).
- **Cities and counties** may also post higher or lower limits for the roads and bridges they manage, and may name other routes for vehicles too heavy for a posted road or bridge.
- A local weight-limit sign does **not** stop you from entering the posted street **by the direct route** to:
  - (a) pick up or deliver goods;
  - (b) deliver materials for real construction or repair of a structure that has a permit;
  - (c) do public utility construction or repairs.

### Axle weight limits (p. 1-23) [CA]

**Single vehicle** (CVC §35550):
- **20,000 lb** — the handbook applies this to the wheels at one end of an axle (**20,500 lb** for buses).
- **10,500 lb** — the handbook applies this to the wheels on any one axle. This limit does **not** apply to vehicles hauling **livestock**.
- Never go over the tire maker's load limit, which is molded on at least one sidewall of the tire.

> **Wording note — beyond the handbook:** the handbook's two sentences look swapped. CVC §35550 itself puts the larger number (20,000) on the whole axle and the smaller number (10,500) on the wheels at one end — the same pattern as the 18,000 / 9,500 pair below. Remember the pair **20,000 / 10,500**; if a test question repeats the handbook's exact wording, give the handbook's number.

**Combinations with a trailer or semitrailer.** Each vehicle in the combination must meet either CVC §35551 or these limits:
- Any one axle: at most **18,000 lb**.
- The wheels at one end of an axle: at most **9,500 lb**.
- Exceptions: a **front steering axle** may carry up to **12,500 lb**; livestock haulers are exempt from the one-end (wheel) limit. The full list of front-axle exemptions is in CVC §35551.5(b).

**Groups of 2 or more axles in a row.** The allowed weight depends on the distance from the first to the last axle of the group. If that distance is **18 ft or less**, use the table in CVC §35551.5(c). If it is **more than 18 ft**, use the table in CVC §35551.5(d).

### Logs and weight-to-axle ratio (p. 1-24) [CA]

- **Logs:** log trucks have special weight limits (CVC §§35552 and 35785). That extra weight is **not allowed on interstate highways**.
- **Weight-to-axle ratio** (CVC §35551): roads and bridges are built to carry only so much weight **per foot of distance between axles**. So a heavy load must not be packed onto one point. Axles close together = less weight allowed; axles spread out = more weight allowed. The allowed weight for any group of 2 or more axles in a row is in the CVC tables.
- **Two sets of tandem axles** (a tandem = two axles close together): each set may carry **34,000 lb** if the distance from the first axle to the last axle of the two sets is **36 ft or more**. Each tandem is capped at **34,000 lb**, and the two tandems together at **68,000 lb** (CVC §35551(b)).

### Loading and unloading; special permit routes; penalties (p. 1-24) [CA]

- **Loading/unloading:** load limits are **not enforced** while you are loading or unloading at, or right next to, a loading or unloading area (CVC §35553).
- **Special permit loads:** you may **not change the route** on your permit. The only exception: to avoid breaking a local city traffic rule, you may detour on **nonresidential streets only** and must return to the route as soon as possible.
- Leaving the permitted route with an **extralegal load** (a load over the legal limits) without a peace officer's approval is a **misdemeanor** (a crime).

### CHP weight enforcement at the scale (p. 1-24) [CA]

- **CHP's written standard:** a vehicle **100 lb or more** over a legal limit may not go on until the overload is adjusted or removed.
- **In practice:** CHP first allows a **200-lb** variation factor (scale allowance). After subtracting the 200 lb, if the vehicle is still **100 lb or more** over the limit for an **axle, axle group, or gross weight**, the driver gets a **citation** and must **adjust the load or get an overweight permit before moving on**.
- Worked example (axle group limit 34,000 lb):
  - Scale reads **34,250 lb** → 250 over − 200 = 50 → less than 100 → no citation.
  - Scale reads **34,350 lb** → 350 over − 200 = 150 → citation, and you must fix the load or get a permit before you continue.
- **HazMat exception:** a HazMat load may be allowed to continue as loaded if unloading or adjusting cannot be done with reasonable safety for the driver and the public. Why: moving hazardous cargo by the roadside can be more dangerous than driving on.
- **Livestock and field-loaded bulk perishable farm products** (for human food, going from the field to the first processing point): the driver is cited but may drive on if a **single truck** is no more than **1,000 lb** over on any one axle or group of axles, or a **combination** is no more than **2,000 lb** over on gross weight. Why: the cargo would spoil or suffer while waiting.

### Permits for oversize loads (p. 1-24) [CA]

- You need a permit to move any vehicle that is over the **width, height or weight** limits on a California street or highway.
- Moving an oversize extralegal load without a permit can bring a **fine, jail, or both**, plus excess-load penalties.
- Where to get a permit: **Caltrans** for state highways; the **city or county** for their own roads.

### Motor carrier permit and UCR (pp. 1-24 to 1-25)

**Motor Carrier Permit (MCP) [CA]** (CVC §34620). Anyone who runs a CMV — **for hire or privately** (not for hire) — needs an MCP from the DMV. For the MCP, a CMV means:
- a self-propelled vehicle of a type named in CVC §34500 (a), (b), (f), (g) or (k);
- a motor truck with **2 or more axles** and a GVWR **over 10,000 lb**;
- any other motor vehicle that carries property for hire.

Not counted as an MCP CMV: household goods carriers (CPUC §5109), **pickup trucks** (CVC §471), and **2-axle daily rental trucks** used for non-commercial purposes and weighing **less than 26,001 lb** gross. Forms come from DMV Motor Carrier Permit Operations (dmv.ca.gov).

**Unified Carrier Registration (UCR).** Interstate or foreign motor carriers that haul property must register under UCR (from the federal Unified Carrier Registration Act of **2005**). Fees can be paid online.

### Speed limits (p. 1-25) [CA]

The top speed in California is **55 mph** for these vehicles (CVC §22406):
- a truck or truck tractor with **3 or more axles**;
- **any vehicle pulling another vehicle**;
- a **school bus** carrying any pupil;
- a **farm labor vehicle** carrying passengers;
- a vehicle carrying **explosives**;
- a **trailer bus**.

For **all other vehicles**: **65 mph** on most highways; **55 mph** on a **two-lane undivided** highway unless a higher limit is posted; **70 mph** only where signs show 70.

**Too slow is also illegal.** You may not drive so slowly that you block the normal flow of traffic — unless you must go slower for safety, to obey the law, or because the size and weight of your vehicle make it unavoidable.

Why: heavy vehicles and towed loads need much more distance to stop and are harder to control at high speed.

Worked examples:
- 3-axle dump truck on a freeway posted 70 → your limit is **55**.
- 2-axle box truck, not towing, on a freeway posted 65 → **65**.
- Pickup towing a small trailer → **55** (it is pulling another vehicle).

### Right lane rule (p. 1-25) [CA]

This rule is for the §22406 vehicles above (the 55-mph list).
- **Signs posted** → drive in the lane or lanes the signs designate.
- **No signs** → drive in the **right-hand lane**, or keep as near as possible to the right edge or curb.
- **Divided highway with 4 or more lanes in your direction** → you may also use the **lane just left of the right lane**.
- **When passing** a vehicle going your way, use: (1) the designated lane, (2) the lane just left of the right lane, or (3) the right lane when that is allowed.

Why: slow, heavy vehicles stay out of the faster left lanes, so traffic flows and there are fewer lane-change conflicts.

### Designated system access (p. 1-25) [CA]

The **designated system** is the network of freeways and highways approved for larger trucks.
- The lane rule does **not** apply when you are: (1) getting ready for a left or right turn, (2) entering or leaving a highway, or (3) using another lane to stay on your planned route.
- **Buses** (but not school buses or trailer buses) may use **any lane** if they are not towing.
- Larger trucks may leave or enter the designated system **only at interchanges or exits with the proper signs**. Off the system, they may travel:
  - along signed routes to reach a **terminal** — a place where freight is consolidated, full loads are unloaded, or combinations are regularly maintained, stored or built;
  - up to **1 mile** from marked exits or entrances to get **food, fuel, lodging or repairs**.

### Slow vehicle rule (p. 1-26) [CA]

On a **two-lane highway** where passing is unsafe, a slow vehicle with **5 or more vehicles** behind it must pull off at the nearest **signed turnout**, or at any other place with enough room to turn out safely, and let them pass. Why: drivers stuck behind a slow truck may try a dangerous pass.

### Hours of service — which rules apply? (p. 1-26)

**Hours of service (HOS)** = limits on how long you may drive and work.

- **Intrastate** (inside California only) → follow **California** HOS **[CA]**. You are intrastate when you do **not**:
  - cross the state line;
  - carry cargo that came from another state;
  - carry cargo going out of California;
  - carry hazardous substances or wastes (49 CFR §171.8).
- **Interstate** → follow **federal** HOS. You are interstate when your cargo comes from out of state, is going out of state, is hazardous substances or wastes, or any mix of these.

Key point: you can be **interstate without leaving California**. Example: you drive from a Los Angeles warehouse to Fresno, but the freight came from Arizona → federal rules.

### Record of duty status (p. 1-26)

A **record of duty status** is your log of hours: driving, on duty but not driving, off duty, and sleeper berth.
- CHP may make added safety and driving rules (CVC §§34501 and 34501.2).
- Your log must show **all** your hours and follow 49 CFR §395.8 and California Code of Regulations Title 13 §§1201–1213.
- **Each driver and each co-driver** keeps the log **in duplicate** while driving, on duty not driving, or resting in the sleeper berth.
- Show it **immediately** when asked by an authorized **CHP employee**, a regularly employed salaried **police officer**, or a **deputy sheriff**.
- In some cases a log is not required.

### Hours of service — federal vs. California (p. 1-27)

| Limit | Federal (interstate) | California (intrastate) [CA] |
|---|---|---|
| Driving after 10 hours in a row off duty | at most **11 hours** | at most **12 hours** |
| On-duty limit for driving | no driving after the **14th hour** after coming on duty | no driving after **16 hours** on duty |
| Work after that limit | non-driving work is allowed | non-driving work is allowed |
| Multi-day limit (hours **on duty**) | no driving after **60 hours on duty in 7 days**; if the carrier runs trucks every day of the week, **70 hours in 8 days**. Restart the 7/8-day period after **34+ hours** off in a row | no driving after **80 hours on duty in any 8 days in a row**; hauling **farm products**: **112 hours** in 8 days. For truck drivers, an 8-day period may end when a **34+ hour** off-duty period begins |
| Rest before driving again | **10 hours** off in a row | **10 hours** off in a row |
| Sleeper berth | the 10 hours may be split into **2 periods**, one at least **8 hours** | same |
| Bad weather not known at trip start | **+2 hours** of driving | **+2 hours** of driving, but never more than **14 hours** of driving or after **16 hours** on duty |

Why: tired drivers react slowly and fall asleep; limits force rest.

Worked example (a driver comes on duty at **5:00 a.m.** after 10 hours off):
- **California intrastate:** may drive up to **12 hours**; if you stay on duty all day, no driving after **9:00 p.m.** (16 hours on duty).
- **Federal interstate:** may drive up to **11 hours**; no driving after **7:00 p.m.** (14th hour).
- Either way, after the limit you may do other work but may not drive until you have **10 hours off in a row**.

The handbook adds that HOS rule changes did not affect **bus drivers** at the time it was written.

> **Beyond the handbook — not on the test:** federal HOS rules have been updated since this handbook (for example, sleeper-berth split options and a rest-break rule). For the test, use the handbook's table.

### Accident reporting (p. 1-26) [CA]

- Since **January 1, 2017**, every driver in a crash in California that causes **death, injury, or property damage over $1,000** must report it to the **DMV** on form **SR 1** (Report of Traffic Accident Occurring in California).
- Deadline: within **10 days**. You or your authorized representative may send it.
- Required **whether or not you caused the crash**, and **even on private property**.
- The SR 1 is **in addition to** any police report or insurance report.
- If you do not report it, your **driving privilege will be suspended**.
- Forms: dmv.ca.gov, DMV offices, or by phone from CHP or DMV.
- **Suspension note:** during a required suspension, a CDL holder may downgrade to a non-commercial license to become eligible for a restricted license. To get the CDL back, you must pass **all knowledge and skills tests again and pay the fees**.
- **Tell your employer** within **5 days** if you crash while driving the employer's vehicle (CVC §16002). Your employer may require you to tell them **immediately**.

### Financial responsibility (pp. 1-27 to 1-28) [CA]

**Financial responsibility (FR)** = proof you can pay for injuries and damage you cause (usually insurance). **Combined single limit (CSL)** = one total amount that covers injury and property damage together.

These minimums do **not** apply to pickups (CVC §471) or 2-axle daily rental trucks under 26,001 lb GVWR used non-commercially.

| What you haul | Minimum coverage |
|---|---|
| General freight, only in vehicles of **10,000 lb** GVWR or less | **$300,000** CSL |
| General freight in vehicles of **10,001 lb** or more | **$750,000** CSL |
| Petroleum products in bulk | **$500,000** (injury/death of 1 person), **$1,000,000** (2 or more persons), **$200,000** (property) — or **$1,200,000** CSL |
| Oil, HazMat, or waste | **$1,000,000** CSL |
| Hazardous substances, compressed gas or liquefied compressed gas in cargo tanks, portable tanks or hopper vehicles over **3,500 water gallons**; Division **1.1, 1.2 or 1.3** explosives; poison gas; highway-route controlled quantities of radioactive materials | **$5,000,000** CSL |

- HazMat and waste information: **DTSC** (Department of Toxic Substances Control) and **CHP**. Not every requirement is listed; DMV Motor Carrier Permit Operations answers questions.
- **Ways to keep FR on file:** a surety bond (**MC 55 M**), a Certificate of Insurance (**MC 65 M**), or a Certificate of Self-Insurance (**MC 131 M**).
- Proof must stay on file the whole time the MCP is active. If the insurance or bond expires or is cancelled, the **DMV suspends the MCP**. To avoid that, have your insurer send in valid coverage.
- **Proof of FR:** a driver must be able to show FR **before operating** the vehicle. The rule is also met if the vehicle has **exempt plates** or is owned, leased or directed by the **U.S. government**.

### 1.5 IRP and IFTA: interstate registration and fuel tax (pp. 1-28 to 1-30)

With few exceptions, a vehicle that needs a CDL and runs in **interstate** commerce must be registered under **IRP** and **IFTA**. These federally required programs share registration fees and fuel taxes fairly among the **48 contiguous U.S. states** and **10 Canadian provinces**. Your **base jurisdiction** is your home state or province. The registrant or licensee may be the vehicle's **owner or operator**.

- **IRP (International Registration Plan) — registration.** Your vehicle gets **apportioned** registration: the fees are split by the distance you drive in each place. The base jurisdiction issues the plates and **cab card** (registration kept in the cab), collects and shares the fees, audits and enforces. You must apply there, provide proper documents, pay the fees, display the credentials, and keep accurate distance records open for review.
- **IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement) — fuel tax.** One license and one set of credentials work in **all member jurisdictions**. The tax depends on the miles driven and fuel used in each one. You file **one quarterly tax return** with your base jurisdiction, which pays the others and audits for them. Keep records that support each return.

**Which vehicles need IRP and IFTA.** A vehicle that carries people or property (for IRP: people only if for hire, and the vehicle must be used in **2 or more** member jurisdictions) and has at least one of:
- **2 axles** and a GVW (or registered GVW) **over 26,000 lb**;
- **3 or more axles**, at any weight;
- use in a **combination** weighing **over 26,000 lb**.

Not included: **recreational vehicles** (both programs). For IRP also not included: vehicles with restricted plates, charter buses and government vehicles — but a charter bus, or a truck, tractor or combination power unit of 26,000 lb or less, **may** choose to register under IRP.

**Trip records — the IVDR.** An **IVDR** (Individual Vehicle Distance Record, also called a **Driver Trip Report**) records the distance driven and fuel bought for one vehicle. Its layout can vary, but the required facts cannot:
- **Distance** (IRP Article IV): trip start and end dates; origin and destination (city and state/province); routes; starting and ending odometer or hub-odometer readings; total distance; distance in each jurisdiction; unit number or VIN.
- **Fuel receipts** (IFTA Procedures Manual P560): date; seller's name and address; gallons or liters; fuel type; price per unit or total sale; unit number or vehicle ID; buyer's name.
- **Write down the odometer** at the **start of the day**, when **leaving a state or province**, and at the **end of the trip or day**.
- Get a **receipt for every fuel purchase** and attach it to the IVDR. Use one IVDR **per vehicle** and list **every** state or province you passed through (the handbook says to enter trips in descending order), even if nearly all miles were in one state.
- Keep the original records at least **4 years** — fees and taxes are calculated from them, and they can be **audited**. Poor records can bring **fines, penalties, and suspension or revocation** of IRP registration and IFTA licenses. More information: irponline.org and iftach.org.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** A tractor-trailer may go 65 mph on a freeway posted 65. → **Correct:** **55 mph** for any truck with 3+ axles or any vehicle towing. The posted 65 is only for vehicles not on the 55-mph list. (p. 1-25)
- **Trap:** Only big rigs are held to 55 mph. → **Correct:** a pickup towing a trailer, a school bus with pupils, and a vehicle hauling explosives are also held to 55. "Pulling another vehicle" includes small trailers. (p. 1-25)
- **Trap:** On a wide freeway, a truck may use any lane except the far left. → **Correct:** the right lane, plus the lane just left of it only on a divided highway with **4 or more** lanes in your direction (or signed lanes). (p. 1-25)
- **Trap:** You must use a turnout when 3 vehicles are behind you. → **Correct:** **5 or more**. (p. 1-26)
- **Trap:** No SR 1 is needed if the crash was not your fault or happened in a parking lot. → **Correct:** report to DMV within **10 days** if there was death, injury or over $1,000 damage — fault and private property don't matter. (p. 1-26)
- **Trap:** The SR 1 goes to CHP or your insurance company. → **Correct:** it goes to the **DMV**, in addition to any police or insurance report. (p. 1-26)
- **Trap:** Tell your employer about a crash in the company truck within 10 days (mixing it with the SR 1). → **Correct:** within **5 days** (or right away if the employer requires it). (p. 1-26)
- **Trap:** 30 days to tell your employer your license was suspended. → **Correct:** **2 business days**. The 30 days is for telling your licensing agency about an out-of-state conviction. (p. 1-16)
- **Trap:** California intrastate drivers get 11 hours of driving and a 14-hour window. → **Correct:** California is **12 hours** driving and **16 hours** on duty; 11/14 is federal. (p. 1-27)
- **Trap:** California intrastate weekly limit is 70 hours in 8 days. → **Correct:** **80 hours** in 8 days (112 for farm products); 60/70 is federal. (p. 1-27)
- **Trap:** You never leave California, so California HOS always applies. → **Correct:** if the cargo came from or is going out of state, or it is hazardous substances/wastes, you are **interstate** and use federal HOS. (p. 1-26)
- **Trap:** The maximum width is 96 inches. → **Correct:** **102 in**. 96 in is the limit a city or county may post on its own roads. (p. 1-22)
- **Trap:** Doubles may be 75 ft with trailers up to 40 ft each. → **Correct:** 75 ft total, but **neither trailer over 28 ft 6 in**. A tractor with one trailer: 65 ft. (p. 1-21)
- **Trap:** A load may stick out 4 ft in front. → **Correct:** **3 ft**; 4 ft only for a load made only of vehicles. (p. 1-22)
- **Trap:** All vehicles may be 14 ft 3 in tall. → **Correct:** **14 ft**; only double-deck buses get 14 ft 3 in. (p. 1-23)
- **Trap:** Diesel trucks may idle 10 minutes, or idle briefly at a school. → **Correct:** **5 minutes** of non-essential idling, and **no idling at all** at a school. (p. 1-20)
- **Trap:** An overweight HazMat load must always be unloaded at the scale. → **Correct:** it may continue as loaded if adjusting it cannot be done with reasonable safety. (p. 1-24)
- **Trap:** A local weight-limit sign means you can never drive that street. → **Correct:** you may use it by the **direct route** to make a pickup or delivery, deliver permitted construction materials, or do utility work. (p. 1-23)
- **Trap:** With a special permit, you may pick a shorter route. → **Correct:** you must stay on the permit route; the only detour is on **nonresidential** streets to avoid breaking a local city rule. Leaving it without a peace officer's OK is a misdemeanor. (p. 1-24)
- **Trap:** Only the owner pays for bridge damage from an overweight truck. → **Correct:** the **driver** is liable, and the owner and driver may **both** have to pay. (p. 1-16)
- **Trap:** You may hold your phone for a quick call. → **Correct:** no holding the phone to talk; dialing only with a **single** button press; no reading or sending texts. (p. 1-16)
- **Trap:** A 2-axle truck of exactly 26,000 lb needs IRP/IFTA. → **Correct:** a 2-axle truck must be **over** 26,000 lb; a 3-axle truck qualifies at **any** weight. (p. 1-29)

## One-minute recap
- Report out-of-state convictions (not parking) to your licensing agency in **30 days**; tell your employer about a suspension in **2 business days**; an H holder must surrender the H in **24 hours** after a disqualifying event. One license only; no hand-held phone or texting; always wear the belt (**4×** death risk if thrown).
- **[CA] 55 mph** for 3+ axle trucks, anything towing, school buses with pupils, farm labor vehicles with passengers, explosives, trailer buses. Keep to the right lane (or the next lane on 4+ lane-each-way divided highways). Turn out with **5+** vehicles behind.
- **[CA] Size:** single vehicle **40 ft**; tractor + trailer **65 ft**; doubles **75 ft** (each trailer ≤ **28 ft 6 in**); load **75 ft**; front overhang **3 ft**; rear overhang ≤ **2/3 wheelbase**; width **102 in**; height **14 ft**.
- **[CA] Weight:** steering axle **12,500**; combination axle **18,000** / wheel end **9,500**; two tandems **34,000** each, **68,000** total. CHP: 200-lb allowance, then cited at **100 lb** over. Stop at all posted CHP inspection sites.
- **[CA] HOS intrastate:** **12** hr driving, **16** hr on duty, **80** hr on duty in 8 days. Federal: **11 / 14 / 60–70**. Both need **10** hr off in a row.
- **[CA] SR 1** to **DMV** within **10 days** for death, injury or over **$1,000** damage — fault and private property don't matter. Tell your employer within **5 days**.
- **[CA] CARB:** no more than **5 minutes** of idling, none at schools; CARB inspects out-of-state trucks too.
- **IRP/IFTA:** interstate vehicles with 3+ axles, or over 26,000 lb; IFTA return **quarterly**; keep IVDR trip records **4 years**.
