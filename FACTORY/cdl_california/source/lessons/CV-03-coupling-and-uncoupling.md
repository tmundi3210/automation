# CV-03 · Coupling and uncoupling

> **Handbook:** Section 6.4–6.4.8, pages 6-9–6-15 · **Test:** Combination Vehicles · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~45 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- The 16 coupling steps for a tractor-semitrailer, in the handbook's order, with the reason for each.
- Which step comes first, next, or just before another one — a common kind of test question.
- What goes wrong if the trailer is too high or too low, and how to prove the kingpin is really locked.
- How high the landing gear must go, and which clearances to check after coupling.
- The 10 uncoupling steps, including where to stop the tractor and why.
- The basic steps for pintle hook, drawbar and gooseneck hitches.

## Learn it

### Key words first
- **Fifth wheel** — the round coupling plate on the back of the tractor (the **lower** fifth wheel). The flat plate under the trailer's nose is the **upper** fifth wheel.
- **Kingpin** — the steel pin pointing down from the trailer's upper plate. Its middle part is the **shank**; its bottom end is the **head** (Figure 6.16, p. 6-16).
- **Jaws** — the parts inside the fifth wheel that close on the kingpin. The **locking lever** (release handle) opens them; a **safety latch** keeps that lever from opening by accident.
- **Landing gear** — the two front legs that hold the trailer up when no tractor is under it. You wind them with a **crank handle**.
- **Glad hands** — couplers joining the tractor air lines to the trailer: red = **emergency (supply)** line, blue = **service** line (CV-02, p. 6-6).
- **Trailer air supply knob** — the red 8-sided knob in the cab. **In** = air to the trailer. **Out** = trailer air off and trailer emergency brakes on. Older rigs use a lever with **"normal"** and **"emergency"** positions (p. 6-5).
- **Chocks** — wedges set against the tires so the wheels can't roll.

### 6.4 Why the steps matter (p. 6-9)
Getting coupling or uncoupling wrong is very dangerous. The handbook's steps are **general** — rigs differ, so also learn the details of the truck you will drive.

### 6.4.1 Coupling a tractor-semitrailer — 16 steps (p. 6-9 to 6-10)

Learn the order as six small groups:

| Group | Steps |
|---|---|
| Get ready | **1** Inspect fifth wheel · **2** Inspect area, chock wheels · **3** Position tractor |
| Approach | **4** Back slowly (just touch) · **5** Secure tractor · **6** Check trailer height |
| Air | **7** Connect air lines · **8** Supply air, check for crossed lines · **9** Lock trailer brakes |
| Hook up | **10** Back under · **11** Tug test |
| Look | **12** Secure vehicle · **13** Inspect coupling |
| Finish | **14** Electrical cord and lines · **15** Raise landing gear · **16** Remove chocks |

**Step 1 — Inspect the fifth wheel (p. 6-9)**
- No damaged or missing parts. Mounting to the tractor is tight; no cracks in the frame.
- Plate is **greased** as required. *Why:* a dry plate makes friction between tractor and trailer, which can cause **steering problems**.
- Set for coupling: **tilted down toward the rear** of the tractor, **jaws open**, safety unlocking handle in the **automatic lock** position, and a **sliding fifth wheel locked**.
- Trailer **kingpin** not bent or broken.
- *Why:* the tilt lets the trailer plate ride up onto the fifth wheel; open jaws let the kingpin enter and lock.
- **Note:** a few websites say "level." The handbook says **tilted down toward the rear** — answer that way.

**Step 2 — Inspect the area and chock the wheels (p. 6-9)**
- Check that nothing and nobody is in the way around the rig.
- Trailer wheels **chocked**, or trailer **spring brakes on**. *Why:* the trailer must not roll when the tractor pushes on it.
- Any cargo is secured. *Why:* the bump of coupling must not shift it.

**Step 3 — Position the tractor (p. 6-9)**
- Line the tractor up **straight in front** of the trailer. **Never back under at an angle.** *Why:* you could shove the trailer sideways and **break the landing gear**.
- Check your line in the outside mirrors, looking down **both sides** of the trailer.

**Step 4 — Back slowly (p. 6-9)**
- Stop when the fifth wheel **just touches** the trailer — don't bump it. *Why:* you still need to secure, check height and hook up air **before** going under.

**Step 5 — Secure the tractor (p. 6-9)**
- **Parking brakes** on, transmission in **neutral**. *Why:* you are about to leave the cab.

**Step 6 — Check the trailer height (p. 6-9)**
- The trailer should sit **low enough that the tractor lifts it slightly** as it backs under. Crank it up or down as needed.
  - **Too low** → the tractor may **hit and damage the trailer nose**.
  - **Too high** → the trailer **may not couple correctly**.
- Check that kingpin and fifth wheel are **lined up**.

**Step 7 — Connect the air lines (p. 6-9)**
- Check each glad hand **seal**. Hook tractor **emergency** to trailer **emergency**, then tractor **service** to trailer **service** (the handbook lists emergency first).
- Support the lines so they won't get pinched or snagged while you back under.
- *Why now:* the trailer's tanks fill through the emergency line (p. 6-6), and you must test the trailer brakes (Step 8) before going under.

**Step 8 — Supply air to the trailer (p. 6-9)**
- In the cab, **push in the air supply knob** (older rigs: lever from **"emergency" to "normal"**). **Wait for normal pressure.**
- **Crossed-line check:**
  - Turn the **engine off** — *why:* so you can **hear** the brakes.
  - Apply the trailer brakes, then release them. Applying → you hear the **brakes move**. Releasing → you hear **air escape**.
  - Watch the gauge for any **big air loss**.
- Once the trailer brakes clearly work, restart the engine and let pressure come back to normal.
- *Why:* with crossed lines, supply air goes down the service line and the trailer tanks never fill. Spring brakes then won't release — and an old trailer with **no** spring brakes could be driven off with **no trailer brakes** (p. 6-6).

**Step 9 — Lock the trailer brakes (p. 6-10)**
- **Pull out** the air supply knob (older rigs: lever from **"normal" to "emergency"**).
- *Why:* pushing the knob in (Step 8) released the trailer brakes. Setting them again keeps the trailer still while you back under it and tug on it.

**Step 10 — Back under the trailer (p. 6-10)**
- **Lowest reverse gear**, **slowly**. *Why:* so you don't hit the kingpin too hard.
- Stop as soon as the kingpin **locks** into the fifth wheel.

**Step 11 — Check the connection: the tug test (p. 6-10)**
- Wind the landing gear **slightly** off the ground.
- **Gently pull forward** with the trailer brakes **still locked**. *Why:* a trailer that is really locked on will hold; if it isn't, you find out now at a crawl, not on the road.

**Step 12 — Secure the vehicle (p. 6-10)**
- **Neutral**, **parking brakes** on, **engine off — and take the key**. *Why:* nobody can move the truck while you're under it.

**Step 13 — Inspect the coupling (p. 6-10)**
- Use a flashlight if you need one.
- **No space** between the upper and lower fifth wheel. *Why:* a gap means the kingpin may be **resting on top of closed jaws** — the trailer could easily come off.
- Get under the trailer and **look at the back of the fifth wheel**: the jaws must be closed around the kingpin **shank**.
- **Locking lever** in **"lock"**; **safety latch** over the lever (on some fifth wheels you set it **by hand**).
- Anything wrong → **don't drive**; have it repaired.

**Step 14 — Electrical cord and line check (p. 6-10)**
- Plug in the trailer's electrical cord and close its **safety catch**.
- Look over the air and electrical lines for **damage**, and make sure they stay clear of **moving parts**.

**Step 15 — Raise the landing gear (p. 6-10)**
- Begin in the crank's **low gear range** (if it has one); once the legs carry **no weight**, switch to **high range**. *Why:* low range lifts the load more easily; high range is faster.
- Wind it **all the way up** — never drive with it partly raised. *Why:* it can snag on **railroad tracks** or other objects.
- **Secure the crank handle.**
- With the trailer's full weight on the tractor, check clearance between:
  - the **rear of the tractor frame and the landing gear** (in a **sharp turn** the tractor must not hit it);
  - the **tops of the tractor tires and the trailer nose**.

**Step 16 — Remove the wheel chocks (p. 6-10)**
- Pull the chocks and **store** them safely. This is the **last** step.

### 6.4.2 Uncoupling a tractor-semitrailer — 10 steps (p. 6-11)

| Group | Steps |
|---|---|
| Set up | **1** Position rig · **2** Ease pressure on jaws · **3** Chock trailer wheels |
| Prepare trailer | **4** Lower landing gear · **5** Disconnect air lines and cable |
| Separate | **6** Unlock fifth wheel · **7** Pull partly clear · **8** Secure tractor · **9** Inspect supports · **10** Pull clear |

1. **Position the rig.** Park on ground strong enough for the trailer's weight (*why:* soft ground lets the legs sink). Keep the tractor **in line** with the trailer (*why:* leaving at an **angle** can damage the landing gear).
2. **Ease the pressure on the locking jaws.** Pull the air supply knob to **lock the trailer brakes**. **Back up gently** to take the load off the jaws (*why:* the locking lever then releases more easily). **Set the parking brakes while still pushing** against the kingpin (*why:* holds the rig with no pressure on the jaws).
3. **Chock the trailer wheels** if the trailer has **no spring brakes, or you are not sure**. *Why:* air can leak from the trailer tank; its emergency brakes then let go and the trailer could roll.
4. **Lower the landing gear.** **Empty** trailer: wind down until the feet are **firmly on the ground**. **Loaded** trailer: after firm contact, give the crank **a few extra turns in low gear** to take some weight off the tractor — **but don't lift the trailer off the fifth wheel**. *Why:* unlatching is easier now, and so is coupling next time.
5. **Disconnect the air lines and electrical cable.** Park the glad hands on the **dummy couplers** behind the cab, or lock the two together (*why:* keeps dirt and water out, p. 6-6). Hang the cable **plug down** (*why:* keeps moisture out). Support the lines so driving the tractor won't damage them.
6. **Unlock the fifth wheel.** Lift the lock on the release handle, then pull the handle to **"open."** Keep your **legs and feet away from the rear tractor wheels** (*why:* you could be badly hurt if the rig moves).
7. **Pull the tractor partly clear.** Drive forward until the fifth wheel slides out from beneath the trailer, then **stop with the tractor frame still under the trailer**. *Why:* if the landing gear **collapses or sinks**, the trailer lands on the frame, not the ground.
8. **Secure the tractor.** **Parking brake** on, **neutral**.
9. **Inspect the trailer supports.** Is the **ground holding** the trailer? Is the **landing gear undamaged**?
10. **Pull the tractor clear.** Release the parking brakes, **check around you**, and drive forward until clear.

### 6.4.3–6.4.8 Pintle hook, drawbar and gooseneck hitches (p. 6-12 to 6-15)
**[CA]** These hitch pages are in the California handbook but rarely appear in practice question banks. **Low exam weight** — know the safety points and roughly what each step does.

Terms: a **pintle hook** is a hook on the tow vehicle; the trailer's **drawbar eye** (a steel ring) drops over its tip, the **horn**, and a **latch** closes on top. A **ball-type drawbar** ends in a **ball cup** that sits on a **coupling ball**, held by a **safety cover bar** and screws. A **gooseneck** hitch is a ball in the truck bed. The **tongue** is the trailer's front end at the hitch. A **breakaway brake switch** is tied to the tow vehicle by a cable called a **lanyard**.

**6.4.3 Coupling a pintle hook (p. 6-12)**
1. Inspect the hook: worn, damaged or missing parts; mount secure. *Why:* a loose mount can let the hook tear off the vehicle — death or serious injury.
2. Open it: pull the **tethered lock pin** (if fitted); lift the lock handle away from the vehicle to free the lock from its seat; swing the latch up toward the vehicle to fully upright; let go of the handle.
3. Set the drawbar eye over the horn and lower it on.
4. Lock it: push the latch shut. Correctly locked = the lock handle has swung up to sit **flush with the top of the latch**. Refit the tethered lock pin (if fitted). *Why:* an unlocked latch can let the trailer separate — death or serious injury.

**6.4.4 Uncoupling a pintle hook (p. 6-13)**
1. Park on **firm, level** ground; **block** the trailer tires.
2. Unhook the electrical connector, the **breakaway brake switch lanyard**, and the **safety chains**.
3. Unlock and open the coupler.
4. **Before** extending the jack, make sure the ground under the jack pad can carry the tongue's weight.
5. Crank the jack down so it takes the tongue's weight.
6. Lift the coupler above the tow vehicle's hitch.
7. Drive the tow vehicle forward.

**6.4.5 Coupling a ball-type drawbar (p. 6-13 to 6-14)**
1. Open up: remove one safety lock screw (keep its self-locking nut), loosen the other, back the adjustment screw out **at least 5 turns**, and swing the safety cover bar fully open.
2. Back the truck **very slowly** until the ball cup sits exactly over the coupling ball.
3. Lower the drawbeam until the cup fully covers the ball.
4. Swing the cover bar closed, refit the screw and nut, and torque both lock screws to **350–400 newton meters (Nm)**. If the cover bar **won't seat properly, you may not travel**.
5. Turn the adjustment screw until there is a **0.3–0.5 mm** vertical gap between the guard disk and the ball cup; lock the setting with the counter nut. (The drawing on p. 6-14 is labelled "25mm"; go with the step text.)
6. Fit the rubber **dust-proof bellows cover** over the visible part of the ball, edge seated on the ball.
7. Grease the inside of the ball cup through the **grease nipple**.

**6.4.6 Uncoupling a ball-type drawbar (p. 6-14)**
1. Make sure the **trailer brake is on**.
2. Take off the bellows cover; loosen the adjustment screw and counter nut; loosen one lock screw and remove the other with its nut.
3. Swing the cover bar fully open, raise the drawbeam until the ball is fully exposed, then move forward **very slowly**. Swing the cover bar back into its housing.
4. Refit the safety lock screw and tighten its self-locking nut.

**6.4.7 Coupling a gooseneck hitch (p. 6-15)** — a bit different from a receiver-and-ball hitch, but not hard.
1. Open the coupler's **clamp latch**; check the ball is **lubricated**.
2. Put the coupler right over the ball, lower the trailer onto it, and **latch the clamp**.
3. Hook up the **safety chains** — by law, **every trailer must have them**.
4. Plug in the trailer light wiring; test **every light, brake lights included**.
5. Lower the trailer jacks fully and stow them, so the trailer's weight rests on the tow vehicle.

**6.4.8 Uncoupling a gooseneck hitch (p. 6-15)**
1. Pull the **safety pin and clip**.
2. Turn the handle to release (Figure 6.15) and lift the trailer off the ball. The coupler goes back to the **load position on its own**.
3. Put the safety pin and clip back.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** "Position the tractor" is step 1 → **Correct:** **inspect the fifth wheel** first. The driving part feels like the start. (p. 6-9)
- **Trap:** fifth wheel **level** → **Correct:** **tilted down toward the rear**. Some websites key "level." (p. 6-9)
- **Trap:** trailer high enough that the tractor slides under without touching → **Correct:** **low enough to be lifted slightly**. Too high = may not couple. (p. 6-9)
- **Trap:** too **low** = won't couple → **Correct:** too low = tractor **hits the nose**; too **high** = may not couple. Easy to swap. (p. 6-9)
- **Trap:** back under first, then hook up the air → **Correct:** Steps 7–9 (air, brake check, lock brakes) all come **before** Step 10. (p. 6-9 to 6-10)
- **Trap:** leave the knob **pushed in** while backing under → **Correct:** **pull it out** to lock the trailer brakes. (p. 6-10)
- **Trap:** keep the engine running for the crossed-line check → **Correct:** **engine off** so you can hear. (p. 6-9)
- **Trap:** a **small gap** between the plates is normal → **Correct:** **no space** at all. (p. 6-10)
- **Trap:** a good tug test means no need to look → **Correct:** you still get under and **look at the back of the fifth wheel**. (p. 6-10)
- **Trap:** landing gear **just off the pavement** → **Correct:** **all the way up**. (p. 6-10)
- **Trap:** uncoupling — **pull forward** to free the jaws → **Correct:** **back up gently**, then set the brakes while pushing. (p. 6-11)
- **Trap:** crank a loaded trailer **until it lifts off** the fifth wheel → **Correct:** a few turns only; never lift it off. (p. 6-11)
- **Trap:** drive the tractor fully out in one go → **Correct:** first stop with the **frame still under the trailer**. (p. 6-11)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 6.3–6.4 #13** What could go wrong if you try to couple with the trailer sitting too high?
→ It **may not couple correctly**. It should sit low enough that the tractor raises it a little while backing under. (Too **low** is also a problem: the tractor may hit and damage the trailer's nose.) (p. 6-9)

**TYK 6.3–6.4 #14** After coupling, what gap should there be between the upper and lower fifth wheel?
→ **None.** A gap means something is wrong — the kingpin may be sitting on top of closed jaws, and the trailer could easily come loose. (p. 6-10)

**TYK 6.3–6.4 #15** True or false: you check that the fifth wheel is locked on the kingpin by looking into the back of the fifth wheel.
→ **True.** Get under the trailer (flashlight if needed) and look at the back of the fifth wheel: jaws closed around the **shank**, locking lever in "lock," safety latch over the lever. (p. 6-10)

**TYK 6.3–6.4 #16** True or false: before driving, the landing gear only has to clear the pavement.
→ **False.** Wind it **all the way up** and secure the crank handle; gear left partly down can snag on railroad tracks or other objects. (p. 6-10)

## One-minute recap
- **Coupling (16):** inspect fifth wheel → area and chocks → position → back until it just touches → secure → check height → connect air → supply air and check for crossed lines → lock trailer brakes → back under → tug test → secure and take key → get under and look → electrical cord → landing gear all the way up → remove chocks.
- Fifth wheel before coupling: **tilted down toward the rear, jaws open**, handle on automatic lock.
- Trailer height: **low enough to be lifted slightly**. Too high = may not couple; too low = nose damage.
- Air hooked up, brakes tested, and trailer brakes **locked before** backing under.
- After coupling: **no gap** between plates; jaws on the **shank**; lever locked; latch over lever.
- **Uncoupling (10):** firm ground, in line → lock trailer brakes, **back up gently**, park while pushing → chock if no spring brakes or unsure → lower the landing gear (loaded: a few extra turns) → lines off (dummy couplers, plug down) → unlock → pull out and **stop with the frame under** → secure → check supports → pull clear.
- Hitches (low weight): pintle handle **flush** with latch when locked; drawbar screws **350–400 Nm**; **safety chains** on every trailer.
