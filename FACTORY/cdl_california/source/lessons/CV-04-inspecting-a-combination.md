# CV-04 · Inspecting a combination vehicle

> **Handbook:** Section 6.5–6.5.2, pages 6-16–6-17 · **Test:** Combination Vehicles · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~30 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- Which extra parts you check on a combination during the walk-around: both fifth-wheel plates, the kingpin, the air and electric lines, a sliding fifth wheel, and the landing gear.
- Where the shut-off valves must be open, and where they must be closed.
- How to test that air reaches every trailer.
- How to test the tractor protection valve, and the pressure range at which it should close.
- How to test the trailer emergency brakes and the trailer service brakes.

## Learn it

### Key words first
- **7-step inspection** — the walk-around inspection method you learned in Section 2 (module GK-04).
- **Lower fifth wheel** — the coupling plate on the tractor. **Upper fifth wheel** / **glide plate** — the flat plate under the front of the trailer.
- **Kingpin** — the steel pin under the trailer's upper plate. From top to bottom it has a **base**, a **shank**, and a **head** (Figure 6.16).
- **Sliding fifth wheel** — a fifth wheel that can be moved forward or back on the tractor frame. **Locking pins** hold it in place.
- **Shut-off valves** — valves in the service and supply lines at the **rear of any trailer that pulls another trailer**. Closing them seals off the lines when nothing is hooked up behind (p. 6-7).
- **Tractor protection valve (TPV)** — keeps air in the tractor if the trailer breaks away or leaks badly. You control it with the **red trailer air supply knob** (p. 6-5).
- **Trailer air supply control** — the red knob (older rigs: a lever). The handbook also calls it the **tractor protection valve control** or the **trailer emergency valve**. **Push in** = air to the trailer. **Pull out** = air off and trailer emergency brakes on.
- **Trailer hand valve** — a hand control that puts on the trailer brakes only. Also called the **trolley valve** (or Johnson bar). Use it **only for testing** (p. 6-5).
- **Charge the trailer air system** — let the air pressure rise to normal, then push the air supply knob in.

### 6.5 Use the same 7-step method (p. 6-16)
- Inspect a combination with the **same 7-step procedure** from Section 2.
- A combination has **more** of the usual parts to check — more tires, wheels, lights, reflectors, and so on.
- It also has some **new** parts to check. Those are listed below.

### 6.5.1 Extra checks during the walk-around (p. 6-16)
Do these **as well as** the Section 2 checks.

**Coupling system areas**

*Lower fifth wheel (on the tractor):*
- Firmly **mounted to the frame**.
- **No missing or damaged** parts.
- **Enough grease**.
- **No gap you can see** between the upper and lower fifth wheel.
- Locking jaws closed around the kingpin's **shank** — **never its head**.
- **Release arm** seated correctly, with the **safety latch/lock engaged**.
- *Why:* these parts are all that hold the trailer on. A gap between the plates, or jaws in the wrong place on the kingpin, can let the trailer come loose (see CV-03, p. 6-10).

*Upper fifth wheel (on the trailer):*
- **Glide plate** firmly mounted to the **trailer frame**.
- **Kingpin not damaged**.

*Air lines and electrical cord:*
- **Electrical cord** pushed firmly into its socket and **secured**.
- **Air lines** correctly coupled at the glad hands, **not leaking**, and fastened with **enough slack for turns**.
- **No damage** on any line.
- *Why slack:* when the rig turns, the distance between tractor and trailer connections changes. A line with no slack can be pulled apart.

*Sliding fifth wheel:*
- The slide is **not damaged** and has **no missing parts**.
- It has **the right amount of grease**.
- **Every locking pin is there and locked**.
- An **air-powered** slide has **no air leaks**.
- It is not set **so far forward** that, in turns, the **tractor frame hits the landing gear** or the **cab hits the trailer**.

**Landing gear**
- Raised **all the way up**; **nothing missing**; not bent or otherwise damaged.
- **Crank handle** in its place and **secured**.
- Power-operated gear: **no air or hydraulic leaks**.
- *Why fully raised:* landing gear that hangs part way down can catch on railroad tracks or other things (p. 6-10).

### 6.5.2 Combination vehicle brake check (p. 6-17)
- Do these checks **in addition to** the air brake checks in Section 5.3 (which you learned for the Air Brakes test).
- Check the brakes on **doubles or triples** the same way as on any combination.
- **[CA]** Triple trailers are **not legal in California**. The handbook still mentions them because other states allow them (p. 7-1).

There are four tests. Learn them as a set:

| Test | What you do | Good result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Air flows to all trailers | Charge the system and apply the trailer hand brake; on the back of the last trailer, open the emergency-line valve, then the service-line valve (close each one after) | Air comes out of **both** lines |
| 2. Tractor protection valve | Charge the system, **engine off**, pump the brake pedal | Knob **pops out** at the maker's pressure, usually **20–45 psi** |
| 3. Trailer emergency brakes | Charge the system, check the trailer rolls freely, stop, **pull out** the knob, tug gently | Trailer brakes **hold** |
| 4. Trailer service brakes | Normal pressure, release parking brakes, roll forward slowly, apply the **hand valve** | You **feel** the trailer brakes come on |

**Test 1 — Check that air flows to all the trailers**
1. Hold the vehicle still with the **tractor parking brake** and/or **wheel chocks**.
2. Wait for **normal air pressure**.
3. **Push the red trailer air supply knob in.** Air now flows into the **emergency (supply)** lines.
4. Use the **trailer hand brake** to send air into the **service** line.
5. Go to the **back** of the rig. On the **last trailer**, open the **emergency-line shut-off valve**. Air should **rush out** — proof that the **whole system is charged**. Then **close** that valve.
6. **Open the service line valve** at the rear. Air here shows that **service pressure reaches every trailer**. (This only works while the trailer hand brake or the brake pedal is applied.) **Close** the valve.
7. No air from one or both lines? Make sure every shut-off valve on the **trailers and dollies** is **OPEN**.
- *Why:* air must reach **all the way to the back**, or not all the brakes will work.

**The shut-off valve rule (p. 6-7):** keep every shut-off valve **open** — **except** those on the **back of the last trailer**, which stay **closed**. In Test 1 you open those last valves only for a moment, then close them again.

**Test 2 — Test the tractor protection valve**
1. **Charge** the trailer air system (normal pressure, air supply knob pushed in).
2. **Shut off the engine.** *Why:* the compressor must stop, so the pressure can only go down.
3. **Pump the brake pedal** (press and release it several times) to lower the pressure in the tanks.
4. The trailer air supply control should **pop out** (or move from **"normal" to "emergency"**) when the pressure falls into the range the manufacturer sets — **usually 20 to 45 psi**.
- *Why it matters:* with a faulty tractor protection valve, a leaking air hose or trailer brake could **empty all the air out of the tractor**. The emergency brakes would then come on, and you could **lose control**.
- **Note:** one practice website gives 25–40 psi. The handbook says **20–45 psi** — use that on the test.

**Test 3 — Test the trailer emergency brakes**
1. **Charge** the trailer air system and make sure the trailer **rolls freely**.
2. **Stop.** **Pull out** the trailer air supply control (or put it in **"emergency"**).
3. Use the tractor to **tug gently** against the trailer. The **trailer emergency brakes should be on** and keep it from moving.
- *Why:* this proves the trailer brakes will come on by themselves if the trailer loses its supply air — for example, if it breaks away.

**Test 4 — Test the trailer service brakes**
1. Make sure the air pressure is **normal**.
2. **Release the parking brakes** and move forward **slowly**.
3. Put on the trailer brakes using the **hand control (trolley valve)**, if the rig has one.
4. You should **feel the trailer brakes grab**. That proves they are **connected and working**.
- **Test** the trailer brakes with the **hand valve**, but in **normal driving** brake with the **foot pedal**. The pedal sends air to the service brakes at **all** the wheels. *Why:* using the hand valve while driving can make the trailer skid (p. 6-5).

**Reminder from Section 5.3 (Air Brakes) — leakage limits (p. 5-8, 5-10)**
With the system fully charged, engine off, and brakes released, the pressure should not drop in 1 minute by more than **3 psi** for a combination of **2** vehicles, or **5 psi** for **3 or more**. If the towed vehicles have no air brakes, the limit for the combination is **2 psi** (static test, p. 5-10).
- **Applied test** (engine off, foot brake held down fully for 1 minute): no more than **4 psi** for **2** vehicles, or **6 psi** for **3 or more**; **3 psi** if the towed vehicles have no air brakes (p. 5-8).

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** all shut-off valves should be open → **Correct:** all open **except** the ones at the rear of the **last** trailer, which are closed. "All open" sounds safest because air must reach every trailer. (p. 6-7)
- **Trap:** on doubles, the valves on the back of the **first** trailer are closed → **Correct:** only the **last** trailer's rear valves are closed; the first trailer tows the second one, so its rear valves stay open. (p. 6-7)
- **Trap:** the service line has air as soon as you push in the knob → **Correct:** the knob fills the **emergency (supply)** line; the **service** line has air only when the **trailer hand brake or brake pedal** is applied. (p. 6-17)
- **Trap:** test the tractor protection valve with the **engine running** → **Correct:** **engine off**, then pump the pedal. (p. 6-17)
- **Trap:** the knob should pop out at **25–40 psi** (one website's number) → **Correct:** usually **20–45 psi** (the manufacturer's range). (p. 6-17)
- **Trap:** to test the trailer emergency brakes, **push in** the knob and tug → **Correct:** **pull out** the knob (or set "emergency"), then tug gently. (p. 6-17)
- **Trap:** test the trailer service brakes with the **foot pedal** → **Correct:** test with the **hand valve** (trolley valve); use the foot pedal for normal driving. (p. 6-17)
- **Trap:** the jaws should grip the **head** of the kingpin → **Correct:** the **shank**. (p. 6-16)
- **Trap:** air lines should be pulled **tight** so they cannot drag → **Correct:** secured with **enough slack for turns**. (p. 6-16)
- **Trap:** slide the fifth wheel as far forward as possible → **Correct:** not so far forward that the tractor frame hits the landing gear or the cab hits the trailer in turns. (p. 6-16)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 6.5 #18** Which shut-off valves stay open, and which ones must be closed?
→ Every shut-off valve stays **open** except those on the **back of the last trailer** — those must be **closed**. (If no air reaches the rear during the air-flow test, check that the valves on the trailers and dollies are open.) (p. 6-7, 6-17)

**TYK 6.5 #19** How do you check that air reaches every trailer?
→ Hold the rig with the tractor parking brake and/or chocks. Once pressure is normal, push the red trailer air supply knob in (this feeds the emergency/supply lines) and apply the trailer hand brake (this feeds the service line). Walk to the back of the rig. On the last trailer, open the **emergency-line shut-off valve** and listen for air rushing out; close it. Then open the **service-line valve**, listen, and close it. If either line gives no air, make sure the shut-off valves on the trailers and dollies are open. (p. 6-17)

**TYK 6.5 #20** How do you test the tractor protection valve?
→ Charge the trailer air system (normal pressure, knob pushed in). **Shut off the engine.** Pump the brake pedal — press and release it again and again — to lower the pressure in the tanks. When the pressure drops into the manufacturer's range, **usually 20–45 psi**, the trailer air supply control should **pop out** (a lever type moves from "normal" to "emergency"). (p. 6-17)

**TYK 6.5 #21** How do you test the trailer emergency brakes?
→ Charge the trailer air system and make sure the trailer rolls freely. Stop. **Pull out** the trailer air supply control (or set it to "emergency"). Then **tug gently** against the trailer with the tractor — the trailer emergency brakes should hold it. (p. 6-17)

**TYK 6.5 #22** How do you test the trailer service brakes?
→ With air pressure at normal, release the parking brakes and roll forward **slowly**. Put on the trailer brakes using the **hand control (trolley valve)**, if the rig has one. You should **feel** them grab — proof they are connected and working. (Use the hand valve only for testing; in normal driving use the foot pedal.) (p. 6-17)

## One-minute recap
- Inspect a combination with the **same 7-step method**, plus extra checks.
- Fifth wheel: firmly mounted, greased, **no gap** between the plates, jaws on the **shank**, safety latch engaged. Glide plate secure, kingpin undamaged.
- Lines: cord plugged in and secured; air lines connected, no leaks, **slack for turns**. Sliding fifth wheel: pins locked, not too far forward. Landing gear: **fully up**, crank handle secured.
- Shut-off valves: **all open except the rear of the last trailer (closed)**.
- **Air-flow test:** knob in + hand brake on → at the back of the last trailer, open the emergency valve, listen for air, close it; then do the same with the service valve.
- **TPV test:** engine off, pump the pedal → knob pops out at **usually 20–45 psi**.
- **Emergency brake test:** knob out → tug gently → brakes hold.
- **Service brake test:** roll slowly → apply the **hand valve** → feel the brakes. Drive with the **foot pedal**.
