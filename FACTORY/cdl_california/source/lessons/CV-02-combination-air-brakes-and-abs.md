# CV-02 · Combination air brakes and trailer ABS

> **Handbook:** Section 6.2–6.3, pages 6-5–6-8 · **Test:** Combination Vehicles · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~40 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- What the trailer hand valve is for — and the two things you must never use it for.
- What the tractor protection valve and the red trailer air supply knob do, and at what pressure the knob pops out.
- The difference between the service line and the emergency line, and what happens when each one breaks or leaks.
- How to connect glad hands, what happens if you cross the air lines, and why dummy couplers matter.
- Where the shut-off valves are, which ones must be closed, and why you chock a trailer without spring brakes.
- Which trailers must have ABS, where the trailer ABS lamp is, and how to brake with ABS.

## Learn it

For the Air Brakes test you learned how the tractor's own air brakes work (Section 5). A combination adds parts that send air back to the trailer and control the trailer's brakes. This module teaches those extra parts. (p. 6-5)

**Key words**
- **psi** = pounds per square inch, the unit on your air pressure gauge.
- **Converter dolly** = the short unit on wheels used to hook up the next trailer (trailer → dolly → second trailer).
- **Relay valve** = a valve on the trailer that sends air from the trailer's own tank to its brakes.
- **Spring brakes** = brakes held on by strong springs and released by air (you learned these for Air Brakes). On a trailer they work as the parking brakes.

**The big picture**
```
Tractor air tanks ──► Tractor protection valve (you control it with the red knob)
                         │
                         ├─► EMERGENCY line (red) = supply ──► fills trailer air tank(s)
                         │
                         └─► SERVICE line (blue) = signal, set by foot brake / hand valve ──► tells relay valve "how much"
                                                                  │
                             trailer air tank ──► relay valve ──► trailer brakes
```

### 6.2.1 Trailer hand valve (p. 6-5)
- Other names: **trolley valve** or **Johnson bar**. It puts on the **trailer brakes only**.
- **Use it only to test the trailer brakes.**
- **Never use it while driving.** Why: braking only the trailer can make the trailer skid. The **foot brake** works every brake on the rig at once, trailers included, so a skid or jackknife is much less likely.
- **Never use it to park.** Why: the air can leak out, and then the brakes let go (on a trailer with no spring brakes). Always park with the **parking brakes**. If the trailer has no spring brakes, also use **wheel chocks**.
- How to test (p. 6-6): before driving, set the trailer brakes with the hand valve (or by pulling out the air supply knob). Then, in a **low gear**, **tug gently** against them to confirm they hold.

### 6.2.2 Tractor protection valve (p. 6-5)
**Job:** protect the tractor's (or truck's) own air supply if the trailer breaks away or gets a bad leak. Why: so the tractor still has air for its own brakes.
- You open and shut it from the cab with the **"trailer air supply"** control.
- It **closes by itself** when air pressure drops low — somewhere between **20 and 45 psi**.
- When it closes, two things happen:
  1. No air can leave the tractor.
  2. Air is let out of the trailer emergency line → the **trailer emergency brakes come on**. You could lose control.

> Handbook vs other sources: one website gives 25–40 psi. The handbook says **20–45 psi** — use that.

### 6.2.3 Trailer air supply control (p. 6-5)
On newer vehicles it is a **red, 8-sided knob** (shaped like a stop sign). It controls the tractor protection valve, so the handbook (p. 6-6) and some practice tests also call it the "tractor protection valve control" — same thing.

| You do / it does | Result |
|---|---|
| **Push in** | Air goes to the trailer |
| **Pull out** | Air to the trailer is shut off, and the **trailer emergency brakes** come on |
| **Pops out by itself** | Air pressure has dropped into the **20–45 psi** range; the tractor protection valve closes |

**Older vehicles:** the control may be a **lever** instead of a knob, and it may **not** work automatically. It has two positions: **"normal"** for pulling a trailer, and **"emergency"** to shut off the air and put on the trailer emergency brakes.

**Do not mix up the knobs:** red 8-sided = trailer air supply. Yellow diamond-shaped = parking brakes (you learned this for Air Brakes, p. 5-4).

### 6.2.4 Trailer air lines (p. 6-5)
Every combination has **2** air lines: the **service line** and the **emergency line**. Both lines link each unit to the next one — for example, tractor and trailer, first trailer and dolly, dolly and second trailer.

| | Service line | Emergency line |
|---|---|---|
| Other names | **Control line**, **signal line** | **Supply line** |
| Color code | **Blue** | **Red** (red hose, red couplers, other red parts) |
| What it carries | Air whose pressure you set with the foot brake or the trailer hand valve. Press harder → more pressure in the line. | Air to fill the trailer air tanks |
| What it connects to / controls | **Relay valves**, which let the trailer brakes come on faster than they otherwise could | The trailer **emergency brakes** |
| If it loses air | A major leak may go unnoticed **until you brake**. Then the leak drops tank pressure fast; if it gets low enough, the trailer emergency brakes come on. (p. 6-7) | Trailer emergency brakes **come on**, and the tractor protection valve **closes** (the knob pops out) |

**The emergency line has 2 jobs:** (1) fill the trailer air tanks, and (2) control the trailer emergency brakes.

It can lose pressure if the trailer breaks loose and tears the hose apart, or if a hose, metal tubing or other part breaks and lets the air out.

### 6.2.5 Hose couplers — glad hands (p. 6-6)
**Glad hands** are the couplers that join the tractor's service and emergency lines to the trailer's lines.
- Each one has a **rubber seal** that keeps air from escaping. **Clean** the couplers and seals before you connect them, and look for **cracks** in the seals (Figure 6.6).
- **To connect:** hold the two couplers at a right angle (**90 degrees**) to each other and push their rubber seals together. Then turn the glad hand on the hose — this joins and locks them.
- **Match the right pair:** **blue = service**, **red = emergency (supply)**. Some lines have metal tags stamped "service" and "emergency".

**Crossed air lines** (service joined to emergency by mistake):
- The supply air goes into the service line instead of filling the trailer tanks.
- **Trailer with spring brakes:** there is no air to release the spring (parking) brakes, so they **stay on**. So if you push in the air supply knob and the trailer spring brakes stay on, suspect the line connections and check them.
- **Older trailer without spring brakes:** if the air in its tank has leaked away, it has **no emergency brakes** and the wheels turn freely. With crossed lines you **could drive away with no trailer brakes** — very dangerous. That is why you always test the trailer brakes before driving (hand valve or pull the knob, then tug gently in a low gear).

**Dummy couplers** (also called "dead-end" couplers): a place to hook the hoses when no trailer is connected. Why: they keep **water and dirt** out of the couplers and air lines. If there are no dummy couplers, you can sometimes lock the two glad hands to each other (it depends on the couplings). Keeping the air supply clean is very important.

### 6.2.6 Trailer air tanks (p. 6-6)
- Each trailer, and each converter dolly, carries **at least one** air tank.
- The tractor fills these tanks through the **emergency (supply) line** — **not** the service line.
- The tanks hold the air that works the trailer brakes. **Relay valves** send that air from the tanks to the brakes.
- The **service line** pressure tells the relay valves **how much** pressure to send to the trailer brakes. You set the service line pressure with the brake pedal (and the trailer hand valve).
- **Drain every tank every day** using its drain valve. Why: if water and oil build up, the brakes may not work correctly. Automatic drains keep most moisture out, but you should **still open the drains** to be sure.

### 6.2.7 Shut-off valves (p. 6-7)
- Where: in the service and supply lines at the **back of trailers that tow other trailers**.
- What for: to close off the air lines when no trailer is being towed behind.
- The rule: **all open**, **except** at the **rear of the very last trailer** — those must be **closed**. Why: open valves at the very end of the rig would let the air escape.

### 6.2.8 Trailer service, parking, and emergency brakes (p. 6-7)
- Newer trailers have **spring brakes**, the same as trucks and tractors.
- Spring brakes are **not required** on **converter dollies and trailers built before 1975**.
- The ones without spring brakes have **emergency brakes** that run on air stored in the trailer tank. They have **no parking brake**.
- Their emergency brakes come on when: the emergency line loses pressure, you pull out the air supply knob, or the trailer gets unhooked.
- A big emergency-line leak shuts the tractor protection valve and sets the trailer emergency brakes. **But** those brakes hold only while there is air in the trailer tank. The air slowly leaks away, and then there are **no brakes**.
- **So: always chock the wheels when you park a trailer without spring brakes.**
- Service line reminder: a major service line leak may not show until you press the brakes; then tank pressure falls quickly, and if it falls far enough, the trailer emergency brakes come on.

### 6.3 Anti-lock brake systems — ABS (p. 6-8)
ABS uses a computer to keep the wheels from locking during hard braking. You met it for Air Brakes and General Knowledge; here are the trailer rules.

#### 6.3.1 Trailers required to have ABS
- Every trailer and converter dolly built on or after **March 1, 1998** must have ABS. Many older ones were given ABS voluntarily.
- Trailers have a **yellow ABS malfunction lamp** on the **left side**, at the **front or rear corner** (Figure 6.7).
- Dollies made March 1, 1998 or later must also have a lamp on the **left side**.
- For an older unit, it can be hard to tell. **Look under the vehicle** for the **ECU** (electronic control unit — the ABS computer) and for **wheel speed sensor wires** running out from behind the brakes.

#### 6.3.2 Braking with ABS
- ABS is **added to** your normal brakes. It does **not** increase or decrease your normal braking power. It works **only when a wheel is about to lock**.
- ABS may **not** make your stops any shorter. What it does is help you **keep control** in hard braking.
- How it works: the computer senses a wheel about to lock, lowers the brake pressure to a safe level, and you keep control.
- ABS on the trailer only — or even on just **1 axle** — still gives you more control when braking.
- If ABS is on the **trailer only**: the trailer swings out less often. But if steering control is lost or a **tractor jackknife** begins, **ease off the brakes** (if it is safe) until you have control again.
- **Brake the way you always have:**
  - Use only as much braking force as you need for a safe, controlled stop.
  - Your braking does not change based on where the ABS is — tractor, trailer, or both.
  - As you slow down, watch the tractor and trailer, and ease off the brakes (if safe) to stay in control.
- If the ABS fails, you **still have your regular brakes**. Drive normally and get it **serviced soon**.
- ABS is **not** a reason to drive faster, follow closer, or be less careful.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** Use the hand valve to park for a short time. → **Correct:** **Never** park with it. The air can leak off and the brakes release. Use the parking brakes (and chocks if no spring brakes). (p. 6-5)
- **Trap:** Put on the hand valve before the foot brake to prevent a jackknife. → **Correct:** Don't. Braking the trailer alone can cause the skid. The hand valve is **only for testing**. (p. 6-5)
- **Trap:** When the emergency line loses air, the tractor protection valve **opens**. → **Correct:** It **closes**, and the trailer emergency brakes come on. "Opens" sounds like "lets air through to help". (p. 6-5)
- **Trap:** A service line leak makes the trailer brakes come on right away. → **Correct:** You may not notice it **until you brake**. That describes the emergency line, not the service line. (p. 6-7)
- **Trap:** The trailer air tanks are filled by the service line. → **Correct:** By the **emergency (supply) line**. The service line only sends the "how much" signal. (p. 6-6)
- **Trap:** The "supply line" is another name for the service line. → **Correct:** Supply line = **emergency** line. Service = control = signal. (p. 6-5)
- **Trap:** Red 8-sided knob = parking brakes. → **Correct:** Red 8-sided = **trailer air supply**. Yellow diamond = parking brakes. (p. 6-5; p. 5-4)
- **Trap:** Shut-off valves should all be open. → **Correct:** All open **except the back of the last trailer**, which is **closed**. (p. 6-7)
- **Trap:** Crossed air lines always lock the trailer brakes. → **Correct:** On a trailer **with** spring brakes, yes — the brakes will not release. On an **old** trailer **without** spring brakes, you may drive away with **no trailer brakes**. (p. 6-6)
- **Trap:** Emergency brakes on a trailer without spring brakes will hold it when parked. → **Correct:** Only while tank air lasts. It leaks away — **chock the wheels**. (p. 6-7)
- **Trap:** Automatic drains mean you never drain the tanks. → **Correct:** **Still open the drains daily** to be sure. (p. 6-6)
- **Trap:** ABS shortens your stopping distance / adds braking power. → **Correct:** It does **not necessarily** shorten stopping and does not add power — it helps you **keep control**. (p. 6-8)
- **Trap:** With ABS you should brake differently (pump or stab). → **Correct:** Brake **as you always have**, the same way whether ABS is on the tractor, trailer, or both. (p. 6-8)
- **Trap:** Trailer ABS lamp is on the right side, or red. → **Correct:** **Yellow**, on the **left** side. (p. 6-8)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK 6.2 #7** Why is it wrong to use the trailer hand valve while you are driving?
→ It brakes only the trailer, which can make the trailer skid (and jackknife). The foot brake applies all the brakes on the rig, so the danger is much lower. Use the hand valve only to test the trailer brakes. (p. 6-5)

**TYK 6.2 #8** Explain what the trailer air supply control does.
→ It controls the tractor protection valve. On newer rigs it is a red 8-sided knob: push it in to supply air to the trailer; pull it out to shut off the air and put on the trailer emergency brakes. It pops out on its own (closing the tractor protection valve) when air pressure drops to 20–45 psi. Older rigs may use a lever with "normal" and "emergency" positions that may not work automatically. (p. 6-5)

**TYK 6.2 #9** What is the service line used for?
→ It carries air whose pressure you control with the foot brake or trailer hand valve; the harder you brake, the higher its pressure. It is connected to the relay valves, which let the trailer brakes come on faster than they otherwise could; its pressure tells the relay valves how much pressure to send from the trailer tanks to the trailer brakes. (p. 6-5, 6-6)

**TYK 6.2 #10** What is the purpose of the emergency air line?
→ Two jobs: it supplies air to fill the trailer air tanks, and it controls the trailer emergency brakes. If it loses pressure, the trailer emergency brakes come on and the tractor protection valve closes (the knob pops out). (p. 6-5)

**TYK 6.2 #11** When you park a trailer that has no spring brakes, why do you need wheel chocks?
→ Its emergency brakes work only from air stored in the trailer tank, and it has no parking brake. That air eventually leaks away, and then there are no brakes to hold the trailer. (p. 6-7)

**TYK 6.2 #12** Where are the shut-off valves located?
→ At the rear of any trailer that tows another trailer, fitted in both the service line and the supply line. Keep them open everywhere except at the rear of the last trailer, where they are closed. (p. 6-7)

**TYK 6.3–6.4 #17** How can you tell whether a trailer has anti-lock brakes?
→ Look for the yellow ABS malfunction lamp on the left side of the trailer (front or rear corner). Every trailer and converter dolly made March 1, 1998 or later must have ABS. On an older unit, check underneath for the ECU and for wheel speed sensor wires leading from the back of the brakes. (p. 6-8)

## One-minute recap
- **Hand valve** (trolley valve, Johnson bar): **test only**. Never while driving (trailer skid), never to park (air leaks off).
- **Tractor protection valve** keeps the tractor's air safe; closes by itself at **20–45 psi**, venting the emergency line so the **trailer emergency brakes** come on.
- **Red 8-sided knob** = trailer air supply: **in** = air to trailer; **out** = air off + trailer emergency brakes. (Yellow diamond = parking brakes.)
- **Service line** = control/signal, **blue**, tells relay valves how hard to brake. **Emergency line** = supply, **red**, fills trailer tanks and controls emergency brakes.
- Emergency line loses air → trailer emergency brakes come on and the tractor protection valve closes. Major service line leak → may go unnoticed **until you brake**.
- **Glad hands**: clean, check seals, couplers at **90°**, match blue-to-blue and red-to-red. Crossed lines: spring brakes won't release — or an old trailer may have **no brakes**. Use **dummy couplers**.
- Drain trailer tanks **every day**. **Shut-off valves** open except at the **back of the last trailer**. No spring brakes (converter dollies and trailers built before **1975** may lack them) → **chock** when parked.
- **ABS** required on trailers/dollies built on or after **March 1, 1998**; **yellow lamp, left side**. It helps control, not stopping distance. Brake normally; if it fails you still have regular brakes.
