# GK-04 · Vehicle inspection

> **Handbook:** Section 2.1.1–2.1.7, pages 2-1–2-9 · **Test:** General Knowledge · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~45 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- Why you inspect, what the law says, and who is responsible for a safe vehicle.
- When to inspect (before, during, after a trip), including the cargo check within the first **50 miles**, then every **150 miles or 3 hours**.
- The defects to look for in tires, wheels, brakes, steering, suspension and exhaust, with the key numbers (**4/32** and **2/32 inch**, **10 degrees**, **¼**).
- The **3 kinds** of required emergency equipment.
- The 7-step inspection method: gauge readings, light colors, the key, and the three brake tests.

## Learn it
*Terms used below:* **defect** = something broken, worn, missing or not working right. **Out-of-service (OOS)** = an official order that the vehicle may not be driven until repaired. **Motor carrier** = the company that runs the vehicle (usually your employer).

### 2.1.1 Why inspect (p. 2-1)
- **Safety is the most important reason**: safety for you and for everyone else on the road.
- Finding a defect early also prevents breakdowns (lost time and money) and crashes.
- Drivers must inspect their vehicles under both **federal and state law**.
- Federal and state **inspectors** may also inspect your vehicle. One they find unsafe is placed **out-of-service** until repaired.

### 2.1.2 Types of vehicle inspections (pp. 2-1 – 2-2)
**Before the trip (pre-trip)** (p. 2-1)
- Inspect every time before you drive.
- Read the **last vehicle inspection report** (the previous driver's written list of problems). If mechanics work on the vehicle, make sure they **released it for service**.
- The **motor carrier** must fix every safety item on the report and **certify** (state in writing) on it that each repair was done or was not needed.
- When you drive, safe operation is **your** job, **not the mechanic's**. *Why:* you are the one in control; the mechanic is not there when something fails.
- When the listed defects have been fixed, you **sign the earlier driver's report**.

**During the trip** (p. 2-2)
- Keep an eye on your **gauges**, and **look, listen, smell and feel** for anything unusual.
- At every stop, check: tires, wheels and rims; brakes; lights and reflectors; brake and electrical connections to the trailer; trailer coupling devices; cargo securement devices.

**After the trip** (p. 2-2)
- At the end of the **trip, day or tour of duty** (work shift), inspect **each vehicle you drove**. You may fill in a vehicle condition report. *Why:* the carrier learns from it which repairs are needed.

### 2.1.3 What to look for (pp. 2-2 – 2-4)

**Tires** (p. 2-2). *Tread* = the rubber pattern that touches the road; *groove* = a channel in the tread.
- Air pressure too high or too low.
- **Tread depth:** front tires at least **4/32 inch** in **every major groove**; all other tires at least **2/32 inch**. No fabric showing through the tread or sidewall. *Why:* front tires steer, so they need the most grip.
- Cuts or other damage; **tread separation** (tread coming off the tire body).
- **Dual tires** (two side by side) touching each other or parts of the vehicle. *Why:* rubbing wears and heats them.
- Mismatched sizes. **Radial and bias-ply tires mixed** (two different inside constructions that do not handle the same).
- Cut or cracked **valve stems** (the tube where you add air).
- **Bus:** regrooved, recapped or retreaded tires are **prohibited on the front wheels**. (Regrooved = new grooves cut into worn tread; recapped/retreaded = new tread on an old tire body.)

*Worked example:* a front tire has 3/32 inch in one major groove → below 4/32 → **defect**. A rear tire with 3/32 inch → **OK** (needs only 2/32).

**Wheels and rims** (p. 2-2). *Rim* = the metal part that holds the tire; *lug nut* = a nut that holds the wheel on; *lock ring* = a split ring that holds the tire on some rims.
- Damaged rims.
- **Rust around the wheel nuts** can mean they are **loose**, so check them. After a **tire change**, stop again a short time later and **recheck the nuts**.
- Missing clamps, spacers, studs or lugs; mismatched, bent or cracked lock rings: both dangerous.
- A wheel or rim **repaired by welding** is **unsafe**.

**Brake drums and shoes** (p. 2-2). *Drum* = the round metal part that turns with the wheel; the brake *shoes* press against it (on disc brakes, *pads* press on a disc).
- Cracked drums; **oil, grease or brake fluid** on the shoes or pads (*why:* they cannot grip); shoes that are broken, missing or worn dangerously thin.

**Steering** (p. 2-2). Key parts (Figure 2.1): steering wheel, steering shaft, gear box, pitman arm, drag link, steering arm, tie rod, steering knuckle, spindle; with power steering, also the power steering cylinder and hydraulic fluid reservoir.
- Missing nuts, bolts, **cotter keys** (split pins that stop nuts coming off) or other parts.
- Bent, loose or broken parts such as the steering column, gear box or tie rods.
- Power steering: check hoses, pumps, fluid level and leaks.
- **Play** (how far the wheel turns before the tires move) of **more than 10 degrees** makes the vehicle hard to steer. On a **20-inch** wheel that is about **2 inches** at the rim.

*Worked example:* a 20-inch wheel moves 3 inches at the rim before the tires react → more than 10 degrees → defect.

**Suspension** (p. 2-3). The suspension carries the weight of the vehicle and cargo and keeps the axles in position, so broken parts are **extremely dangerous**. *Leaf spring* = a stack of curved steel strips; *spring hanger* = the bracket joining a spring to the frame; *torque rod* = a bar that holds the axle in place. Look for:
- Spring hangers that let the axle move out of position.
- Missing or broken leaves. Missing **¼ or more** of the leaves = **out-of-service**, but **any** defect can be dangerous.
- Broken leaves in a multi-leaf spring, or leaves shifted so they could hit a tire or other part.
- **Leaking shock absorbers.**
- Any part that holds an axle in position (torque rod or arm, u-bolt, spring hanger) that is cracked, damaged or missing.
- Damaged or leaking **air suspension**; loose, cracked, broken or missing **frame members**.

*Worked example:* a spring has 8 leaves. ¼ of 8 = 2, so 2 or more missing = out-of-service.

**Exhaust** (p. 2-3). *Why it matters:* a broken exhaust can let **poisonous fumes into the cab or sleeper berth**. Look for loose, broken or missing pipes, mufflers, tailpipes, vertical stacks, brackets, clamps, bolts or nuts; exhaust parts **rubbing** fuel system parts, tires or other moving parts; and exhaust **leaks**.

**Emergency equipment** (pp. 2-4 – 2-5). **Three kinds are required:**
1. **Fire extinguisher(s)**, properly charged and rated.
2. **Spare electrical fuses**, unless the vehicle has circuit breakers.
3. **Warning devices for parked vehicles**, for example **3 red reflective triangles**, **6 fusees** or **3 liquid-burning flares**.

The handbook prints "6 fuses" in that list; there it means **fusees** (road flares), not electrical fuses.
**Optional** (p. 2-5): tire chains (where winter conditions require them), tire-changing equipment, emergency phone numbers, accident reporting kit.

### 2.1.4 The CDL vehicle inspection test (p. 2-4)
- To get a CDL you must also pass a hands-on **vehicle inspection test** at a real vehicle (details in Section 11). It checks that you can tell whether your vehicle is safe.
- For each item you must **point to or touch it, name it, and explain why** you check it.
- **Cargo (trucks):** before every trip, check the truck is **not overloaded** and the load is **balanced and secured**. With **HazMat**, check for the right **papers and placards**.

### 2.1.5 The 7-step inspection method (pp. 2-4 – 2-8)
Always follow the **same order**; a fixed routine helps you remember every step.

| Step | What you do | Engine |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Overall condition, area around | Off |
| 1 | Review the last inspection report | Off |
| 2 | Engine compartment | Off |
| 3 | Start engine, inspect inside the cab | **On** |
| 4 | Engine off, check lights | **Off, key with you** |
| 5 | Walk-around | Off |
| 6 | Check signal lights | Off |
| 7 | Start engine, test brakes | **On** |

**Approach** (p. 2-4): look for damage or the vehicle **leaning to one side**; check under it for **fresh** oil, coolant, grease or fuel; check the area for hazards (people, vehicles, objects, low wires, limbs).

**Step 1 – Vehicle overview** (p. 2-4): review the last report (drivers may have to write one daily). You sign it **only when** it listed defects **and** the carrier certified them as fixed or not needing repair.

**Step 2 – Engine compartment** (p. 2-4): **first** make sure the **parking brakes are on and/or the wheels are chocked**. (*Why:* the vehicle must not roll while you work in front of it.) If you tilt the cab, secure loose items first. Check: engine oil; coolant level and hoses; power steering fluid and hose; washer fluid; battery fluid, connections and tie-downs; automatic transmission fluid (engine may need to run); **belts** (alternator, water pump, air compressor) for tightness and wear (learn how much "give" a correctly adjusted belt has, and check every belt); **leaks** of any fluid; wiring insulation for cracks and wear. Then close and secure the hood, cab or door.

**Step 3 – Start the engine and inspect inside the cab** (pp. 2-4 – 2-6)
- Parking brake **on**; gearshift in **neutral** (**park** if automatic). Start the engine and **listen** for unusual noises.
- **ABS** (anti-lock brakes) dash light should **light up briefly, then go out**. If it **stays on**, the ABS has a fault. Trailers: the **yellow lamp on the left rear** works the same way.

| Gauge (p. 2-5) | Normal |
|---|---|
| Oil pressure | Normal **within seconds**. Figure 2.5: **5–20 psi** idling, **35–75 psi** operating. Low, dropping or **fluctuating** → **stop immediately** (no oil can ruin the engine fast). |
| Air pressure | **50 → 90 psi within 3 minutes**; build to **governor cut-out**, usually **120–140 psi**. Know your vehicle. |
| Ammeter / voltmeter | Normal range |
| Coolant and engine oil temperature | Rise **gradually** to normal |
| Warning lights and buzzers | Oil, coolant, charging and ABS lights go **out right away** |

*psi* = pounds per square inch. *Governor cut-out* = the pressure where the compressor stops pumping (you learned it for Air Brakes). Note: the Air Brakes section (p. 5-9) says 50–90 psi in 3 minutes is for older **single** air systems, and **dual** systems should build about 85–100 psi in **45 seconds**. For a question on the section 2.1 check, answer **50–90 psi within 3 minutes**.

- **Controls** (p. 2-5): check for looseness, sticking, damage or wrong setting: steering wheel, clutch, accelerator, brake controls (foot, trailer, parking, retarder), transmission controls, inter-axle differential lock, horn(s), wipers/washer, and all light switches.
- **Mirrors and windshield** (p. 2-5): no cracks, dirt, illegal stickers or other view blockers; clean and adjust.
- **Emergency equipment** (p. 2-5): as listed in 2.1.3.
- **Safety belt** (p. 2-6): firmly attached, adjusts and buckles correctly, no rips or fraying.

**Step 4 – Engine off, check lights** (p. 2-6): parking brake set, engine off, **take the key with you**. Turn on the **low beams** and **4-way flashers**; get out.

**Step 5 – Walk-around** (pp. 2-6 – 2-8)
- At the front: low beams on, **both** 4-way flashers working; check the **high beams** with the dimmer switch. Then headlights and 4-ways off; parking, clearance, side-marker and identification lights on; **right** turn signal on. Wipe lights, reflectors and glass clean as you go.
- Walk order: **left front → front → right side → right rear → rear → left side.**

| Where | Key checks |
|---|---|
| Every wheel | Rim and wheel: no missing, bent or broken studs, clamps, lugs or spacers. Tire: inflated, valve stem and cap in place, no serious cuts, bulges or tread wear. **Wrench-test rust-streaked lug nuts.** Front hub oil level OK; **wheel bearings/seals not leaking**. Rear duals: not rubbing, nothing stuck between, same type and size. |
| Suspension and brakes (each axle) | Springs, spring hangers, shackles, u-bolts, shock absorbers; torque rod arms and bushings; axles secure, drive axles not leaking gear oil; lift axle mechanism and air ride parts. Brake drum or disc, hoses (no rubbing wear), **brake adjustment**. |
| Left front side | Driver's door glass clean; latches and locks work |
| Front | Front axle; steering system with no loose, worn, bent, damaged or missing parts — **grab the steering mechanism** to test for looseness; windshield, wiper arm spring tension, wiper blades (no damage, no "stiff" rubber, secure) |
| Right side | **Cab-over-engine:** primary and secondary safety cab locks engaged. Fuel tanks secure, not leaking, crossover line secure, enough fuel, caps on |
| Under/along the side | Engine rear and transmission not leaking; exhaust secure and **not touching wires, fuel or air lines**; frame and cross members not bent or cracked; air lines and wiring secured; spare tire secure, right size, inflated |
| Cargo (trucks) | Blocked, braced, tied, chained; header board and stakes sound; tarp secured so it cannot **tear, billow or block mirrors or lights**; oversize signs, flags, lamps and **permits with the driver**; doors latched, security seals in place |
| Rear | Lights and reflectors; license plate present and clean; **splash guards** fastened, not dragging or rubbing tires; tailboards up; end gates secured in stake sockets; rear doors closed and locked |
| Left side | Same as the right side, **plus batteries** (if not under the hood): box secure with cover, batteries can't move, not broken or leaking; fluid level, cell caps and vents (not on maintenance-free batteries) |

**Light colors** (pp. 2-6 – 2-8)

| Light or reflector | Color |
|---|---|
| Front clearance, identification, parking lights; front reflectors | **Amber** |
| Front turn signals | **Amber or white** |
| Side markers (lights and reflectors) | **Red at rear, amber elsewhere** |
| Rear clearance and identification lights, rear reflectors, taillights | **Red** |
| Rear turn signals and stop lights | **Red, yellow or amber** |

**Step 6 – Signal lights** (p. 2-8): get in, turn off all lights. Turn on the **stop lights** (set the trailer hand brake or have a helper press the pedal) and the **left** turn signal. Get out and check them. **Brake, turn-signal and 4-way flasher checks must be done separately** (*why:* with several on at once you cannot tell which one works). Then get in, turn off lights you don't need, check required **papers** (trip manifests, permits), **secure loose items** in the cab (they can jam the controls or hit you in a crash), and start the engine.

**Step 7 – Start the engine and check the brakes** (p. 2-8)
- **Hydraulic brake leak test:** pump the pedal **3 times**, then press firmly and **hold 5 seconds**. The pedal should **not move**; if it does, suspect a leak and have it fixed before driving. (With air brakes, do the checks in Sections 5 and 6 instead: Section 5 is the Air Brakes material you already learned; Section 6 covers combination vehicles.)
- **Parking brake test:** seat belt on; set the power unit parking brake, release the trailer's; **low gear**; **gently pull forward** against it — it must hold. With a trailer, repeat the other way round (trailer brake set, power unit released). Not holding = faulty; get it fixed.
- **Service brake test:** drive about **5 mph**, push the pedal firmly. **Pulling** to one side, a strange pedal feel or a **late** stop can mean brake trouble.
- Anything unsafe must be repaired first: **federal and state law bar you from driving an unsafe vehicle.**

### 2.1.6 Inspection during a trip (p. 2-8)
- Check regularly: instruments, air pressure gauge (air brakes), temperature and pressure gauges, ammeter/voltmeter, mirrors, tires, lights, cargo and cargo covers. Anything odd you **see, hear, smell or feel**: find out what it is.
- **Cargo securement** (trucks and truck tractors carrying cargo): first check within the **first 50 miles**, then every **150 miles or 3 hours, whichever comes first**. *Why:* cargo shifts and tie-downs loosen as you drive.

*Worked examples:* at 60 mph, 150 miles takes 2.5 hours → the **150-mile** limit comes first. At 30 mph, 3 hours covers only 90 miles → the **3-hour** limit comes first.

### 2.1.7 After-trip inspection and report (p. 2-9)
- You may have to write a **daily report** on each vehicle you drove, listing anything that affects **safety** or could cause a **breakdown**. The carrier uses it to plan repairs.
- **Keep a copy in the vehicle for 1 day**, so the next driver can learn about the problems.

*Beyond the handbook — not on the test:* federal rules make the carrier keep the original report for 3 months. The test's answer for the copy in the vehicle is **1 day**.

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** "The main reason to inspect is that the law requires it" → **Correct:** **safety**. The law and cost savings are real reasons, so they tempt. (p. 2-1)
- **Trap:** "The mechanic signed off, so the mechanic is responsible" → **Correct:** **you** are responsible once you drive. (p. 2-1)
- **Trap:** 2/32 front and 4/32 others → **Correct:** **4/32 front, 2/32 others**. Easy to swap. (p. 2-2)
- **Trap:** "Rust around lug nuts is only cosmetic" → **Correct:** the nuts may be **loose**; wrench-test them. (pp. 2-2, 2-6)
- **Trap:** "A rim repaired by welding is fixed" → **Correct:** welded wheels or rims are **not safe**. (p. 2-2)
- **Trap:** leaf spring out-of-service only when **half** the leaves are gone → **Correct:** **¼ or more**; and any defect can be dangerous. (p. 2-3)
- **Trap:** tire chains are required emergency equipment → **Correct:** **optional**. Required: extinguisher, spare fuses, warning devices. (pp. 2-4, 2-5)
- **Trap:** 6 triangles → **Correct:** **3** triangles; "6" is the number of **fusees**. (p. 2-4)
- **Trap:** "air pressure should build within 45 seconds" → **Correct for section 2.1:** **50–90 psi within 3 minutes**. The 45 seconds is the dual-air test from Air Brakes, which you studied recently. (pp. 2-5, 5-9)
- **Trap:** "Coolant temperature should be normal right after start-up" → **Correct:** temperatures rise **gradually**; it is **oil pressure** that must be normal within seconds. (p. 2-5)
- **Trap:** "An ABS light that stays on is normal until you drive" → **Correct:** it should go **out**; staying on = ABS fault. (p. 2-4)
- **Trap:** pump 5 times, hold 3 seconds → **Correct:** pump **3**, hold **5**. (p. 2-8)
- **Trap:** cargo check in the first 100 miles, or 150 miles/3 hours "whichever comes **last**" → **Correct:** first **50 miles**, then 150 miles/3 hours, **whichever comes first**. (p. 2-8)

## Handbook review questions — answered
**TYK Subsection 2.1 #1** What is the top reason for inspecting your vehicle?
→ **Safety**, for you and for everyone else on the road. (p. 2-1)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #2** What should you check while on a trip?
→ Gauges and instruments (air pressure, temperature, pressure, ammeter/voltmeter), mirrors, tires, lights, and cargo and cargo covers; use your senses. At stops: tires, wheels and rims; brakes; lights and reflectors; brake and electrical connections to the trailer; trailer coupling devices; cargo securement devices. Cargo securement: within the first 50 miles, then every 150 miles or 3 hours, whichever comes first. (pp. 2-2, 2-8)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #3** Name some main steering system parts.
→ Steering wheel, steering shaft, steering column, gear box, pitman arm, drag link, steering arm, tie rod, steering knuckle, spindle; with power steering, the power steering cylinder and hydraulic fluid reservoir. (p. 2-2)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #4** Name some suspension system defects.
→ Spring hangers that let the axle move out of position; missing or broken leaves (¼ or more missing = out-of-service); broken or shifted leaves that could hit a tire; leaking shock absorbers; cracked, damaged or missing axle-positioning parts (spring hangers, torque rods or arms, u-bolts); damaged or leaking air suspension; loose, cracked, broken or missing frame members. (p. 2-3)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #5** Which three kinds of emergency equipment are required?
→ Fire extinguisher(s); spare electrical fuses (unless the vehicle has circuit breakers); warning devices for parked vehicles (3 red reflective triangles, 6 fusees or 3 liquid-burning flares). (p. 2-4)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #6** How much tread must front tires have, and how much do the other tires need?
→ Front: **4/32 inch** in every major groove. Other tires: **2/32 inch**. (p. 2-2)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #7** What do you check at the front of the vehicle on the walk-around?
→ Low beams, high beams and both 4-way flashers work; front axle; steering system (no loose, worn, bent, damaged or missing parts; grab it to test for looseness); windshield, wiper arms and blades; front lights and reflectors clean, working and amber (front turn signals amber or white). (p. 2-6)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #8** What problem do you look for at the wheel bearing seals?
→ **Leaks** — they must not leak. (p. 2-7)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #9** What number of red reflective triangles do you need on board?
→ **Three.** (p. 2-4)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #10** What is the leak test for hydraulic brakes?
→ Pump the pedal **3 times**, then press firmly and hold **5 seconds**. The pedal should not move; if it does, there may be a leak, so fix it before driving. (p. 2-8)

**TYK Subsection 2.1 #11** Why keep the starter key in your pocket during the inspection test?
→ Step 4 says to take the key with you after turning off the engine. The handbook does not state the reason; the safety logic is that **no one can start or move the vehicle** while you are around or under it. (p. 2-6)

## Practice test (real-exam style)
1. What is the most important reason for inspecting your vehicle?
   a) Safety for yourself and other road users
   b) Inspectors can put an unsafe vehicle out-of-service
   c) Breakdowns cost time and money

2. You are checking tires. Which one is a defect?
   a) A front tire with 5/32 inch in every major groove
   b) A rear tire with 3/32 inch of tread
   c) A front tire with 3/32 inch in one major groove

3. You see rust around the wheel nuts. This may mean:
   a) The wheel was recently changed
   b) The nuts are loose
   c) The rim was repaired by welding

4. Steering wheel play of more than how much makes the vehicle hard to steer?
   a) 5 degrees
   b) 10 degrees
   c) 15 degrees

5. A leaf spring is missing ¼ of its leaves. What is true?
   a) The vehicle will be put out-of-service
   b) It is legal if the other leaves are not cracked
   c) It is only a problem when fully loaded

6. Which of these is NOT required emergency equipment?
   a) A properly charged and rated fire extinguisher
   b) Warning devices for parked vehicles
   c) Tire chains

7. How many red reflective triangles must you carry?
   a) 2
   b) 3
   c) 6

8. You start the engine. The ABS light on the dash comes on and stays on. This means:
   a) It is normal; the light goes off once you drive
   b) The trailer ABS is being tested
   c) The ABS is not working properly

9. After you start the engine, oil pressure should:
   a) Come up to normal within seconds
   b) Rise slowly over about 3 minutes
   c) Stay low until the engine is warm

10. In the vehicle inspection section, air pressure should build from 50 to 90 psi within:
    a) 45 seconds
    b) 2 minutes
    c) 3 minutes

11. Why do you put the key in your pocket during the inspection test?
    a) So no one can start or move the vehicle while you inspect it
    b) So the battery does not run down
    c) Because the examiner must hold it

12. During the walk-around, the clearance lights and reflectors on the front should be:
    a) Red
    b) Amber

## One-minute recap
- **Safety** is the #1 reason to inspect; the law requires it, and **you** (not the mechanic) are responsible.
- Inspect before, during and after every trip. Cargo: first **50 miles**, then every **150 miles or 3 hours** (whichever first). Report copy stays in the vehicle **1 day**.
- Tires: **4/32 inch** front, **2/32** others; no radial/bias mix; no regrooved, recapped or retreaded tires on a **bus's front wheels**.
- Rust at wheel nuts = maybe **loose**; welded rims **unsafe**; steering play over **10 degrees** too much; **¼** of leaf spring missing = out-of-service; broken exhaust = **poison fumes**.
- Required: **fire extinguisher**, **spare fuses** (unless circuit breakers), **warning devices** (**3** triangles, 6 fusees or 3 flares).
- Gauges: oil pressure normal **within seconds**; air **50–90 psi in 3 minutes**; cut-out **120–140 psi**; temperatures rise **gradually**; ABS light on, then off.
- Step 4: engine off, **key in your pocket**. Lights: **amber front, red rear**. Check brake lights, turn signals and 4-ways **separately**.
- Step 7: **pump 3, hold 5** (hydraulic); pull gently against the parking brake in low gear; service brakes at **5 mph**, watch for pulling.
