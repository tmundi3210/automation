# 00 · Start here — California CDL written tests: General Knowledge + Combination Vehicles

You passed Air Brakes. Two written tests are left. This pack teaches both from zero, using the official **California Commercial Driver Handbook (DL 650)** — the book the DMV writes its questions from.

## What's in this pack
- **18 lessons**: GK-01 … GK-14 (General Knowledge) and CV-01 … CV-04 (Combination Vehicles).
- Every lesson has the same parts: **Learn it** (plain-English teaching, with the reason behind each rule) → **Numbers & terms to memorize** → **Exam traps** → **Handbook review questions — answered** → **Flashcards** → **Practice test** (3 options, like the real test) with answer key → **One-minute recap**.
- Totals: **338 practice questions**, **657 flashcards**, and all **98** of the handbook's own "Test Your Knowledge" questions answered. The handbook says those questions "may be on your test".
- Every lesson was fact-checked line by line against the handbook by a separate reviewer. Page numbers like "(p. 2-16)" point to the handbook page, so you can always check.

**Test-day facts**
- Touchscreen test. It shows right away when an answer is wrong, and how many questions are left. Test-takers report a skip option that brings a few questions back at the end.
- No official time limit. The DMV will not start a knowledge test within about 30 minutes of closing.
- Phone, smartwatch, notes, or anyone helping you = **automatic fail** (p. 1-10).
- You get **3 tries per test** on one application (p. 1-4). Tests you already passed stay passed — you do not retake Air Brakes.
- **Language:** the handbook lists English, Arabic, Chinese, Punjabi, Russian and Spanish (p. 1-10). Federal English-proficiency enforcement for truck drivers has been tightening since 2025, so **confirm with the DMV which languages it offers when you book** (1-800-777-0133). These lessons prepare you to take it in English.
- The DMV often flips the wording you practiced: "Which is **NOT**…", "all **EXCEPT**…", "True or **False**". Read every question twice.
- In a DMV study, **39.5%** failed GK and **43.9%** failed Combination on the first try. Most misses are exact numbers and trap wording, which is what the Numbers and Exam traps sections are for.

## Answer the HANDBOOK way
Websites, practice apps, federal law and the California car handbook sometimes give a different answer. **The DMV test follows this handbook.**

| Topic | Test answer (handbook) | What you may see elsewhere |
|---|---|---|
| 1st violation of an out-of-service order | **90 days** (2nd in 10 years: 1 year · 3rd: 3 years) | 180 days (current federal law) |
| Dim your high beams | within **500 ft** of an oncoming vehicle **and** when following within **500 ft** | 300 ft when following (car handbook) |
| Hazmat placard size | at least **9.84 in (250 mm)** square, on point | 10¾ in (older manuals) |
| Tell your employer your license was suspended | by the end of the **next business day** (p. 1-4) — or **2 business days** (p. 1-16). The handbook says both; pick whichever is offered | 30 days (that is for convictions) |
| Tie-down strength | total working load limit at least **½** the cargo weight | 1½× |
| CDL needed for passengers [CA] | more than **10** people, including the driver | 16 or more (federal) |
| Fifth wheel before coupling | **tilted down toward the rear** of the tractor | "level" |
| Tractor protection valve / red knob pops out | **20–45 psi** | 25–40 psi |
| Trailer hand valve | GK (p. 2-9): may hold a combination from rolling back when starting. **Combination test (6.2): only for testing trailer brakes** — never to park, never while driving, never to straighten a jackknife | — |

## The most-missed questions
Built from web research into large California CDL question banks and "most-missed" statistics, with every answer checked against the handbook. The lesson in brackets teaches it.

**General Knowledge**
1. An **empty** truck needs a **longer** stopping distance than a loaded one — less weight, less traction. [GK-07]
2. Total stopping distance at 55 mph = **419 ft** (142 perception + 61 reaction + 216 braking). [GK-07]
3. Double your speed → about **4×** the braking distance. [GK-07]
4. Following distance: **1 second per 10 ft** of length, **+1 second above 40 mph**. A 30-ft truck at 55 mph → **4 s**. [GK-08]
5. Someone tailgates you → **increase your following distance**. Don't speed up, and no brake-light tricks. [GK-08]
6. Tight right turn → turn wide **as you complete** it (button hook), never swing left first. [GK-08]
7. Convex mirrors make things look **smaller and farther away**. [GK-06]
8. Warning triangles within **10 minutes**. Divided highway: **10, 100, 200 ft behind**. Two-lane road: within 10 ft of a corner, **100 ft behind, 100 ft ahead**. Hill or curve: the rear one goes **100–500 ft** back. [GK-06]
9. Retarders: turn them **off** on wet, icy or snowy roads — they can skid the drive wheels. [GK-05]
10. Back toward the **driver's side**, with a helper. With a trailer, turn the wheel the **opposite** way. [GK-05]
11. Pick your downhill gear **before** the grade, usually **lower** than your climbing gear. [GK-05, GK-11]
12. Long downgrade: brake to about **5 mph below** your safe speed, release, repeat. [GK-11]
13. Railroad crossing: stop **15–50 ft** from the rail, **never shift** on the tracks. Clearing a single track takes **at least 14 s**, a double track **more than 15 s**. [GK-11]
14. Use **high beams whenever safe and legal**. Dim within **500 ft**. [GK-10]
15. Hot tires: **never** let air out. Radiator cap: only when cool enough to **touch bare-handed**. [GK-10]
16. Tire blowout: hold the wheel firmly and **stay off the brake** until you slow down. [GK-12]
17. Something in your path: steering is usually faster than stopping — go **right**. Off the road: avoid braking until about **20 mph**. [GK-12]
18. ABS: **brake normally**. It does not shorten stopping distance. No stab braking with ABS. [GK-12]
19. Drive-wheel braking skid: **stop braking**, countersteer. [GK-12]
20. At a crash, **protect the area first**. [GK-13]
21. Fire: never pull into a service station, don't open the hood, keep cargo doors shut, aim at the **base**. A B:C extinguisher is for **electrical and burning liquids**. [GK-13]
22. Alcohol in a CMV: **.04** is DUI. **Any** detectable alcohol = **24 hours** out of service. Only time sobers you up. [GK-02, GK-13]
23. Check cargo within the first **50 miles**, then every **3 hours or 150 miles**, and after every break. [GK-14]
24. Tie-downs: at least **1 per 10 ft**, never fewer than **2** (a 20-ft load needs 2). [GK-14]
25. The bridge formula allows **less** weight on axles that are **closer** together. [GK-14]
26. Tread depth: **4/32 in** front tires, **2/32 in** others. Carry **3** red reflective triangles. [GK-04]
27. [CA] Trucks with 3+ axles and anything towing: **55 mph** max. SR-1 to DMV within **10 days** (injury, death, or over $1,000 damage). [GK-03]
28. [CA] Intrastate hours: **12 hours driving, 16 hours on duty** (11/14 is federal). [GK-03]
29. Serious violations: **2 in 3 years = 60 days**, 3 = 120 days. Excessive speeding = **15+ mph** over. [GK-02]
30. You may haul hazmat without an H endorsement **only if the load does not need placards**. [GK-13]

**Combination Vehicles**
1. A **fully loaded** rig is **10×** more likely to roll over than an empty one. Keep cargo low and take turns slowly. [CV-01]
2. In a sudden move, the **last trailer** is most likely to tip. A 2.0 rating means it tips **2×** as easily as the tractor. [CV-01]
3. Empty rigs and **bobtail** tractors take **longer** to stop. [CV-01]
4. Trailer skid (jackknife): **release the brakes**. Never use the hand valve to straighten it. You usually spot it first in your **mirrors**. [CV-01]
5. Trailer hand valve: **only for testing** trailer brakes. Never for parking, never while driving. [CV-02]
6. Emergency line loses air → the tractor protection valve **closes** and the trailer emergency brakes come on. [CV-02]
7. **Red 8-sided** knob = trailer air supply: push in to supply air, pull out for the trailer emergency brakes. **Yellow diamond** = parking brakes. [CV-02]
8. **Red** = emergency (supply) line, which fills the trailer tanks. **Blue** = service (control) line, which carries the brake signal. [CV-02]
9. The service line comes apart → nothing happens **until you brake**. [CV-02]
10. Crossed air lines: a trailer **with** spring brakes won't release. An old trailer **without** spring brakes can be driven with **no trailer brakes**. [CV-02]
11. Shut-off valves: **open** everywhere except the rear of the **last** trailer (**closed**). [CV-02, CV-04]
12. Dummy couplers keep **dirt and water** out of the lines. [CV-02]
13. Trailer ABS lamp: **yellow**, **left side**. Required on trailers and dollies built **on or after March 1, 1998**. [CV-02]
14. Coupling starts: **inspect the fifth wheel** → inspect the area and chock → position the tractor. Lock the trailer brakes **before** backing under, then do the **tug test**. [CV-03]
15. Trailer height: low enough to be **raised slightly**. Too high = may not couple. Too low = you hit the trailer nose. [CV-03]
16. After coupling: **no space** between the upper and lower fifth wheel, with the jaws around the **shank** of the kingpin. [CV-03, CV-04]
17. Landing gear: raised **all the way**, crank handle secured. [CV-03]
18. Uncoupling: stop with the **tractor frame still under the trailer**. [CV-03]
19. Tractor protection valve test: **engine off**, pump the pedal — the knob pops out at **20–45 psi**. [CV-04]
20. Trailer emergency brakes: pull the knob out, tug gently. Trailer service brakes: move slowly and apply the **hand valve**. [CV-04]
