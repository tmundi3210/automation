# GK-02 · Disqualifications, violation points, hands-free rules

> **Handbook:** Section 1.3.1–1.3.9, pages 1-14–1-16 · **Test:** General Knowledge · **Exam weight:** High · **Study time:** ~35 min

## What you'll be able to answer
- The illegal blood alcohol level for CMV drivers, and the penalty for a small amount of alcohol below it.
- How long you lose your CDL for major offenses, serious traffic violations, out-of-service order violations, railroad crossing violations and hands-free/texting violations (1st, 2nd, 3rd time).
- The six railroad crossing offenses.
- How your personal-car record affects your CDL, and who cannot hold a HazMat endorsement.
- **[CA]** How points are counted, how long convictions stay on your record, and when you become a "negligent operator."

## Learn it

**Key words.** **CMV** = commercial motor vehicle (a truck or bus that needs a CDL). **Disqualified** ("lose your CDL") = not allowed to drive a CMV for a set time, or for life. **Conviction** = a court finds you guilty. **BAC** = blood alcohol concentration, the percent of alcohol in your blood. Most disqualification periods in this section are **minimums** ("at least").

### 1.3.1 General (p. 1-14)
Disqualified for any reason = you may not drive a CMV.

### 1.3.2 Alcohol, leaving the scene of an accident, and felonies (p. 1-14)
- **The limit:** driving a CMV with a BAC of **.04 percent or more** is illegal.
  *Why:* even a little alcohol slows your reactions, and a heavy vehicle leaves no room for error.
- **Implied consent:** by driving a CMV you are treated as having already agreed to alcohol testing. That is why refusing a test is punished like driving drunk.

**Major offenses: a first conviction = CDL lost for at least 1 year.**
1. Driving a CMV at .04 BAC or higher.
2. Driving a CMV under the influence of alcohol.
3. Refusing a blood alcohol test.
4. Driving a CMV under the influence of a controlled substance (a regulated or illegal drug).
5. Leaving the scene of an accident that involved a CMV.
6. A felony (serious crime) in which a CMV was used.
7. Driving a CMV while your CDL is suspended or revoked.
8. Causing a death by negligent (careless) CMV driving.

| Situation | CDL lost for |
|---|---|
| 1st major offense | **at least 1 year** |
| 1st major offense in a CMV **placarded for HazMat** (showing hazardous-material warning signs) | **at least 3 years** |
| **2nd** major offense | **life** |
| Using a CMV for a felony involving **controlled substances** | **life**, even the first time |

**Under .04 still stops you.** Any measurable alcohol below .04 = **out of service for 24 hours** (no driving for 24 hours).

*Examples:* BAC .02 → 24 hours out of service. BAC .05, first offense → at least 1 year. Same, but hauling a placarded HazMat load → at least 3 years.

### 1.3.3 Serious traffic violations (p. 1-14)
These are **serious traffic violations**:
- speeding **15 mph or more** over the posted limit ("excessive speeding");
- reckless driving;
- improper or erratic (unpredictable) lane changes;
- following too closely;
- a traffic offense in a CMV linked to a crash in which someone died;
- driving a CMV without ever getting a CDL, or without your CDL on you;
- driving a CMV without the correct CDL class and/or endorsements.

*Example:* limit 55 mph. Driving 70 (15 over) is a serious violation. Driving 65 is a ticket, but not "excessive speeding."

**Penalties (violations in a CMV):**
- **2** within **3 years** → at least **60 days**
- **3 or more** within **3 years** → at least **120 days**

One serious violation alone does not bring a disqualification. *Why:* repeats show a risky pattern.

### 1.3.4 Violations of out-of-service orders (p. 1-14)
An **out-of-service order** is an official order that you (or your vehicle) may not drive for a set time or until a problem is fixed. The 24-hour alcohol order above is one example. If you drive anyway:
- **1st** violation → at least **90 days**
- **2nd** within **10 years** → at least **1 year**
- **3rd or more** within **10 years** → at least **3 years**

*Why:* the order was given because driving right now is unsafe. Notice the **10-year** window (most other rules use 3 years).

> **Note:** Federal rules (49 CFR 383.51) give 180 days / 2 years / 3 years. The test follows the handbook: **90 days / 1 year / 3 years**.

### 1.3.5 Railroad-highway grade crossing violations (pp. 1-14 to 1-15)
A **grade crossing** is a place where a road crosses railroad tracks at the same level.
- **1st** violation → at least **60 days**
- **2nd** within **3 years** → at least **120 days**
- **3rd** within **3 years** → at least **1 year**

Unlike serious violations, the **first** railroad violation already costs 60 days.

Breaking a federal, state or local law or rule on any of these **6 offenses** counts:

| Who | Offense |
|---|---|
| Drivers not required to always stop | 1. Not stopping before the crossing when the tracks are not clear |
| Drivers not required to always stop | 2. Not slowing down to check that no train is coming |
| Drivers who must always stop (for example all passenger and HazMat vehicles, p. 2-35) | 3. Not stopping before going onto the crossing |
| All drivers | 4. Not having enough room to get all the way across without stopping |
| All drivers | 5. Disobeying a traffic control device, or an officer's instructions at the crossing |
| All drivers | 6. Getting stuck because the underside of the vehicle is too low (not enough undercarriage clearance) |

*Why:* a train cannot stop quickly. A truck stuck on the tracks can cause a disaster.

### 1.3.6 HazMat endorsement background check and disqualifications (p. 1-15)
For an H (HazMat) endorsement you must give **fingerprints** and pass a **background check**. You are denied the endorsement, or you lose it, if you:
- lack lawful permanent resident status in the U.S.;
- renounce (give up) your U.S. citizenship;
- are wanted for, or under indictment (formally charged) for, certain felonies;
- were convicted of certain felonies in a civilian or military court;
- were officially judged to lack mental capacity, or were involuntarily committed to a mental health facility (49 CFR §1572.109);
- are judged a security threat by the **TSA** (Transportation Security Administration).

More information: universalenroll.dhs.gov. *Why:* hazardous materials could be used to harm many people.

### 1.3.7 Traffic violations in your personal vehicle (p. 1-15)
Your CDL depends on how you drive **any** vehicle.
- A federal law, the **Motor Carrier Safety Improvement Act (MCSIA) of 1999**, makes certain moving-violation convictions in your own car a reason to take away your CMV driving privilege.
- Car driving privilege suspended, revoked or cancelled for **traffic-law** violations (parking does not count) → your **CDL privilege is lost too**.
- Car privilege suspended, revoked or cancelled for **alcohol, controlled substances or a felony** → **CDL lost for 1 year**. A **second** such violation, in your car **or** a CMV → **life**.
- You **cannot get a "hardship" license** (a limited license for necessary driving during a suspension) to drive a CMV.

*Why:* the habits you have in a car come with you into a truck.

### 1.3.8 [CA] Violation point counts (p. 1-15)
**How long convictions stay on your record** (convictions while driving a CMV or while holding a CDL, together with any disqualification actions):

| Type | Kept for |
|---|---|
| Major violations | **55 years** |
| Out-of-service violations | **15 years** |
| Accidents and serious violations | **10 years** |
| Railroad crossing violations | **4 years** |
| Minor convictions | **3 years** |

Memory hook, worst to least: **55 – 15 – 10 – 4 – 3**.

**Points:**
- **1 point:** an accident caused by unsafe driving; normally, any accident you caused, helped cause, or were at fault for.
- **2 points:** a conviction for reckless driving, hit-and-run, or DUI (driving while under the influence of alcohol, drugs or both).
- **In a CMV, a violation counts 1½ times** its points. *Why:* the same mistake is more dangerous in a heavy vehicle.
  *Example:* a 1-point violation in a CMV = 1½ points; a 2-point violation in a CMV = 3 points.

**Negligent operator** (a CMV driver with too many points; such a driver loses the privilege to drive):
- **4 points in 12 months**, or
- **6 points in 24 months**, or
- **8 points in 36 months**.

The limit can be higher in two cases:
- If you ask for and attend a **hearing**, you may be allowed **6, 8 or 10** points (instead of 4, 6 or 8), as long as those points were not earned in a noncommercial Class C vehicle.
- A **Class A or B** driver without a special certificate or endorsement may get **2 extra points** before being called negligent.

**Other states:** convictions reported from other states go on your California record and can lead to CDL penalties. If your CDL is from another state, California reports your convictions here to that state.

**Traffic school:** a commercial driver ticketed in a **noncommercial** vehicle may qualify for traffic school (CVC §42005(c)).

### 1.3.9 [CA] Violations of the hands-free or texting law (p. 1-16)
The **hands-free law** bans holding a phone to talk and dialing with more than one button press while driving; the **texting law** bans sending or reading texts while driving (both taught in GK-03, p. 1-16). The CDL penalties below apply **in any vehicle**, your own car included:
- **2nd** violation within **3 years** → at least **60 days** + **1 point**
- **3rd or later** within **3 years** → at least **120 days** + **1 point**

*Why:* a phone takes your eyes, hands and attention off the road.

### Summary: every disqualification period (pp. 1-14 to 1-16)

| Offense | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | Window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Major offenses (alcohol, test refusal, drugs, leaving the scene, felony with a CMV, driving while suspended, fatal negligence) | 1 year (3 years if HazMat placarded) | life | — | not stated |
| Felony involving controlled substances, using a CMV | life | — | — | — |
| Serious traffic violations (CMV) | none listed | 60 days | 120 days (3 or more) | 3 years |
| Out-of-service order violations | 90 days | 1 year | 3 years (3 or more) | 10 years |
| Railroad crossing violations | 60 days | 120 days | 1 year | 3 years |
| [CA] Hands-free / texting (any vehicle) | none listed | 60 days + 1 point | 120 days + 1 point | 3 years |
| Car privilege suspended for alcohol, drugs or felony | 1 year | life (car or CMV) | — | not stated |
| Measurable alcohol under .04 | 24 hours out of service | — | — | — |

## Exam traps
- **Trap:** .08 is the CMV limit → **Correct:** **.04** or more. .08 tempts because many people know it from car driving. (p. 1-14)
- **Trap:** Under .04 you may keep driving → **Correct:** any measurable alcohol = **24 hours out of service**. (p. 1-14)
- **Trap:** Refusing the test avoids a penalty → **Correct:** refusal = at least **1 year**, same as a DUI (implied consent). (p. 1-14)
- **Trap:** A second major offense = 3 years → **Correct:** **life**. "3 years" feels familiar because it is the HazMat number. (p. 1-14)
- **Trap:** One serious violation = 60 days → **Correct:** it takes **2 in 3 years**. The *railroad* rule is the one that gives 60 days for a first violation. (p. 1-14)
- **Trap:** Excessive speeding starts at 10 mph over → **Correct:** **15 mph or more**. (p. 1-14)
- **Trap:** First out-of-service violation = 180 days → **Correct:** **90 days** (handbook). 180 days is the federal number found in some practice banks. (p. 1-14)
- **Trap:** Out-of-service repeats are counted over 3 years → **Correct:** over **10 years**. (p. 1-14)
- **Trap:** Tickets in your own car can't touch your CDL → **Correct:** car suspensions can cost your CDL, and hands-free/texting violations count in **any** vehicle. (pp. 1-15, 1-16)
- **Trap:** You can get a hardship license to keep driving a truck → **Correct:** not for a CMV. (p. 1-15)
- **Trap:** A CMV violation counts double → **Correct:** **1½ times**. (p. 1-15)
- **Trap:** Accidents stay on the record 3 years → **Correct:** **10 years**; 3 years is for minor convictions. (p. 1-15)

## One-minute recap
- CMV alcohol limit **.04**; measurable alcohol under .04 = **24 hours out of service**; driving a CMV = consent to testing.
- Major offenses: **1 year** first time, **3 years** if HazMat placarded, **life** the second time; a drug felony using a CMV = **life** at once.
- Serious violations (speeding **15+ mph over**, reckless, erratic lane changes, tailgating, etc.): **2 in 3 years = 60 days**, **3+ = 120 days**.
- Out-of-service violations: **90 days / 1 year / 3 years** over **10 years** (handbook numbers, not federal).
- Railroad crossings: **60 days / 120 days / 1 year** over **3 years**; six offenses.
- Your car counts: alcohol/drug/felony suspension = **1 year**, second = **life**; no hardship license for a CMV.
- **[CA]** Record kept **55/15/10/4/3 years**; 2 points for reckless, DUI, hit-and-run; CMV violations count **1½×**; negligent operator at **4/12, 6/24, 8/36**.
- **[CA]** Hands-free/texting in any vehicle: 2nd in 3 years = **60 days + 1 point**; 3rd+ = **120 days + 1 point**.
